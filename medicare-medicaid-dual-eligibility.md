# Medicare-Medicaid Dual Eligibility

> **Educational only, NOT legal, medical, or financial advice.** Dual eligibility
> rules, Medicaid benefits, and D-SNP availability vary significantly by state.
> Contact your **state Medicaid agency** for state-specific rules.
> **verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

Approximately **12 million Americans** are enrolled in both Medicare and Medicaid
simultaneously — they are "dual eligible" (also called "dually enrolled").
This population tends to be among the most vulnerable: older adults and people
with disabilities who have both age/disability-based Medicare eligibility and
low enough income/assets to qualify for Medicaid. Understanding the coordination
between the two programs is essential for managing healthcare costs in this population.

---

## Part 1 — Who is dual eligible?

### Common pathways to dual eligibility

1. **Older adults (65+) with low income:** A person turns 65, qualifies for
   Medicare based on age, and also has income/assets low enough to qualify for
   Medicaid in their state.
2. **Disabled individuals under 65:** A person qualifies for Medicare after the
   24-month SSDI waiting period (or ALS/ESRD immediately) AND meets their
   state's Medicaid income/asset requirements.
3. **Nursing home residents:** Many people who enter nursing home care
   eventually spend down their assets, qualify for Medicaid, and remain enrolled
   in Medicare — this is one of the most common and important dual-eligible scenarios.

---

## Part 2 — Full duals vs. partial duals

### Full dual eligibles (full benefit duals)

Full duals are entitled to **the complete range of Medicaid benefits** for their
state. This means Medicaid covers:
- Services Medicare doesn't cover at all: **long-term services and supports
  (LTSS)**, including nursing facility care beyond 100 days, home and community-
  based services (HCBS), personal care assistance
- Medicare cost-sharing: premiums, deductibles, and copays (like QMB above)
- Additional Medicaid services: dental, vision, hearing, transportation

**Cost-sharing for full duals:** Medicaid pays what Medicare doesn't. For most
covered services, full duals pay **$0 or minimal copays**. Medicare providers
cannot bill full duals for cost-sharing.

### Partial dual eligibles (partial benefit duals)

Partial duals receive **limited Medicaid benefits** — primarily Medicaid helps
pay some of their Medicare expenses (premiums, possibly deductibles/coinsurance).
This corresponds to the Medicare Savings Programs (QMB, SLMB, QI):
- A QMB enrollee is a partial dual (Medicaid pays cost-sharing but not full LTSS)
- A SLMB/QI enrollee has even more limited Medicaid coverage (Part B premium only)

See `medicare-savings-programs` for MSP income limits and details.

---

## Part 3 — Coordination of benefits: who pays first?

**Medicare is always the primary payer** for Medicare-covered services.
Medicaid is always the **payer of last resort**.

The standard sequence:
1. Medicare pays its share (e.g., 80% after deductible for Part B services)
2. Medicaid pays some or all of the Medicare cost-sharing remainder
3. The dual-eligible beneficiary owes little to nothing (for full duals and QMB partial duals)

For services Medicare doesn't cover at all (personal care, nursing home past
100 days): Medicaid is the only payer (when coverage qualifications are met).

## Part 4 — What Medicaid adds for full duals

The most consequential benefit Medicaid adds is coverage for **long-term services
and supports (LTSS)** — care Medicare explicitly does not cover:

### Nursing facility care

Medicare covers up to 100 days of SNF care per benefit period (requires qualifying
hospitalization; see `medicare-part-a-fundamentals`). After 100 days, Medicare
pays nothing. **Medicaid can cover indefinite nursing home care** for eligible
beneficiaries — but qualifying requires meeting income and asset limits (often
requiring spend-down of assets to the state's resource limit, typically ~$2,000
in countable assets for an individual).

### Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS)

Many states offer **Medicaid HCBS waivers** that provide alternatives to nursing
home care: personal care aides, adult day programs, home modifications, respite
care, supported living. HCBS availability varies significantly by state —
some states have long waiting lists.

### Other Medicaid-only benefits for duals

- Dental (routine), vision (eyeglasses), hearing aids — not covered by Original Medicare
- Transportation to medical appointments
- Some states: additional mental health, substance use disorder services beyond Medicare

## Part 5 — D-SNPs: integrated Medicare-Medicaid coverage

**Dual Eligible Special Needs Plans (D-SNPs)** are Medicare Advantage plans
designed specifically for dual eligibles that **coordinate Medicare and Medicaid
benefits** into one plan:
- One plan, one ID card
- Shared care coordination and care plans
- Aligned networks of providers across both programs
- Often feature **$0 premium** and **$0 or very low copays**
- Extra benefits targeting dual needs: transportation, OTC allowance, dental, vision, hearing

### 2026 D-SNP developments

- **Monthly SEP (2025+):** Full-benefit dual eligibles can enroll in or switch
  between **integrated D-SNPs** on a **monthly** basis — not just during the
  annual enrollment period. This allows faster access to integrated care.
- CMS tightened cost-sharing limits for D-SNP PPOs in 2026 to reduce out-of-
  network cost-sharing burden on states.
- CMS is prioritizing D-SNP integration, requiring states and MA plans to
  develop better coordination structures.

### D-SNP vs. separate Medicare and Medicaid enrollment

| | D-SNP | Separate Original Medicare + Medicaid |
|--|-------|--------------------------------------|
| Coordination | Integrated | Separate billing, separate ID cards |
| Convenience | One point of contact | Multiple contacts |
| Network | Plan's network | Any Medicare and Medicaid provider |
| OOP costs | Often $0 | Medicaid wraps Medicare, often $0 |
| Drug coverage | Bundled Part D | Separate Part D + Medicaid drug coverage |

## Part 6 — Automatic Extra Help (LIS)

All dual eligibles (both full and partial) are **automatically enrolled in
Extra Help** (the Part D Low Income Subsidy), which reduces Part D drug costs
to small copays ($1.55 for generics, $4.65 for brand drugs at full LIS in 2026).
No separate application is required.

---

## Part 7 — State variation

Medicaid is a **joint federal-state program** with significant state variation:
- **Eligibility thresholds** differ by state (income and asset limits)
- **HCBS waiver availability** and waiting lists differ
- **Benefits** beyond the federal minimum differ
- **D-SNP availability** differs by county/state

A dual eligible in North Carolina will have different Medicaid benefits and
available D-SNP options than one in Texas or New York. Always verify with
the state Medicaid agency.
