# NC 988 Crisis Line Integration and Crisis Services System

## Overview

North Carolina operates a multi-tiered crisis response system anchored by the **988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline**, integrated with peer support services, mobile crisis teams, and a real-time bed registry. NC has been recognized as a national leader in 988 integration innovations.

## 988 in North Carolina

**Launch**: 988 went live nationally (and in NC) on July 16, 2022.

**NC 988 statistics (2 years, 2022–2024)**:
- Nearly **190,000 calls, texts, and chats** handled by NC crisis counselors
- Monthly contact volume nearly doubled in year two
- Staffed by local NC crisis centers coordinated statewide

**How it works in NC**:
- Callers routed to NC-based crisis counselors (not out-of-state call centers)
- Available 24/7/365 via call, text, or chat
- For Spanish speakers: press 2
- For Veterans: press 1
- For LGBTQ+ youth: press 3

## Peer Warmline Integration (Feb 2024)

**New as of February 20, 2024**: NC launched a **Peer Warmline** as an option within 988:
- Phone: **1-855-PEERS-NC** (1-855-733-7762)
- Available directly OR as a warm transfer option from 988
- Staffed by **Certified Peer Support Specialists (CPSS)** with lived experience with mental illness or SUD
- For people who prefer to talk with a peer rather than a clinical counselor
- Works in tandem with 988 — not a replacement, but an additional access point

## BH SCAN + 988 Integration (2026 Innovation)

NC announced a major upgrade: **NC's online mental health bed registry (BH SCAN)** now integrates directly with 988:
- **Hourly automated updates** to bed availability (previously manual/less frequent)
- NC is the **first state** to implement hourly automated bed availability
- 988 counselors can now see real-time bed availability statewide when arranging crisis placements
- Reduces wait times for individuals needing inpatient or residential crisis care

## Mobile Crisis Teams

LME-MCOs operate **mobile crisis response teams** across NC regions:
- Available 24/7
- Respond to homes, community settings, ERs
- Can avoid unnecessary hospitalization
- Funded through Medicaid (for eligible members) and state funds

**2026 Mobile Crisis Dispatch Program**: NCDHHS announced a new program (March 2026) specifically to improve mobile crisis response dispatch — directing resources more efficiently to people in mental health crises.

## Facility-Based Crisis Services

NC Medicaid covers multiple levels of facility-based crisis care:

| Setting | Description |
|---|---|
| **Crisis Receiving Facilities (CRF)** | Short-term crisis stabilization (hours to days); walk-in or mobile crisis diversion |
| **Community Crisis Centers** | Residential-level crisis stabilization (up to 14 days) |
| **Crisis Stabilization Units (CSU)** | Structured residential crisis stabilization |
| **Inpatient Psychiatric** | CCP 8B — hospital-based psychiatric admission |

## LME-MCO Crisis Services

Each LME-MCO operates its own crisis system:
- 24/7 crisis lines (distinct from 988 but integrated)
- Mobile crisis teams
- Crisis residential beds
- Crisis case management and follow-up
- Example: [Trillium Health Resources Crisis Services](https://www.trilliumhealthresources.org/members-recipients/crisis-services)

## Peer-Led Crisis Expansion (2024)

NCDHHS announced plans in April 2024 to expand **peer-led crisis services**:
- Increase peer support integration within crisis settings
- Train peers to work alongside clinical staff in mobile crisis and stabilization units
- Leverage CPSS workforce to extend crisis capacity statewide

## Crisis-to-Care Continuum

NC's crisis system is designed as a continuum:
1. **Prevention**: 988 call/text/chat, peer warmline
2. **Mobile response**: Mobile crisis teams dispatched to scene
3. **Short-term stabilization**: Crisis Receiving Facilities (hours)
4. **Residential stabilization**: Community Crisis Centers (days)
5. **Inpatient**: Psychiatric hospital admission (weeks)
6. **Outpatient follow-up**: Appointment within 7 days post-crisis
