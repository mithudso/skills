# Matrix Model Psychosocial Treatment

## Core Concepts
- Manualized, 16-week intensive outpatient program (IOP) developed in the 1980s at the Matrix Institute (Los Angeles) for stimulant use disorders, primarily cocaine and methamphetamine
- Integrates cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), relapse prevention (RP), motivational interviewing (MI), contingency management (CM), family education, and 12-step facilitation into a single structured protocol
- "Therapeutic scaffolding" model: external structure and accountability substitute for impaired internal regulation during early recovery
- SAMHSA-listed evidence-based practice; rated research-based by the Washington State Institute for Public Policy (WSIPP)
- Delivered by trained counselors via manualized workbooks; standardized across sites to ensure fidelity

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Methamphetamine depletes dopamine and serotonin, impairing prefrontal cortical executive function and self-regulation — the 16-week timeline is calibrated to the protracted withdrawal phase (anhedonia, cravings peak weeks 1–5)
- CBT component targets automatic drug-use cognitions and teaches functional coping to replace drug-use routines
- Relapse prevention training builds self-efficacy by identifying high-risk situations, internal cues, and behavioral chains preceding use
- Family education sessions address enabling patterns and rebuild social support networks, leveraging social learning theory
- Urine drug testing (twice-weekly) provides objective feedback and operant conditioning reinforcement of abstinence
- 12-step integration leverages social identity and group cohesion mechanisms to sustain motivation post-treatment

## Clinical Picture
- Methamphetamine use disorder affects ~1.8 million Americans/year (NSDUH 2022); ~30% of people who use meth develop a use disorder
- Stimulant use disorder lacks FDA-approved pharmacotherapy; psychosocial treatment remains the primary evidence-based intervention
- Matrix is primarily indicated for non-dependent to moderately severe stimulant use disorder in outpatient settings; severe cases may require residential step-down first
- Comorbid psychiatric conditions (depression, psychosis, ADHD) are common and addressed through psychoeducation sessions within the protocol
- Treatment gap is large: only ~10–15% of people with stimulant use disorder receive any treatment in a given year

## Evidence & Treatment
- Methamphetamine Treatment Project (MTP) RCT (n=978, 1999–2001, NIDA-funded): Matrix participants showed significantly higher in-treatment abstinence (31% more meth-free urines vs TAU) and higher treatment completion (27–38% more likely to complete vs TAU)
- Long-term follow-up (6–12 months post-treatment): abstinence advantages attenuated — Matrix and TAU converged, suggesting in-treatment gains require continued support to sustain
- Protocol structure: 3 group sessions/week (early recovery skills, relapse prevention, family education), biweekly individual counseling, 12-step attendance, twice-weekly urine testing for 16 weeks
- SAMHSA's National Registry of Evidence-Based Programs and Practices (NREPP) designates Matrix as effective for meth, cocaine, and alcohol
- Combining Matrix with contingency management (CM) further enhances abstinence outcomes; CM provides tangible reinforcement Matrix alone lacks

## Key Facts
- **16 weeks** — standard program duration; can extend to 52 weeks for complex cases
- **3×/week** group sessions in weeks 1–8; tapered to 2×/week in weeks 9–16
- **31%** more meth-free urines vs TAU during active treatment (MTP RCT)
- **978** participants in the pivotal NIDA MTP trial — largest meth treatment RCT at time of publication
- **0** FDA-approved medications for meth use disorder as of 2026; Matrix is the primary guideline-recommended approach
