# Sodium Oxybate + SUD History: Risk Management

## Core Dilemma
- Sodium oxybate is **first-line for NT1 with cataplexy** (AASM strong recommendation); undertreated narcolepsy in recovery impairs function and is itself a relapse risk
- Active moiety is GHB — Schedule III, with documented abuse, dependence, and withdrawal syndrome
- SUD history is a **precaution, not an absolute contraindication** per AASM 2021 guidelines; clinicians must weigh bilateral harms
- No large RCT exists specifically in patients with SUD history; evidence is observational (narcolepsy registries) plus expert consensus

## REMS Program (XYWAV and XYREM REMS)
- Single certified central pharmacy dispenses directly to enrolled patients; no retail stocking
- Prescriber enrollment required; prescribers must counsel patients on abuse/misuse/respiratory risks before each prescription
- Supply limit: 30-day maximum per dispensing; no overlapping active prescriptions permitted system-wide
- REMS enrollment form must be signed by patient or caregiver acknowledging serious risks
- Suspected abuse, misuse, or diversion is documented and triggers prescriber notification
- 2016–2017 program data: 17,037 enrolled patients, 31 suspected abuse cases (0.18%), 22 diversion cases across 146,426 shipments
- Some states impose additional DEA registration requirements beyond federal REMS

## SUD-Specific Risk Stratification

**Lower risk** — consider prescribing with enhanced monitoring:
- Remote stimulant SUD (>5 years stable recovery), no GHB/GBL history
- Remote cannabis SUD in stable recovery
- Patient-initiated disclosure, engaged in SUD treatment program

**Moderate risk** — prescribe cautiously, require MAT prescriber coordination:
- Alcohol use disorder history (any duration) — oxybate's GABA-B agonism produces cross-tolerance with alcohol; the cross-tolerance works both directions (oxybate acts as partial substitution therapy for alcohol, increasing craving potential in some patients)
- Recent stimulant SUD (<2 years recovery) without GHB history
- Active buprenorphine/methadone MAT — see co-prescription caution below

**High risk** — exhaust alternatives first; if oxybate used, maximize safeguards:
- Prior GHB or GBL misuse (any history)
- Active or recent polysubstance SUD
- Active benzodiazepine use disorder (pharmacodynamic overlap: CNS depression)
- Active alcohol use (hard contraindication — absolute prohibition during oxybate)
- Unstable psychiatric comorbidity with impulsivity

## Monitoring Protocol for SUD History Patients
- **Urine drug screening**: random UDS at baseline and periodically; test for GHB metabolites if available, plus alcohol biomarkers (EtG/EtS)
- **Pill/bottle counts**: volume dispensed vs. consumed should align; REMS dispensing logs provide independent verification
- **Caregiver-controlled dispensing**: in high-risk cases, dispense to a trusted partner or family member who administers doses and secures supply between doses
- **MAT prescriber communication**: written coordination with buprenorphine or methadone prescriber; shared monitoring plan
- **Visit frequency**: monthly initially for SUD history patients vs. standard 3-month intervals
- **Alcohol abstinence**: required for all patients; document at every visit; UDS alcohol markers for at-risk patients

## Xywav vs. Xyrem in SUD Context
- Pharmacodynamically identical — same GHB moiety, same abuse potential, same REMS
- No evidence Xywav reduces misuse risk vs. Xyrem
- Some clinicians prefer Xywav for SUD patients as psychological/administrative distinction (reframing from "GHB" to a newer branded formulation) — not pharmacologically meaningful but may improve patient acceptance
- Xywav preferred for its 92% sodium reduction benefit regardless of SUD history

## CNS Depressant Co-prescription Cautions
- **Buprenorphine (MAT)**: pharmacodynamic synergism — profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, death reported; if co-prescribed, minimize doses, monitor closely for respiratory depression; FDA label warns explicitly
- **Benzodiazepines**: contraindicated; do not co-prescribe
- **Alcohol**: absolute contraindication; 99.5% of patients in REMS surveys understand this prohibition
- **Other opioids**: avoid; if unavoidable, short course at lowest effective dose with monitoring

## Alternative When Oxybate Too Risky: Pitolisant
- Pitolisant (Wakix) — histamine H3 receptor antagonist/inverse agonist; **only non-scheduled narcolepsy drug**
- Efficacy: reduces EDS comparably to modafinil/sodium oxybate; reduces cataplexy frequency (magnitude lower than oxybate but clinically meaningful)
- No abuse potential, no REMS, no DEA scheduling — preferred first-line in patients where oxybate risk is unacceptable
- Common adverse effects: headache, insomnia, nausea, anxiety
- Key limitation: cataplexy reduction is less robust than sodium oxybate — for severe, disabling cataplexy, oxybate may remain necessary with maximal safeguards

## Pediatric Considerations
- Sodium oxybate FDA-approved ≥7 years old for narcolepsy
- SUD history in minors (including parental SUD affecting household access) triggers even stricter monitoring: caregiver-controlled dispensing required, locked storage, separate dispensing to non-using caregiver only

## Evidence Base
- AASM 2021 clinical practice guideline: strongly recommends sodium oxybate for narcolepsy; notes SUD history as precaution, not absolute contraindication
- REMS 2016–2017 outcomes: abuse rate 0.18% of enrolled patients — lower than community GHB misuse rates, suggesting REMS limits population-level harm
- No RCT of sodium oxybate specifically in SUD history narcolepsy patients; evidence gap acknowledged by AASM
- Cross-tolerance with alcohol: established in animal models and clinical case series; supports heightened caution in alcohol SUD specifically

## Key Facts
- Sodium oxybate: Schedule III (not Schedule I like street GHB); REMS-restricted single pharmacy
- Street GHB: Schedule I; sodium oxybate was explicitly carved out into Schedule III for therapeutic use
- Xywav and Xyrem share the same REMS program (XYWAV and XYREM REMS)
- Lumryz (once-nightly formulation): also sodium oxybate, high sodium, same abuse profile and REMS requirements
