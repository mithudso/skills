# NC Conditional Discharge for First Drug Offenses (GS 90-96 and 90-96.01)

> **Educational only, NOT legal advice.** Verified-as-of: 2026-06-22.

GS 90-96 gives first-time drug offenders an opportunity to avoid a conviction through a deferred prosecution program. Successful completion results in dismissal — no adjudication of guilt, and no criminal conviction.

---

## Overview: What Is Conditional Discharge?

The court, **without entering a judgment of guilt** and with the defendant's consent, defers further proceedings and places the defendant on probation under specified terms. Upon successfully fulfilling probation, the court dismisses the charges. The disposition is not deemed a conviction under NC law.

---

## Eligibility Requirements (GS 90-96(a))

### Defendant Must:
1. **Not have been previously convicted** of:
   - Any felony offense under any state or federal law
   - Any drug offense under GS Chapter 90 Article 5 (the NC Controlled Substances Act)
   - Any offense under any statute of the United States or any state relating to controlled substances or drug paraphernalia

2. **Not have previously received conditional discharge** under GS 90-96 (the seven-year rule applies — see below)

3. **Consent** to the placement on probation

### Seven-Year Lookback Rule
A prior drug offense occurring **more than seven years before the date of the current offense** is not counted for purposes of determining whether a prior 90-96 discharge has already been used. This means a defendant who used the program more than seven years ago may be eligible again.

### Qualifying Offenses
- Misdemeanor possession of a controlled substance (Schedules I–VI) under GS 90-95(a)(3)
- Possession of drug paraphernalia (GS 90-113.22 or GS 90-113.22A)
- Felony possession of a controlled substance under GS 90-95(a)(3) (Class I felony level possession — not PWIMSD or trafficking)

**Note:** 90-96 does **not** apply to trafficking offenses (GS 90-95(h)) or PWIMSD (GS 90-95(a)(1)).

---

## How the Program Works

1. **Plea not entered** — defendant does not plead guilty; proceedings are deferred
2. **Probation** — court imposes terms, typically including:
   - Drug education program approved by NC DHHS
   - Participation in Treatment for Effective Community Supervision (TECS) Program (optional under GS 90-96(a))
   - Periodic drug testing
   - No new criminal offenses
   - Regular reporting
3. **Successful completion** — court **discharges** the defendant and **dismisses** the charges
4. **Violation** — if defendant violates probation conditions, proceedings resume and the case proceeds normally

---

## Outcome: Not a Conviction

Upon discharge and dismissal:
- "Discharge and dismissal shall be without court adjudication of guilt"
- "Shall not be deemed a conviction for purposes of this Article or any Article or Chapter"
- The 90-96 disposition **cannot be used as a prior conviction** for habitual felon purposes or prior record level calculation under Structured Sentencing

---

## Expungement After 90-96 Discharge

After a 90-96 conditional discharge and dismissal, the defendant may petition for expungement of the arrest and dismissal records. NC Second Chance Act (GS 15A-145 / 15A-145.2) expungement procedures apply. See [nc-expungement-record-sealing](nc-expungement-record-sealing.md) for full details.

---

## GS 90-96.01 — First Offense Treatment Alternative

GS 90-96.01 provides a separate but related pathway: a **treatment alternative to incarceration** for first-time drug offenders. Key differences from 90-96:

| Feature | GS 90-96 | GS 90-96.01 |
|---|---|---|
| Court's role | Defer proceedings, then dismiss | Treatment as condition; separate mechanism |
| Focus | Diversion before adjudication | Treatment-oriented, may follow charge |
| Drug education | Often sufficient | More intensive treatment program |
| Overlap | Available for same qualifying offenses | Supplements 90-96 options |

In practice, GS 90-96 is the primary first-offense diversion vehicle. GS 90-96.01 is often used alongside or for defendants who need more structured treatment programming.

---

## Relationship to Drug Treatment Courts (GS 7A, Article 62)

Some defendants eligible for 90-96 may instead be referred to NC Drug Treatment Courts (also called NC Recovery Courts). Drug Treatment Courts provide intensive judicial supervision and treatment and are typically used for more serious drug dependency cases. See [nc-drug-treatment-courts](nc-drug-treatment-courts.md).
