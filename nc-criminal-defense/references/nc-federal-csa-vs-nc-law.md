# Federal CSA vs. NC Drug Law

> **Educational only, NOT legal advice.** Both federal and NC state law can apply to the same conduct. Verified-as-of: 2026-06-22.

---

## Two Parallel Systems

In North Carolina, drug offenses may be prosecuted under **NC state law**, **federal law**, or **both**. Double jeopardy does not bar separate prosecutions because the federal and state governments are separate sovereigns ("dual sovereignty doctrine").

---

## Schedule Comparison: Federal vs. NC

| Drug | Federal Schedule | NC Schedule | Key Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marijuana | **Schedule I** | **Schedule VI** | NC created a lower-impact 6th schedule; marijuana is NOT Schedule I in NC |
| Heroin | Schedule I | Schedule I | Same |
| LSD | Schedule I | Schedule I | Same |
| MDMA/Ecstasy | Schedule I | Schedule I | Same |
| Psilocybin | Schedule I | Schedule I | Same |
| Cocaine | Schedule II | Schedule II | Same |
| Fentanyl | Schedule II | Schedule II | Same |
| Oxycodone | Schedule II | Schedule II | Same |
| Methamphetamine | Schedule II | Schedule II | Same |
| Ketamine | Schedule III | Schedule III | Same |
| Buprenorphine | Schedule III | Schedule III | Same |
| Benzodiazepines | Schedule IV | Schedule IV | Same |
| Pregabalin | Schedule V | Schedule V | Same |
| THC (synthetic) | Schedule I | Schedule VI | Same NC deviation as marijuana |

**Practical effect:** For most drugs other than marijuana/THC, NC and federal schedules align. The marijuana discrepancy means NC state prosecution is less severe than federal prosecution for marijuana offenses.

## Federal Controlled Substances Act (21 U.S.C. § 801 et seq.)

### Key Provisions

- **21 U.S.C. § 841**: Primary offense — knowing/intentional manufacture, distribution, or dispensing of a controlled substance; or possession with intent to distribute. This is the federal equivalent of NC's PWIMSD and trafficking offenses.
- **21 U.S.C. § 844**: Simple possession — federal analogue of NC's GS 90-95(a)(3)
- **21 U.S.C. § 813 (Analogue Act)**: Treatment of controlled substance analogues as Schedule I when intended for human consumption
- **21 U.S.C. § 846**: Attempt and conspiracy — federal conspiracy charges can reach conduct that would not be a NC crime

### Federal Mandatory Minimums (21 U.S.C. § 841(b)) — Key Examples

Federal mandatory minimums often exceed NC trafficking minimums for comparable weights:

| Drug + Quantity | Federal Mandatory Minimum | Federal Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| Marijuana: 100–999 kg (or 100–999 plants) | 5 years | 40 years |
| Marijuana: 1,000+ kg (or 1,000+ plants) | 10 years | Life |
| Cocaine: 500g–4.99 kg | 5 years | 40 years |
| Cocaine: 5+ kg | 10 years | Life |
| Heroin: 100g–999g | 5 years | 40 years |
| Heroin: 1+ kg | 10 years | Life |
| Fentanyl: 40g–399g | 5 years | 40 years |
| Fentanyl: 400+ g | 10 years | Life |
| Meth (pure): 5g–49g | 5 years | 40 years |
| Meth (pure): 50+ g | 10 years | Life |

**Prior felony drug convictions double these minimums.** Two prior drug felony convictions can result in life mandatory minimum.

## Federal Analogue Act (21 U.S.C. § 813) vs. NC GS 90-89.1

| Feature | Federal Analogue Act | NC GS 90-89.1 |
|---|---|---|
| Statute | 21 U.S.C. § 813 | GS 90-89.1 |
| Treat analogue as | Schedule I | Schedule I |
| "Intended for human consumption" required? | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| Substantial similarity standard | Chemical structure OR pharmacological effect similar to Sch. I/II | Chemical structure OR pharmacological effect substantially similar to Schedule I/II |
| Burden of proof | Government must prove intent for human consumption | Same |

**Practical difference:** Federal courts have developed significant case law on the analogue Act's "substantially similar" standard. NC's state courts interpret GS 90-89.1 similarly but apply NC evidence rules and standards independently.

## When Federal Prosecution Is More Likely in NC

Federal prosecution (in US District Court, EDNC or MDNC) is more likely when:
1. **Trafficking quantities** — large amounts cross quantities where federal interest is high
2. **Interstate nexus** — drugs crossed state lines or federal mail/wire was used
3. **Federal-state task force involvement** — DEA, FBI, or other federal agencies investigated
4. **Conspiracy charges** — federal conspiracy charges allow prosecution of broader networks
5. **Prior record** — defendants with prior convictions face greater federal sentencing enhancement
6. **Firearms present** — federal 18 U.S.C. § 924(c) gun-drug combination charges carry additional consecutive mandatory minimums

**Small possession cases are almost never federally prosecuted** in NC absent extraordinary circumstances.

## Supremacy Clause Implications

The Supremacy Clause (U.S. Const. Art. VI) means federal drug law is supreme over NC state drug law when they conflict. However:
- A state **not** scheduling a drug does **not** prevent federal prosecution
- A state **lowering penalties** for a drug does not exempt conduct from federal prosecution
- NC's Schedule VI treatment of marijuana does NOT provide any protection against federal DEA enforcement

---

## NC Drug Law vs. Federal for Defense Attorneys

Defense attorneys in NC must assess both the state charge and federal exposure simultaneously. Key considerations:
- Can the case be handled in state court without federal interest?
- If federal charges are filed, can the defendant benefit from safety valve (18 U.S.C. § 3553(f)) to avoid mandatory minimum?
- Federal cooperation/substantial assistance departures (USSG § 5K1.1) are negotiated separately from NC plea bargaining
