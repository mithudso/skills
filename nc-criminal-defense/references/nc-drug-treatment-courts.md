# NC Drug Treatment Courts / Recovery Courts (GS Chapter 7A, Article 62)

> **Educational only, NOT legal advice.** Program details, availability, and eligibility vary by county. Verified-as-of: 2026-06-22.

---

## Overview

North Carolina enacted the Drug Treatment Court Act in 1995 (GS Chapter 7A, Article 62, GS 7A-790 et seq.). What began as Drug Treatment Courts have since been renamed **NC Recovery Courts** to reflect expanded scope beyond drug offenses to include mental health, veteran status, and DWI cases. NC Recovery Courts are administered through the Administrative Office of the Courts (AOC) — NC Judicial Branch.

---

## Types of NC Recovery Courts

| Court Type | Primary Population |
|---|---|
| Adult Treatment Court (ATC) | Adults with substance use disorder charged with qualifying offenses |
| Juvenile Recovery Court | Juveniles with substance use or mental health issues |
| DWI (Impaired Driving) Court | Repeat DWI offenders with substance use issues |
| Veterans Treatment Court | Veterans with trauma/SUD co-occurring issues |
| Mental Health Court | Adults with qualifying mental health diagnoses |

This spoke focuses primarily on **Adult Treatment Court** as it is most relevant to drug offense defense.

---

## Statutory Foundation

- **GS 7A-790 to 7A-798** (Chapter 7A, Article 62): NC Drug Treatment Court Act
- **GS 7A-272**: District court jurisdiction (note: the actual eligibility criteria appear in Article 62, not in GS 7A-272 specifically)
- Administered by AOC; local programs require local implementation agreements

---

## Eligibility Criteria (GS 7A-792)

A defendant is eligible when:

1. **Charged with a qualifying offense:**
   - Misdemeanor or felony carrying **maximum penalty of 10 years or less**
   - The offense was **committed as a result of drug use or drug addiction**

2. **Chemical dependency acknowledged:** The defendant must acknowledge a **personal chemical dependency or history of substance abuse** (including alcohol)

3. **No disqualifying factors:**
   - Typically excludes defendants charged with violent felonies, sex offenses, or offenses with a mandatory minimum that would prevent completion of the program
   - Specific exclusions vary by county/court program

4. **Voluntary participation:** Defendant must consent

## How Recovery Courts Work

### Intensive Judicial Supervision Model

NC Recovery Courts are "intensive, judicially supervised court intervention" — this distinguishes them from standard probation. Key components:

- **Regular court appearances** before the drug court judge (typically weekly or bi-weekly at start)
- **Frequent drug/alcohol testing**
- **Treatment participation** (outpatient, intensive outpatient, or residential as assessed)
- **Case management services**
- **Sanctions and incentives**: graduated system — positive achievements receive incentives; violations result in sanctions (community service, brief jail stays, phase setback) before termination

### Program Phases

Most NC Adult Treatment Courts operate on a **multi-phase structure** (typically 3–5 phases lasting 12–18+ months total):

| Phase | Focus | Typical Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Stabilization, intensive supervision, frequent court | 3–4 months |
| Phase 2 | Treatment compliance, skill-building | 3–4 months |
| Phase 3 | Maintenance, employment/education, reducing court appearances | 3–4 months |
| Phase 4 (if applicable) | Community reintegration | 2–3 months |
| Graduation | Successful completion | Ceremony + outcome |

### Successful Completion

Upon graduation, depending on the program design and prosecutor agreement:
- Charges may be **dismissed**
- Or the defendant receives a **reduced sentence**
- Some programs operate as **post-conviction** (defendant pleads guilty, program completion determines final disposition)
- Outcome varies: pre-plea (no conviction if successful) vs. post-plea (conviction with sentence modification)

## Drug Treatment Court vs. GS 90-96 (Conditional Discharge)

| Feature | GS 90-96 (Conditional Discharge) | Recovery Court |
|---|---|---|
| Prior felony allowed? | **No** — bars eligibility | **Sometimes** — depends on the offense |
| Intensity | Standard probation conditions | Intensive judicial supervision |
| Offense types | Possession/paraphernalia only | Broader qualifying offenses |
| Conviction on entry | None (deferred proceedings) | Varies (pre or post-plea) |
| Outcome if successful | Dismissal + no conviction | Varies — dismissal or sentence reduction |
| Court involvement | Minimal | Heavy (regular appearances) |
| Treatment required | Drug education program | Full SUD treatment program |

**For minor first-offense drug charges:** GS 90-96 is simpler and faster.
**For individuals with significant substance use disorder:** Recovery Court provides more intensive support.

---

## Federal Intersection: FIRST STEP Act

The FIRST STEP Act (18 U.S.C. § 3611) encourages state drug treatment courts. Some NC federal-case defendants (particularly in cases where charges originate from federal-state task forces) may be offered participation in state recovery courts as a condition of federal pretrial diversion — though this is not common.

---

## County Availability

Not all NC counties have Recovery Courts. The NC Judicial Branch maintains a current list of operational courts. Contact the local court system or the NC AOC Recovery Court Program for availability.
