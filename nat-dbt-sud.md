# DBT for Substance Use Disorders (DBT-SUD)

## Origin and SUD Adaptation

Marsha Linehan developed DBT in the late 1980s for borderline personality disorder (BPD), targeting the emotional dysregulation and impulsivity that drive self-harm and suicidality. In the 1990s, Linehan's team extended it to SUD, adding SUD-specific principles and protocols while preserving the four-component structure. DBT-SUD is now the recognized treatment of choice for co-occurring BPD+SUD, supported by five RCTs across labs in the US, Netherlands, and Canada.

## Four Standard Components

- **Individual therapy** — weekly 50-min sessions; targets motivation, applies DBT principles to current behavior, troubleshoots skill use
- **Skills training group** — weekly ~2.5h; teaches the four modules didactically
- **Phone coaching** — between-session access to therapist for crisis and skill coaching; especially important at craving peaks
- **Consultation team** — therapist support structure to prevent burnout and maintain fidelity

In SUD settings without full-program resources, **DBT-ST (skills training only)** is feasible and shows within-group reductions in substance use and improved emotion regulation.

## Four Skill Modules and SUD Relevance

**Mindfulness** (foundational to all others): Non-judgmental observation of thoughts, emotions, and cravings without acting on them. Key technique for SUD: **urge surfing** — treat a craving as a wave to observe and ride out rather than suppress or obey.

**Distress Tolerance** (acceptance skills for crisis): Crisis survival for high-risk moments — the acute window when relapse is most likely.
- **TIPP**: Temperature (cold water/ice to down-regulate arousal), Intense exercise, Paced breathing, Progressive relaxation
- **ACCEPTS**: Activities, Contributing, Comparisons, Emotions, Pushing away, Thoughts, Sensations (distraction menu)
- **STOP**: Stop / Take a step back / Observe / Proceed mindfully
- Directly targets meth craving peaks, which are intense, time-limited, and strongly tied to physiological arousal

**Emotion Regulation** (change skills): Identify and reduce emotional vulnerability; act opposite to emotion-driven urges.
- **PLEASE**: treat Physical illness, balanced Eating, avoid mood-Altering substances, balanced Sleep, Exercise — addresses lifestyle factors that amplify emotional reactivity
- **Opposite action**: act against the urge driven by an emotion (e.g., approach rather than avoid when ashamed; don't use when craving)
- Particularly critical for meth SUD: dopamine system dysregulation produces flat affect, anhedonia, and emotional lability in early recovery — the exact deficits emotion regulation skills target

**Interpersonal Effectiveness**: DEAR MAN (assertively ask for what you want), GIVE (maintain relationships), FAST (maintain self-respect). Addresses meth-specific relationship damage in recovery — rebuilding trust, making amends, setting boundaries with using peers.

## DBT-SUD–Specific Adaptations

**Dialectical abstinence**: The central SUD-specific principle. Radical commitment to abstinence *and* compassionate, non-self-destructive response to relapse when it occurs. Resolves the abstinence-violation effect (lapse → full relapse because "I've already failed") without tolerating chronic use.

**Clear mind**: Dialectic between *addict mind* (controlled by craving/use) and *clean mind* (abstinent but complacent about relapse risk). Clear mind — aware of addiction vulnerability while not actively using — is the treatment target.

**Attachment strategies**: Specific techniques to maintain the therapeutic relationship when use occurs, avoiding both abandonment (enabling drift) and punitive rupture.

## BPD + Meth SUD Comorbidity

Estimated 15–30% of meth SUD patients meet BPD criteria. Both share the same core phenotype: emotional dysregulation, impulsivity, identity instability, and trauma history. For this population, full DBT (all four components) is strongly indicated. DBT outperforms CBT for BPD+SUD on substance use outcomes; Linehan's 2002 RCT showed therapist adherence to the DBT manual predicted drug-free urinalyses throughout treatment and at 12-month follow-up.

## Evidence Summary

| Study | Population | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Linehan et al. 2002 (RCT) | BPD + heroin SUD | DBT reduced heroin use vs. CTBE; adherence predicted drug-free UA |
| Haktanir & Callender 2020 (meta-analysis, 6 RCTs) | SUD | DBT superior to alternative treatment and waitlist for remission/abstinence at post-treatment |
| Frontiers (2021) | BPD ± SUD | Reduced substance use, improved social functioning, enhanced emotion regulation |
| DBT-ST reviews | SUD without BPD | Within-group reductions in use; improved emotion regulation |

No dedicated meth RCT exists, but stimulant SUD subgroup analyses are positive. Given no FDA-approved pharmacotherapy for meth SUD, DBT fills a critical gap. **DBT + contingency management** shows additive effects.

## Practical Delivery Notes

- Full DBT requires specialized training (Behavioral Tech Institute); not universally available in SUD programs
- DBT-ST (group skills training alone) is the feasible entry point in most SUD treatment settings
- DBT-A (adolescent adaptation) adds family component; relevant for younger meth-involved youth
- Internet-delivered DBT-SUD (iDBT-SUD) shows feasibility and potential efficacy in recent RCT (2024)
