## Kleine-Levin Syndrome (KLS)

KLS is a rare recurrent hypersomnia defined by **discrete, fully reversible episodes** of hypersomnia separated by complete normality. The episodic pattern is the single most important diagnostic feature and the sharpest distinction from narcolepsy and secondary narcolepsy (which are continuous disorders).

---

### Epidemiology

- Prevalence: 1–2 per million (rare)
- Onset: adolescent predominance; median age 15–16 years (range 5–45)
- Sex: ~70% male
- Course: typically 1–4 years of recurrent episodes then **spontaneous remission**, usually by mid-20s; a minority have decades of episodes
- Worldwide distribution; Jewish descent may be over-represented in some case series

---

### Cardinal Clinical Features

**During episodes** (last 2–21 days; mean ~10 days):
- Sleeps 16–20 h/day; cannot be fully aroused
- Cognitive impairment: derealization, confusion, amnesia, dreamlike states
- Behavioral disturbances in a subset: hyperphagia, hypersexuality, irritability, apathy
- Hallucinations or depressed mood in some cases

**Between episodes:**
- Sleep duration, alertness, and cognition are **completely normal**
- Patient returns to full function at school, work, and socially
- This inter-episode normality is pathognomonic — it rules out narcolepsy, idiopathic hypersomnia, and secondary narcolepsy, all of which produce continuous EDS

**Frequency:** median 3–6 episodes/year; precipitants include febrile illness, sleep deprivation, alcohol, or stress

---

### ICSD-3 Diagnostic Criteria

All must be met:
1. Recurrent episodes of excessive sleepiness lasting ≥2 days (up to 5 weeks), occurring ≥2 times
2. Episodes recur at least once every 18 months (typically >once/year)
3. During episodes: at least one of — cognitive dysfunction/derealization, behavioral change (hyperphagia, hypersexuality), altered perception
4. Between episodes: normal sleep, alertness, and cognition
5. Not better explained by another sleep, neurologic, psychiatric, or substance-use disorder

---

### MSLT and PSG Findings

| Phase | MSLT | CSF Orexin-A |
|---|---|---|
| During episode | Markedly abnormal: MSL 0–3 min, SOREMPs present — mimics NT1 | May be transiently reduced (functional suppression) |
| Between episodes | **Normal** (MSL >8–10 min, no SOREMPs) | Normal |

**Key rule**: NT1-like MSLT that normalizes completely between episodes = KLS until proven otherwise. NT1-like MSLT that is **persistently abnormal between episodes** argues against KLS and toward true narcolepsy.

---

### Pathophysiology

- Mechanism unknown; best supported model: **functional hypothalamic and thalamic dysfunction** (not structural destruction)
- FDG-PET during episodes shows hypometabolism in thalamus and frontoparietal areas that resolves between episodes — consistent with functional suppression, not cell death
- 2023 Neurology NI PET study found microglial activation during episodes, supporting an **inflammatory or autoimmune trigger**
- Many episodes begin with a viral illness; autoimmune hypothesis pursued but no validated autoantibody identified
- CSF orexin is **not chronically deficient** (unlike NT1) — any reduction during episodes is transient and functional

---

### Treatment

| Approach | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Lithium carbonate** | Best studied; reduces episode frequency by ~40–50% in some series; considered first-line preventive |
| **Amantadine** | Acute episodes; modest functional improvement |
| **Stimulants** (modafinil, amphetamines) | Acute episode EDS management; do not abort episodes |
| Education | Most important intervention — school/employer accommodation during episodes |

No treatment cures KLS or reliably aborts episodes. Reassurance that spontaneous remission typically occurs is central to management.

---

### Differential — KLS vs Narcolepsy vs Secondary Narcolepsy

| Feature | KLS | NT1 / NT2 | Secondary Narcolepsy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Symptom pattern | Episodic — fully normal between episodes | Continuous daily EDS | Continuous daily EDS |
| Episode length | Days to weeks | n/a | n/a |
| Inter-episode MSLT | Normal | Abnormal (SOREMPs) | Abnormal |
| CSF orexin | Normal between episodes | <110 pg/mL (NT1) | Variable |
| Cataplexy | Absent | Present (NT1) | Variable |
| Onset age | Adolescent peak | Any age | Depends on cause |
| Spontaneous remission | Common | Rare | Depends on cause |

---

### Differential — KLS vs Meth-Related EDS

Meth-withdrawal or binge-crash EDS is **continuous**, not episodic. A meth user presenting with episodic hypersomnia (days-long sleep episodes with complete normality between) should have KLS considered, but the mechanism is entirely different and the substances are not causally linked. Binge-crash cycles can superficially mimic episodic hypersomnia but lack the derealization, complete arousal failure, and MSLT normalization between episodes that define KLS.
