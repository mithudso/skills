# Bupropion + Naltrexone Combination Pharmacotherapy

## Core Concepts
- **No FDA-approved treatment exists** for methamphetamine use disorder (MUD) as of 2026; bupropion+naltrexone is the leading pharmacotherapy candidate
- Combination uses **extended-release oral bupropion** (450 mg/day) plus **extended-release injectable naltrexone** (380 mg every 3 weeks / ~XR-NTX)
- ADAPT-2 = *Accelerated Development of Additive Pharmacotherapy Treatment*, NIDA CTN-0068, Phase 2/3 RCT published NEJM 2021
- Both drugs are already FDA-approved for other indications (bupropion: depression/smoking cessation; naltrexone: alcohol and opioid use disorder), enabling off-label use now
- Sequential parallel comparison design (SPCD) allowed efficient 12-week trial with adaptive randomization across 8 US sites

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **Bupropion** inhibits reuptake of dopamine and norepinephrine → mild dopamine elevation that reduces meth craving and attenuates withdrawal-related negative affect (anhedonia, fatigue)
- **Naltrexone** blocks μ-opioid receptors → suppresses opioid-mediated potentiation of dopamine reward from stimulant use; disrupts the reinforcing euphoria of methamphetamine
- Combination targets dopaminergic reward via **two orthogonal pathways**: direct reuptake inhibition (bupropion) and indirect opioidergic modulation (naltrexone)
- Neither drug alone showed consistent efficacy in prior MUD trials; the combination produces additive or synergistic effect beyond individual components
- Proposed mechanism: bupropion stabilizes baseline dopamine tone (reducing craving/withdrawal), while naltrexone blunts acute reward — together decreasing both drive to use and positive reinforcement on use

## Clinical Picture
- MUD prevalence: ~2.5 million Americans meet DSM-5 criteria; 32,537 stimulant-involved overdose deaths in 2022 (majority methamphetamine), up >10-fold from 2010
- ADAPT-2 enrolled 403 adults ages 18–65 with moderate-to-severe MUD (DSM-5), reporting meth use ≥18 of last 30 days at screening
- Primary outcome: **≥3 of 4 meth-negative urine drug screens (UDS)** in weeks 5–8 (responder threshold); secondary outcomes included abstinence weeks 11–12

## Evidence & Treatment
- **ADAPT-2 primary result (NEJM 2021)**: 13.6% responder rate in active arm vs 2.5% placebo; OR 7.35 (95% CI 1.80–30.1); NNT ~9 for response
- **Heavy-user subgroup signal**: Benefit concentrated in participants with higher baseline use; effect size was substantially larger among those using meth ≥18 days/month — the intended target population — validating the subgroup enrichment strategy
- **2024 extended-observation analysis** (Addiction, Li et al.): continued treatment through week 12 associated with incremental UDS improvement beyond the week 6 effect; response persisted and deepened
- **Follow-on Phase 3 RCT** (NCT06233799, UCSD) launched July 2024, ~360 participants, primary completion October 2026 — designed to generate FDA NDA-grade evidence
- Behavioral therapy (contingency management) remains standard of care; pharmacotherapy is additive — see meth-contingency-management for CM evidence base

## Key Facts
- Bupropion dose in ADAPT-2: **450 mg/day XR** (above standard 300 mg depression dose)
- Naltrexone dose: **380 mg IM every 3 weeks** (identical to Vivitrol dosing for AUD/OUD)
- Responder rate vs placebo: **13.6% vs 2.5%** (absolute risk difference ~11%)
- Weeks of UDS negativity (weeks 11–12) in active arm: ~21% vs ~4% placebo in extended analysis
- No serious adverse events specific to the combination; seizure risk from bupropion monitored (no excess seizures observed); injection-site reactions from naltrexone reported
