# Serum/Plasma Orexin-A Monitoring in Post-Meth Hypersomnia

## Why Serum Orexin Matters Here

CSF orexin-A ≤110 pg/mL is the diagnostic gold standard for NT1 but requires lumbar puncture — impractical for serial monitoring during post-meth recovery. Tracking orexin trajectory over 3–18 months of abstinence is clinically meaningful; serum offers a repeatable, outpatient-friendly alternative even though it cannot substitute for CSF diagnostically.

## The Core Problem: Peripheral ≠ Central

Serum/plasma orexin-A does **not** directly reflect CNS orexin tone. Key reasons:

- Orexin is also produced peripherally (gut, adrenal glands, testes), so blood levels blend central and peripheral signals
- In NT1, peripheral orexin production is largely intact — most NT1 patients have normal or near-normal serum orexin-A despite undetectable CSF levels; blood measurement cannot diagnose NT1 by itself
- Assay reliability is an additional obstacle: studies using similar immunoassay methodology have reported markedly different plasma HCRT-1 values; some researchers question whether the immunoreactive signal in blood is authentic HCRT-1 at all (Andersen et al., *Brain Communications*, 2021)

## What Serum CAN Detect in Post-Meth Context

Post-meth hypersomnia differs from NT1 in one critical respect: meth suppresses **both** CNS orexin neurons and peripheral production simultaneously, producing acute serum orexin-A suppression detectable above assay noise. This creates within-patient signal utility:

- Acute meth withdrawal: serum orexin-A measurably suppressed
- 3+ months abstinence: rising toward normal in parallel with subjective hypersomnia improvement
- Persistent suppression at 12 months: signals possible permanent secondary narcolepsy (Mechanism 3 — irreversible neurotoxic loss), warranting escalation to CSF measurement

Because the question is **trajectory within one patient** — not comparison to a population cutoff — the peripheral/central mismatch is less disqualifying than in NT1 diagnosis.

## Assay Availability

- ELISA kits commercially available (Phoenix Pharmaceuticals, Bachem); LC-MS/MS emerging as more reliable
- **Not FDA-cleared** as a diagnostic test; reference lab send-out at academic centers
- Platform variability is real: RIA and LC-MS/MS correlate well for CSF but plasma results diverge across labs; always use the same assay platform and lab for serial comparisons in the same patient
- Mayo Clinic Laboratories offers ORXNA (spinal fluid only; plasma assay is research-use)

## Proposed Serial Monitoring Protocol (Theoretical)

| Timepoint | Interpretation |
|-----------|---------------|
| 4 weeks abstinence | Baseline post-acute; expect suppression |
| 3 months | First meaningful recovery signal |
| 6 months | Rising trajectory confirms Mechanism 1/2 recovery |
| 12 months | Persistent suppression → CSF orexin; consider permanent NT diagnosis |

Document assay platform and laboratory at first draw; never switch mid-course.

## Comparison to Other Monitoring Tools

| Tool | Measures | Limitation |
|------|----------|------------|
| DAT-SPECT | Dopamine terminal density | DA system, not orexin; different recovery curve |
| PSG/MSLT | Functional sleepiness | Requires ≥2-week meth washout; resource-intensive |
| Serum orexin-A | Orexin system recovery trajectory | Not FDA-cleared; platform variability; not 1:1 with CSF |

Serum orexin-A is **complementary**, not competing — it tracks the orexin axis longitudinally without LP burden.

## Current Practice Status

Not standard of care; primarily research applications. Emerging as a practical serial biomarker option for academic sleep centers managing post-meth hypersomnia. Escalate to CSF orexin if serum remains persistently suppressed at 12 months abstinence or if diagnostic certainty is required for permanent NT classification.
