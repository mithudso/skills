# Sex and Gender Differences in Meth Use

## Core Concepts
- **Telescoping effect**: Women develop methamphetamine use disorder faster than men — progression from first use to regular use averages 1.6 years (women) vs. 2.6 years (men)
- **Severity paradox**: Women use at lower rates (lifetime prevalence 2.2% vs. 5.4% in men) but accumulate greater psychiatric and functional impairment per year of use
- **Hormonal modulation**: Estradiol amplifies drug-evoked dopamine signaling via mGlu5 interactions; progesterone attenuates vulnerability — creating a cycle-dependent fluctuation in craving
- **Gender-responsive treatment**: Women show better treatment retention and outcomes than men despite entering treatment with more severe profiles

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **Estradiol-mGlu5-dopamine axis**: Estradiol upregulates mGlu5 receptor signaling in nucleus accumbens, potentiating dopamine release in response to METH — directly increases reinforcing salience in premenopausal women
- **Progesterone counter-effect**: Progesterone attenuates DA signaling and reduces drug craving; menstrual cycle phase predicts craving intensity and relapse risk
- **Cortisol dysregulation**: Young female METH-dependent individuals show significantly elevated cortisol vs. male counterparts, associated with greater psychiatric symptom burden
- **Sexually dimorphic neuroadaptation**: Female rats show faster METH self-administration acquisition, greater escalation under extended access, and more robust incubation of craving during abstinence
- **Prefrontal vulnerability**: METH self-administration produces sex-specific changes in postsynaptic glutamate transmission in PFC; females show distinct withdrawal-time-dependent neuronal dysfunction patterns
- **Genetic factors**: Sex-specific SNP associations in dopamine metabolism genes (DAT, COMT), serotonin transporter (SLC6A4), BDNF, and CHRNA5 confer differential dependence risk

## Clinical Picture
- **Epidemiology**: Annual METH use 8.7/1,000 adult males vs. 4.7/1,000 females; ~52.9% of users develop diagnosable use disorder regardless of sex
- **Treatment admission trend**: Female admissions rose from 19.2% (2008) to 28.3% (2017) vs. male 11.7% to 20.9% — women's share growing faster
- **Female-predominant comorbidities**: Higher rates of depression, PTSD, panic disorder, bulimia, suicidal ideation; sleep disturbances 75.6% vs. 52.4% in males
- **Trauma nexus**: 44% of female METH users report sexual abuse before age 15 vs. 24% of males; trauma history is primary initiating context for many women
- **Overdose mortality**: Males account for 67.5% of METH overdose deaths vs. 32.5% female; pulmonary hypertension risk is higher in women; cardiomyopathy predominates in men

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Naltrexone sex differences**: Women with METH use disorder show lower drug-liking scores and larger naltrexone treatment effect than men (sex-moderated pharmacotherapy response)
- **Topiramate**: More effective in males than females in stimulant use disorder trials — highlights need for sex-stratified trial reporting
- **Bupropion**: Adolescent males showed better response than females in clinical studies; bupropion+naltrexone (ADAPT-2 trial) not disaggregated by sex in primary analysis
- **Treatment retention**: Women average 3.9 months in treatment vs. 2.8 months for men; women more likely to seek treatment and sustain engagement
- **Gender-responsive principles**: Trauma-informed care, childcare integration, and addressing weight/body-image concerns (initial use motivation for many women) improve retention and outcomes

## Key Facts
- Women reach regular meth use ~1 year faster than men (1.6 vs. 2.6 years from first use)
- Male METH users averaged 14.9 years of use before treatment vs. 10.6 years for females — classic telescoping
- Female METH users are 2.6× more likely to be heavy users presenting to emergency departments (OR 2.6, p<0.0001)
- ~44% of female METH users report pre-age-15 sexual abuse as an initiating context for use
- Women stay in METH treatment 39% longer than men (3.9 vs. 2.8 months average)
