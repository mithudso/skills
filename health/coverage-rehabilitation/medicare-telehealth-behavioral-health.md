# Medicare Telehealth Coverage for Behavioral Health

## Overview

Medicare's telehealth framework for behavioral health has undergone more change between 2020 and 2026 than in the prior 20 years combined. The COVID-19 public health emergency created sweeping telehealth flexibilities; Congress permanently retained several for mental health specifically (via CAA 2021), while other expansions remain temporary. Three distinct regulatory buckets apply: (1) permanent changes from CAA 2021 §123; (2) temporary flexibilities extended through Dec 31, 2027 by CAA 2026; and (3) the in-person requirement — codified in statute but legislatively delayed until January 2028.

## Pre-COVID Medicare Telehealth — Why BH Was Restricted

Traditional Medicare telehealth rules (pre-March 2020) required:
1. **Geographic restriction**: beneficiary must be in a Health Professional Shortage Area (HPSA) or rural county
2. **Originating site requirement**: beneficiary must be in a qualified medical facility (not home)
3. **Real-time audio-visual**: synchronous video/audio connection required
4. **Provider location**: provider must be in US (not international)

These rules effectively blocked most urban/suburban beneficiaries from accessing telehealth BH — the population with the greatest commercial telehealth BH growth.

## CAA 2021, Section 123 — Permanent BH Telehealth Framework

The Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2021 (H.R. 133, 116th Congress), **Section 123** ("Expanding Access to Mental Health Services Furnished Through Telehealth"), permanently amended SSA §1834(m) to:

### 1. Geographic Waiver for Mental Health Telehealth
- **Mental health visits**: geographic restriction lifted permanently — a beneficiary in any location (urban, suburban, rural) can receive Medicare-covered mental health via telehealth [KFF, "What to Know About Medicare Coverage of Telehealth," 2026; CMS MFT/MHC page]
- Applies to services for the diagnosis, evaluation, or treatment of a mental health disorder

### 2. Home as Permanent Originating Site for Mental Health
- Beneficiary's home is a permanent originating site for mental health telehealth
- No longer required to travel to a clinic or facility
- Home must be within the US

### 3. Audio-Only (Telephone) Mental Health Telehealth — Permanent
- **Permanent** under 42 CFR 410.78, as finalized in the CY 2022 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule final rule [LII/Cornell CFR §410.78; CMS CR12549 / MM12549]
- The definition of "interactive telecommunications system" permanently includes two-way, real-time audio-only communication technology for any telehealth service furnished to a patient in their **home**, if:
  - The distant-site physician or practitioner is **technically capable** of using audio-video, AND
  - The patient **is not capable of, or does not consent to**, video technology
- Audio-only is not available simply as a patient preference when the patient is capable of using video
- Modifier "FQ" (Medicare) or "93" (CPT) must be appended to claims for audio-only visits
- Reimbursement is slightly lower than video visits
- **Note**: Audio-only coverage for *non–mental health* services remains temporary (through Dec 31, 2027 per CAA 2026)

## In-Person Visit Requirement — Codified But Delayed Until January 2028

CAA 2021 §123 also enacted an in-person requirement: beneficiaries must have a non-telehealth in-person visit with their behavioral health provider within **6 months prior to the first mental health telehealth service**, and **annually thereafter** (every 12 months for ongoing telehealth). This requirement is codified at 42 CFR 410.78(b)(3)(xiv).

**However, this requirement has not yet taken effect.** Subsequent legislation has repeatedly delayed implementation; as of June 2026, it is scheduled to apply to services furnished **on or after January 1, 2028** [KFF, "What to Know About Medicare Coverage of Telehealth," 2026; CMS CY 2026 PFS Final Rule]. FQHC and RHC in-person requirements face the same delay (effective after January 1, 2028) [CMS CY 2026 PFS Final Rule].

**When it does take effect**: New patients need an in-person visit within 6 months before starting telehealth mental health services. Established patients (those who received mental health services on or before January 30, 2026) will be required to have at least one in-person visit every 12 months rather than the 6-month rule. Exceptions must be documented in the medical record.

## COVID Telehealth Flexibilities — Extended Through December 31, 2027

The Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (signed February 3, 2026) extended the following through **December 31, 2027** [KFF, 2026; NASW statement, 2026; healthlawdiagnosis.com, Feb 2026]:
- **Geographic/originating site waivers** for non–mental health telehealth: beneficiaries may receive services in any location
- **Expanded practitioners**: occupational therapists, physical therapists, speech-language pathologists, audiologists continue
- **FQHC/RHC telehealth**: FQHCs and RHCs may provide mental health visits via telehealth without meeting the in-person service requirement (through Dec 31, 2027)
- **Audio-only for non–BH services**: continued through Dec 31, 2027
- **Hospice recertification** via telehealth: through Dec 31, 2027
- **Delay of 6-month in-person requirement** for telemental health: through January 1, 2028

**CBO score**: The CBO scored the CAA 2026 temporary extension through December 2027 as costing **$3.8 billion from 2026 to 2028** [KFF, 2026]. CBO has not scored a permanent extension.

**OTP telehealth**: Opioid Treatment Programs may furnish intake, periodic assessment, and counseling add-on codes via audio-video; audio-only is permitted when audio-video is unavailable. Beginning January 1, 2025, OTPs may furnish the OTP intake add-on code for methadone initiation via two-way audio-video. See CMS OTP billing guidance for current G-codes [CMS OTP Payment Rates].

## Ryan Haight Act and DEA Telemedicine Rules

The Ryan Haight Online Pharmacy Consumer Protection Act (2008) required an in-person medical evaluation before prescribing controlled substances via telemedicine. Two separate mechanisms now apply post-PHE:

### DEA Buprenorphine Final Rule — Permanent (Effective Dec 31, 2025)
- DEA/HHS published two final rules on **January 17, 2025** (90 FR 5941); effective date delayed to **December 31, 2025** [Federal Register 2025-01049; SAMHSA statement Jan 2025]
- Practitioners may prescribe buprenorphine (Schedule III) for OUD via telemedicine **without a prior in-person exam**
- Initial supply: up to a **6-month total supply** from the date of the first prescription
- Conditions: PDMP check required; pharmacist must verify patient identity before filling; audio-only telemedicine is permitted for Schedule III–V OUD medications under this rule
- This is a permanent rule — not contingent on further legislative extensions

### DEA Fourth Temporary Extension — Schedule II–V (Through Dec 31, 2026)
- DEA/HHS issued a **fourth temporary extension** of COVID-19 telemedicine flexibilities for prescribing controlled medications, effective January 1, 2026 through **December 31, 2026** [Federal Register 2025-24123, Dec 31, 2025]
- Covers Schedule II–V controlled substances (including Schedule II stimulants such as ADHD medications) prescribed via telemedicine without an in-person exam
- After December 31, 2026, permanent rules for Schedule II stimulant telehealth prescribing remain unfinalized as of June 2026

### Practical Impact
- Psychiatrists and prescribers can maintain buprenorphine patients via telehealth permanently under the DEA final rule
- ADHD stimulant prescribing via telehealth (Schedule II) continues under the temporary extension through Dec 31, 2026; permanent framework still pending

## New BH Provider Types in Medicare — LMHC and LMFT (Effective Jan 1, 2024)

CAA 2023 (not CAA 2021) permanently added two new provider types to Medicare Part B BH coverage, implemented via the CY 2024 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule Final Rule [CMS MFT/MHC page; CMS CY 2024 PFS Final Rule]:
- **Licensed Marriage and Family Therapists (MFTs/LMFTs)**: effective January 1, 2024; can bill Medicare independently for outpatient BH services, including telehealth; paid at 75% of clinical psychologist rate under the PFS; taxonomy code 106H00000X
- **Mental Health Counselors (MHCs/LMHCs)**: effective January 1, 2024; can bill Medicare independently; paid at 75% of clinical psychologist rate; taxonomy code 101YM0800X

Both can provide telehealth services under the CAA 2021 geographic waiver. This significantly expanded the pool of Medicare-eligible BH telehealth providers.

## Medicare Advantage Telehealth for BH

- MA plans can offer telehealth beyond Original Medicare; many offer unlimited telehealth BH
- MA plans may waive in-person requirement, cost-sharing, or frequency limits on telehealth BH
- MA telehealth BH benefits are a plan choice (supplemental); compare plan Evidence of Coverage

## Policy Outlook

- Strong bipartisan support for permanent extension of Medicare telehealth flexibilities beyond Dec 31, 2027 (Telehealth Modernization Act introduced 119th Congress, H.R. 5081 / S. 2709)
- CBO scored the temporary 2027 extension at $3.8B (2026–2028); permanent extension has not been scored
- Key pending issues: permanence of ADHD stimulant telehealth prescribing (DEA); permanent extension of audio-only for non-BH; remote patient monitoring for BH
- The in-person requirement for mental health telehealth — currently delayed to January 2028 — will require ongoing legislative attention
