# Medicaid Section 1115 Waivers for SDOH and Housing

## Overview

Social determinants of health — housing instability, food insecurity, transportation barriers, and economic hardship — account for an estimated 40% of modifiable health determinants (County Health Rankings model: 40% social/economic, 30% health behaviors, 20% clinical care, 10% physical environment [RWJF/UW Population Health Institute: https://www.countyhealthrankings.org/findings-and-insights/2023-county-health-rankings-national-findings-report]) but have historically been outside Medicaid's statutory scope (Medicaid must pay for "medical assistance," not social services). Section 1115 demonstrations allow CMS to waive certain statutory requirements and test coverage of services addressing SDOH, within limits. [KFF, "Medicaid Section 1115 Waivers: The Basics," https://www.kff.org/medicaid/medicaid-section-1115-waivers-the-basics/]

## Federal Policy Framework

### Section 1115 Authority
- Statutory authority: **42 U.S.C. § 1315** (Social Security Act § 1115). Secretary of HHS may approve "experimental, pilot, or demonstration projects likely to promote the objectives of [Medicaid]" and waive requirements under SSA § 1902. [SSA § 1115 text: https://www.ssa.gov/OP_Home/ssact/title11/1115.htm]
- Waiver of most § 1902 state plan requirements is permitted; cannot waive constitutionally-rooted fair hearing rights or federal matching payment systems
- Budget neutrality is a **CMS policy requirement** (not defined in statute or regulation): estimated federal cost over the demonstration period cannot exceed what would have been spent without it; calculated on a per-enrollee basis [KFF 1115 Basics]
- **5-year initial approval; renewals typically 3–5 years** (statute provides for up to 3-year extension when state requests renewal in the 6-month window ending 1 year before expiration; Trump-era CMS approved some 10-year renewals) [MACPAC: https://www.macpac.gov/subtopic/section-1115-research-and-demonstration-waivers/]

### 2023 CMS HRSN Guidance (Rescinded March 2025 — Existing Approvals Retained)
**Important policy status note (verified 2026-06-22):** On November 16, 2023, CMS issued CMCS Informational Bulletin (CIB) on coverage of services and supports to address health-related social needs (HRSNs) [https://www.medicaid.gov/federal-policy-guidance/downloads/cib11162023.pdf], accompanied by a Medicaid and CHIP HRSN Framework. On **March 4, 2025**, CMS rescinded the November 2023 CIB, a December 2024 follow-up CIB, and the HRSN framework [Rescission bulletin: https://www.medicaid.gov/federal-policy-guidance/downloads/cib03042025.pdf]. The rescission does **not** affect existing approved 1115 HRSN waivers; new applications are evaluated case-by-case. [AAMC: https://www.aamc.org/advocacy-policy/washington-highlights/cms-rescinds-guidance-addressing-health-related-social-needs-medicaid]

Key features of the 2023 framework (still reflected in existing approved waivers):
- **Approvable**: services that address "health-related social needs" (HRSNs) — clinical-social nexus where the social factor directly affects medical condition management; 15 enumerated HRSN service categories primarily covering housing and nutrition
- **Not approvable**: general social services, open-ended housing subsidies (rent beyond narrow duration/population limits), general food assistance equivalent to SNAP
- Must target a defined clinical population (not universal SDOH coverage)
- **HRSN spending cap**: 3% of total annual state Medicaid expenditures; HRSN infrastructure spending further capped at 15% of total HRSN expenditure authority [KFF HRSN Waiver Watch: https://www.kff.org/medicaid/section-1115-medicaid-waiver-watch-a-closer-look-at-recent-approvals-to-address-health-related-social-needs-hrsn/]
- As of early 2024, 8 states received Biden-era HRSN 1115 approvals: Arizona, Arkansas, California, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, and Washington [KFF HRSN Waiver Watch]

## Approvable SDOH Services Under 1115

### Housing-Related Services
| Service | What It Covers | NOT Covered |
|---|---|---|
| Housing navigation | Search for housing, applications, coordinate with housing agencies | Open-ended rent payments |
| Tenancy support services | Coaching to maintain housing stability (conflict resolution, lease compliance, tenant rights education, eviction prevention) | Long-term housing subsidies |
| Transition to community living | Help transitioning from institutional settings (SNF, hospital, IMD) to housing | Ongoing rent assistance |
| Temporary housing/rent assistance | Short-term bridge housing for clinical transition — **up to 6 months, once per demonstration period** (approved in AZ, NY, OR, WA) | Permanent supportive housing subsidy |

### Nutrition/Food-Related Services
- **Medically tailored meals (MTM)**: meals designed for specific medical conditions (diabetes, heart failure, renal disease, HIV); evidence-based; duration limits apply (up to 3 meals/day for 6 months, renewable in 6-month increments if eligibility criteria continue to be met)
- **Healthy food assistance**: grocery vouchers or produce prescriptions for high-risk conditions (varies by state)
- General food assistance (SNAP-equivalent) — **not approvable**

### Transportation
- Medicaid Non-Emergency Medical Transportation (NEMT) already required in state plan; 1115 can extend to broader SDOH-linked transport
- Rideshare partnerships for medical appointments (Lyft, Uber Health pilots)

### Community Health Workers (CHWs)
- States increasingly cover CHW services via State Plan Amendments under § 1905(a)(19) preventive services authority — not only 1115 waivers
- CMS approved CHW State Plan Amendments in several states (AZ, KS, KY, MI, NY) in 2023; clinician referral typically required for Medicaid billing [KFF CHW state policies: https://www.kff.org/medicaid/state-policies-for-expanding-medicaid-coverage-of-community-health-worker-chw-services/]
- CHWs conduct SDOH screening, navigation, coaching
- Requires state to define CHW training/certification requirements

### Other SDOH Services
- Caregiver respite: short-term relief for unpaid caregivers (approvable for medically complex populations)
- Interpersonal violence support services
- Climate risk mitigation (devices, home modifications) for medically vulnerable — Oregon's HRSN includes climate-related devices

## CalAIM — California's SDOH Exemplar (launched January 2022)

California's CalAIM (California Advancing and Innovating Medi-Cal) is the most comprehensive SDOH 1115 waiver, launched January 1, 2022. [CA DHCS: https://calaim.dhcs.ca.gov/pages/community-supports]

**Enhanced Care Management (ECM)**: whole-person care management for high-need populations (homeless, repeated ED users, justice-involved, aging with complexity). Includes SDOH screening, care coordination, and linkage.

**Community Supports (CS)**: 14 original optional services MCOs can offer (populations with HRSNs), expanded to **15** with Transitional Rent added (optional for MCPs from July 1, 2025; **mandatory January 1, 2026**). [DHCS Community Supports Policy Guide Vol. 2: https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/Documents/MCQMD/DHCS-Community-Supports-Policy-Guide-Volume-2.pdf]

Complete list (per DHCS, April 2025):
1. Housing Transition/Navigation Services
2. Housing Deposits (short-term bridge)
3. Housing Tenancy and Sustaining Services
4. Short-Term Post-Hospitalization Housing
5. Recuperative Care (Medical Respite)
6. Caregiver Respite
7. Day Habilitation Programs
8. Assisted Living Facility Transitions
9. Community or Home Transition Services
10. Personal Care and Homemaker Services
11. Environmental Accessibility Adaptations
12. Medically-Supportive Food/Meals/Medically Tailored Meals
13. Sobering Centers
14. Asthma Remediation
15. Transitional Rent *(mandatory for MCPs January 1, 2026 — up to 6 months of rental assistance)*

**Outcomes (as of 2024)**: Over 239,000 Medi-Cal members served; more than 75,000 used a housing support between July 2023 and June 2024. [DHCS CS Supplemental Fact Sheet: https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/CalAIM/Documents/DHCS-Medi-Cal-Community-Supports-Supplemental-Fact-Sheet.pdf]

## HUD-HHS Partnership

- CMS and HUD Memorandums of Agreement allow Medicaid housing navigation to coordinate with HUD housing voucher systems
- **Housing and Services Resource Center** (HHS-HUD): technical assistance to states connecting Medicaid and HUD programs
- Supportive housing: Medicaid pays for services portion; HUD/housing authority pays for rent subsidy — **split financing model** (Medicaid cannot pay rent but can pay for the clinical services wrap)

## ARP Provisions Expanding SDOH-Adjacent Medicaid Coverage

Three American Rescue Plan Act (ARP) provisions complement 1115 SDOH waivers by expanding coverage and federal match rates:

### ARP § 9817 — HCBS FMAP Enhancement (verified 2026-06-22)
- Provided a **10 percentage-point FMAP increase** for Medicaid home and community-based services (HCBS), effective **April 1, 2021 – March 31, 2022** (earning period); states could spend funds through approximately March 2025 (extensions approved for ~half of states)
- Supplement-not-supplant requirement: funds must add to — not replace — existing state HCBS spending
- All 50 states and D.C. approved; estimated **$35 billion** total injection into HCBS system
- [CMS ARP § 9817 page: https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/home-community-based-services/home-community-based-services-guidance-additional-resources/strengthening-and-investing-home-and-community-based-services-for-medicaid-beneficiaries-american-rescue-plan-act-of-2021-section-9817]

### ARP § 9813 — Community-Based Mobile Crisis Services (verified 2026-06-22)
- Created **SSA § 1947**: new state option for qualifying community-based mobile crisis intervention services, April 1, 2022 – March 31, 2027 (5-year window)
- **Enhanced FMAP: 85%** for the first 12 fiscal quarters a state qualifies; standard FMAP thereafter
- Requirements: 24/7 availability; trauma-informed/de-escalation/harm-reduction training; at least one behavioral health professional per team; may include peer support specialist; established community linkages
- Coverage authorized via state plan, § 1915(b)/(c) waiver, or § 1115 demonstration
- [CMS mobile crisis state option page: https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/benefits/behavioral-health-services/state-option-provide-qualifying-community-based-mobile-crisis-intervention-services; KFF state take-up: https://www.kff.org/medicaid/issue-brief/a-look-at-state-take-up-of-arpa-mobile-crisis-services-in-medicaid/]

### Medicaid Reentry § 1115 Demonstration — Pre-Release Services for Incarcerated Individuals (verified 2026-06-22)
- **April 17, 2023 State Medicaid Director Letter (SMDL)**: CMS invited states to apply for § 1115 demonstrations allowing Medicaid coverage of a **pre-release services package for up to 90 days** before expected release — overcoming the longstanding Medicaid "IMD exclusion for incarcerated individuals" bar
- Statutory authority: implements SUPPORT Act § 5032 (2018), which directed HHS to issue guidance on § 1115 demonstrations for justice-involved individuals
- Covered services can include physical/behavioral health care in corrections settings + pre- and post-release care management
- As of mid-2023, 16+ states submitted applications; California and Washington among first approved
- [CMS reentry demonstration page: https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/section-1115-demonstrations/reentry-section-1115-demonstrations; CMS press release: https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/hhs-releases-new-guidance-encourage-states-apply-new-medicaid-reentry-section-1115-demonstration]

## Limits and Policy Tensions

1. **Budget neutrality**: every HRSN dollar must be offset by demonstrable savings (hospitalization reduction, ED avoidance); limits scale; HRSN spending capped at 3% of annual state Medicaid expenditures
2. **Scope creep**: Congress limited Medicaid to "medical assistance"; longstanding prohibition on "room and board" has been loosened but only for narrow populations and durations
3. **Infrastructure gap**: Medicaid billing infrastructure (encounter data, provider enrollment) not designed for housing agencies and food banks
4. **Waiver churn and federal policy shifts**: 1115s expire; the March 2025 rescission of HRSN guidance creates uncertainty for new waiver applications and renewals; existing approvals retained
5. **State funding dependency**: Federal waiver authority alone insufficient — NC HOP suspended July 2025 when state legislature did not appropriate matching funds despite active federal approval
6. **Equity**: SDOH benefits may not reach the most marginalized if enrollment into managed care (which often administers SDOH benefits) is incomplete
