# Medicaid and Federal Financing for Crisis Services

## Overview

The US behavioral health crisis response system is financed through a patchwork of Medicaid, block grant, state general fund, and federal 988 funding. The American Rescue Plan Act of 2021 (ARP, P.L. 117-2) created a major new Medicaid authority for mobile crisis teams via **ARP Section 9813**, which added **SSA Section 1947** to Title XIX. The National Suicide Prevention Lifeline's transition to 988 launched July 16, 2022 under the National Suicide Hotline Designation Act of 2020 (P.L. 116-172). Understanding these streams is essential for states building a continuum of crisis care.

## 988 Lifeline — Federal Funding Structure

### SAMHSA Grants (Federal)
- SAMHSA administers 988 Lifeline network grant funding to Vibrant Emotional Health (the network administrator) and state/territorial capacity grants
- FY2026 administrator cooperative agreement: **$255 million** awarded to Vibrant Emotional Health (announced May 15, 2026; NOFO cap was $231M) [verified-as-of 2026-06-22; source: SAMHSA press release 20260515]
- Funds call center capacity, training, follow-up services, and state/tribal capacity-building
- **Does not fund** mobile crisis dispatch or crisis stabilization units directly
- Additional state and tribal 988 infrastructure grants distributed separately

### State 988 Funding — Telecom Surcharge
- States may impose a 988 surcharge on phone bills, authorized by the National Suicide Hotline Designation Act of 2020 (analogous to 911 funding)
- **~12 states** have enacted 988 telecom fees as of mid-2025 (incl. Virginia, Colorado, Washington, Nevada, Minnesota, California, Oregon, Delaware, Maryland, Vermont, and others); early adopters collected $8–$44.3M in CY2023 [source: KFF, "Demand for 988 Continues to Grow at Third Anniversary"; NASHP State Tracker]
- This is materially lower than early projections; the skill's prior "~30 states" figure was overstated

### Medicaid Funding for 988-Connected Services
- 988 calls themselves are not Medicaid billable
- **Post-call follow-up** by licensed BH professionals: billable under Medicaid as outreach/care management if state has authorized
- Mobile crisis dispatch triggered by 988: billable under ARP/SSA §1947 authority (see below)
- Crisis stabilization following 988 referral: billable under Medicaid if meeting coverage criteria

## ARP Mobile Crisis Authority (ARP §9813 / SSA §1947)

ARP Section 9813 (P.L. 117-2, enacted March 11, 2021) amended Title XIX of the Social Security Act by adding **Section 1947**, titled "State Option to Provide Qualifying Community-Based Mobile Crisis Intervention Services." [source: congress.gov PLAW-117publ2; CMS SHO 21-008; medicaid.gov §1947 page]

### Coverage
- States may elect to cover mobile crisis intervention services as a **state plan benefit** (no waiver required)
- Coverage period: **April 1, 2022 – March 31, 2027** (five-year window)
- **Enhanced FMAP: 85%** federal match for mobile crisis services for **up to 12 fiscal quarters** within that window (vs. standard FMAP of ~50–77%) [verified-as-of 2026-06-22]

### Eligible Population
- Individuals with mental health or co-occurring SUD conditions in a **community crisis situation**
- Services delivered outside hospital or facility setting (home, street, community)

### Covered Services (SSA §1947 / CMS SHO 21-008)
- Screening and assessment
- Stabilization and de-escalation
- Crisis intervention in the community
- Coordination with and referrals to health, social, and other services
- Duration: typically time-limited to the acute crisis episode (active crisis intervention)

### Team Requirements
- Must include **at least one behavioral health professional** plus other trained professionals or paraprofessionals [source: CMS SHO 21-008; SHVS explainer]
- Training requirements: trauma-informed care, de-escalation, harm reduction
- Must be available **24/7, every day of the year**
- **Law enforcement**: the statute does not prohibit law enforcement from participating on teams; states retain flexibility in team composition. CMS guidance consistently emphasizes behavioral health-led response as the policy preference ("behavioral health experts, not law enforcement, should be first responders"). Co-responder models (officer + BH professional) are one existing model states may use. [source: SHVS summary; CMS SHO 21-008]

### State Uptake
As of **September 2024**, approximately **20 states plus DC** had received CMS approval for ARP §1947 mobile crisis state plan amendments [source: CMS newsroom press release, Biden-Harris Administration mobile crisis awards; KFF ARPA mobile crisis take-up brief]. The prior claim of "~35 states" was overstated.

## Medicaid Billing for Crisis Services by Type

| Service | Medicaid Authority | Cost-Sharing |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile crisis (community) | ARP §9813/SSA §1947 SPA (2022–2027, 85% FMAP up to 12 qtrs); 1915(b) waiver; rehabilitative services | No cost-sharing for crisis per CMS guidance |
| Crisis stabilization unit (CSU) | Rehabilitative services (SSA §1905(a)(13)); 1915(c) waiver; 1115 waiver | Varies by state |
| Psychiatric emergency (hospital ED) | Medicare Part A inpatient; Part B emergency; Medicaid inpatient/outpatient | Standard cost-sharing |
| Short-term crisis residential (>16 beds) | IMD exclusion limits Medicaid payment; 15-day MCO exception (42 CFR §438.6(e)); 1115 waiver | Varies |
| Crisis respite (≤16 beds) | 1915(c) HCBS; 1915(i); rehabilitative services | Varies |
| 24/7 crisis line (warm transfers, follow-up) | Not directly billable; follow-up billable as care management | N/A |

## Crisis Residential and the IMD Exclusion

The IMD exclusion (see medicaid-imd-ccbhc.md) limits Medicaid payment for facilities with >16 beds for mental illness:
- Crisis stabilization units ≤16 beds: Medicaid billable as rehabilitative services
- Crisis residential >16 beds: subject to IMD exclusion
- **15-day exception (managed care)**: Under **42 CFR §438.6(e)**, states may make monthly capitation payments to MCOs/PIHPs for enrollees aged 21–64 receiving inpatient mental health or SUD crisis residential treatment in an IMD for stays of **no more than 15 days** per month [source: eCFR current; Federal Register 2020-24758]
- **1115 SUD waivers**: many states use 1115 waivers to pay for SUD residential crisis care in IMD-sized facilities
- **SUPPORT Act IMD state plan option**: states may cover short-term SUD residential in IMD for MAT-related crisis

## CCBHC Crisis Services Requirement

CCBHCs are required to provide **nine required service categories** (SAMHSA CCBHC Certification Criteria 2023), of which crisis services is one [source: SAMHSA ccbhc-criteria-2023.pdf]:
- 24/7 crisis coverage (phone or in-person); mobile crisis response within 3 hours
- Mobile crisis
- Crisis peer support
- Walk-in crisis
- Crisis stabilization (residential or non-residential)

CCBHCs bill through PPS which bundles crisis service costs into the daily encounter rate.

## Block Grant Funding (SABG / MHBG)

### Substance Abuse Block Grant (SABG / now SUBG)
- Annual appropriation approximately **~$1.9B** (PHSA §1935 authorized $1.86B/year for FY2018–FY2022; subsequent appropriations have maintained roughly similar levels) [source: CRS R46426 SAMHSA Overview]
- Note: SAMHSA distributed $475M as an **initial/first allotment** of FY2026 SUBG awards (announced February 4, 2026); this is a partial distribution, not the total annual appropriation [source: SAMHSA press announcement 20260204]
- Must use 20% for primary prevention; remaining for SUD treatment including crisis (detox, withdrawal management)

### Community Mental Health Block Grant (MHBG)
- Annual appropriation approximately **~$700M–$1B** [source: CRS R46426; SAMHSA FY2026 initial allotment $319M = first partial distribution]
- Note: SAMHSA distributed $319M as an **initial/first allotment** of FY2026 MHBG awards (announced February 4, 2026) [source: SAMHSA press announcement 20260204]
- Must use 5% for children's mental health services
- Crisis services are a core MHBG use; much state crisis infrastructure is MHBG-funded

### MHBG Set-Asides for Crisis / Diversion
- Recent appropriations have included targeted MHBG set-asides for: assisted outpatient treatment (AOT), early psychosis intervention (EPIP), crisis alternatives to ED

## State Crisis Financing Models

| State | Key Features |
|---|---|
| North Carolina | 988 surcharge enacted 2023; ARP §1947 mobile crisis SPA approved; LME-MCOs operate mobile crisis and CSUs under Tailored Plan contracts |
| California | ARP §1947 mobile crisis SPA + CalAIM crisis residential as Community Support; 1115 waiver covers crisis residential |
| New York | OMH-funded crisis stabilization centers + Medicaid billing; peer-run crisis respite beds |
| Washington | Certified Community Behavioral Health Agencies (CCBHAs, Washington-specific); Medicaid-financed 24/7 crisis; early 988 telecom fee adopter |
| Colorado | CO Crisis Services system: statewide 24/7 walk-in, mobile crisis, and crisis residential financed through state fund + Medicaid + MHBG; early 988 telecom fee adopter |
