# Medicaid Rehabilitative and Behavioral Health Services

Medicaid provides behavioral health coverage through a mix of mandatory and optional state plan benefits. The rehabilitative services option is a flexible authority allowing states to cover a broad range of mental health and SUD services beyond what is explicitly required.

> **Scope note**: This skill covers the rehabilitative services option primarily as applied to behavioral health and SUD. Physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech-language pathology, and school-based rehabilitative services are also covered under 42 CFR 440.130(a)–(d) but are not detailed here.

---

## Mandatory vs. Optional Medicaid Benefits

### Mandatory Benefits (All States Must Cover) [^1][^2]

Federal law requires states to cover:
- Inpatient hospital services (medically necessary)
- Outpatient hospital services
- Physician services
- Rural health clinic services
- Nursing facility services
- Home health services

**Mental health/SUD within mandatory benefits**: States define "medical necessity" individually, subject to CMS approval. Basic psychiatric services can be covered within hospital inpatient/outpatient authority.

### Optional Benefits Relevant to Mental Health and SUD [^1][^2]

States may elect these optional benefits through state plan amendments:

| Benefit | Coverage notes | State uptake (KFF 2022 survey, n=45)[^6] |
|---------|---------------|------------------------|
| Rehabilitative services (1905(a)(13)) | Broadest authority; flexible delivery | Near-universal |
| Prescribed medicines | Psychotropic medications, MAT | All states |
| Targeted case management | Coordination for behavioral health populations | Widespread |
| Clinic services | Community mental health centers (CMHCs) | Widespread |
| FQHC services | Federally Qualified Health Center behavioral health | All states |
| Inpatient psychiatric (age <21, age 65+) | IMD services for exempted age groups | Optional; varies |
| Psychosocial rehabilitation (Clubhouse model) | Day treatment, clubhouse programs | 34 states |
| Peer support | Certified peer specialists | 40 states |
| Supported employment (IPS-SE) | Individual Placement and Support model | Not separately tracked in 2022 survey |
| Telehealth for behavioral health | Audio/video behavioral telehealth | Majority of states; most expanded permanently post-COVID |
| Outpatient detoxification | Medically supervised withdrawal (outpatient) | 34 states |
| Inpatient detoxification (ASAM Level 4) | Medically managed intensive inpatient | 39 states |
| Partial hospitalization | Intensive structured programming | 35 states |

## The Rehabilitative Services Option: Section 1905(a)(13)

### Statutory Definition [^3]

42 U.S.C. § 1396d(a)(13)(C) defines rehabilitative services as:

> "any medical or remedial services (provided in a facility, a home, or other setting) recommended by a physician or other licensed practitioner of the healing arts within the scope of their practice under State law, for the maximum reduction of physical or mental disability and restoration of an individual to the best possible functional level"

The regulatory implementation is at **42 CFR 440.130(d)**, which defines rehabilitative services as:

> "Medical or remedial services recommended by a physician or other licensed practitioner of the healing arts, within the scope of his practice under State law, for maximum reduction of physical or mental disability and restoration of a recipient to his best possible functional level."

**Key interpretive point**: Restoration to best possible functional level — does NOT require prior performance of the activity, only prior capability.

### Note on the 2007 Proposed Rule [^4]

CMS published a proposed rule (CMS-2261-P, 72 FR 46141, Aug. 13, 2007) that would have added written rehabilitation plan and case record requirements to 42 CFR 440.130. That rule was subject to repeated congressional moratoria (MMSEA of 2007, P.L. 110-173; Supplemental Appropriations Act of 2008) and was **withdrawn in November 2009 without being finalized**. The current operative definition remains 42 CFR 440.130(d) as quoted above.

### Scope of Covered Services [^3]

Under the rehab option, states can cover:
- Individual and group psychotherapy
- Individual and group counseling
- Crisis intervention and stabilization
- Training in independent living skills
- Recovery support and relapse prevention
- Employability skills training
- Relationship skills training
- Psychiatric rehabilitation services
- Community integration support
- Illness management and recovery (IMR)
- Family psychoeducation

**Delivery flexibility**: Services may be delivered in community settings (not just clinical offices) and by paraprofessionals and peer specialists — a major advantage over more restrictive service definitions.

### Provider Types [^3]

Broader than some other Medicaid service categories:
- Licensed therapists and counselors (LCSW, LPC, MFT)
- Psychologists
- Nurses under supervision
- Paraprofessionals under clinical supervision
- Certified peer support specialists (in states that have defined this)

## Medicaid SUD Treatment Coverage

### Coverage Across ASAM Continuum [^5][^6]

All states cover some SUD treatment, but the breadth varies significantly:

| ASAM Level | Service | Medicaid coverage |
|------------|---------|-------------------|
| 1.0 | Outpatient SUD counseling | All states (via rehab option or clinic services) |
| 2.1 | Intensive Outpatient (IOP) | Most states |
| 2.5 | Partial Hospitalization | 35 states (KFF 2022) |
| 3.1–3.7 | Residential treatment | Varies; limited by IMD exclusion (see note) |
| 4.0 | Medically managed inpatient | 39 states (ASAM Level 4; KFF 2022) |
| OTP/Opioid services | MAT in OTPs | All states |

**IMD exclusion impact**: Residential SUD facilities with >16 beds are classified as IMDs for adults 21–64, restricting federal Medicaid payment — see `medicaid-imd-ccbhc` for detail.

### ASAM Criteria in Medicaid [^5][^7]

The American Society of Addiction Medicine (ASAM) criteria are increasingly required or referenced in Medicaid SUD medical necessity determinations:

- CMS guidance for Section 1115 SUD waiver states requires residential providers to meet ASAM criteria or **other nationally recognized, evidence-based, SUD-specific program standards** (ASAM is specified as an example, not the only option) within **12–24 months of waiver approval**. [^7]
- States implementing SUD 1115 waivers must use evidence-based, SUD-specific individual placement criteria and implement independent utilization management within 24 months of approval.
- ASAM criteria are **encouraged but not universally required** under a standard state plan (non-waiver).

**ASAM 6 dimensions** for placement decisions:
1. Acute intoxication/withdrawal potential
2. Biomedical conditions and complications
3. Emotional/behavioral/cognitive conditions
4. Readiness to change
5. Relapse/continued use/continued problem potential
6. Recovery/living environment

### Medication-Assisted Treatment (MAT) [^2][^5]

All states cover MAT in Medicaid:
- **Methadone** (for OUD): covered via OTP bundled services or clinic services
- **Buprenorphine** (sublingual, injectable, implantable): covered under pharmacy benefit + prescribing visits
- **Naltrexone** (oral and injectable): covered under pharmacy benefit
- **Naloxone**: covered (often without copay under state emergency prescription standing orders)

States may apply prior authorization requirements to MAT, though CMS has encouraged removing administrative barriers.

## Community Mental Health Center (CMHC) Authority

States can cover CMHC services through:
1. **Clinic services option** (1905(a)(9) / 42 CFR 440.90): enables outpatient mental health clinic coverage — defined as "preventive, diagnostic, therapeutic, rehabilitative, or palliative services furnished by a facility that is not part of a hospital but is organized and operated to provide medical care to outpatients" [^3]
2. **Rehabilitative services option** (1905(a)(13) / 42 CFR 440.130(d)): broadest delivery flexibility
3. **PHP authority**: partial hospitalization at CMHCs (not just hospital-based)

CMHCs serve as safety-net providers for Medicaid beneficiaries; coverage through multiple state plan authorities provides redundancy.

---

## Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs)

FQHCs receive enhanced Medicaid reimbursement (Prospective Payment System / PPS) for all services including: [^8]
- Primary care with integrated behavioral health
- Mental health and substance use counseling
- Case management
- Enabling services

FQHC authority is at **42 U.S.C. § 1396d(a)(2)(C)** and (l)(2) — FQHCs can bill behavioral health services regardless of state's other optional benefit elections; FQHC coverage is a mandatory state obligation under federal law.

---

## Peer Support Services

As of July 1, 2022, **40 states** cover peer support services under Medicaid for adults. [^6] CMS published FAQ guidance on June 5, 2024, clarifying peer support coverage policy (building on the August 2007 State Medicaid Director letter). [^9]

Key features:
- States most commonly authorize peer support under the **rehabilitative services option** (1905(a)(13)) or clinic services option
- State certification/training requirements for peer specialists are set by each state (CMS does not prescribe a specific national credential)
- CMS encourages expansion to emergency department and inpatient settings

---

## State Variation and Design Considerations

Medicaid behavioral health coverage varies enormously by state:

| Design axis | Range across states |
|-------------|---------------------|
| Provider types eligible | Licensed therapists only → paraprofessionals |
| Service settings | Clinic-only → community/home-based |
| Prior authorization burden | None → extensive for every service type |
| Peer support coverage | 40 of 45 surveyed states (KFF 2022) |
| Residential SUD coverage | None → 1115 waiver with ASAM requirements |
| Telehealth availability | Majority of states allow audio/video BH telehealth |

Managed care organizations (MCOs) administer Medicaid behavioral health in most states — MCOs can further restrict or expand coverage within CMS-approved parameters.

## Anti-Patterns / Common Mistakes

- **Assuming all states cover the same services**: Medicaid optional benefits mean coverage varies significantly; always check state-specific benefit.
- **Treating the rehab option as limited to PT/OT**: it covers a broad range of behavioral health services including counseling, peer support, and community integration.
- **Assuming ASAM criteria mandatory in all Medicaid**: required by CMS in 1115 waiver states for residential SUD (as one acceptable standard); encouraged but not universally required under standard state plan.
- **Understating peer support uptake**: as of July 2022, 40 of 45 surveyed states cover peer support — it is now a widespread, not rare, Medicaid benefit.
- **Applying IMD exclusion to all residential**: applies to adult (21–64) settings with >16 beds; different rules for children and older adults.
- **Citing the 2007 Federal Register proposed rule as current authority**: CMS-2261-P was withdrawn in 2009 without being finalized; the operative definition is 42 CFR 440.130(d).
