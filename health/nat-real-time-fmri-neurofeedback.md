# Real-Time fMRI Neurofeedback for Craving Regulation

## Core Concepts
- **Real-time fMRI neurofeedback (rtfMRI-NF):** Closed-loop system where BOLD signal from a target ROI is continuously extracted, displayed as a visual thermometer or graph, and fed back to participants in-scanner so they can volitionally downregulate activity
- **Closed-loop downregulation:** Participants use cognitive strategies (imagery, reappraisal, distraction) while monitoring feedback; successful suppression of craving circuits reinforces the strategy via operant learning
- **Target circuits:** Anterior insula (interoceptive salience/craving), anterior cingulate cortex (ACC; conflict monitoring, negative affect), nucleus accumbens (NAc/ventral striatum; reward anticipation, cue reactivity)
- **Individualized ROI:** Modern protocols extract subject-specific craving networks via localizer runs (cue-reactivity task pre-scan) rather than anatomical masks, improving signal specificity
- **Connectivity-based NF:** Emerging approach feeds back functional connectivity between circuits (e.g., PFC–insula) rather than single-region activation, targeting dysregulated top-down regulation

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Insula encodes interoceptive drug cue salience; elevated insula BOLD during cue exposure correlates with self-reported craving intensity across nicotine, cocaine, and alcohol
- ACC mediates reward-based learning during neurofeedback; whole-brain analyses show dorsal ACC as a mediator of insula downregulation success
- NAc (ventral striatum) activation to drug cues is blunted following successful NF training sessions, persisting on post-training cue-reactivity scans
- Downregulation success involves recruitment of lateral PFC (dlPFC/vlPFC) — participants who engage PFC more strongly show greater insula suppression, consistent with top-down inhibitory control models
- Operant learning mechanism: BOLD thermometer provides millisecond-to-TR (2–3 s) delayed reinforcement; learning accrues across runs within a session and consolidates across sessions

## Clinical Picture
- Nicotine use disorder is the most-studied indication; trials demonstrate that smokers can reduce ACC and insula activation to smoking cues, accompanied by reduced self-reported urge ratings
- Frontiers 2019 cohort: First-session neurofeedback response (downregulator vs upregulator profile) predicted 6-month abstinence vs relapse in tobacco use disorder patients
- Alcohol use disorder: Whole-brain rtfMRI-NF studies show feasibility for craving downregulation; selecting optimal feedback methods (connectivity vs activation vs multivariate) is an active area (PubMed 37326428)
- Cocaine craving: NCT04155632 (neurocircuit strategy) is a registered protocol using rtfMRI-NF to reduce cocaine cue reactivity; evidence base smaller than nicotine
- Internet gaming disorder RCT (JMIR 2025): 2 sessions of dopaminergic midbrain NF in at-risk individuals achieved significant downregulation of gaming cue responses

## Evidence & Treatment
- **NicoNF paradigm (Hartwell et al.):** Nicotine-dependent smokers receiving individualized ACC/insula feedback attenuated cue-elicited BOLD and craving relative to yoked-control feedback; effect sustained at follow-up (PMC4688028)
- **Sustained craving reduction:** Single-session rtfMRI-NF produced sustained nicotine craving reduction correlated with dependence severity; heavier smokers showed larger post-session craving drops (PMC3819983)
- **rtfMRI vs EEG neurofeedback tradeoffs:** rtfMRI offers superior spatial resolution (~mm), enabling precise subcortical targeting (NAc, insula); EEG offers temporal resolution (~ms), portability, cost <$50K vs >$500K/scanner, and scalability; EEG-fMRI hybrid approaches proposed as a middle path
- **Session dose:** Current trials use 2–10 sessions; 2-session internet gaming RCT (2025) showed efficacy, but addiction trials typically employ 3–6 sessions with 1–3 runs each
- **Systematic review (PubMed 39197715, 2024):** Confirmed feasibility across substance use disorders with decreased craving and improved regulatory outcomes; heterogeneity of protocols remains a major limitation

## Key Facts
- BOLD signal feedback delay is ~4–6 seconds (hemodynamic lag), limiting temporal precision versus EEG (~10 ms)
- MRI scanner cost of ~$500–800/hour is the primary scalability barrier; a full rtfMRI-NF protocol (3–6 sessions) can cost $2,000–5,000+ per patient
- Individualized ROI localization (vs. group-level anatomical masks) improves NF signal-to-noise and craving reduction outcomes in head-to-head comparisons
- ~60–70% of participants in nicotine trials achieve above-chance downregulation within a single session; non-regulators ("NF non-responders") remain a challenge
- No rtfMRI-NF paradigm has achieved FDA indication for any substance use disorder as of 2026; all evidence is investigational
