# SBIRT for Methamphetamine Use Disorder in the Emergency Department

## Why the ED Matters

The ED is the highest-reach intervention point for meth use disorder. Over 39% of substance-related ED visits in 2022 involved stimulants — most patients have had no prior SUD treatment contact. Acute meth effects (agitation, chest pain, psychosis, trauma) create a "teachable moment" when pain and fear can motivate change. SBIRT provides the protocol to capture it.

## The SBIRT Framework

**Screening → Brief Intervention → Referral to Treatment**

Originally developed for alcohol (D'Onofrio et al.), adapted for stimulants. Three distinct clinical actions, not a single encounter.

---

## 1. Screening

Goal: identify severity in under 3 minutes.

| Tool | Notes |
|------|-------|
| **ASSIST Module B** | WHO tool; most validated for stimulant-specific ED screening; yields low/moderate/high risk scores |
| **DAST-10** | Drug screen including meth; 10-item self-report; scores 0–10; ≥3 warrants brief intervention |
| **NIDA Quick Screen / Modified ASSIST** | Designed for rapid ED use; single-question screen then drill-down |
| **CAGE-AID** | 4-item; fastest; low specificity for stimulants but useful as initial gate |

For meth in the ED: **ASSIST Module B** is the preferred tool when time permits; NIDA Quick Screen when it doesn't.

---

## 2. Brief Intervention

**Time: 5–15 minutes. Does not require patient readiness for change.**

Core approach: **Motivational Interviewing (MI)**
- Reflective listening, open-ended questions
- Roll with resistance (avoid direct confrontation)
- Develop discrepancy (current behavior vs. stated values)
- Affirmation of autonomy

**5Rs structure** (adapted from tobacco; fits meth BI):
1. **Relevance** — connect meth use to the specific reason they're in the ED today
2. **Risks** — personalize short- and long-term risks (cardiac, psychosis, relationship, legal)
3. **Roadblocks** — explore barriers to change or treatment
4. **Rewards** — elicit patient's own reasons for change
5. **Repetition** — seed for future encounters; one intervention is rarely sufficient

Key principle: plant seeds, not ultimatums. ED brief intervention for meth produces modest effect sizes vs. alcohol — but the ED is often the only point of contact, making even modest effects clinically significant.

---

## 3. Referral to Treatment

**Warm handoff >> written referral.** Written referrals for meth rarely result in treatment entry.

### Warm Handoff Mechanisms
- **Peer recovery specialist (PRS)** initiates the next-step conversation *before patient leaves ED* — most effective model; peer-shared lived experience with meth resonates
- **Bridge Clinic** — same-day SUD appointment; patient leaves with confirmed time/place
- **Social worker-facilitated transfer** — comparable 30-day treatment linkage to peer (~30% in RCTs)
- **PDAP-style ED protocols** — structured referral pathways with follow-up call

### Meth-Specific Referral Targets
- **Contingency management (CM) programs** — only evidence-based pharmacotherapy-level intervention for meth; ED can introduce concept and refer; cannot run in-ED
- **Matrix Model outpatient programs** — structured 16-week curriculum for stimulant use
- Note: No FDA-approved pharmacotherapy for meth use disorder (unlike OUD/buprenorphine)

---

## Peer Recovery Specialist Model

ED-based PRS programs (e.g., **ED LEADS**, HERO, PATHways) show:
- 55% provide SBIRT services alongside general recovery support
- 24/7 on-call availability in mature programs
- Comparable treatment linkage rates to social workers at 30 days
- Superior patient rapport for stimulant users due to shared experience

PRS role: completes warm handoff, accompanies patient to first appointment when possible, provides ongoing recovery support post-discharge.

## Evidence Base

| Domain | Evidence Level |
|--------|----------------|
| SBIRT for alcohol in ED | Strong (RCTs, systematic reviews) |
| SBIRT for drugs (any) in ED | Moderate (8/10 drug studies show treatment effect) |
| SBIRT specifically for meth/stimulants in ED | Emerging — CEDAR/UCSF peer navigator model, NIDA CTN stimulant trials 2022–2024 |
| Warm handoff vs. written referral | Consistent superiority across SUD types |
| PRS model for stimulant ED patients | Promising; non-inferior to social worker in RCTs |

Effect sizes for meth SBIRT are smaller than alcohol SBIRT — but the ED captures a population that would otherwise have zero treatment contact.

---

## Implementation Barriers and Solutions

| Barrier | Solution |
|---------|---------|
| ED staff stigma toward meth users | SBIRT training + PRS integration normalizes encounters |
| Patient intoxicated/psychotic at presentation | Defer intervention; **plan follow-up before discharge** |
| Time constraints | Offload BI and referral to PRS or social worker; MD/NP completes screen only |
| Competing clinical priorities | Embed PRS in ED workflow; SBIRT is parallel, not sequential |
| EHR documentation gaps | SBIRT-specific flowsheet fields; DAST-10 as structured field |
