# Medicaid Eligibility for Immigrants and Non-Citizens

## Overview

Medicaid eligibility is determined by both immigration status and income/residency criteria. The Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Reconciliation Act (PRWORA, 1996) established the federal framework; subsequent laws have modified it significantly.

## Categories of Non-Citizens

### Qualified Aliens (May Receive Federal Medicaid If Other Criteria Met)

- Lawful Permanent Residents (LPRs / "green card" holders)
- Refugees, asylees, Cuban-Haitian entrants
- Conditional entrant aliens
- Parolees admitted for at least 1 year
- Battered immigrants and their children (under VAWA)
- Aliens whose deportation is being withheld
- Iraqi/Afghan Special Immigrant Visa holders
- Afghan and Ukrainian humanitarian parolees
- Compact of Free Association (COFA) migrants (Pacific Islanders — see below)
- Amerasian immigrants
- Veterans and active-duty military (and immediate family)

### Non-Qualified Aliens

- Undocumented immigrants
- Visitors, tourists, students, temporary workers (F, B, H visas, etc.)
- DACA recipients (pending court decisions — see below)

## The 5-Year Bar

Many **qualified aliens** are subject to a **5-year waiting period** after obtaining qualified status before they may receive federal Medicaid:

- **Subject to 5-year bar**: Most LPRs, most parolees admitted for ≥1 year
- **Bar starts**: Date of entry to the US as a qualified alien (or date of status change)
- **Coverage during the bar**: Emergency Medicaid only (see below); states may fund non-emergency coverage with state funds

## Exempt from 5-Year Bar (Federal Medicaid Available Immediately)

- Refugees, asylees, Cuban-Haitian entrants, parolees under the Refugee Act
- Battered immigrants (domestic violence survivors) under VAWA
- Withholding of deportation or removal
- Iraqi/Afghan SIV holders
- Afghan humanitarian parolees (EAD-based parolees, 2021–2022 cohort)
- Ukrainian humanitarian parolees (Uniting for Ukraine program)
- Amerasian immigrants (from Vietnam, Korea, etc.)
- Veterans and active-duty armed forces and their spouses/children
- Certain American Indians born abroad

## Emergency Medicaid (All Immigration Statuses)

All individuals — regardless of immigration status — may receive **Emergency Medicaid** if they:
1. Meet income and residency requirements for regular Medicaid
2. Require treatment for an **emergency medical condition** (sudden onset, without immediate treatment would result in serious jeopardy to health/life/organ function)

Emergency Medicaid covers: emergency room care, stabilization, labor and delivery, emergency dialysis for ESRD. It does NOT cover scheduled/routine care, most organ transplants, or prenatal care unless the state opts to cover pregnancy as emergency.

## PRUCOL (Permanently Residing Under Color of Law)

Some states extend full Medicaid coverage to **PRUCOL** individuals — those with no formal immigration status but who are effectively permanently residing in the US with some form of government knowledge or acquiescence. PRUCOL is NOT a federal Medicaid eligibility category; it's a state-funded option. New York has the broadest PRUCOL coverage.

## DACA Recipients (2026 Status)

- A federal rule (promulgated 2024) classified DACA recipients as "lawfully present" for ACA marketplace purposes
- In **2025**, a federal court **blocked this rule** — DACA recipients are NOT eligible for federal Medicaid or ACA marketplace subsidies under current federal policy
- **State-funded alternatives**: California (Medi-Cal for all income-eligible regardless of status), Illinois, New York, Oregon, Washington, Colorado offer state-funded coverage for DACA recipients and other non-qualifying immigrants
- Status may change with ongoing litigation; monitor developments

## COFA Migrants

Citizens of Compact of Free Association nations (Marshall Islands, Federated States of Micronesia, Palau) live in the US legally but lost federal Medicaid eligibility under PRWORA (1996). The Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2021 **restored federal Medicaid eligibility** for COFA migrants effective March 2021. COFA migrants are now treated as qualified aliens and are generally exempt from the 5-year bar.

## H.R.1 (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, 2025) Changes

Effective **October 1, 2026**, the One Big Beautiful Bill restricts federal matching (FMAP) for non-emergency Medicaid services to:
- Lawful permanent residents (subject to 5-year bar rules)
- Cuban-Haitian entrants
- COFA migrants

**Impact**: Federal FMAP will no longer be available for non-emergency Medicaid to many parolee categories, PRUCOL, and other groups previously covered in some states. States may continue coverage using state-only funds but will not receive federal match.

**DACA**: H.R.1 specifically bars federal Medicaid funding for DACA recipients.

## State Options (Beyond Federal Minimum)

States may extend coverage beyond federal minimums using state funds:
- **Pregnancy**: States may cover emergency pregnancy services (including labor/delivery) for undocumented immigrants; some states cover full prenatal care
- **Children**: Many states extend CHIP/Medicaid to children regardless of immigration status
- **General**: California, Illinois, and New York have the most expansive state-funded immigrant health programs

## Verification

Immigration status for Medicaid is verified through:
- **DHS SAVE (Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements)** system
- Documentation: green card, refugee resettlement documents, immigration court orders, etc.
- Emergency Medicaid may be available immediately while immigration status is being verified
- Self-attestation of citizenship for those claiming US citizenship; states submit to DHS SAVE for verification
