# GLP-1 Receptor Agonists for Addiction

## Core Concepts
- GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) — incretin-mimetic peptides originally approved for T2DM and obesity; now under investigation for substance use disorders (SUD)
- Key agents: semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy, weekly SC or oral), liraglutide (Victoza/Saxenda, daily SC), exenatide (shorter-acting); differ in half-life, dosing, and CNS penetration
- GLP-1 receptors expressed centrally in ventral tegmental area (VTA), nucleus accumbens (NAc), prefrontal cortex (PFC), and hippocampus — distinct from peripheral metabolic targets
- Addictive potential stems from dopamine surge in mesolimbic reward circuitry; GLP-1RAs dampen this signal without blocking receptors outright
- SUD targets studied: alcohol use disorder (AUD), nicotine, cocaine, opioids, amphetamines

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **Mesolimbic dopamine suppression:** GLP-1RA activation in VTA/NAc reduces alcohol- and drug-induced dopamine release, blunting reward salience and reinforcement
- **GABAergic modulation:** Semaglutide alters central GABA neurotransmission; implicated in reduced ethanol intake in rodents independent of caloric reduction
- **Serotonin interaction:** GLP-1RAs induce serotonin release via 5-HT2C receptors, contributing to reduced impulsivity and reward-seeking behavior
- **Glutamatergic and cholinergic effects:** Modulation of cholinergic-dopaminergic reward pathways alters drug cue salience; reduces reinstatement responding in animal models
- **Cue-induced craving attenuation:** GLP-1R activation in PFC reduces reactivity to drug-associated cues, a key relapse driver; effect seen with both alcohol and cocaine stimuli
- **Peripheral-to-central signaling:** Vagal afferents carry GLP-1 signals to nucleus tractus solitarius, then to reward regions — peripheral dosing can exert central anti-addictive effects

## Clinical Picture
- SUD affects ~17% of adults globally; AUD alone affects ~107 million people worldwide; existing pharmacotherapies (naltrexone, acamprosate, disulfiram) show modest efficacy and poor adherence
- Observational data (2023–2025): GLP-1RA users show lower rates of incident AUD, alcohol intoxication hospitalization, and AUD relapse vs. matched non-users
- Population-based studies associate GLP-1RA use with 30–50% reduced risk of alcohol-related diagnoses in diabetic/obese cohorts
- Anecdotal reports of patients spontaneously reducing alcohol, nicotine, and opioid use while on semaglutide for obesity spurred formal investigation
- Animal model findings are consistent across species (rats, mice, non-human primates) but transient ethanol-reduction effects (≤48 hours) with liraglutide/semaglutide raise questions about durability

## Evidence & Treatment
- **AUD RCTs:** Phase 2 trials (NCT05520775 — semaglutide for AUD) ongoing; preliminary data show reduced AUDIT scores, fewer heavy drinking days, and lower craving ratings vs. placebo
- **Nicotine:** NCT05530577 evaluating semaglutide effects on nicotine intake; preclinical data show dose-dependent reductions in nicotine self-administration
- **Cocaine/stimulants:** Exenatide reduced cocaine seeking and relapse-like behavior in rodents; no human RCT data yet; Phase 1 safety trials planned
- **Opioids:** GLP-1RAs attenuate opioid reward in preclinical models; no dedicated human opioid SUD trials reported as of mid-2026
- **Systematic reviews (2024–2025):** Meta-analyses confirm GLP-1RA use associated with reduced alcohol consumption and relapse; evidence rated low-to-moderate quality due to small samples and short follow-up
- Current consensus: promising mechanistic and early-phase clinical signal; not yet guideline-recommended for SUD; off-label use is experimental

## Key Facts
- GLP-1 receptors are present in NAc, VTA, PFC, and hippocampus — CNS expression confirmed in humans via PET and postmortem studies
- Semaglutide 0.5–2.4 mg/week (obesity dosing) is the range used in addiction trials; addiction-specific dosing not yet established
- Liraglutide/semaglutide ethanol-reduction effects in rodents last ≤48 hours per dose, suggesting need for sustained dosing or combination strategies
- Population studies show 30–50% lower incidence of AUD-related hospitalizations in GLP-1RA-treated patients vs. controls
- Phase 2/3 AUD trials (2024–2026) enrolling; results expected to inform first guideline-level evidence by 2027–2028
