# Safe Messaging: SUD/Meth Patients, Suicide & Overdose

## Core principle: asking does not increase risk

Directly asking about suicidal ideation does **not** increase risk — this has been definitively disproven across decades of research. The opposite is true: empathic, direct inquiry reduces isolation, opens intervention, and strengthens therapeutic alliance. The Zero Suicide framework (zerosuicide.edc.org) endorses universal screening in all SUD settings.

**Use:** "I want to ask you directly — are you having any thoughts of ending your life?"  
**Avoid:** "You're not thinking of hurting yourself, are you?" — the negative framing cues a "no."

## Suicide contagion: what not to do

Glamorizing or dramatizing suicide elevates risk in vulnerable populations. Even when a patient frames suicide as relief or escape, clinicians must not mirror that framing.

- Do not describe suicide as a solution, dignified exit, or rational response to pain  
- Do not reinforce hopelessness while validating distress — validate the pain, not the conclusion  
- Redirect: *"I hear that you're in tremendous pain. That pain is real, and there are ways to reduce it."*

## Stigma-free language: overdose and relapse

Stigmatizing language causes patients to conceal use → less disclosure → worse outcomes. This is not preference, it is a patient-safety issue.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| addict / junkie | person with substance use disorder |
| drug abuse death | died by overdose |
| fell off the wagon | experiencing a relapse |
| replacement drug | medication for opioid/SUD treatment |
| clean / dirty (urine) | positive / negative |

## Naloxone: harm reduction as safe messaging

Prescribing naloxone *is* safe messaging. Framing matters critically:

**Use:** *"This is like a seatbelt — I hope you never need it, but here it is just in case."*  
**Avoid:** *"Because you're going to relapse."* — that frame communicates hopelessness and undercuts self-efficacy.

Harm reduction framing (non-judgmental, matter-of-fact) increases treatment engagement. If the patient uses, *"please do it with someone present"* is a concrete, actionable risk-reduction request — not enabling.

## Means restriction: motivational framing

Never frame means counseling as punishment or distrust.

- **Use:** *"temporary safeguard"* — not *"proving you can't be trusted"*  
- Involve family/support person **with patient consent**  
- Motivational anchor: *"What would it mean to you if six months from now things looked different?"*  
- Revisit regularly — means restriction is time-limited, not permanent

## Meth-specific communication barriers

1. **Active psychosis:** safe messaging cannot land during a psychotic episode. Wait for psychosis to clear; attempt conversation then. Do not rush clinical asks.
2. **Cognitive impairment:** meth damages working memory and abstract reasoning. Use simplified, concrete language; repeat key points; supplement verbal with written materials.
3. **Healthcare stigma history:** meth patients disproportionately report traumatic healthcare encounters. Spend explicit time validating before clinical asks — rapport is not a preamble, it is the intervention.

## Relapse communication

A relapse/slip does not erase recovery progress — and framing it that way preserves engagement.  
**Use:** *"A slip doesn't undo your recovery. Let's talk about what happened."*  
**Avoid:** language that implies recovery was voided or that the patient has failed.

## Training resources

- **Zero Suicide** — free online training, workforce modules (zerosuicide.edc.org)  
- **ASIST** (Applied Suicide Intervention Skills Training) — 2-day skills-based training
