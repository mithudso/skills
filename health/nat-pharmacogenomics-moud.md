# Pharmacogenomics of MOUD Response

## Core Concepts
- **Pharmacogenomics** studies how inherited genetic variation shapes drug response — metabolism, receptor binding, and clinical outcomes — enabling precision prescribing in OUD treatment
- **Key genes**: OPRM1 (mu-opioid receptor), CYP3A4/CYP3A5 (buprenorphine oxidation), UGT2B7 (buprenorphine glucuronidation), CYP2D6 (methadone/tramadol metabolism), ABCB1 (P-glycoprotein drug efflux)
- **OPRM1 A118G** (rs1799971): most-studied pharmacogenomic variant in addiction — substitutes asparagine→aspartate at position 40 of the receptor; G allele frequency ~15–20% in European, ~3% in African, ~40–50% in East Asian populations
- Current MOUD pharmacotherapies are only effective in 60–70% of patients, highlighting unmet need for genotype-guided selection
- Pharmacogenetic stratification remains largely research-stage; no FDA-cleared genotype-guided MOUD dosing algorithm exists as of 2025

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **OPRM1 A118G → reduced receptor expression**: G allele carriers show lower mu-opioid receptor density in anterior cingulate cortex, nucleus accumbens, and thalamus; results in blunted endogenous opioid signaling and altered reward processing
- **Naltrexone mechanism of OPRM1 moderation**: G allele reduces receptor availability, lowering opioid reward during a lapse — naltrexone's blockade is more consequential (and perhaps more effective) in this context; G carriers show ~2-fold greater response to naltrexone for alcohol use disorder (meta-analysis); OUD-specific evidence is more limited but directionally consistent
- **Buprenorphine metabolism — CYP3A4/3A5**: buprenorphine undergoes N-dealkylation to norbuprenorphine primarily via CYP3A4; CYP3A4 inducers (rifampin, carbamazepine) reduce plasma buprenorphine by 50–70%; CYP3A4 inhibitors (azole antifungals, protease inhibitors) raise levels; CYP3A5*3 (loss-of-function, ~94% of Europeans) reduces hepatic CYP3A5 activity
- **Buprenorphine glucuronidation — UGT2B7**: converts buprenorphine and norbuprenorphine to inactive glucuronides; UGT2B7*2 (rs7439366) associated with altered buprenorphine-to-norbuprenorphine ratio and variable dosing requirements
- **CYP2D6 in MOUD context**: less critical for buprenorphine than for codeine/tramadol; relevant for methadone (CYP2D6 + CYP3A4 co-metabolism) and in polysubstance contexts — ultra-rapid metabolizers (gene duplication, ~1–2% of Europeans, ~6% of East Africans) face elevated opioid conversion risk
- **ABCB1 (MDR1) efflux**: P-glycoprotein limits CNS penetration of methadone and buprenorphine; ABCB1 C3435T variant associated with differential methadone plasma levels and withdrawal symptom burden

## Clinical Picture
- Inter-individual variation in MOUD response is substantial: dose requirements for buprenorphine vary 4–8-fold across patients; pharmacogenomic factors account for an estimated 20–30% of this variance
- OPRM1 G allele carriers receiving naltrexone (XR-NTX) show significantly better opioid abstinence rates in European-ancestry cohorts; effect size smaller in East Asian populations despite higher G allele frequency — suggests epistasis with other loci
- CYP3A4 poor/intermediate metabolizers accumulate higher buprenorphine levels at standard doses — risk of precipitating respiratory depression in combination with benzodiazepines or CNS depressants
- A 2023 GWAS meta-analysis (n > 5,000) identified SNPs in OPRM1, OPRD1, and ABCB1 as top associated loci for MOUD retention; UGT2B7 variants correlated with buprenorphine plasma trough levels
- Neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome (NOWS) severity in infants exposed to buprenorphine also moderated by infant OPRM1 A118G genotype — G allele associated with milder NOWS scores

## Evidence & Treatment
- **COMBINE trial** and reanalysis: OPRM1 Asn40Asp (A118G) moderates naltrexone efficacy in alcohol use disorder — Asp40 (G) carriers had 87.1% good clinical outcome on naltrexone vs. 54.8% on placebo; this evidence base informs OUD extrapolation
- **Precision addiction medicine**: pharmacogenetic panels (e.g., GeneSight, Genomind) include CYP2D6/CYP3A4 for methadone risk stratification; none yet FDA-cleared specifically for buprenorphine dose guidance
- **Drug interaction management**: CYP3A4 phenotyping relevant when buprenorphine co-prescribed with antiretrovirals (efavirenz is strong inducer — may require 30–50% dose increase); rifampin co-administration generally contraindicated
- **Clinical trial design**: NIDA CTND trials increasingly incorporate OPRM1 and CYP genotyping as secondary endpoints; pharmacogenetic stratification recommended in MOUD trial design guidelines
- **Implementation barriers**: cost (~$200–500/panel), time-to-result, prescriber unfamiliarity, and lack of actionable algorithms limit routine clinical uptake; point-of-care genotyping platforms (buccal swab, 2–4 hr turnaround) under development

## Key Facts
- OPRM1 G allele carriers show ~2× better naltrexone response in alcohol/OUD meta-analyses; G allele present in ~15–20% of European-ancestry patients
- CYP3A4 metabolizes ~50% of buprenorphine dose; enzyme inducers can halve plasma levels within 1–2 weeks of co-administration
- UGT2B7*2 allele frequency ~47% in Europeans; associated with altered norbuprenorphine accumulation and variable dose requirements
- MOUD pharmacotherapy is effective in only 60–70% of patients — genetic factors estimated to explain 20–30% of response variance
- No FDA-approved pharmacogenomic test exists for MOUD prescribing selection as of 2025; off-label panel use growing in academic addiction medicine centers
