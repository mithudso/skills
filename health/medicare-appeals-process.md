# Medicare Appeals Process

> **Educational only, NOT legal advice.** Medicare appeals have strict
> deadlines. If a significant amount of money or medical care is at stake,
> consider getting help from a State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP)
> counselor (free), a patient advocate, or an attorney.
> **verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

Medicare beneficiaries have the right to appeal when Medicare (or a Medicare
plan) denies coverage, payment, or a service. The appeals process has **five
levels**, each with its own deadline and monetary threshold. The process differs
slightly between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage, and between standard
and expedited (urgent) appeals.

---

## Part 1 — When you can appeal

You can appeal when:
- Medicare denies payment for a service you received
- Medicare denies a prior authorization request
- Your Medicare Advantage or Part D plan denies coverage for care or drugs
- Medicare or a plan says a service is not medically necessary
- You're told you must leave a hospital, SNF, or home health earlier than expected
  (these have special rapid-appeal rights — see Part 4)
- A claim was paid at a lower amount than you believe it should be

You receive a notice of denial (**MSN — Medicare Summary Notice** for Original
Medicare; **EOB or coverage denial notice** for MA plans) that explains the
reason for denial and your appeal rights.

---

## Part 2 — Original Medicare: Five-level appeals process [^1][^2]

### Level 1: Redetermination (Medicare Administrative Contractor)

- **Who reviews:** Your Medicare Administrative Contractor (MAC)
- **File by:** Within **120 days** of receiving your MSN (date on the notice)
- **Decision timeframe:** 60 days (standard); 72 hours (expedited)
- **How:** Complete form CMS-20027 or send a written request to the MAC on your MSN
- **Include:** Your name, Medicare ID, date of service, item or service, reason you disagree, any supporting documentation (doctor's letter, medical records)

### Level 2: Reconsideration (Qualified Independent Contractor / IRE)

- **Who reviews:** An Independent Review Entity (IRE) — separate from Medicare/MAC
- **File by:** Within **180 days** of receiving the redetermination decision
- **Decision timeframe:** 60 days (standard); 72 hours (expedited)
- **How:** Submit reconsideration request to the IRE listed in your Level 1 decision letter
- **Note (2026):** For Part C (MA) appeals, C2C Innovative Solutions handles new IRE appeals as of May 2026, replacing the prior entity

### Level 3: ALJ (Administrative Law Judge) Hearing

- **Who reviews:** An ALJ at the Office of Medicare Hearings and Appeals (OMHA)
- **File by:** Within **60 days** of receiving the reconsideration decision
- **2026 minimum amount in controversy:** **$200**
  (your claimed amount must be at least $200; you can aggregate multiple claims)
- **Decision timeframe:** 90 days (standard)
- **How:** Request via OMHA; hearing can be in-person, by video, or by phone
- **Note:** ALJ hearings are independent of Medicare's contractors; this level often
  reverses earlier denials with proper documentation

### Level 4: Medicare Appeals Council (MAC Review)

- **File by:** Within **60 days** of ALJ decision
- **No monetary threshold** at this level
- **Decision timeframe:** 90 days (standard)
- **Who reviews:** The Departmental Appeals Board's Medicare Appeals Council
- This is a desk review (no new hearing); submit written arguments and any new evidence

### Level 5: Federal District Court

- **File by:** Within **60 days** of Appeals Council decision
- **2026 minimum amount in controversy:** **$1,960**
  (only claims of sufficient dollar value can reach federal court)
- Reserved for significant denied claims; requires an attorney in most cases
- Decisions are binding; appeals can proceed to Circuit Court of Appeals

## Part 3 — Medicare Advantage appeals (Organization Determinations)

MA appeals follow a similar structure but begin with an **organization determination**:

### Initial request (Organization Determination)

- You (or your provider) request a service or prior authorization
- The MA plan issues an **organization determination** approving or denying
- **Timeframe for plan decision:** 14 days (standard); 72 hours (expedited/urgent)

### Level 1 (MA): Plan Reconsideration

- Request reconsideration from the MA plan itself
- **File by:** Within 60 days
- **Timeframe:** 30 days (standard); 72 hours (expedited)

### Level 2 (MA): IRE (Independent Review Entity)

- Same IRE process as Original Medicare (C2C Innovative Solutions in 2026)
- **Timeframe:** 30 days (standard); 72 hours (expedited)

### Levels 3–5

Same as Original Medicare: ALJ → Appeals Council → Federal Court, with same
monetary thresholds ($200 for ALJ; $1,960 for federal court in 2026).

---

## Part 4 — Expedited / urgent appeals (hospital discharge, SNF, home health)

When you are facing a **discharge from a hospital, SNF, or home health service
that you believe is premature**, you have special **rapid appeal** rights:

1. **Request a "fast appeal"** from the hospital's Quality Improvement Organization
   (QIO) or your MA plan — **before or on the day of discharge**
2. The QIO or plan **must decide by midnight of the next calendar day**
3. You can remain in the hospital/SNF **without financial liability** during
   the review (standard cost-sharing does not apply until the appeal is decided)
4. If denied, you can continue up the five-level appeals chain

> This is critical: if you are told you must leave and you believe it's premature,
> immediately request a fast appeal. The 72-hour window is legally protected.

---

## Part 5 — Part D coverage determination appeals

For denied prescription drugs:
- **Coverage determination:** request your plan cover a drug (may include
  formulary exception)
- **Timeframe:** 72 hours standard; 24 hours expedited
- **Redetermination:** same plan, 7-day standard / 72-hour expedited
- **IRE:** same structure as other appeals
- **ALJ → Council → Federal Court:** same five levels

For formulary exceptions (non-formulary drug or tier exception):
- Submit physician documentation that the requested drug is medically necessary
  and formulary alternatives have been tried or would not work

## Part 6 — Practical tips for winning appeals

1. **Act fast.** Deadlines are strict; a late filing can bar the appeal entirely.
   Calendar the deadline the day you receive the denial.

2. **Get your doctor involved immediately.** A letter of medical necessity from
   your physician explaining why the denied service was medically necessary is
   the single most important piece of evidence.

3. **Request your case file.** You are entitled to the evidence file the
   Medicare contractor used to make its decision — review it for errors.

4. **State the specific legal standard.** Denials for "not medically necessary"
   have a defined Medicare coverage standard; reference the applicable LCD/NCD
   (Local/National Coverage Determination) that supports your claim.

5. **Cite Medicare guidelines, not just your need.** Medicare adjudicators are
   bound by coverage policy; align your appeal argument to Medicare's own rules.

6. **Free help:** Contact your state's **SHIP** (State Health Insurance Assistance
   Program) — federally funded free counselors who help beneficiaries with appeals.
   Find SHIP: shiptacenter.org.

7. **ALJ reversals are common.** About 40–60% of cases reaching the ALJ level
   are reversed in favor of the beneficiary; don't stop at Level 1 or 2.
