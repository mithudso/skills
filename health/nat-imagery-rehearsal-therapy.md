# Imagery Rehearsal Therapy (IRT) for Nightmare Disorder

## What It Is

IRT is the first-line behavioral treatment for nightmare disorder and PTSD-related nightmares. Core premise: nightmares are **learned behaviors** — conditioned responses that can be unlearned through waking-state rehearsal of a rescripted narrative. This is not exposure therapy; the patient never relives the nightmare. They rewrite it, then rehearse the new version.

## Mechanism

Nightmares function as conditioned stimulus-response loops: internal arousal or sleep-stage transitions cue the consolidated nightmare script. IRT breaks the conditioning by:
1. Constructing a new, non-threatening narrative in waking state
2. Daily imaginal rehearsal of the NEW dream (5–20 min)
3. Repeated rehearsal overwrites the conditioned script at consolidation

The ending doesn't need to be realistic — any change is acceptable. Realism is not the therapeutic ingredient; rehearsal frequency is.

## Krakow Protocol (3 sessions)

**Session 1 — Psychoeducation:** Nightmares are learned, changeable behaviors. Sleep restriction is NOT required. Nightmare diary introduced.

**Session 2 — Rescription:** Patient selects one recurrent nightmare. Writes a new version changing any element (ending, setting, characters, outcome). No constraint on how different the new version must be.

**Session 3 — Rehearsal:** Daily waking rehearsal of the new script. Monitor with nightmare diary. Session count is flexible — group and single-session formats have been validated.

## Evidence Base

- **Krakow et al., JAMA 2001** (RCT, n=168 female sexual assault survivors): IRT significantly reduced nightmare frequency, severity, and PTSD symptoms; sustained at 3-month follow-up.
- **Meta-analysis (Casement & Swanson, 2012):** Large effects on nightmare frequency, sleep quality, and PTSD symptoms; sustained 6–12 months post-treatment.
- **AASM clinical guideline:** Recommends IRT for nightmare disorder, including PTSD-related nightmares.
- **APA 2022 PTSD guidelines:** IRT included as a conditional recommendation.
- **2018 Cochrane review:** Moderate-to-strong evidence for IRT and nightmare-focused behavioral interventions.

IRT has the largest evidence base of any non-pharmacologic nightmare treatment.

## Relevance in Meth Recovery

Three distinct nightmare types occur in meth recovery — IRT applies differently to each:

**1. PTSD trauma nightmares** (most common IRT target)
PTSD comorbidity rates in stimulant SUD are high (~40–60%). Recurrent trauma nightmares with PTSD content are the canonical IRT indication. Standard protocol applies without modification.

**2. Drug-cue nightmares / use dreams**
Recurrent dreams of using, intense craving dreams, or relapse-scenario dreams in early recovery. These are conditioned-response nightmares functionally analogous to PTSD nightmares. IRT adaptation: rescript to include successful refusal, a sober outcome, or change the scenario entirely. Small pilot data are positive; mechanism is identical to the PTSD application. Drug-cue nightmares that wake the patient with activated craving are a relapse risk factor — rescripting reduces that activation.

**3. Meth withdrawal REM rebound (not an IRT target)**
Intense, vivid dreaming in weeks 1–4 of withdrawal reflects neurobiological REM rebound, not a conditioned nightmare script. These are generally non-recurrent and not distressing in the nightmare-disorder sense. IRT is not indicated for withdrawal REM rebound alone; distinguish from the above two types before starting treatment.

## Combined With Prazosin

Prazosin (alpha-1 blocker) reduces noradrenergic hyperarousal driving nightmare intensity. IRT changes nightmare content via behavioral conditioning. The two mechanisms are complementary and non-overlapping. Combining both is emerging practice in trauma-PTSD clinics; no combination RCT exists specifically in the SUD population, but clinical rationale is strong.

## Delivery in SUD Settings

- 1–3 sessions; feasible within SUD treatment episode length
- Group IRT validated; adapts well to trauma-SUD group formats (e.g., combined with Seeking Safety)
- Telehealth/online delivery validated in non-SUD populations; reasonable to apply in SUD settings
- Nightmare diary required for monitoring; low burden

## Key Distinctions

| Nightmare type | IRT indicated? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| PTSD trauma nightmares | Yes — first line | Standard Krakow protocol |
| Drug-cue/use dreams | Yes — adapted | Rescript to refusal/sober outcome |
| Meth withdrawal REM rebound | No | Neurobiological, not conditioned |
| Idiopathic nightmare disorder | Yes | Same protocol |
