# Modafinil as Agonist Replacement in Stimulant SUD

## Core Concepts
- Agonist replacement rationale: substitute a lower-abuse-potential dopaminergic agent to reduce craving and illicit drug reward — same logic as methadone for opioids
- Modafinil is Schedule IV (US); lower dependence potential than amphetamines due to slow DAT occupancy kinetics and absence of dopamine release
- Atypical DAT inhibitor: blocks reuptake without causing reverse transport (no vesicular dopamine release), producing blunted, sustained dopamine elevation vs. cocaine's sharp spike
- Framed as "partial agonist substitution" even though modafinil is not a receptor agonist — the analogy refers to dopaminergic tone normalization
- Clinical role sharpest when patient has comorbid excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS/narcolepsy), making modafinil doubly indicated

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Primary action: competitive DAT inhibition → increased synaptic dopamine in nucleus accumbens and prefrontal cortex
- Binds DAT in a pose distinct from cocaine (extrahelical site); lower intrinsic efficacy at DAT produces gradual, moderate dopamine rise — less rewarding, less reinforcing
- Secondary targets: NET inhibition (norepinephrine), reduced GABA tone, increased glutamate and histamine via orexin system activation
- PET imaging: 200–400 mg modafinil occupies ~50–70% of DAT in human striatum — sufficient to blunt cocaine binding without producing significant euphoria
- Orexin/hypocretin activation improves wakefulness and may attenuate drug-cue reactivity; relevant for meth-withdrawal hypersomnia
- Animal models: decreases methamphetamine self-administration and cue-induced reinstatement in rats; chronic dosing reduces cocaine self-administration in primates

## Clinical Picture
- Cocaine use disorder: ~1.4 million Americans meet criteria; no FDA-approved pharmacotherapy exists as of 2025
- Amphetamine-type stimulant (ATS) use disorder: globally ~35 million users; meth predominates in US and Southeast Asia
- Stimulant withdrawal produces protracted hypersomnia, fatigue, and dysphoria — states modafinil can directly address, improving treatment retention
- Comorbid EDS/narcolepsy in stimulant users: under-recognized; some stimulant use may represent self-medication of undiagnosed narcolepsy or idiopathic hypersomnia
- RCT populations skewed toward cocaine use disorder; less data for meth specifically except ATS meta-analyses

## Evidence & Treatment
- 2024 systematic review & meta-analysis (26 RCTs, 9 psychostimulants including modafinil): no significant reduction in ATS use, craving, or treatment retention for modafinil vs. placebo overall
- US subgroup signal: 6 US-based RCTs showed modafinil superior to placebo for cocaine abstinence; European/international studies did not replicate — possibly reflects differences in cocaine purity, polysubstance use, or concurrent opioid agonist therapy
- Key positive RCT (Dackis et al.): 94 cocaine-dependent patients, 300 mg/day modafinil + CBT; modafinil group significantly more likely to achieve abstinence (p < 0.05) than placebo + CBT
- Dosing in trials: 200–400 mg/day; most use 400 mg; higher doses associated with more serious adverse events without added efficacy
- Modafinil may be most appropriate when: (1) patient has comorbid EDS; (2) patient cannot tolerate or declines amphetamine-class agonist replacement; (3) adjunct to contingency management when fatigue is a relapse driver

## Key Facts
- DAT occupancy: ~50–70% at standard doses (200–400 mg) per [11C]-PE2I PET study
- Cocaine-free urines in positive US RCT subgroup: ~40% modafinil vs. ~18% placebo at end of 8-week trial
- 2024 meta-analysis: OR for abstinence not significant (confidence intervals crossed 1.0) across full pooled ATS dataset
- Modafinil half-life: ~12–15 hours; once-daily dosing feasible for adherence
- Abuse liability: low in clinical populations; rare case reports of modafinil dependence exist (typically >1,000 mg/day)
- Schedule IV status means easier prescribing than Schedule II stimulants (amphetamine, methylphenidate) for off-label SUD use
