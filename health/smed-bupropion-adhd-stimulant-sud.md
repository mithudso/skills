# Bupropion for ADHD Comorbid with Stimulant Use Disorder

## Core Concepts

- Bupropion is second-line (not first-line) for ADHD; modest efficacy vs. stimulants, but uniquely valuable when stimulants are contraindicated by active cocaine or meth SUD
- Sold as Wellbutrin (depression/ADHD off-label), Zyban (smoking cessation), and Contrave (naltrexone/bupropion combo for obesity)
- Dual rationale in stimulant SUD: reduces ADHD symptom burden AND attenuates stimulant withdrawal dysphoria and craving via shared dopaminergic pathway
- ADHD + stimulant SUD co-occurrence is high (~50% of cocaine-dependent patients meet ADHD criteria); treating ADHD in this population reduces relapse risk
- Naltrexone + bupropion is the most evidence-supported pharmacotherapy for methamphetamine use disorder currently available

## Mechanisms / Neurobiology

- **NRI (norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor):** increases synaptic NE, supporting prefrontal executive function — the primary ADHD mechanism
- **NDI (dopamine reuptake inhibitor):** weak DAT inhibition elevates synaptic dopamine in mesolimbic and mesocortical circuits, blunting withdrawal-phase dopamine deficit
- **Mechanism of anti-craving:** stimulant withdrawal produces a dopamine deficit state (hypodopaminergia); bupropion's weak NDI activity partially compensates, reducing dysphoria-driven craving without producing the reinforcing burst of cocaine/meth
- **Naltrexone synergy:** naltrexone (mu-opioid antagonist) blocks the euphoric/rewarding component of stimulant use; combined with bupropion's dysphoria attenuation, the pair attacks both motivation arms — reward and relief
- **No abuse potential:** bupropion's DAT affinity is ~100-fold weaker than cocaine; slow oral kinetics prevent the rapid DA spike that produces reinforcement
- Effect size for ADHD: SMD ~0.3–0.5 vs. ~0.9 for amphetamines; clinically meaningful but clearly inferior to stimulants

## Clinical Picture

- Target population: ADHD adults with active or recently remitted cocaine or methamphetamine SUD where stimulant prescription poses diversion/misuse risk
- Also used when ADHD co-occurs with methamphetamine-induced psychosis history, cardiovascular contraindications to stimulants, or severe insomnia
- ADHD in meth SUD populations is often under-detected; meth-induced cognitive deficits mimic ADHD symptoms — diagnosis requires ≥1 month abstinence for validity
- Withdrawal phase (first 1–4 weeks) is the highest-craving window; bupropion's dopaminergic support is most relevant during this period

## Evidence & Treatment

- **Cocaine + ADHD (Levin et al., 2002):** 12-week RCT; bupropion SR significantly reduced cocaine-positive urines, self-reported use, and craving vs. placebo in adults with comorbid ADHD and cocaine dependence
- **Meth SUD (Elkashef et al., 2008; Heinzerling et al., 2010):** bupropion SR vs. placebo — benefit mainly in low-to-moderate meth users, especially males; no significant benefit in heavy-use subgroup
- **ADAPT-2 trial (NIDA, 2021):** landmark RCT (n=403); injectable naltrexone + oral bupropion XL vs. placebo — 13.6% vs. 2.5% response rate (p<0.001) for moderate-to-severe meth use disorder; NNT ≈ 9
- **Ongoing (NCT06233799):** double-blind RCT (n=360) testing naltrexone XR + bupropion XL for meth use disorder; actively enrolling as of 2025
- **Guideline status:** SAMHSA and ASAM recognize naltrexone + bupropion as a recommended regimen for meth use disorder (no FDA-approved pharmacotherapy exists); bupropion monotherapy is second-line for ADHD when stimulants are contraindicated by active SUD

## Key Facts

- ADAPT-2 response rate for naltrexone + bupropion: **13.6% vs. 2.5% placebo** (~6× greater odds)
- Bupropion ADHD effect size (SMD): ~**0.3–0.5** vs. **0.9** for amphetamines
- Seizure risk threshold: bupropion contraindicated above **450 mg/day** (or 300 mg/day IR formulation) due to dose-dependent seizure risk
- Onset for ADHD benefit: typically **3–4 weeks** (unlike stimulants' same-day response)
- Roughly **50% of cocaine-dependent patients** meet lifetime ADHD diagnostic criteria in clinical samples
