# Medicare Part D — Prescription Drug Coverage

> **Educational only, NOT insurance, medical, or financial advice.**
> Drug plan formularies, premiums, and thresholds change annually.
> Verify at **[medicare.gov/plan-compare](https://www.medicare.gov/plan-compare)**.
> **verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

Medicare Part D is the **prescription drug benefit** for Medicare beneficiaries.
It is delivered through private insurance plans contracted with CMS — either
**standalone Prescription Drug Plans (PDPs)** paired with Original Medicare, or
bundled into **Medicare Advantage Prescription Drug (MA-PD)** plans. The Inflation
Reduction Act of 2022 made major structural changes effective 2025–2026,
including capping what beneficiaries pay out of pocket.

---

## Part 1 — The 2026 three-phase structure

The Medicare donut hole (coverage gap phase) was **fully eliminated in 2025**.
As of 2026, Part D has **three phases**:

### Phase 1: Deductible

- You pay **100% of drug costs** until the plan's deductible is met.
- The maximum allowed deductible is **$615 in 2026** (plans may offer lower
  deductibles or no deductible).
- Some plans waive the deductible for certain drug tiers (e.g., generics).

### Phase 2: Initial Coverage

- After the deductible, you pay your plan's **copay or coinsurance** (based on
  the drug's tier) and the plan pays the rest.
- This phase continues until your **total out-of-pocket drug spending reaches
  $2,100** in 2026.
- "Out-of-pocket" counts: amounts you pay personally (deductible, copays,
  coinsurance, and amounts paid by others on your behalf). It does **not**
  include your plan's premium.

### Phase 3: Catastrophic Coverage

- Once your OOP spending hits **$2,100**, you pay **$0** for all covered drugs
  on the plan's formulary for the rest of the calendar year.
- This is the biggest change from previous years — there is no further cost
  sharing once you hit the cap.
- The $2,100 OOP cap applies only to drugs on your plan's **formulary**; off-
  formulary costs do not count toward the cap.

### What happened to the donut hole?

The "donut hole" (coverage gap) that previously existed between initial coverage
and catastrophic phases — where beneficiaries once paid 25–100% of drug costs —
was **eliminated in 2025** by the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) of 2022.
Pre-2025 guidance about 25% coverage-gap cost-sharing is now outdated.

## Part 2 — Formularies and drug tiers

Every Part D plan has a **formulary** — the list of covered drugs — organized
into **tiers** with different cost-sharing at each tier:

| Typical tier | Drug type | Cost-sharing |
|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Tier 1 | Preferred generics | Lowest copay (often $0–$10) |
| Tier 2 | Non-preferred generics | Low copay |
| Tier 3 | Preferred brand-name | Moderate copay/coinsurance |
| Tier 4 | Non-preferred brand-name | Higher coinsurance (25–50%) |
| Tier 5 | Specialty drugs | Highest coinsurance (25–33%) |

> **Tier structure varies by plan.** Some plans have 6 tiers; copays and
> coinsurance percentages differ. Always check your specific plan's formulary
> for your drugs before enrolling at medicare.gov/plan-compare.

### Formulary exceptions

If your drug is not on the formulary (or placed on a high-cost tier):
- You can **request a formulary exception**, asking the plan to cover the drug
  or place it on a lower tier
- Requires documentation from your prescribing physician showing the drug is
  medically necessary and alternatives have been tried/would not work
- A denied exception is **appealable** through the Medicare appeals process

## Part 3 — Standalone PDP vs. MA-PD

| | **Standalone PDP** | **MA-PD (Medicare Advantage + D)** |
|--|-------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Paired with | Original Medicare (A+B) | Medicare Advantage plan |
| Enrollment | Separate plan | Built into the MA plan |
| Formulary | Plan-specific | Plan-specific |
| Can use Medigap | Yes (Medigap doesn't cover drugs directly) | No — can't use Medigap with MA |
| Network | Pharmacy network | Pharmacy network |

If you are in Original Medicare and want drug coverage, you must enroll in a
standalone **PDP**. If you join an MA plan, drug coverage is usually included
(MA-PD) — enrolling in a separate PDP while in MA is generally not allowed.

## Part 4 — Late enrollment penalty

If you don't enroll in Part D when you're first eligible AND don't have other
**creditable drug coverage** (coverage that is at least as good as Medicare's
standard Part D), you face a **permanent late enrollment penalty**:

**Penalty = 1% × national base beneficiary premium × number of full uncovered months**

- 2026 national base premium: approximately **$39.06/month** (verify at CMS)
- Example: 24 months without creditable coverage = 24% × base premium added to
  your Part D premium **for as long as you have Part D**

**Creditable coverage** includes: most employer or union drug coverage, TRICARE,
VA coverage, FEHBP. When you lose creditable coverage, you have **63 days** to
enroll without penalty.

> Always get written proof of creditable coverage from an employer or other
> plan when you leave — you will need this to prove continuous coverage if
> enrolling in Part D later.

## Part 5 — Low Income Subsidy (Extra Help / LIS)

**Extra Help** (formally the Low Income Subsidy or LIS) is a federal program that
pays most or all of Part D costs for lower-income beneficiaries:
- **Premiums:** Extra Help can pay all or part of the Part D plan premium
- **Deductible:** reduced or eliminated
- **Copays:** reduced to small amounts ($1.55 for generics, $4.65 for brand-name
  drugs in 2026 at Full LIS; verify at SSA.gov)
- **No coverage gap:** Extra Help eliminates cost-sharing during the coverage gap

**Automatic enrollment:** Beneficiaries enrolled in a **Medicare Savings Program
(QMB, SLMB, or QI)** are automatically enrolled in Extra Help.

**Applying:** Apply through the Social Security Administration (SSA.gov) or
state Medicaid office. Unlike MSPs, there is no asset limit test under the IRA
changes for full Extra Help (as of 2024 changes). Income limits vary; roughly
150% FPL for full subsidy and 185% FPL for partial.

See `medicare-savings-programs` for MSP income limits and eligibility.

## Part 6 — IRMAA on Part D

Higher-income beneficiaries also pay an IRMAA surcharge on Part D premiums
(in addition to the Part B IRMAA). The Part D IRMAA ranges from approximately
**$14.50 to $91.00/month** added to the plan's premium in 2026, based on the
same MAGI brackets as Part B IRMAA. See `medicare-irmaa-income-adjustments`.

## Part 7 — How to choose a Part D plan

1. **List all your current medications** (name, dosage, frequency)
2. **Enter them at medicare.gov/plan-compare** — the tool shows which plans
   cover them, at what tier, and estimated annual cost
3. **Check pharmacy network** — preferred pharmacy tiers have lower cost-sharing;
   verify your pharmacy is preferred or in-network
4. **Compare total annual cost** = (monthly premium × 12) + estimated drug costs,
   not just premium alone
5. **Check the deductible** — a plan with no deductible saves money if you use
   drugs regularly; a high-deductible plan may be cheaper if you rarely use drugs
6. **Review plan ratings** — CMS star ratings (1–5) reflect quality and member
   experience
7. **Reassess every year** — formularies change annually; your current plan may
   drop your drug or move it to a higher tier
