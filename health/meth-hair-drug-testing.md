## Mechanism of Incorporation

Methamphetamine enters growing hair via three routes: passive diffusion from blood supply at the follicle, excretion through scalp sweat glands, and sebum deposition. Once incorporated, meth (and its amphetamine metabolite) binds melanin in the hair cortex — this melanin-binding effect is clinically significant. Head hair grows ~1 cm/month; a standard 3 cm proximal sample covers approximately 90 days.

**Melanin binding and racial bias.** Dark hair binds more drug per equivalent internal dose than light hair — in vitro studies show up to 34-fold differences between dark and blonde hair for similar drug exposure. African-American hair retains measurably higher concentrations than Caucasian hair at equivalent internal exposure. This is not a testing artifact; it reflects differential melanin content. All hair results require interpretation in this context; direct numeric comparisons across individuals with different hair color are unreliable.

## SAMHSA Cutoffs

| Stage | Analyte | Cutoff |
|---|---|---|
| Screening | Methamphetamine | 500 pg/mg |
| Confirmation (GC-MS/LC-MS) | Methamphetamine | 500 pg/mg |
| Confirmation | Amphetamine (metabolite) | 300 pg/mg |

A confirmed positive requires both meth ≥500 pg/mg AND amphetamine ≥300 pg/mg. This dual requirement helps distinguish true ingestion from environmental contamination (see below). SAMHSA does not yet publish federal mandatory guidelines for hair testing; these cutoffs reflect SAMHSA's proposed standards and are widely used by forensic labs and the courts.

## Detection Window and Segment Analysis

Standard 3 cm sample = ~90-day (3-month) window. Longer samples extend coverage:

- **0–3 cm proximal:** ~0–90 days prior
- **3–6 cm:** ~90–180 days prior
- Body hair (grows ~0.3 cm/month): 6–12 month window but cannot be segmented for timeline reconstruction

Segment analysis can approximate cessation timing within ±30 days and identify discrete heavy-use episodes. A patient claiming 8 weeks of abstinence who shows drug in the 0–2 cm segment is a relapse signal worth clinical follow-up.

## Distinguishing Ingestion from Environmental Contamination

Passive meth exposure (smoking in the environment, contaminated surfaces) deposits drug on the hair exterior. Key differentiation:

- **Wash procedure:** SAMHSA-recommended wash steps reduce external contamination but do not eliminate it
- **Metabolite ratio:** Environmental contamination produces meth with little or no amphetamine metabolite. Confirmed ingestion produces both. If amphetamine is absent, the result should be interpreted cautiously as possible external contamination
- **Porosity:** Bleached/porous hair allows drug from the cortex to leach into wash media — labs should analyze wash fractions separately

## Limitations and Confounds

**Cosmetic treatments.** Bleaching, dyeing, chemical relaxers, and perming degrade drug concentrations 30–80% by damaging the cortex and leaching analytes. Damaged or heavily bleached hair may produce false negatives. Document hair treatment history when interpreting a negative result from someone with a known use history.

**Older hair segments.** Hair beyond ~6 cm is subject to cumulative environmental contamination, cosmetic damage, and analyte degradation. Reliability decreases; results from segments >6 months old are interpreted cautiously.

**Body hair substitution.** When no head hair is available, body/axillary hair provides a longer but unsegmentable window. The growth rate (~0.3 cm/month) means body hair reflects drug exposure over 6–12 months without timeline resolution.

**In utero exposure.** Neonatal hair can detect prenatal methamphetamine exposure; meconium is preferred as the primary matrix but hair is an acceptable alternative when meconium is unavailable.

## Role in Meth Treatment Monitoring

| Use Case | Hair Testing | Urine Testing |
|---|---|---|
| Initial assessment — establish pre-treatment use history | Preferred | Limited (2–4 day window) |
| Ongoing sobriety verification | Not ideal (lag time) | Preferred |
| Relapse detection after documented abstinence | Useful if relapse is weeks old | Better for recent relapse |
| Legal/custody/employment baseline | Primary matrix | Secondary |

**Key clinical point:** Hair is a retrospective tool. A positive hair result does not tell you whether the patient is currently using — only that use occurred during the sampled window. For real-time sobriety monitoring in a treatment program, urine (or oral fluid) remains the standard. Hair is most useful at intake for establishing a pre-treatment use pattern, and in medicolegal contexts where a documented 90-day history is needed.
