# Solriamfetol Pharmacology

## Core Concepts
- **Branded as Sunosi**; FDA-approved March 2019 for excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) in narcolepsy and obstructive sleep apnea (OSA)
- Mechanism class: **dopamine and norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (DNRI)** — structurally and mechanistically distinct from modafinil and amphetamines
- DEA Schedule IV controlled substance (same as modafinil); lower abuse potential than Schedule II stimulants
- Approved doses: 75 mg and 150 mg once daily (oral); renally dose-adjusted; half-life ~7.1 hours
- Not a releaser: no significant dopamine or norepinephrine efflux; no meaningful MAO inhibition or receptor agonism at therapeutic concentrations

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **DAT inhibition**: IC50 = 2.9 μM; Ki = 14.2 μM — blocks dopamine transporter, increasing synaptic dopamine
- **NET inhibition**: IC50 = 4.4 μM; Ki = 3.7 μM — blocks norepinephrine transporter; NET affinity slightly higher than DAT
- **TAAR1 agonism**: EC50 = 10–16 μM at human and rodent TAAR1; at clinically relevant concentrations, TAAR1 activation may contribute independently to wake promotion and may modulate dopaminergic tone without the full stimulant profile
- Pharmacodynamically distinct from modafinil (which targets DAT weakly and has uncertain TAAR1 activity) and amphetamines (which are active releasers); solriamfetol's pure reuptake-inhibition profile produces less locomotor activation and less anxiogenic effect in preclinical models
- Wake-promoting effect is believed to arise from combined increased synaptic dopamine (arousal gating) and norepinephrine (alertness/attention circuitry), with TAAR1 agonism providing additional stabilization of monoaminergic tone

## Clinical Picture
- Primary indication: EDS in narcolepsy type 1 and type 2, and residual sleepiness in CPAP-adherent OSA
- Onset of effect: within 1 hour; taken in the morning; duration ~9 hours of wakefulness promotion
- Common adverse effects: headache (~17%), nausea (~9%), decreased appetite (~8%), insomnia (~5%), anxiety (~4%); generally dose-dependent
- Cardiovascular signals: mean increases of ~1.5–3 mmHg systolic BP and ~2–4 bpm heart rate at 150 mg; clinically meaningful in patients with pre-existing hypertension, arrhythmia, or major cardiovascular disease — requires monitoring
- Contraindicated within 14 days of MAO inhibitor use; renal impairment requires dose reduction (max 75 mg/day for eGFR 15–59; avoid if eGFR <15)

## Evidence & Treatment
- **TONES program** (phase 3 RCTs): TONES 3 (narcolepsy) and TONES 4 (OSA) demonstrated significant dose-dependent improvement in ESS scores (–3.8 to –5.2 vs –1.4 placebo) and MWT sleep latency (+10–12 min vs +2 min placebo) at 75 and 150 mg
- Network meta-analyses (2022–2024) rank solriamfetol as **superior to modafinil and pitolisant on MWT latency** but with less favorable cardiovascular benefit-risk ratio than pitolisant — particularly in OSA with cardiovascular comorbidities (Lancet eClinicalMedicine, 2024)
- FAERS retrospective analysis (Frontiers in Pharmacology, 2024): sodium oxybate and pitolisant show more balanced efficacy-safety profiles; solriamfetol has higher cardiovascular adverse event reporting vs pitolisant
- Modafinil comparison: in narcoleptic mouse models, solriamfetol produced less locomotor hyperactivation and fewer anxiety-related behaviors than modafinil; theta oscillation augmentation blunted with solriamfetol vs robust dose-dependent response with modafinil — consistent with different wake-circuit engagement
- Positioning guideline: preferred when MWT-measurable wake maintenance is the clinical priority and cardiovascular risk is low-moderate; pitolisant preferred when cardiovascular safety margin is paramount; oxybate preferred when cataplexy co-management needed

## Key Facts
- Half-life: ~7.1 hours (enables single morning dose without late-day insomnia risk vs longer-acting agents)
- MWT improvement over placebo in narcolepsy: +10–12 minutes (clinically meaningful threshold ~2–3 min)
- ESS improvement over placebo: ~3.8–5.2 points at 150 mg (MCID for ESS ≈ 1–3 points)
- Cardiovascular signal: +1.5–3 mmHg SBP / +2–4 bpm HR at 150 mg — screen all patients at baseline
- TAAR1 EC50 (10–16 μM) overlaps with therapeutic plasma concentrations, making TAAR1 agonism a pharmacologically plausible contributor to mechanism
