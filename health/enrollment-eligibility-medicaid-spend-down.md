# Medicaid Spend-Down (Medically Needy Pathway)

## What Is Spend-Down?

The "medically needy" or "spend-down" pathway allows individuals whose income exceeds the standard Medicaid limit to qualify by "spending down" excess income on medical expenses. Once incurred medical expenses reduce their countable income to the eligibility standard, they qualify for Medicaid coverage.

**States offering spend-down:** Optional — not all states have medically needy programs. As of 2024, approximately 35 states + DC have some form of medically needy spend-down. North Carolina has a spend-down program.

## How Spend-Down Works

### Step 1: Calculate the Deductible

- Determine countable income under non-MAGI (SSI-related) rules
- Subtract the medically needy income limit (MNIL) — set by each state
- The difference = the "spend-down" amount (sometimes called the "deductible")

**Example:**
- Monthly countable income: $1,500
- NC MNIL (single adult): $242/month (2025, varies by household size)
- Spend-down amount: $1,500 − $242 = $1,258/month

**Important:** The MNIL is set very low in most states, making spend-down amounts substantial and difficult to meet regularly.

### Step 2: Incur Medical Expenses

The individual must incur medical expenses (from any provider — not just Medicaid-enrolled) that total the spend-down amount within the eligibility period.

**Types of expenses that count:**
- Unpaid medical bills (past or current)
- Already-paid medical expenses (receipts needed)
- Premiums for other health insurance
- Medicare premiums
- Prescription drug costs

**Important:** Expenses must be for the applicant or certain family members within the household.

### Step 3: Eligibility Period

States choose one of two approaches:
- **Monthly spend-down period:** Must meet deductible each month to have Medicaid for that month
- **6-month period (most common):** Must meet accumulated deductible over 6 months; Medicaid then covers remaining expenses for the period

**NC approach:** NC uses a 6-month eligibility period. If you accumulate $7,548 in bills over 6 months ($1,258 × 6), Medicaid covers remaining expenses for that 6-month period.

### Step 4: Coverage Starts

- Once spend-down threshold is met, Medicaid coverage begins
- Coverage is retrospective for the expenses that met the deductible
- Going forward: Medicaid covers bills for remainder of the eligibility period
- Must reapply / redetermine at end of each eligibility period

## Who Uses Spend-Down?

Primarily:
- Elderly adults with moderate income above SSI/MNIL but who face high medical costs
- Disabled individuals whose SSDI income exceeds Medicaid limits but who have ongoing medical expenses
- Individuals in nursing home care (before transferring to full LTC Medicaid)
- Cancer patients with high treatment costs

## NC Spend-Down Specifics

**NC medically needy income limit (MNIL) 2025:**
- Individual: ~$242/month
- Couple: ~$317/month
- Varies by household size; updated annually

**NC programs with spend-down:**
- Aged, Blind, and Disabled (ABD) Medicaid — primary spend-down program
- NC does NOT have spend-down for families/children under MAGI programs

**NC asset limit for spend-down eligibility:**
- Must still meet non-MAGI asset test (~$2,000 individual)
- Home, one vehicle, burial funds excluded

## Interaction with Retroactive Medicaid

Bills incurred during the retroactive period (3 months before application) can count toward the spend-down — potentially allowing the individual to meet the deductible retroactively, triggering retroactive coverage for the same expenses.

## Spend-Down vs. Medicaid Buy-In (Working Adults with Disabilities)

Different from spend-down: Some states offer Medicaid "Buy-In" programs where working adults with disabilities pay a premium to maintain Medicaid even with higher income. NC has a limited version (Work First Families / limited Buy-In options). Spend-down is expense-based; Buy-In is premium-based.

## Limitations of Spend-Down

1. **Unpredictability:** Coverage only when expenses are incurred; not continuous coverage
2. **High threshold:** MNIL is very low in most states; spend-down amounts are high
3. **Documentation burden:** Must track and document all incurred expenses
4. **Retroactive only:** Often activated by past expenses; planning ahead is difficult
5. **Not available in all states:** ~15 states have no medically needy program
