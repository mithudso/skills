Chronotherapy for meth recovery = deliberate manipulation of biological clock timing (light, darkness, melatonin, behavioral anchors) to correct meth-induced circadian phase delay. Distinct from social-rhythm therapy (psychosocial structure) and general light-therapy (broad evidence). This skill covers the phase-shifting protocol.

## Why meth disrupts circadian timing

Chronic meth use disrupts the SCN via two parallel mechanisms:

1. **Molecular**: meth activates a methamphetamine-sensitive circadian oscillator (MASCO) — a non-SCN oscillator that does NOT use canonical clock genes (Per1/Per2/Bmal1). Simultaneously, chronic amphetamine shifts Per1/Per2 in striatum from nocturnal to diurnal expression and flips Bmal1 from diurnal to nocturnal — inverting the molecular clock. Melatonin normally resets SCN phase by inducing Per1/Per2; meth suppresses melatonin during use, removing this reset signal.

2. **Behavioral**: binge cycles (using nights, sleeping days) + blackout curtains + artificial light at night = light-exposure desynchronization. Result = DSWP-like delayed circadian phase in early recovery.

## Assessment

- **DLMO** (dim-light melatonin onset): gold standard; salivary melatonin sampled q30–60 min under <10 lux starting 5–6h before estimated sleep time; onset = first sample ≥3 pg/mL sustained
- **Actigraphy**: 2-week wrist actigraphy sufficient for clinical phase estimation when DLMO not available; tracks sleep midpoint (MSF = mid-sleep on free days, a validated phase proxy)
- **MEQ** (Morningness-Eveningness Questionnaire): subjective chronotype screen; differentiates pre-existing delayed chronotype from meth-induced delay (important — see Chronotype section)

## Phase-delay correction protocol (standard meth recovery pattern)

Five elements applied simultaneously:

| Element | Protocol | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Anchor wake time** | Fix wake time; advance 1h every 3–5 days until target reached | Strengthens zeitgeber signal |
| **Morning bright light** | 10,000 lux, 30 min immediately after wake | Phase-advances SCN; timing critical — must follow phase response curve (morning = advance zone) |
| **Evening light restriction** | Blue-light blocking glasses after sunset (~2h before target sleep) | Reduces iatrogenic melatonin suppression |
| **Timed melatonin** | 0.5–1 mg IR, 5–6h before target sleep onset | Phase-shifts via Per1/Per2 induction; common error = taking at bedtime (sedating but minimal shift) |
| **Meal timing** | Breakfast within 30 min of wake | Entrains peripheral clocks (liver, gut) via feeding-fasting cycle |

Phase advancement ~1h/day achievable with combined morning light + afternoon melatonin + gradually advanced sleep schedule.

## Melatonin dosing considerations

- **0.5 mg** (physiological) more effective for phase-shifting than 10 mg; high doses sedate but don't advance phase more
- **Timing > dose**: administer relative to current DLMO, not clock time; if DLMO unknown, estimate 5–6h before habitual sleep onset
- **IR formulation** for sleep onset / phase shift; SR formulation for sleep maintenance complaints
- Full pharmacology → meth-withdrawal-melatonin-circadian

## Chronotype caveat

Some meth users have a pre-existing delayed chronotype that preceded use — meth was partly self-medicating circadian-work/school schedule conflict. Distinguish:

- **Pre-existing delayed chronotype**: MSF (free-day sleep midpoint) was late before meth; MEQ score extreme-evening; family history of late chronotype
- **Meth-induced delay**: sleep timing was normal before use; delay emerged with heavy use or binge patterns

Target: correct meth-induced delay toward the individual's pre-morbid chronotype, NOT toward an arbitrary "normal" morning schedule. Forcing an extreme evening chronotype to 6 am wake causes chronic social jetlag, which increases relapse risk.

## Shift-work/night-work paradox

Workers in early recovery who work nights face a chronotherapy conflict: medical protocol pushes morning; work schedule pulls toward night. Resolution: **stabilize any consistent schedule first** — consistency across days is more therapeutic than direction. Once the schedule is stable, apply the protocol relative to that fixed anchor, not toward a conventional morning.

## Timeline for re-entrainment

| Disruption level | Expected timeline |
|---|---|
| Mild–moderate (months of use) | 2–4 weeks with consistent protocol |
| Severe/long-term (years, extreme inversion) | 4–8 weeks |
| Persistent DSWP pattern after 8 weeks | Refer to chronobiologist; consider chronotype as a fixed trait |

Objective endpoints: DLMO normalizing to 21:00–22:00, morning cortisol peak on salivary assessment, subjective wake energy improving.
