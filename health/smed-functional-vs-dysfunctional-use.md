# Functional vs Dysfunctional Stimulant Self-Medication

## Core Concepts
- **Functional self-medication**: stimulant use that normalizes a deficit state (ADHD, narcolepsy, depression), improves role functioning, and does not escalate or produce loss of control — aligns with Khantzian's psychopharmacological specificity (1997)
- **Dysfunctional use / stimulant use disorder (StUD)**: DSM-5 pattern of ≥2/11 criteria in 12 months — craving, role failure, continued use despite harm, escalation, loss of control; severity mild (2–3), moderate (4–5), severe (≥6)
- **ADHD self-medication paradox**: amphetamines that cause stimulation in neurotypical users produce calming, pro-attentional effects in ADHD via prefrontal dopamine/norepinephrine optimization — same drug, opposite functional outcome
- **DSM-5 therapeutic exception**: tolerance and withdrawal are explicitly excluded from StUD criteria when stimulants are taken as prescribed under medical supervision — physiological adaptation ≠ disorder
- **Khantzian's specificity principle**: stimulant-preferring individuals disproportionately show ADHD, depression, avoidant traits, and low energy states — drug choice tracks the affect dysregulation needing correction

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Therapeutic doses (amphetamine 5–30 mg, methylphenidate 5–20 mg) increase synaptic dopamine and NE in prefrontal cortex via reuptake blockade and/or vesicular release, enhancing top-down attentional control and dampening default-mode network interference
- ADHD involves hypofunctional mesolimbic reward circuitry (reduced D2/D3 receptor density, low tonic dopamine), creating reward deficiency that drives compulsive stimulation-seeking — the neurobiological substrate for self-medication vulnerability
- Non-therapeutic doses (supraphysiologic pulsatile DA surges >150% baseline) activate nucleus accumbens reward salience, promote DeltaFosB accumulation, and drive incentive sensitization — the mechanism separating recreational/compulsive use from therapeutic normalization
- Chronic supraphysiologic exposure downregulates dopamine transporter (DAT) and D2 receptors, deepening reward deficiency at baseline and reinforcing escalation — a self-perpetuating dysfunctional cycle (see meth-dopaminergic-neurotoxicity.md)
- Norepinephrine effects at locus coeruleus modulate arousal and vigilance; therapeutic NE amplification addresses narcolepsy and ADHD hypersomnolence without the compulsive reward loop

## Clinical Picture
- ADHD prevalence: ~5% children, ~2.5% adults globally; untreated adults have 1.5× elevated risk of developing any SUD, and ~20–30% of adults presenting for SUD treatment meet criteria for ADHD
- Non-prescribed stimulant use in college students: 5–35% past-year prevalence; 40% of prescribed ADHD patients endorse at least one misuse behavior; diversion rates 16–36%
- Key clinical differentiation markers: dose titration behavior (functional users stay at or below prescribed dose; dysfunctional users escalate), presence of performance/role gains vs. increasing impairment, affective state when not using (euthymia vs. craving/dysphoria), and pattern of social/occupational consequences
- Novel stimulant self-medication (NPS, high-dose caffeine, modafinil) increases when access to ADHD diagnosis/treatment is poor; self-medicators report effectiveness for symptoms but higher adverse effect burden — paranoia, insomnia, lymphadenopathy
- Comorbid mood disorders (MDD, bipolar II) double the complexity: stimulants may address anhedonia/anergia functionally while simultaneously lowering seizure threshold, inducing mixed states, or masking bipolar cycling

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Treat ADHD to prevent SUD**: meta-analyses show ADHD pharmacotherapy reduces — not increases — subsequent SUD risk; withholding stimulants from co-occurring SUD+ADHD patients is not evidence-based
- **Assessment frameworks**: AUDIT-C adapted for stimulants; DAST-10; Conners' Adult ADHD Rating Scale (CAARS) to document pre-treatment symptom burden; Severity of Dependence Scale (SDS) validated for DSM-5 StUD cutoff
- **Treating StUD with co-occurring ADHD**: extended-release formulations (Vyvanse, Concerta) preferred — lower abuse liability, smoother PK profile; NIDA CTN StUD Task Force (2024 update) supports stimulant therapy continuation in patients with both conditions
- **Non-stimulant alternatives for equivocal cases**: atomoxetine (NE reuptake inhibitor), viloxazine, bupropion — no abuse potential; useful when SUD risk is high but ADHD diagnosis is confirmed
- **Lancet Psychiatry 2024 meta-analysis**: amphetamines most effective pharmacotherapy for adult ADHD (effect size ~0.6); behavioral interventions alone insufficient for moderate-severe presentations

## Key Facts
- DSM-5 excludes tolerance/withdrawal from StUD when stimulants taken as prescribed — the single most clinically misapplied distinction
- ADHD patients show paradoxical calming response at therapeutic doses; neurotypical individuals show euphoria/stimulation at same doses — a practical diagnostic probe
- Adults with untreated ADHD are 2× more likely to develop cocaine use disorder specifically (dopamine-deficiency self-medication pattern)
- Non-prescribed stimulant use among college students: most common stated motivation is academic performance (>70% of surveys), not euphoria — suggests functional rather than hedonic self-medication
- Khantzian's 1997 HARP paper (2,756+ citations) established psychopharmacological specificity as the foundational framework; stimulants linked specifically to depressive/avoidant/ADHD affect profiles
