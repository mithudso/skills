# CHIP Enrollment and Eligibility

## What Is CHIP?

The Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP) — established by Title XXI of the Social Security Act (Balanced Budget Act of 1997) — provides health coverage to uninsured children in families with incomes too high for Medicaid but unable to afford private insurance.

**Key distinction from Medicaid:**
- Medicaid: entitlement program — anyone who qualifies must be covered
- CHIP: block grant — states receive federal funds with matching rate; Congress must periodically reauthorize
- CHIP reauthorized through 2029 by CHIP Reauthorization Act provisions

## CHIP Program Types

States operate CHIP in one of three ways:
1. **Medicaid expansion CHIP** — states expand Medicaid up-income limits to cover children above standard Medicaid thresholds
2. **Separate CHIP** — states run a distinct program alongside Medicaid with different rules and benefits
3. **Combination** — states use both approaches for different income bands

## Federal Income Limits and State Variation

**Federal minimum CHIP floor:** 200% FPL for children (the "Ratchet Rule" ensures states cannot lower limits below 1997 levels)

**Most states cover children up to:**
- 200%–300% FPL: most states
- Some states up to 400% FPL or higher
- As of 2025: median state CHIP limit ~250% FPL

**NC Health Choice (NC's CHIP program):**
- Children aged 6–18: 133%–216% FPL (above standard NC Medicaid which covers 0–6 at higher FPL, and expansion Medicaid covers adults 19+)
- Infants and children 0–5: higher FPL thresholds under Medicaid
- 2025 NC CHIP income limit: up to 216% FPL (~$65,000/year family of 4)

## Who Is Eligible

**Categorical requirements:**
- Children under 19 (some states extend to 21 for foster care)
- US citizen or lawfully present immigrant
- State resident
- Uninsured (not covered by Medicaid or adequate employer coverage)
- Income within state CHIP limit

**Immigrant children:**
- CHIPRA 2009: states may elect to cover lawfully present immigrant children and pregnant women without 5-year waiting period using federal CHIP funds
- North Carolina exercises this option

**Uninsured requirement:**
- Most states have a "crowd-out" provision — waiting periods (1–12 months) if child had employer coverage recently, to prevent employers from dropping coverage in favor of CHIP
- NC waiting period: 6 months (exceptions for loss of coverage not due to non-payment)

## Benefits

CHIP must cover at minimum:
- Inpatient and outpatient hospital services
- Physicians and surgical services
- Lab and X-ray services
- Well-baby / well-child care
- Dental (in separate CHIP programs)
- Vision (in separate CHIP programs)

Separate CHIP programs may have modest cost-sharing (premiums, copays) — capped at 5% of household income annually. Medicaid-expansion CHIP has no cost-sharing.

**NC Health Choice benefits include:** physician visits, specialist care, inpatient/outpatient hospital, lab, X-ray, mental health, SUD treatment, prescription drugs, dental, vision, and transportation to medical appointments.

## Enrollment Process

### Same as Medicaid (Single Application)

Post-ACA, CHIP and Medicaid applications are unified. Applying for one checks eligibility for both:

1. Apply at NC FAST (epass.nc.gov), local DSS, or HealthCare.gov
2. System determines whether child goes to Medicaid or CHIP based on income
3. If income too high for Medicaid but within CHIP range → CHIP
4. If income too high for both → referred to marketplace (APTCs available for children)

**HealthCare.gov "Express Lane"** eligibility: children found eligible for other programs (SNAP, etc.) may be enrolled in CHIP without completing a full Medicaid/CHIP application (participating states only).

### Required Documentation (children)
- Birth certificate (age verification)
- Proof of citizenship/immigration status
- Proof of household income
- Proof of residency

### Timeliness Standard
- CHIP enrollment: 45 days (same as Medicaid MAGI groups)

## Continuous Eligibility for Children

**12-month continuous eligibility (required):**
The Consolidated Appropriations Act 2023 (CAA 2023) made 12-month continuous eligibility for children mandatory nationwide. Children who gain Medicaid or CHIP eligibility keep coverage for 12 months regardless of income changes — with very limited exceptions (e.g., loss of citizenship, moving out of state, child ages out at 19).

This was previously optional; all states must comply by January 1, 2024.

**Impact:** Eliminates churning — children losing and regaining coverage multiple times per year due to minor income fluctuations.

## CHIP vs Medicaid vs Marketplace for Children

| Feature | Medicaid | CHIP | Marketplace |
|---|---|---|---|
| Income limit | Varies (0–133% FPL typical) | 133–300%+ FPL | Any income |
| Premium | None | Modest (separate CHIP) | APTC subsidies |
| Cost-sharing | None (children) | Capped at 5% income | Varies by plan |
| Dental/vision | Yes | Yes (separate CHIP) | Sometimes |
| Application | epass.nc.gov / DSS | Same as Medicaid | healthcare.gov |
| Best for | Lowest income | Middle income uninsured | Higher income or employer ref |

## CHIP Funding

- Federal matching rate: E-FMAP — enhanced FMAP above regular Medicaid rate
- CHIP FMAP historically 15 percentage points above Medicaid FMAP
- ACA added additional 23 percentage points through 2019; maintained at high levels through various extensions
- Federal CHIP allotments provided annually; states with high enrollment can apply for redistribution
