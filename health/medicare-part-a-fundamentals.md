# Medicare Part A — Hospital Insurance

> **Educational only, NOT insurance, medical, or financial advice.** Medicare
> costs change annually and some 2026 figures reflect rules under active
> legislative change. Verify at **[medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov)** and
> **[cms.gov](https://www.cms.gov)** before relying on any figure.
> **verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

Medicare Part A is the "hospital insurance" piece of Original Medicare. It covers
inpatient hospital stays, skilled nursing facility (SNF) care following a qualifying
hospital admission, hospice care, and limited home health services. For most
people it has **no monthly premium** — but it does have a per-benefit-period
deductible and coinsurance for longer stays.

---

## Part 1 — Eligibility: who gets Part A and at what cost

### Age 65+

Most people are automatically enrolled if they are already receiving Social Security
or Railroad Retirement Board (RRB) benefits. Those not yet collecting SS must
actively enroll during their **Initial Enrollment Period (IEP)** — see
`medicare-enrollment-periods-penalties`.

### Under-65 pathways

| Pathway | Waiting period | Notes |
|---------|---------------|-------|
| **SSDI (disability)** | 24-month waiting period after benefits begin | IEP opens 3 months before the 25th disability-benefit month |
| **ALS (Lou Gehrig's disease)** | **No waiting period** — Medicare starts the month disability benefits begin | Statutory exception; no 24-month delay |
| **ESRD (End-Stage Renal Disease)** | Generally the 4th month of dialysis (or 1st month if on home dialysis) | Special IEP rules apply; transplant recipients have separate rules |

### Premium-free vs. buy-in (2026) [^1]

| Work-quarter history | Monthly Part A premium |
|---------------------|----------------------|
| **40+ quarters** (~10 years) of Medicare-covered employment (you or qualifying spouse) | **$0** — premium-free (~99% of beneficiaries) |
| **30–39 quarters** | **$311/month** |
| **Fewer than 30 quarters** | **$565/month** |

> A "quarter" is any calendar quarter in which you had earnings and paid
> Medicare payroll taxes. Spouses can qualify on the other spouse's record if
> the working spouse has 40+ quarters.

## Part 2 — The benefit period: the core mechanic

**Understanding the benefit period is essential** — it is not a calendar year.

- A benefit period **begins** the day you are admitted as a hospital inpatient
  (or admitted to a SNF).
- It **ends** when you have been out of all inpatient hospital or SNF care
  for **60 consecutive days**.
- There is **no limit** to the number of benefit periods you can have in a year.
- The Part A **deductible resets with every new benefit period** — so if you
  have two hospitalizations separated by ≥60 days, you owe the deductible twice.

**Why this matters:** a typical annual deductible is one-and-done per year; the
Part A deductible can hit multiple times if you have multiple unrelated hospital
admissions with 60-day gaps between them.

## Part 3 — Inpatient hospital coverage and cost-sharing

### 2026 cost-sharing table [^1][^2]

| Days in a benefit period | What Medicare pays | What you pay |
|--------------------------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Days 1–60** | All covered costs after the deductible | **$1,736 deductible** (once per benefit period) |
| **Days 61–90** | Remaining costs | **$434/day** coinsurance |
| **Days 91–150** (lifetime reserve) | Remaining costs while reserve days remain | **$868/day** coinsurance |
| **Beyond day 150** (after reserve days exhausted) | Nothing | **100% of all costs** |

### Lifetime reserve days

You have exactly **60 lifetime reserve days** total — not per benefit period, not
per year. They kick in on day 91 of any hospitalization. Once used, they are
gone permanently. Cost in 2026: **$868/day**. [^1]

### What inpatient hospital care includes

Part A covers: semi-private room and meals; general nursing care; medications
administered during the inpatient stay; lab tests, X-rays, and other diagnostic
services; operating and recovery room; intensive care; physical therapy and
other rehabilitation services; blood (after the first 3 pints).

Part A does **not** cover: private-duty nursing; TV/phone charges; private room
(unless medically necessary); personal comfort items.

> **Note on physician fees:** doctors who see you while you are inpatient
> bill separately under **Part B**, not Part A. The Part A deductible does not
> cover physician professional fees.

## Part 4 — Observation status: the SNF eligibility trap

**This is one of Medicare's most consequential and least-understood distinctions.**

Hospitals may classify a patient as being under **"observation status"** rather
than as a formal inpatient admission. The difference is invisible to the patient
(same bed, same nurses, same tests) but has major cost and coverage consequences:

| | **Inpatient admission** | **Observation status** |
|---|---|---|
| Billed under | Part A | Part B |
| Counts toward 3-day SNF qualifying stay | **Yes** | **No** |
| Patient pays drug costs | Part A formulary | Part B 20% coinsurance on each drug (often much higher) |
| Long stays | Part A coinsurance after day 60 | Part B coinsurance for each service |

**The SNF trap:** if you spend days in observation status, those days do NOT count
toward the 3-consecutive-day inpatient stay required to unlock SNF coverage.
A patient who spends 5 days "observed" and then needs SNF care gets zero
Part A SNF coverage — even though they never left the building.

**2026 update:** CMS finalized a new appeals process following a class-action
lawsuit. Medicare beneficiaries who were admitted as inpatients but later
reclassified to observation now have the right to appeal that reclassification. [^3]

**Patient right:** hospitals must give written notice (**MOON form** — Medicare
Outpatient Observation Notice, Form CMS-10611) when placing you on observation
status. Demand to know your status and request the MOON if not provided.

## Part 5 — Skilled nursing facility (SNF) coverage

### Qualifying requirements

1. You must have been formally admitted as a **hospital inpatient for at least
   3 consecutive days** (the day of discharge does not count; observation days
   do not count).
2. You must enter the SNF **within 30 days** of leaving the hospital (generally).
3. You need skilled nursing care or rehabilitation (skilled care = services
   that require a licensed nurse or therapist; custodial care alone does not qualify).
4. The SNF must be Medicare-certified.

### SNF coverage and cost-sharing (2026) [^1][^2]

| Days in the benefit period | What Medicare pays | What you pay |
|---------------------------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Days 1–20** | 100% of covered services | **$0** (no separate SNF deductible if Part A deductible already met this benefit period) |
| **Days 21–100** | All but the daily coinsurance | **$217/day** |
| **After day 100** | Nothing | **100% of all costs** |

> The SNF benefit resets with each benefit period (60 days out of inpatient care
> restarts the clock). A new hospitalization followed by a new SNF admission
> in a new benefit period gets a fresh 100-day count.

### What SNF care covers (when qualifying)

Skilled nursing care; physical, occupational, and speech-language therapy;
medications; meals; semi-private room; social services. SNF coverage is for
the **skilled care component** — not indefinite residential placement.

### Common pitfall

Medicare does **not** cover long-term custodial nursing home care (help with
bathing, dressing, eating when no skilled medical care is needed). That is
a distinct service covered by Medicaid (for those who qualify) or paid
out of pocket. Many people confuse "nursing home" with Medicare SNF coverage.

## Part 6 — Hospice benefit

### Who qualifies

- Certified by two physicians (your attending and the hospice medical director)
  as terminally ill with a life expectancy of **6 months or less** if the
  illness follows its normal course.
- Patient elects hospice by signing an **election statement** that waives the
  right to Medicare payment for curative treatment of the terminal diagnosis
  (and related conditions).

### Election periods

1. **90-day period** #1
2. **90-day period** #2
3. **Unlimited 60-day periods** thereafter
   - For period 3 and beyond, a hospice physician or nurse practitioner must
     conduct a **face-to-face encounter** within 30 days before each renewal
     to recertify eligibility.

### What hospice covers under Part A

Part A covers virtually all hospice-related services with minimal cost-sharing:
- Physician/nursing services; pain management and symptom control
- Medications related to the terminal diagnosis (**patient pays up to $5 copay
  per prescription** for outpatient drugs related to comfort/symptom control)
- Short-term inpatient care for pain/symptom management
- **Respite care** (short inpatient stay to give family/caregivers a break):
  patient pays a **small copay — approximately 5% of the Medicare-approved
  amount** for each respite-care day, capped at the Part A inpatient hospital
  deductible for that year
- Social work, counseling, chaplain services, and bereavement support
- Durable medical equipment and medical supplies

### What hospice does NOT cover under Part A

- Curative treatment for the terminal diagnosis (waived by election)
- Care for unrelated conditions continues to be covered by Medicare Parts A/B
- Room and board at a nursing facility (though Part A may cover skilled nursing
  if needed for an unrelated condition)

### Revoking hospice

A patient can revoke hospice election at any time and return to standard Medicare
coverage for curative treatment. Upon revocation, the remaining days in the
current benefit period are forfeited; a new election may be made at any time.

## Part 7 — Home health under Part A

Part A can cover home health services when:
- You had a qualifying 3-day inpatient hospital stay within 14 days of
  starting home health care; **or**
- Part B covers home health in most other circumstances (see `medicare-part-b-fundamentals`)

**What is covered:** intermittent skilled nursing; physical, occupational, and
speech therapy; home health aide services (when also receiving skilled care);
social services; medical equipment and supplies.

**Cost:** No deductible or coinsurance for Medicare-approved home health services
(though there may be a 20% coinsurance for durable medical equipment).

**Limitation:** Home health is for intermittent (not around-the-clock) skilled care.
Custodial home care (ADL help with no skilled component) is not covered.

---

## Part 8 — What Part A does NOT cover

| Not covered by Part A | Where to look |
|-----------------------|---------------|
| Physician professional fees during an inpatient stay | Part B |
| Outpatient services (doctor visits, lab, imaging) | Part B |
| Prescription drugs (outpatient) | Part D |
| Long-term custodial nursing home care | Medicaid (if eligible) or private pay |
| Dental, vision, hearing | Generally not covered (some MA plans add these) |
| Routine check-ups | Part B |
| Private-duty nursing | Not covered by Medicare |
| Private hospital room (unless medically necessary) | Not covered |

## Part 9 — Medigap and Part A gaps

Original Medicare's Part A cost-sharing — especially the per-benefit-period
deductible and the $434/$868 daily hospital coinsurance — is a major exposure.
**Medigap (Medicare Supplement)** policies can cover some or all of these gaps.
See `medicare-medigap-supplement-plans` for plan letters and what each covers.
