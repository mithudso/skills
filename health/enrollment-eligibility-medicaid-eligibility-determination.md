# Medicaid Eligibility Determination

## Overview

Medicaid is a joint federal-state program. Federal law sets minimum eligibility requirements; states may expand coverage beyond these floors. Since the ACA (2010), states have the option to expand Medicaid to nearly all adults under 138% FPL.

## Mandatory Coverage Groups (Federal Minimums)

States MUST cover:
- Children up to 133% FPL (effectively 138% with 5% income disregard)
- Pregnant women up to 133% FPL
- Parents/caretaker relatives of dependent children up to state-set income threshold
- Adults up to 133% FPL (in Medicaid expansion states — optional, but 40+ states have expanded)
- Elderly (65+) and people with disabilities meeting SSI financial criteria
- Individuals who are medically needy (states may cover those who spend down excess income on medical bills)

## Optional (But Widely Adopted) Groups

- Adults 19–64 under 138% FPL (Medicaid expansion — 41 states + DC as of 2026, including NC which expanded October 2023)
- Medically needy / spend-down
- Breast and cervical cancer treatment
- Working people with disabilities (Buy-In programs)
- Foster care youth up to age 26

## The Eligibility Determination Process

### Step 1: Application
- Online (state Medicaid portal or HealthCare.gov in most states), in-person, phone, or paper
- States must provide all application pathways
- No application fee permitted

### Step 2: Identity and Residency Verification
- State resident (no minimum duration requirement)
- US citizen or qualified immigrant
- SSN (or proof of application for SSN) required

### Step 3: Income Determination
Two methodologies (detailed in separate skill):
- **MAGI-based** (most non-elderly, non-disabled adults and children)
- **Non-MAGI** (elderly, disabled, long-term care)

### Step 4: Asset/Resource Test
- MAGI groups: NO asset test (post-ACA)
- Non-MAGI groups (SSI-related): asset limit ~$2,000 individual / $3,000 couple (varies by state; some states have eliminated)

### Step 5: Verification
- Electronic verification preferred (state data hub → SSA, IRS, DHS databases)
- Self-attestation accepted initially; verified within 90 days
- Income reasonably compatible standard: agency cannot require documentation if self-attestation is consistent with available data

### Step 6: Decision
- Timeliness standard: 
  - MAGI groups: 45 days
  - Non-MAGI/disability groups: 90 days
  - Emergency/urgent cases: as expedited as possible
- Approved: coverage effective date rules apply (see retroactive Medicaid skill)
- Denied: must receive written notice with reason, appeal rights (fair hearing)

## Citizenship and Immigration Status

**Eligible:**
- US citizens
- Lawful Permanent Residents (LPRs) after 5-year bar (PRWORA 1996) — most states comply; some states use state funds to cover LPRs in first 5 years
- Refugees, asylees, Cuban/Haitian entrants — no 5-year bar
- Children and pregnant women with CHIPRA option: states may use CHIP/Medicaid for lawfully present immigrants regardless of 5-year bar
- DACA recipients: not eligible for federal Medicaid; may be eligible for state-funded coverage (varies)
- Undocumented immigrants: Emergency Medicaid only (emergency conditions, not ongoing coverage)

## Continuous Eligibility

- Children: 12-month continuous eligibility regardless of income changes (required by Consolidated Appropriations Act 2023)
- Adults: Continuous enrollment through redetermination cycle (annual in most states)
- COVID continuous enrollment ended March 2023; states completed unwinding by June 2024 (12-month unwinding period)

## Redetermination

Annual review required. States must:
- Use available data before requesting documentation from beneficiary
- Provide 30-day advance notice
- Ex parte renewal (automatic renewal using existing data) must be attempted first

## Appeals / Fair Hearings

If denied or terminated:
- Right to fair hearing before state agency
- Aid Continuing: benefits may continue during appeal if timely request
- 90-day hearing decision timeline (federal standard)
- Further appeal to state court possible
