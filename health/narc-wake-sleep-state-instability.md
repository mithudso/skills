# Wake-Sleep State Instability Model

## Core Concepts

- **State Instability Model**: Narcolepsy's pentad of symptoms (EDS, cataplexy, sleep paralysis, hypnagogic/hypnagogic hallucinations, disturbed nocturnal sleep) is unified as a single pathophysiology — failure to maintain sharp, stable boundaries between wake, NREM sleep, and REM sleep
- **Orexin as boundary enforcer**: ~70,000 lateral hypothalamic orexin neurons act as a stabilizing bias on the flip-flop switch; their loss does not reduce total sleep (preserved at ~8 h/24 h) but destroys state consolidation — bouts fragment and intrude on each other
- **NT1 vs NT2 distinction**: Narcolepsy type 1 (NT1) = orexin deficiency + cataplexy; CSF orexin-A ≤110 pg/mL in >90% of NT1 patients; type 2 (NT2) = EDS without cataplexy, normal or intermediate orexin levels — state instability is present but milder
- **REM as the unstable third state**: Orexin loss destabilizes NREM→REM and wake→REM boundaries; REM sleep elements (atonia, dreaming, REMs) intrude into wake and bleed across the NREM transition — explaining cataplexy, sleep paralysis, and hypnagogic hallucinations as partial, dissociated REM states
- **Instability is bidirectional**: Wake intrudes into sleep (fragmented nocturnal sleep, frequent arousals) and sleep intrudes into wake (EDS, unintended sleep episodes, SOREMPs) — the model predicts both simultaneously, which monodirectional models cannot

## Mechanisms / Biology

- **Autoimmune destruction**: NT1 results from 85–95% loss of orexin neurons in the lateral hypothalamus, driven by CD4+ and CD8+ T cells targeting hypocretin peptides; tightly associated with HLA-DQB1*06:02 (present in ~98% of NT1 patients vs ~25% of general population)
- **Loss of OX2R-mediated wake consolidation**: Orexin excites histaminergic TMN neurons via OX2R — the dominant pathway for wake bout consolidation; without this, monoaminergic tone is insufficient to prevent VLPO flip-back during emotional/metabolic fluctuations
- **REM-state dissociation mechanism**: Brainstem REM-on neurons (sublaterodorsal nucleus) are normally suppressed during wake by orexin-driven monoaminergic inhibition; orexin loss allows partial REM-on activation during wake, producing isolated REM components (atonia → cataplexy/sleep paralysis; mentation → hallucinations)
- **Emotional cataplexy trigger**: Strong emotions activate limbic pathways (amygdala → lateral hypothalamus) that transiently suppress orexin activity even in healthy subjects; in NT1, with no orexin buffer, this produces complete switch-flip to atonia within 10–30 seconds
- **Circadian relay loss**: Orexin normally relays SCN circadian drive to wake-promoting nuclei, creating a pronounced circadian peak of wakefulness in the afternoon; NT1 patients show attenuated circadian amplitude — afternoon sleep attacks occur even at low homeostatic pressure
- **Pandemrix link**: Adjuvanted H1N1 vaccine (Pandemrix) used in 2009–2010 pandemic increased NT1 incidence ~3-fold in Nordic countries among HLA-DQB1*06:02 carriers; molecular mimicry between influenza nucleoprotein and hypocretin receptor-2 is the leading hypothesis

## Clinical Picture

- **Prevalence**: NT1 ~25–50 per 100,000 globally (some registries report 14/100,000 using strict criteria); NT2 ~65/100,000; onset peaks at 15–25 years; mean diagnosis delay is 8–12 years
- **Pentad symptom prevalence in NT1**: EDS 100%; cataplexy 70–80%; hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations 60–80%; sleep paralysis 25–50%; disturbed nocturnal sleep 50–70%
- **SOREMPs as diagnostic marker**: Sleep onset REM periods (REM latency <15 min on PSG; ≥2 of 5 naps on MSLT with mean latency ≤8 min) reflect the REM-state instability; MSLT sensitivity for NT1 ~78%, specificity ~90%
- **ICSD-3/DSM-5 NT1 diagnostic threshold**: CSF orexin-A ≤110 pg/mL (or ≤1/3 of normal mean) is sufficient for NT1 diagnosis without MSLT if cataplexy is present; MSLT is required when CSF is unavailable or borderline (110–200 pg/mL)
- **Orexin knockout mouse phenotype**: Chemelli 1999 — KO mice had normal total sleep but mean wake bout length dropped from ~90 min to ~12 min, with frequent direct wake→REM transitions; precisely replicates the state instability model predictions

## Evidence & Treatment

- **Wake-promoting agents (first-line EDS)**: Modafinil/armodafinil (dopamine reuptake inhibition; usual dose 100–400 mg/day); solriamfetol (dopamine/NE reuptake inhibitor; 75–150 mg/day); pitolisant (H3 inverse agonist, increases histamine/NE/dopamine; 4.5–35.7 mg/day) — all address wake-side instability only
- **Sodium oxybate (NT1 gold standard)**: Gamma-hydroxybutyrate; 6–9 g/night in two doses; consolidates NREM sleep, suppresses SOREMPs, reduces cataplexy ≥90% and EDS; mechanism: GABA-B agonism stabilizes sleep-state boundary from the sleep side
- **Orexin receptor agonists (emerging)**: Danavorexton (TAK-994 oral prodrug) Phase 2 (2024): 11.1-point MWT improvement vs placebo; first class to target the root bistability failure rather than downstream monoamines; also reduces cataplexy — evidence that state instability is orexin-driven, not a separate cataplexy circuit
- **Combination rationale from the model**: Wake stabilizers (modafinil) + sleep consolidators (oxybate) + REM boundary agents (TCAs/SSNRIs for cataplexy) address three separate boundary failures simultaneously — supports combinatorial treatment logic derived from the state instability framework
- **Key negative finding**: Stimulants and wake-promoting agents do not reduce cataplexy or sleep paralysis despite improving EDS — consistent with the model's prediction that wake-side props cannot restore REM boundary stability, which requires either REM-state modulation (sodium oxybate, antidepressants) or true orexin replacement

## Key Facts

- 90% orexin neuron loss threshold collapses bistability; 50% loss produces intermediate fragmentation — explaining NT2-like presentations in early autoimmune progression
- Normal consolidated wake: single ~14–16 h bout; NT1 without treatment: fragmented into multiple bouts averaging <1 h each
- CSF orexin-A normal range: 200–400 pg/mL; NT1 diagnostic cut-off ≤110 pg/mL; intermediate (111–200 pg/mL) may indicate evolving NT1 or NT2
- HLA-DQB1*06:02 carrier risk: ~25% of general population; present in ~98% of NT1 patients with cataplexy and undetectable CSF orexin
- Orexin KO mouse data: wake bout CV (coefficient of variation) increases ~4-fold vs wild-type, quantifying the instability; restored to near-normal with orexin microinjection into LC
