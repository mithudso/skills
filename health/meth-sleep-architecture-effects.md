# Stimulant Effects on Sleep Architecture

## Core Concepts
- **REM suppression**: Amphetamines and meth acutely reduce REM % and extend REM latency via monoaminergic hyperactivation of the locus coeruleus (NE) and dorsal raphe (5-HT), both of which tonically inhibit REM-generating circuits in the brainstem
- **SWS (N3) suppression**: Meth diminishes slow-wave sleep even at sub-sedating doses; reduces sleep spindle density in NREM2 and attenuates slow oscillations and delta power, impairing the restorative architecture of NREM sleep
- **Sleep fragmentation**: Stimulants increase sleep-onset latency, nocturnal arousals, and WASO; even when total sleep time is preserved, sleep architecture is structurally degraded
- **Rebound hypersomnia**: Cessation triggers compensatory SWS and REM rebound proportional to accumulated sleep debt; the rebound is dose-dependent and requires threshold-level suppression to manifest
- **Re-dosing cycle**: Stimulant-induced sleep deprivation worsens EDS the following day; users re-dose to counter EDS, perpetuating suppression → withdrawal EDS → re-dosing in a self-reinforcing loop

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **Monoamine release/reuptake blockade**: Meth reverses DAT, NET, and VMAT2, flooding synapses with DA, NE, and 5-HT; elevated NE/5-HT tone suppresses REM-on (cholinergic/glutamatergic) neurons in the sublaterodorsal nucleus
- **Orexin co-activation**: Meth potently activates orexin (hypocretin) projections to monoaminergic wake nuclei; sustained orexinergic drive maintains pathological wakefulness, contributing to both sleep suppression and neurotoxic orexin circuit stress
- **Adenosine debt**: Stimulant-forced wakefulness accumulates extracellular adenosine (sleep pressure); acute re-dosing transiently blunts adenosinergic sleepiness, masking — but not eliminating — the biological drive to sleep
- **Circadian disruption**: Dopamine modulates the SCN circadian clock; chronic meth disrupts dopaminergic SCN input, shifting and fragmenting circadian rhythms independently of sleep pressure mechanisms
- **Neuroadaptation and tolerance**: Repeated cycles of sleep suppression downregulate D2 receptors and DAT; this impairs arousal capacity at baseline, making EDS more severe between doses and requiring escalating doses to achieve the same wake-promoting effect

## Clinical Picture
- **Acute use PSG**: Reduced total sleep time, elevated REM latency, decreased REM %, decreased N3 %, increased N1 %, increased sleep-onset latency, and increased WASO; rhesus monkey model (Frontiers Neurosci 2022) documented ~30% acute TST reduction at 0.3 mg/kg meth
- **EDS in active heavy users**: Chronic meth users report EDS and cognitive impairment during waking hours that mirrors narcolepsy-like daytime sleepiness; the EDS is driven by degraded sleep architecture, not orexin deficiency
- **Re-dosing escalation**: Self-reported re-dosing to combat post-crash EDS is documented in clinical populations; ADHD/narcolepsy patients using illicit stimulants to manage legitimate EDS are particularly vulnerable to this escalation pattern
- **Sleep deprivation sequelae**: Cumulative REM and SWS loss impairs emotional regulation, working memory, and impulse control, worsening ADHD and narcolepsy symptom burden — increasing perceived medication "need"
- **Polysomnographic signature**: PSG during active use shows suppression patterns distinct from both healthy controls and withdrawal-phase sleep; spindle deficits and delta attenuation are most sensitive markers

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Frontiers Neurosci / PMC9021839 (2022)**: Rhesus monkey model quantified meth sleep architecture; 0.3 mg/kg acute administration reduced N3 and REM time; night-2 rebound confirmed N3 + REM increase and decreased REM latency (p<0.05); rebound requires threshold prior suppression
- **PMC8776271 (2022)**: PSG of meth-dependent patients at mean 81 days abstinence showed persistent SWS elevation (N3 23.3%), elevated N1 (12.7%), reduced sleep efficiency (75.7%), and prolonged REM latency (116 min) — sleep architecture does not normalize at 2–3 weeks
- **ADHD stimulant meta-analysis (AASM literature)**: Stimulant medications (amphetamine salts, methylphenidate) increase sleep-onset latency and reduce sleep duration dose-dependently; timing optimization (avoiding late afternoon doses) reduces but does not eliminate architecture disruption
- **Behavioral management**: Sleep hygiene + fixed sleep schedules reduce adenosine debt and stabilize circadian rhythm in patients on therapeutic stimulants; evening light restriction augments this
- **Therapeutic stimulants vs illicit meth**: Prescription amphetamine salts at therapeutic doses cause comparable REM/SWS suppression to low-dose meth but with more predictable pharmacokinetics and without the extreme dose-escalation associated with illicit use

## Key Facts
- Meth-induced TST reduction in acute dosing: **~30%** in non-human primate model at 0.3 mg/kg
- REM % during active heavy meth use: falls from normal **20–25%** toward **10–15%** or lower; REM latency can exceed 150 min
- SWS rebound onset: occurs **night 2** after meth cessation; lasts approximately **7 days** before transitioning to insomnia phenotype
- Sleep efficiency at 81 days abstinence: **75.7%** vs normal ≥85%; confirming persistent architecture disruption months post-cessation
- MSLT washout requirement before valid narcolepsy testing: **≥14 days** per AASM; likely **4–6 weeks** in chronic heavy users
