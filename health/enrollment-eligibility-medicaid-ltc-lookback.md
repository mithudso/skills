# Medicaid Long-Term Care 5-Year Look-Back

## Overview

When individuals apply for Medicaid to cover nursing home or other long-term services and supports (LTSS), states look back 60 months (5 years) from the application date to review asset transfers. Transfers of assets for less than fair market value during this period may result in a penalty period of Medicaid ineligibility.

**Legal basis:** OBRA 1993 and the Deficit Reduction Act (DRA) 2005, codified at 42 U.S.C. §1396p(c)

**Who this affects:** Primarily nursing home applicants, not community-based MAGI Medicaid applicants.

## The 60-Month Look-Back Period

- **Window:** 60 months immediately before the application date (or the date of institutionalization, whichever is later)
- **What is reviewed:** All asset transfers by the applicant or their spouse
- **Burden:** Applicant must document all asset transfers during look-back period

## Disqualifying Transfers

Any transfer of assets for less than fair market value (gifts, selling below market price, adding someone to title without adequate compensation) triggers a potential penalty.

**Common disqualifying transfers:**
- Giving money or property to children or family members
- Adding a family member's name to a bank account or real estate deed without receiving fair payment
- Transferring the deed to a home to a child (unless exempt)
- Creating an irrevocable trust that removes assets from individual's control
- Purchasing a life estate that doesn't meet statutory requirements

## Exempt Transfers (No Penalty)

Certain transfers are exempt from penalty even within the look-back period:

| Transfer | Exemption |
|---|---|
| Transfer to spouse | Exempt (assets return to community spouse) |
| Transfer to blind or disabled child | Exempt |
| Transfer to a "caretaker child" (adult child who lived in the home and provided care preventing institutionalization for ≥2 years) | Exempt |
| Transfer to a sibling with equity interest who lived in the home for ≥1 year | Exempt |
| Transfer to trust for sole benefit of disabled person under 65 | Exempt |
| Transfer that would cause undue hardship | Waivable |

## Penalty Period Calculation

**Formula:**
- Penalty Period (months) = Total Uncompensated Transfers ÷ Average Monthly Private-Pay Nursing Home Rate (statewide)

**NC average private-pay rate (2025):** Approximately $7,500–$8,500/month (varies by county)

**Example:**
- Transferred $75,000 to adult child in month 30 of look-back
- NC private pay rate: $8,000/month
- Penalty period: $75,000 ÷ $8,000 = 9.375 months → rounded down = 9 months penalty

**During penalty period:** Medicaid will not pay nursing home costs. Individual must pay out of pocket or receive care from another source.

**Penalty start date:** Penalty begins when the individual:
1. Is otherwise eligible for Medicaid (income/assets spent down to limits)
2. Is receiving nursing home care
3. Has submitted the application

(Not when the transfer occurred — this is important and often misunderstood)

## The Half-A-Loaf Strategy (and DRA 2005 changes)

Before DRA 2005, a common strategy was to give away half of assets and use the remaining half to fund the penalty period. DRA 2005 changed the penalty start date to make this more difficult, but some planning is still possible under certain circumstances.

**Post-DRA:** Penalty starts when applicant is otherwise eligible AND receiving institutional care — meaning applicant must already be in nursing home, depleted to Medicaid eligibility, and THEN the penalty begins. This means individuals may face a penalty period with no assets to pay for care.

## Medicaid-Compliant Annuities

Assets converted to income via annuities may be exempt from look-back if:
- Irrevocable and non-assignable
- Actuarially sound (payback period doesn't exceed life expectancy)
- Provides equal monthly payments (no deferral/balloon)
- States named as remainder beneficiary up to Medicaid costs paid

Medicaid-compliant annuities are a planning tool to convert assets to income for the community spouse while qualifying the institutionalized spouse.

## Irrevocable Trusts and Look-Back

**Irrevocable trusts funded during look-back:** Transfer into irrevocable trust is a disqualifying transfer; penalty applies.

**Irrevocable trusts funded BEFORE look-back period (>5 years ago):** No penalty; assets properly removed from countable resources.

**Special needs trusts (SNT):** Transfer to SNT for individual's own benefit is NOT exempt for a Medicaid LTC applicant over 65 (different rules from child disability trusts).

## Community Spouse Protections

When one spouse enters nursing home Medicaid:
- **Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA):** Community spouse may keep between $30,828 and $154,140 (2025) in assets
- **Minimum Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance (MMMNA):** Community spouse keeps at least $2,555/month (2025) income

These protections exist to prevent impoverishment of the healthy spouse.

## The 5-Year Plan Strategy

To avoid look-back issues, many families engage in "Medicaid planning" 5+ years before anticipated need:
- Transfer assets to family members or irrevocable trusts > 5 years before application
- After 5 years, look-back window has passed — no penalty
- This requires predicting nursing home need 5 years in advance, which is difficult

**Note:** This is legal planning; "Medicaid fraud" is different (misrepresenting assets on applications).

## NC Specifics

- NC has not expanded look-back beyond federal 60-month minimum
- NC provides for hardship waiver in certain circumstances
- NC uses statewide average private-pay nursing home rate for penalty calculation
- NC Medicaid policy: NC DHHS Policy Manual, Chapter 55 (F0400 series — Resource Rules)
