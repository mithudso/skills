# Cataplexy Neurobiology

## Core Concepts
- Cataplexy: sudden bilateral skeletal muscle weakness/paralysis during wakefulness, consciousness preserved; pathognomonic of narcolepsy type 1 (NT1)
- Caused by inappropriate intrusion of REM-sleep atonia circuitry into wakefulness — REM motor-suppression machinery activates while the patient is awake
- Root cause: loss of orexin/hypocretin neurons (lateral hypothalamus); NT1 patients have 85–95% depletion of ~70,000 orexin neurons; CSF hypocretin-1 ≤110 pg/mL is diagnostic
- Etiology: autoimmune destruction of orexin neurons in HLA-DQB1*06:02 carriers (~98% of NT1); H1N1 vaccination and infection linked to accelerated onset in susceptible individuals
- NT2 (narcolepsy without cataplexy): orexin levels typically normal; cataplexy absent by definition

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **Amygdala trigger circuit**: strong positive emotions (laughter, surprise, excitement) activate the central nucleus of the amygdala (CeA), which sends dense GABAergic projections to wake-promoting monoaminergic nuclei — locus coeruleus (LC, noradrenergic) and dorsal raphe (DR, serotonergic)
- **LC/raphe silencing**: GABAergic CeA output suppresses LC norepinephrine neurons and DR serotonin neurons; in an intact system, orexin continuously excites LC/DR to oppose this; without orexin, CeA activation goes unopposed → LC/DR silent → loss of descending excitatory drive to spinal motor neurons
- **Downstream atonia pathway**: silenced LC/DR disinhibit REM-atonia circuitry in the sublaterodorsal nucleus (SLD) / ventromedial medulla; SLD glycinergic/GABAergic neurons hyperpolarize spinal alpha-motor neurons → flaccid muscle paralysis identical to REM atonia
- **Ventrolateral periaqueductal gray (vlPAG) collapse**: vlPAG and lateral pontine tegmentum normally gate REM during wakefulness; without orexin to stabilize vlPAG, CeA input collapses the gate
- **Emotional specificity**: positive > negative emotions trigger attacks because reward/humor circuits engage amygdala more rapidly; negative emotions (fear, anger) also trigger attacks but less reliably — differential recruitment of basolateral vs. central amygdala subregions
- **Norepinephrine depletion confirmed**: post-mortem NT1 brains show significant locus coeruleus neurodegeneration (2025 biorxiv data), beyond mouse/dog models, reinforcing the LC as a primary effector site

## Clinical Picture
- Prevalence: NT1 affects ~1 in 2,000 (0.02–0.05%); cataplexy present in ~70% of narcolepsy patients at diagnosis
- Attack range: partial (jaw drop, head nod, knee buckling, facial weakness) to complete postural collapse; duration typically seconds to <2 min; fully reversible
- Emotional triggers ranked: laughter/humor (~90% of patients), surprise, positive excitement; anger and physical exertion less common; rarely spontaneous
- Onset latency: cataplexy often emerges months to years after excessive daytime sleepiness; median diagnostic delay 8–10 years from symptom onset
- "Status cataplecticus": prolonged or clustered attacks (hours), rare; precipitated by abrupt withdrawal of anticataplectic medications

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Sodium oxybate (GHB)**: first-line FDA-approved for cataplexy; consolidates nocturnal sleep → reduces REM fragmentation → reduces daytime attacks; 60–85% cataplexy reduction in pivotal trials (Xyrem multicenter RCT); dose 4.5–9 g/night in two split doses
- **Pitolisant (Wakix)**: H3 inverse agonist → enhances histamine, NE, DA, ACh release; FDA-approved 2019; HARMONY-CTP trial: 65% reduction in weekly cataplexy rate vs. placebo; first non-scheduled option
- **SNRIs/TCAs**: venlafaxine, clomipramine — suppress REM via NE/serotonin reuptake inhibition, directly opposing LC/raphe silencing; effective but off-label; rebound cataplexy on abrupt discontinuation
- **Solriamfetol**: approved for EDS but minimal anticataplectic effect — confirms NE/5-HT pathway specificity of cataplexy vs. dopamine pathway of sleepiness
- **Emerging**: orexin receptor 2 agonists (TAK-994, AXS-12) in clinical trials 2024–2025; direct replacement of missing orexin signal; phase 2 data show near-elimination of cataplexy attacks

## Key Facts
- CSF hypocretin-1 ≤110 pg/mL (or <1/3 mean normal controls) = NT1 diagnostic cutoff (ICSD-3)
- HLA-DQB1*06:02 present in ~98% of NT1 patients vs. ~25% general population
- Cataplexy attacks reflect REM atonia duration: typical attack = 10–60 sec, mirroring a REM epoch's atonia phase
- Sodium oxybate reduces cataplexy by ~85% at 9 g/night; dose-response relationship is steep between 4.5 and 9 g
- Orexin neuron loss >85% required before cataplexy appears clinically — threshold effect, not linear
