# Narcolepsy Type 1 vs Type 2

## Core Concepts
- **NT1 (cataplexy-positive):** EDS + cataplexy OR EDS + CSF hypocretin-1 ≤110 pg/mL (or <1/3 of normal mean); cataplexy alone sufficient if hypocretin is low
- **NT2 (cataplexy-negative):** EDS + MSLT criteria (mean sleep latency ≤8 min, ≥2 SOREMPs) with normal CSF hypocretin; cataplexy absent by definition
- **ICSD-3 reclassified:** NT1 was previously "narcolepsy with cataplexy"; NT2 was "narcolepsy without cataplexy" — now unified under hypocretin biology as primary axis
- **Conversion exists:** ~10% of NT2 patients develop cataplexy and reclassify to NT1 over time; cataplexy onset can lag EDS by years
- **CSF tap not always required:** NT1 can be diagnosed clinically if definite cataplexy + MSLT criteria; hypocretin testing reserved for atypical cases

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **NT1 etiology:** Autoimmune-mediated selective destruction of ~70,000 hypothalamic orexin/hypocretin neurons; HLA-DQB1*06:02 present in >85% of NT1 vs ~25% of general population
- **NT2 etiology:** Unknown; hypotheses include partial orexin neuron loss, impaired OX1R/OX2R signaling, or a distinct non-orexin mechanism; CSF hypocretin typically normal (>200 pg/mL)
- **Orexin receptor duality:** OX2R in tuberomammillary nucleus primarily maintains wakefulness; both OX1R + OX2R required to suppress REM — NT1's total orexin loss explains both EDS and cataplexy
- **REM dysregulation:** NT1 shows SOREM within minutes of sleep onset; REM atonia bleeds into wakefulness (cataplexy) and into sleep onset (hypnagogic hallucinations, sleep paralysis)
- **NT2 neuroimaging:** Some NT2 patients show reduced hypothalamic orexin neuron density on post-mortem studies, but not to NT1 degree; functional MRI shows attenuated amygdala-hypothalamus connectivity during emotional arousal

## Clinical Picture
- **Prevalence:** NT1 ~14/100,000; NT2 ~65/100,000 — NT2 more common but possibly over-diagnosed due to secondary mimics
- **Cataplexy specificity:** Full-body collapse triggered by laughter/surprise is >99% specific for NT1; partial cataplexy (jaw drop, knee buckling) requires careful history; emotion-independent weakness is not cataplexy
- **Pentad (NT1):** EDS, cataplexy, hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations, sleep paralysis, disrupted nocturnal sleep — full pentad in ~10%; EDS + cataplexy most diagnostically reliable
- **NT2 differential:** Must exclude sleep deprivation, sleep apnea, idiopathic hypersomnia (IH), and circadian disorders before NT2 diagnosis; IH lacks SOREMPs and has longer sleep inertia
- **Onset:** Bimodal peaks at ages 15–25 and 35–45; mean diagnostic delay historically 8–10 years (improving with awareness)

## Evidence & Treatment
- **EDS (both types):** Modafinil 200–400 mg/day and armodafinil 150–250 mg/day are first-line; pitolisant (H3 inverse agonist) 18–36 mg/day FDA-approved; solriamfetol 75–150 mg/day (SORAS trial: ESS −5.4 vs −1.6 placebo)
- **Cataplexy (NT1-specific):** Sodium oxybate (GHB) 6–9 g/night in split doses addresses both cataplexy AND EDS; low-sodium oxybate (Lumryz) allows single nightly dose; venlafaxine 75–225 mg off-label common
- **Comparative efficacy:** Network meta-analysis: sodium oxybate 9 g/day, modafinil, and pitolisant 40 mg/day show similar EDS efficacy by ESS; sodium oxybate superior for cataplexy frequency reduction
- **NT2 treatment gap:** No RCTs specifically powered for NT2; stimulants/wake-promoters used identically to NT1 for EDS; no established cataplexy treatment since NT2 definitionally lacks it
- **Emerging:** TAK-861 (oral OX2R agonist, Phase 3 2024) targets the upstream deficiency in NT1; danavorexton (IV OX2R agonist) showed proof-of-concept; disease-modifying immunotherapy trials ongoing at early-onset window

## Key Facts
- CSF hypocretin-1 ≤110 pg/mL is diagnostic for NT1 with >99% specificity and ~87% sensitivity
- HLA-DQB1*06:02 has OR ~251 for NT1; strongest HLA disease association known
- Sodium oxybate schedule-I → schedule-III status (as oxybate salt mixture) due to abuse liability; requires REMS program
- ~25% of NT2 patients eventually show hypocretin deficiency on repeat testing or at autopsy — suggests NT2 is a heterogeneous category
- Pitolisant is not a controlled substance — preferred in patients with substance use history or in occupational contexts where stimulants are prohibited
