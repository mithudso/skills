# Ibogaine: Mechanism, Evidence & Safety

## Core Concepts

- Ibogaine is an indole alkaloid from *Tabernanthe iboga* (Central African shrub); used ritually in Bwiti tradition; investigated for opioid use disorder (OUD) and other addictions
- "Opioid withdrawal interruption" — a single session is reported to dramatically compress multi-day withdrawal into hours; survivors describe reset of craving and tolerance
- Active metabolite **noribogaine** (O-desmethylibogaine) has a plasma half-life of weeks vs. ibogaine's ~7 hours; likely drives the sustained post-treatment effect
- Ibogaine is a **psychedelic** at therapeutic doses (8–20 mg/kg), producing oneirogenic visions lasting 12–24 hours; this distinguishes it from NMDA antagonists like ketamine
- Schedule I in the US; legal or unregulated in Mexico, New Zealand, and Canada — most clinical use occurs offshore

## Mechanisms

- **NMDA receptor antagonism** — blocks glutamate-NMDA receptors; attenuates tolerance, dependence, and withdrawal-driven neuroadaptation (same target class as ketamine/memantine)
- **κ- and μ-opioid receptor activity** — low-micromolar affinity; resensitizes μ-opioid receptors, may explain tolerance reset and reduced withdrawal severity
- **SERT inhibition** — ibogaine and noribogaine are potent serotonin reuptake inhibitors; contributes to antidepressant-like and anti-craving effects
- **Sigma receptor binding** — modulates neurosteroid/stress signaling; may contribute to dissociative and neuroprotective effects
- **Dopamine system normalization** — interacts with DAT; restores functional dopamine signaling in reward/motivational circuits disrupted by chronic opioid use
- **18-MC (18-methoxycoronaridine)** — ibogaine congener; TAAR1 agonist; preclinical anti-addictive effects without hallucinogenic activity; Phase I safety trials ongoing

## Clinical Evidence

- **Opioid withdrawal**: Open-label and observational studies consistently show ibogaine interrupts acute opioid withdrawal within 1–4 hours; subjective COWS scores drop dramatically by 24 hours
- **Opioid use disorder outcomes**: A Stanford-affiliated observational study (2023) in veterans showed significant reductions in PTSD, depression, and alcohol/drug use at 1-month follow-up after a single treatment
- **Multisite safety analysis (Research Square, 2025)**: 19,071 patients; 6 deaths within 72 hours occurred exclusively in OUD patients (6/10,382), none in 8,689 non-OUD patients — suggesting OUD-specific electrolyte/cardiac vulnerability
- **Sustained effects**: Noribogaine's weeks-long half-life correlates with prolonged abstinence and craving reduction in self-report data; no randomized controlled trials yet completed in US

## Safety & Cardiac Risk

- **QTc prolongation** — ibogaine inhibits the hERG potassium channel; average QTc increase ~95 ms in monitored settings; ~50% of patients exceed QTc >500 ms (torsades de pointes threshold)
- **Cardiac deaths**: ~33 fatalities documented 1990–2024; most in unregulated settings (underground clinics, self-administration); preexisting CVD and polysubstance use are predominant risk factors
- **Absolute contraindications**: structural heart disease, prolonged QTc at baseline, family history of sudden cardiac death, concurrent QT-prolonging drugs, hypokalemia/hypomagnesemia
- **Required monitoring**: pre-treatment ECG, electrolyte panel, cardiac monitoring during and 24+ hours post-administration; IV access mandatory in clinical settings
- **Risk amplifiers**: opioid withdrawal-associated autonomic instability, electrolyte depletion, and concurrent CNS depressants (opioids, benzodiazepines) compound arrhythmia risk

## Regulatory & Pipeline

- **US**: Schedule I under CSA; no FDA-approved indication; investigational use requires IND; growing congressional interest post-veteran studies (VETS Act introduced 2023–2024)
- **International**: legal in New Zealand (special controlled status), Mexico (major treatment hub), Canada (no explicit schedule); Australia permits TGA-authorized use
- **18-MC**: non-hallucinogenic iboga alkaloid congener; TAAR1 agonist mechanism; preclinical efficacy across multiple drug classes; avoids psychedelic liability and may have improved cardiac profile
- **Phase II trials**: NYU, MAPS-adjacent researchers, and private sponsors advancing trials for OUD and PTSD in international jurisdictions; US Phase II IND expected mid-2020s

## Key Facts

- Ibogaine plasma half-life: ~7 hours; noribogaine half-life: weeks to months
- Average QTc increase in monitored clinical settings: **~95 ms**
- ~50% of patients exceed QTc **>500 ms** peak; normalizes in all patients eventually
- ~33 confirmed deaths 1990–2024 (predominantly unregulated settings)
- Therapeutic dose range: **8–20 mg/kg** (oral); psychedelic effects last **12–24 hours**
