# Alcohol Neurobiology: GABA/NMDA/Reward

## Core Concepts
- Ethanol is a **CNS depressant** acting primarily as a positive allosteric modulator (PAM) of GABA-A receptors and a negative allosteric modulator of NMDA receptors simultaneously
- **GABA-A potentiation**: ethanol binds at transmembrane domains (TM2/TM3 interface) of α/β subunits, prolonging Cl⁻ channel open time → enhanced inhibitory tone
- **NMDA antagonism**: ethanol blocks the channel pore in a voltage-dependent manner, attenuating Ca²⁺ influx and reducing excitatory glutamatergic transmission
- **Mesolimbic reward**: ethanol indirectly stimulates dopamine release in nucleus accumbens via VTA disinhibition (suppressing GABAergic interneurons) and β-endorphin-mediated μ-opioid receptor activation
- **Neuroadaptation**: chronic exposure drives homeostatic counter-regulation — GABA-A receptor downregulation/desensitization and NMDA receptor upregulation/sensitization

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Acute ethanol increases VTA dopamine neuron firing by ~40–60% via disinhibition of local GABA interneurons; nucleus accumbens dopamine rises correlate with subjective reward
- GABA-A subunit composition shifts with chronic exposure: α1 subunits (fast-desensitizing, sedating) are downregulated; α4/δ subunits (extrasynaptic, tonic inhibition) are preferentially lost, reducing overall inhibitory reserve
- NMDA receptor upregulation involves increased GluN2B subunit expression and insertion of new receptors at synapses — the molecular basis for excitotoxic risk during withdrawal
- Endogenous opioid peptides (β-endorphin, enkephalin) are released by ethanol in the hypothalamus and striatum, amplifying dopaminergic reward signal; naltrexone's efficacy exploits this link
- CRF (corticotropin-releasing factor) and dynorphin/κ-opioid systems become sensitized with repeated cycles, driving negative affect and craving during abstinence — the "dark side" of addiction neuroplasticity

## Clinical Picture
- AUD affects ~29.5 million Americans (NIAAA 2022); only ~7% receive pharmacotherapy
- Tolerance develops within days–weeks via receptor downregulation; cross-tolerance to benzodiazepines and other GABA-A PAMs occurs by the same mechanism
- **Alcohol withdrawal syndrome (AWS)**: onset 6–24 h after last drink; peak severity 24–72 h; seizure risk highest at 24–48 h; delirium tremens (DT) in ~5% of untreated severe cases, mortality 1–5%
- CIWA-Ar score ≥10 indicates pharmacotherapy need; ≥15 is high-risk for seizures or DTs
- Wernicke encephalopathy (thiamine depletion) can co-occur; triad: confusion, ataxia, ophthalmoplegia — requires IV thiamine 500 mg TID before glucose administration

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Benzodiazepines** (lorazepam, diazepam, chlordiazepoxide) are first-line for AWS by restoring GABA-A-mediated inhibition; symptom-triggered dosing reduces total BZD use vs. fixed-schedule
- **Acamprosate** (calcium acetylhomotaurinate) reduces NMDA/glutamate hyperactivity and modestly potentiates GABA; NNT ~12 for maintained abstinence at 6 months (Cochrane 2010)
- **Naltrexone** 50 mg/day (oral) or 380 mg/month (XR-injectable) blocks μ-opioid reward amplification; reduces heavy-drinking days by ~25% vs. placebo (COMBINE trial, JAMA 2006, n=1383)
- **Nalmefene** (EU-approved) reduces nucleus accumbens dopamine release; as-needed dosing approved for harm reduction (not full abstinence)
- Baclofen (GABA-B agonist) 30–60 mg/day shows efficacy in reducing craving; evidence strongest in liver disease populations where naltrexone is contraindicated

## Key Facts
- Lethal BAC: ~0.4–0.5 g/dL (400–500 mg/dL) in naïve individuals; tolerant individuals may function at 0.3+ g/dL
- AWS seizures are generalized tonic-clonic; first seizure occurs in ~10% of hospitalized withdrawal patients
- NMDA upregulation persists for weeks after detox — "protracted withdrawal" window of relapse vulnerability
- Dopamine D2 receptor density in striatum is reduced ~20% in AUD, contributing to anhedonia and craving
- Disulfiram (Antabuse) blocks aldehyde dehydrogenase → acetaldehyde accumulation → flushing/vomiting; adherence limits real-world utility
