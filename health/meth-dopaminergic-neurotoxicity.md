# Methamphetamine Dopaminergic Neurotoxicity

## Core Concepts
- METH selectively destroys striatal dopamine (DA) axon terminals — cell bodies survive while presynaptic endings degenerate ("chemical axotomy"), reducing available synaptic DA output by 20–50% in heavy users
- Two functional proteins anchor the damage: DAT (dopamine transporter, reuptake) and VMAT2 (vesicular monoamine transporter 2, storage); both are reduced in chronic users by post-translational modification and terminal degeneration
- The resulting hypo-dopaminergic state produces anhedonia, avolition, and dysphoria — a reward-deficit syndrome that persists long after acute withdrawal resolves
- Dose escalation is partly neurobiologically driven: depleted DA tone raises the threshold stimulus needed to activate reward circuitry, compelling users to take more drug to achieve the same hedonic response
- Relevance to self-medication: in individuals with pre-existing low DA tone (ADHD, narcolepsy-related fatigue, depression), METH may initially normalize anhedonia before its neurotoxicity worsens the underlying deficit

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **VMAT2 disruption**: METH redistributes DA from vesicles to cytosol by alkalinizing the vesicular lumen and reversing VMAT2; cytosolic DA auto-oxidizes, generating OH•, H₂O₂, and ONOO⁻ — the primary ROS that destroy the terminal
- **DAT internalization**: Acute METH triggers rapid, transient DAT palmitoylation suppression and surface internalization within minutes; chronic exposure produces lasting DAT protein loss (~20–30% in PET studies) through oxidative terminal degeneration
- **Hyperthermia synergy**: Core temperature ≥40°C (common in binge use) amplifies ROS generation and directly impairs VMAT2 function; animal models show that preventing hyperthermia alone substantially attenuates DA terminal damage
- **D2 receptor downregulation**: Striatal D2 receptors decrease in proportion to DA terminal loss; reduced D2 signaling in ventral striatum further blunts reward responsivity and reinforces the hedonic set-point shift
- **Partial recovery**: DAT PET binding increases ~20% after 14 months of abstinence, reflecting surviving terminal sprouting — not reversal of lost terminals; full normalization is rarely documented even after years

## Clinical Picture
- PET neuroimaging (DAT and VMAT2 ligands) shows 20–30% reduction in striatal DA terminal density in chronic METH users vs. age-matched controls
- Persistent anhedonia is the cardinal post-acute symptom: clinically significant anhedonic features present in ~50–70% of METH users during early abstinence (weeks 2–8), correlating with the degree of DAT loss
- Reduced D2/D3 receptor availability in striatum predicts relapse: lower baseline striatal dopamine signaling is associated with 2–3× higher relapse rates within 6 months
- METH users show 5–10× elevated risk of Parkinsonism in later life, consistent with DA terminal reserve depletion lowering the threshold for clinical motor symptom onset
- Cognitive impairments (working memory, attention, executive function) track with DAT loss and partially overlap with ADHD symptom profiles, obscuring differential diagnosis in dual-diagnosis patients

## Evidence & Treatment
- No FDA-approved pharmacotherapy restores METH-damaged DA terminals; bupropion (150–300 mg/day) modestly increases DA/NE tone and showed reduced METH use in a 2021 meta-analysis (OR 1.52) — effects larger in low-to-moderate users
- Naltrexone + bupropion combination (COMBINE-like approach) reduced METH-positive urines 30% vs. placebo in a 2021 NEJM trial (n=403), likely via partial attenuation of the reward deficit
- Contingency management (CM) produces the strongest evidence for abstinence (d ≈ 0.58), and abstinence itself drives the ~20% DAT recovery observed at 14 months
- N-acetylcysteine (NAC, 1200–2400 mg/day) replenishes glutathione and reduces cue-induced craving in pilot RCTs; no terminal regeneration shown in humans yet
- Cooling interventions during acute METH intoxication are the best-validated neuroprotective strategy in primate models; clinical protocol: avoid hot environments, hydrate, treat hyperthermia aggressively

## Key Facts
- Standard binge protocol (4 × 10 mg/kg, 2 h apart) depletes rat striatal DA terminals ≥50%; equivalent human exposure requires repeated high-dose binges over months
- 60% DAT depletion measurable by autoradiography 48 h after METH exposure in mice; human PET shows ~20–30% chronic reduction
- Anhedonia severity at 3 weeks post-cessation is among the strongest predictors of 6-month relapse; treating it pharmacologically improves retention in treatment programs
- VMAT2 mRNA and protein levels decrease within 15 minutes of high-dose METH in rat striatum, persisting ≥6 days post-exposure
- Each incremental dose increase in escalating METH users corresponds to measurable drops in striatal D2 receptor availability, not just tolerance — structural not just functional change
