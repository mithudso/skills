# Addiction Genetics & Epigenetic Mechanisms

## Core Concepts
- Addiction is polygenic — no single gene determines risk; hundreds of common variants each contribute small effects
- Heritability (h²) quantifies the proportion of phenotypic variance attributable to genetic factors, estimated via twin/family studies
- Epigenetics = heritable changes in gene expression without DNA sequence alteration; key mechanisms are DNA methylation, histone modification, and non-coding RNA
- Gene × environment (G×E) interactions are central: identical genotypes produce different addiction risk depending on stress exposure, trauma, and drug access
- GWAS (genome-wide association studies) identify common SNPs at p < 5×10⁻⁸; candidate-gene studies test biologically plausible loci (OPRM1, ADH1B, GABRA2)

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **DNA methylation**: Addition of methyl groups to CpG sites silences promoters; chronic drug exposure hypermethylates synaptic plasticity genes (e.g., BDNF, FosB) in nucleus accumbens, locking in compulsive states
- **Histone acetylation**: HATs add acetyl groups → open chromatin → increased transcription; HDACs remove them → silencing; cocaine elevates H3/H4 acetylation at ΔFosB and CDKN1A promoters, sensitizing reward circuitry
- **OPRM1 A118G (rs1799971)**: Reduces µ-opioid receptor expression ~3-fold; G allele carriers show blunted endorphin response and altered naltrexone efficacy; minor allele frequency ~15–25% in Europeans
- **ADH1B Arg47His (rs1229984)**: His47 allele increases acetaldehyde accumulation after alcohol, causing aversive flushing; reduces alcohol use disorder (AUD) risk 3–5× in carriers; enriched in East Asian populations
- **GABRA2**: Encodes GABA-A α2 subunit; risk haplotype associated with AUD and conduct disorder in Caucasians; may operate via GABRG1 linkage disequilibrium; modulates inhibitory tone in prefrontal-limbic circuits
- **Stress-epigenetic pathway**: Early-life adversity (ACEs) induces lasting hypomethylation of CRF (corticotropin-releasing factor) promoters and hypermethylation of NR3C1 (glucocorticoid receptor), priming HPA hyperreactivity and increasing relapse vulnerability decades later

## Clinical Picture
- AUD heritability: ~50% (twin meta-analyses, thousands of pairs); cocaine/opioid use disorder: 60–70%
- SNP-based heritability (h²_snp) for AUD: 5.6–10%, indicating substantial unexplained "missing heritability" from rare variants and gene-environment interaction
- Family history of AUD confers 3–4× increased lifetime risk; first-degree relatives of opioid-dependent individuals have 6–8× elevated risk
- GWAS of opioid use disorder: 8 published studies (n = 104 to 10,544); 6 identified genome-wide significant loci, but replication across ancestries remains incomplete
- Epigenetic marks (LINE-1 methylation, MAOA methylation) detectable in blood as potential biomarkers of addiction vulnerability; not yet clinical standard

## Evidence & Treatment
- Naltrexone pharmacogenomics: A118G G-allele carriers show greater reduction in heavy drinking days and craving in some RCTs (COMBINE trial subgroup, n ≈ 800); FDA label does not require genotyping
- ALDH2*2 carriers (East Asian): extreme acetaldehyde sensitivity; disulfiram-like adverse effects limit alcohol use; naturally protective but near-absent in European populations
- HDAC inhibitors (e.g., valproate, class I/II HDACs) under investigation for cocaine relapse prevention; preclinical data promising; no FDA-approved indication yet
- CRISPR-based epigenome editing (dCas9-DNMT3A, dCas9-TET1) used in rodent models to toggle addiction-related methylation marks; clinical translation 10+ years away
- ACE screening (adverse childhood experiences score ≥ 4) identifies patients with epigenetically primed HPA dysregulation warranting trauma-informed, long-term treatment planning

## Key Facts
- ~50% of AUD risk is heritable; ~60–70% for stimulant/opioid use disorders
- OPRM1 A118G G allele: found in ~15–25% of Europeans, ~50% of Asians; reduces µ-receptor density ~3-fold
- ADH1B His47 allele reduces AUD risk 3–5× via aversive acetaldehyde accumulation
- ΔFosB accumulation in nucleus accumbens after repeated drug exposure involves histone H3K9 acetylation and is detectable for weeks after last drug use
- Childhood trauma (ACE score ≥ 4) multiplies addiction risk 7–10× via persistent epigenetic dysregulation of CRF/NR3C1 axis
