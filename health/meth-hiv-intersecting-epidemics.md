# Meth and HIV Intersecting Epidemics

## Core Concepts
- Methamphetamine use and HIV form a syndemic — each amplifies the other's biological and behavioral harms
- MSM account for the greatest overlap: meth use rates among MSM are ~20× higher than the general population
- Meth drives HIV acquisition via disinhibited sexual behavior; HIV/ART interactions then alter meth pharmacokinetics
- HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder (HAND) and meth neurotoxicity share overlapping dopaminergic and neuroinflammatory pathways
- Integrated care models addressing both substance use disorder and HIV are essential; siloed treatment produces poor outcomes

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **Behavioral pathway:** Event-level analyses confirm meth use immediately before/during sex independently increases condomless sex probability; 16% of incident HIV infections in a 4,295-person longitudinal MSM cohort were attributable to meth use
- **Immune dysregulation:** Meth increases CD4+ T-cell activation and exhaustion, elevates C-reactive protein, and accelerates immune senescence independent of HIV status; co-exposure compounds CD4 decline
- **Viral replication:** Meth inhibits cellular restriction factors (e.g., APOBEC3G), directly facilitating HIV infection of primary monocytes and promoting viral replication at the cellular level
- **Blood-brain barrier (BBB):** Meth reduces BBB integrity, increasing CNS viral penetration, microglial activation, and neuroinflammation — the core substrate for HAND progression
- **PK interaction:** Ritonavir (CYP2D6 inhibitor) raises plasma meth levels 3–10-fold, dramatically increasing overdose risk and CNS toxicity in PLWH on PI-based ART
- **NeuroAIDS amplification:** HIV-1 + meth co-exposure in astrocytes triggers ER stress/unfolded protein response; combined insult exceeds each alone in dopaminergic and glutamatergic neurotoxicity

## Clinical Picture
- HIV prevalence among MSM who primarily inject meth is ~50% higher than among MSM who inject other drugs (8-city US study)
- Meth use among PLWH is estimated at 5–15%, versus <1% in the general population; meth accounted for up to one-third of new HIV diagnoses in one 5,000-person online cohort of sexual/gender minority persons
- PLWH who use meth show worse ART adherence, higher viral loads, increased ART resistance, and faster CD4 decline compared to non-using PLWH
- HAND prevalence is higher in PLWH who use meth; deficits in attention, processing speed, and executive function worsen with co-exposure duration
- Meth users on PrEP have suboptimal adherence, reducing PrEP efficacy and increasing transmission risk at the population level

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Naltrexone + bupropion (NEJM 2021):** Extended-release naltrexone + extended-release bupropion (12 weeks) produced significantly higher meth abstinence response vs placebo in moderate-to-severe MUD — the only FDA-approved-agent combination with RCT support
- **Contingency management (CM):** Strongest evidence base for meth use disorder; SAMHSA 2024 advisory endorses CM for MUD; better outcomes in patients with preserved executive function; effective in HIV clinic settings
- **Mirtazapine:** Phase 2 RCT in meth-using MSM showed reduced meth use and sexual risk behaviors vs placebo; used off-label in integrated HIV/SUD clinics
- **Integrated HIV + SUD care:** Co-located or co-managed HIV care and addiction treatment improves ART adherence, virologic suppression, and retention vs referral-based siloed models (Ryan White program data)
- **PrEP + harm reduction:** Combining injectable cabotegravir PrEP (removes daily adherence barrier) with CM or motivational interviewing is an emerging strategy for meth-using MSM at high HIV risk

## Key Facts
- Meth use among MSM is 20× the general-population rate
- 16% of incident HIV infections in a major MSM cohort were attributable to meth use
- Ritonavir raises plasma meth concentrations 3–10× via CYP2D6 inhibition
- Meth + HIV co-exposure causes additive/synergistic BBB disruption and dopaminergic neurotoxicity exceeding either alone
- Naltrexone + bupropion combination is the only pharmacotherapy with positive phase 3 RCT data for methamphetamine use disorder (2021 NEJM trial)
