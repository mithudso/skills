# Medicare IRA Drug Price Negotiation & Part D Reforms

The Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 introduced the most significant Medicare drug pricing changes in two decades. Key reforms affecting 2026 beneficiaries:

## 1. The Medicare Drug Price Negotiation Program

### How it works
CMS directly negotiates with drug manufacturers to establish a **Maximum Fair Price (MFP)** — the ceiling price Part D plans pay. The negotiated MFP applies at the pharmacy counter, so beneficiaries pay based on the lower price.

### Negotiation eligibility criteria
- Drugs must be among the highest Medicare drug expenditures
- **Small molecule drugs:** eligible for selection 7 years after first FDA approval; negotiated price takes effect at year 9
- **Biologic drugs:** eligible 11 years after FDA approval; negotiated price takes effect at year 13
- Drugs with orphan drug designations (broadened by 2025 reconciliation law) are excluded
- Drugs developed by qualifying small biotech firms may be excluded temporarily

### Round 1: 10 drugs with negotiated prices effective January 1, 2026

| Drug | Condition |
|------|-----------|
| Eliquis (apixaban) | Blood clots, stroke prevention in AFib |
| Xarelto (rivaroxaban) | Blood clots, stroke prevention |
| Jardiance (empagliflozin) | Diabetes, heart failure |
| Farxiga (dapagliflozin) | Diabetes, heart failure, CKD |
| Januvia (sitagliptin) | Diabetes |
| Fiasp/NovoLog (insulin aspart) | Diabetes |
| Entresto (sacubitril/valsartan) | Heart failure |
| Stelara (ustekinumab) | Psoriasis, Crohn's disease |
| Enbrel (etanercept) | Psoriasis, rheumatoid arthritis |
| Imbruvica (ibrutinib) | Blood cancers (CLL, MCL) |

**Program savings (CMS estimates):** If 2026 prices had applied in 2023, Medicare would have saved ~$6 billion; beneficiaries ~$1.5 billion annually.

### Round 2: 15 additional drugs, effective January 1, 2027
CMS signed manufacturer agreements in early 2025 for the next cohort. Future rounds: 20 drugs for 2028, expanding further through 2029+.

## 2. The "Pill Penalty" Controversy

The 4-year gap between small molecule (7 yr) and biologic (11 yr) negotiation eligibility is called the **"pill penalty"** — critics argue it:
- Creates a disincentive to develop traditional small-molecule medicines
- Favors biologics (which already have longer exclusivity)
- May reduce the pipeline of oral medications

**2025 executive action:** President Trump's Executive Order 14273 directed HHS to work with Congress to "end the distortion" and align small molecule eligibility with the 11-year biologic timeline. Legislative proposals include extending small molecule eligibility from 7 to 11 years post-approval.

## 3. Medicare Inflation Rebate Requirement

Drug manufacturers must pay rebates to Medicare if they raise prices faster than inflation (CPI-U) for drugs covered under Part B or Part D. This applies to price increases after a baseline date. Manufacturers that fail to pay face excise taxes. This is separate from negotiation and applies broadly — not just to selected drugs.

## 4. The Prescription Payment Plan (monthly cap option)

**Problem it solves:** Before 2025, beneficiaries who needed expensive drugs often hit catastrophic spending in the first few months of the year — paying large out-of-pocket amounts upfront.

**How it works (effective 2025, continuing 2026):**
- Beneficiaries can opt in to spread their **entire annual out-of-pocket drug costs** across 12 equal monthly installments
- With the $2,100 OOP cap in 2026, that equals roughly **~$175/month maximum**
- Opt in by contacting your Part D plan directly
- Applies to standalone PDPs and MA-PD plans

**Who benefits most:** People who take expensive specialty drugs and would otherwise exhaust their OOP cap in Q1, causing large bills before the catastrophic phase kicks in.

## 5. The $35 Insulin Cap

Under the IRA, insulin covered under Part D is capped at **$35/month per product** for all Medicare beneficiaries — regardless of Extra Help status. This was extended to Part B-covered insulin (used with insulin pumps) as well.

## Key Limitations & 2026 Policy Context

- **Negotiated prices are not automatically available to commercial insurance** — they apply only within Medicare
- The 2025 reconciliation law broadened orphan drug exclusions, which delayed negotiations for high-spending drugs like Keytruda (pembrolizumab) and Opdivo (nivolumab) — these would have otherwise been in early rounds
- Legal challenges from pharmaceutical manufacturers have been largely unsuccessful (courts upheld CMS authority)
- The Trump administration has shown ambivalence toward the broader IRA while maintaining insulin cap provisions

## Practical Impact for Beneficiaries

| Scenario | What Changes in 2026 |
|----------|---------------------|
| On Eliquis for AFib | Pay MFP (negotiated price) at pharmacy; likely lower than previous cost-sharing tier |
| On Jardiance for heart failure | MFP applies; may see lower monthly cost |
| High drug spender | Use Prescription Payment Plan to avoid front-loaded large bills |
| Diabetic on insulin | $35/month cap per insulin product |
| On biologic with orphan designation | Drug may be excluded from negotiation — no guaranteed price reduction |
