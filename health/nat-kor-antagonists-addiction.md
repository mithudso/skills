# Kappa-Opioid Receptor Antagonists for SUD

## Core Concepts
- The kappa-opioid receptor (KOR), encoded by OPRK1, is activated primarily by the endogenous peptide dynorphin; KOR activation produces dysphoria, aversion, and anxiety — opposite of mu-opioid reward
- KOR antagonists block dynorphin-driven aversion, restoring dopaminergic tone and attenuating stress-induced relapse without direct abuse liability
- Key distinction from mu antagonists: KOR antagonists target the negative reinforcement arm of addiction (stress/withdrawal dysphoria) rather than euphoric reinforcement
- Principal compounds: nor-BNI and JDTic (prototypical, ultra-long duration, weeks-long KOR blockade), aticaprant/JNJ-67953964 (short-acting, t½ suitable for once-daily dosing, 30× selectivity KOR vs. MOR/DOR)
- Development class distinct from KOR agonists (spiradoline, U50488H), which worsen dysphoria and are not therapeutic

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Dynorphin/KOR signaling is upregulated by chronic stress and repeated drug exposure; elevated dynorphin suppresses nucleus accumbens dopamine release via presynaptic KOR on mesolimbic terminals
- KOR activation in the amygdala, bed nucleus of stria terminalis (BNST), and locus coeruleus drives corticotropin-releasing factor (CRF)-dependent anxiety and withdrawal aversion
- Stress-induced reinstatement of drug seeking in rodents requires KOR activation; KOR antagonist pretreatment selectively blocks stress- (not cue-) induced reinstatement in many models
- Aticaprant reverses anxiety-like behavior during alcohol withdrawal and dose-dependently reduces alcohol deprivation effect (ADE) in both male and female rats
- JDTic (10 mg/kg i.p., 2-hour pretreatment) decreased alcohol self-administration and cue-induced reinstatement of alcohol seeking in preclinical models
- Aticaprant + naltrexone combination fully prevented alcohol "relapse" drinking in rodents — synergy attributed to complementary MOR reward and KOR stress-axis blockade

## Clinical Picture
- AUD represents the primary SUD indication for KOR antagonist development due to strong preclinical stress-relapse evidence and high comorbidity with depression/anxiety
- Depression-SUD comorbidity: ~30–40% of AUD patients have comorbid MDD; elevated dynorphin may underlie both anhedonia and craving, making KOR a shared therapeutic target
- Aticaprant was evaluated in Phase IIa/II trials for MDD with anhedonia as the primary endpoint; Phase III (J&J) for MDD failed in March 2025, leading to discontinued development
- JDTic development was halted due to cardiac toxicity signals; PF-4455242 also discontinued for safety concerns — toxicity remains a class challenge for older compounds
- No published Phase II/III RCTs have been completed specifically in AUD or other SUDs to date (as of 2026)

## Evidence & Treatment
- Preclinical strength: KOR antagonists consistently reduce stress-induced reinstatement, withdrawal anxiety, and escalated intake across alcohol, cocaine, and opioid models
- Aticaprant Phase IIa (LY-2456302/CERC-501): showed antidepressant and anti-anhedonia signals; adequate safety and PK for daily dosing; did not advance SUD-specific trials before MDD program termination
- JDTic preclinical data (2012): 10 mg/kg reduced alcohol-seeking and withdrawal anxiety; cardiac QTc prolongation halted clinical development
- Combination strategy (aticaprant + naltrexone) outperformed either monotherapy for ADE in rats, supporting a future combination trial hypothesis
- No FDA-approved KOR antagonist for any SUD indication; research pipeline as of 2025–2026 focuses on next-generation selective analogs without the long-duration liability of nor-BNI/JDTic

## Key Facts
- Aticaprant selectivity: ~30-fold KOR vs. MOR/DOR; oral bioavailability suitable for once-daily clinical dosing
- JDTic/nor-BNI duration: KOR blockade lasting 2–3 weeks after single dose — mechanistic advantage for compliance, but safety liability for dose titration
- J&J Phase III aticaprant for MDD: discontinued March 2025 for lack of efficacy; SUD development de-prioritized
- ~30–40% comorbidity between AUD and MDD drives rationale for dual-target KOR strategy
- Dynorphin/KOR axis is distinct from the CRF stress axis (see add-corticotropin-releasing-factor-stress) but converges on the BNST/amygdala anxiety circuitry
