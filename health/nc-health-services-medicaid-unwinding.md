# NC Medicaid Continuous Coverage Unwinding (2023–2024)

## Background

During the COVID-19 pandemic (March 2020 onward), federal law **prohibited states from disenrolling Medicaid beneficiaries** as a condition of receiving enhanced federal matching funds. This meant NC Medicaid enrollment grew substantially — reaching ~2.5 million enrollees — without eligibility redeterminations.

When the **continuous coverage requirement ended** (federal public health emergency ended), states were required to redetermine eligibility for their entire enrolled population. This was called **"Medicaid unwinding."**

**NC Start**: NC DHHS began the unwinding process in **June 2023**.

## NC's Unwinding Strategy and Results

### National Performance
NC achieved the **nation's lowest rate of Medicaid disenrollments** during unwinding:
- NC: ~**12%** of completed redeterminations resulted in disenrollment
- National average: ~**32%**
- NC was significantly below all other states

### Disenrollment Numbers
| Period | Monthly Avg Disenrollments | Total |
|---|---|---|
| June–Nov 2023 (first 6 months) | ~26,414/month | ~158,500 total |
| Dec 2023–June 2024 | ~11,993/month | ~84,000 total |

The rate dropped **54%** after Medicaid Expansion took effect December 1, 2023 — providing an alternative eligibility pathway for many adults who would have otherwise lost coverage.

### Why NC Performed Better

NC's lower disenrollment rate attributed to:
1. **Medicaid Expansion timing** (Dec 2023): Adults losing standard Medicaid could potentially qualify for expansion
2. **NCDHHS proactive outreach**: Direct mail, phone, text, community partnerships to reach enrollees for renewals
3. **Ex parte redeterminations**: Using available data (tax records, SSA, SNAP) to automatically renew eligible members without requiring them to act
4. **County DSS partnerships**: Local offices engaged in targeted renewal assistance
5. **NC FAST (Families Accessing Services through Technology)**: NC's eligibility system handled redetermination processing

## Procedural vs. Substantive Terminations

Federal CMS tracked two types of disenrollments:

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Procedural terminations** | Lost coverage for paperwork/administrative reasons (couldn't be reached, didn't return forms) — may still be eligible |
| **Substantive terminations** | Actually found ineligible (income over limit, moved out of state, aged off) |

NC, like most states, had a high proportion of **procedural terminations** early in unwinding — a major policy concern because these individuals may have remained eligible. NC worked to reduce procedural terminations through outreach and ex parte renewals.

## National Context

GAO (2025 report) found that Medicaid disenrollments after COVID-19 varied widely across states and populations. Children and young adults were disproportionately disenrolled in many states. NC's strong performance is cited as a model.

## Intersection with Medicaid Expansion

NC's December 2023 Medicaid Expansion was strategically timed — occurring **mid-unwinding** — which created a safety net for adults who had COVID-era Medicaid but were losing it due to income being slightly above the pre-expansion threshold. Approximately 450,000 adults enrolled in expansion in the first 5 months (by May 2024), some of whom were unwinding-displaced enrollees re-qualifying under expansion.

## Post-Unwinding Status

By mid-2024, NC completed its primary unwinding redeterminations. The Medicaid caseload stabilized, with expansion adding new enrollees on an ongoing basis. The combined effect: **NC's Medicaid enrollment remained historically high**, with strong coverage of low-income adults, children, and individuals with disabilities.
