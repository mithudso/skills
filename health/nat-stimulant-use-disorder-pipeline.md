# Pharmacotherapy Pipeline for Stimulant Use Disorder

## Core Concepts
- No FDA-approved pharmacotherapy exists for cocaine use disorder (CUD) or methamphetamine use disorder (MUD) — a critical gap given ~5.5 million Americans with stimulant use disorder (2023 NSDUH)
- 2024 ASAM/AAAP Clinical Practice Guideline provides the first joint evidence grading for off-label medications; conditional recommendations only for a select few agents
- Contingency management (CM) — voucher-based or prize-based reinforcement of abstinence-verified urine screens — is the only intervention with strong, replicated evidence for both CUD and MUD
- Treatment gap for stimulants is wider than for opioids: no equivalent of methadone/buprenorphine agonist replacement; agonist substitution trials (dextroamphetamine, modafinil) have largely failed to show broad clinical benefit
- 1.5% of Americans 12+ met criteria for stimulant use disorder in the past year (2024 NSDUH)

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Cocaine blocks dopamine transporter (DAT), norepinephrine transporter (NET), and serotonin transporter (SERT); methamphetamine reverses DAT and vesicular monoamine transporter 2 (VMAT2), flooding the synapse
- Mesocorticolimbic hypodopaminergia in protracted abstinence (depleted D2/D3 receptor density in striatum) drives anhedonia, craving, and relapse — the primary pharmacological target
- Mirtazapine: NaSSA mechanism (α2 antagonist + 5-HT2/5-HT3 blockade) may attenuate methamphetamine-induced dopamine release; also has mild stimulant-mimicking properties without euphoria, reducing MSM-associated risk behaviors
- Bupropion: DAT/NET reuptake inhibitor acts as a weak dopaminergic agonist; attenuates cocaine/meth reward by partial substitution and blunted cue reactivity
- Naltrexone: μ-opioid receptor antagonist reduces stimulant-induced dopamine release in the nucleus accumbens via opioid-dopamine cross-talk; combination with bupropion targets complementary circuits
- Dopamine stabilizers (e.g., OSU6162/PNU-96391): partial agonists at D2/D3 receptors that normalize dopaminergic tone bidirectionally — reduce hyperactivity when DA is high, attenuate hypoactivity during withdrawal

## Clinical Picture
- Methamphetamine: U.S. overdose deaths involving stimulants exceeded 32,000 in 2022 (CDC); psychosis, cardiovascular toxicity, and neurotoxicity dominate the clinical picture
- Cocaine: $8B annual U.S. economic burden; high comorbidity with alcohol use disorder (~60% co-occurrence) complicates treatment
- MSM (men who have sex with men) population faces intersecting meth/HIV epidemic; mirtazapine has specific evidence in this subgroup (NMRC trial, 2015)
- Protracted abstinence syndrome: anhedonia, cognitive deficits, and sleep disturbance persist weeks to months after last use — the behavioral phenotype driving relapse; no pharmacological bridge analogous to MOUD
- Treatment retention is poor without CM; primary care integration is actively studied (2024 PMC) as a delivery mechanism for off-label prescribing

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Contingency management**: meta-analyses show 15–30% absolute increase in abstinence rates; SAMHSA 2024 advisory formally endorsed CM; 2025 NYSDOH HTA confirmed superiority to all pharmacological comparators; access barriers include legal prize caps in some states (federal limit historically $75/week lifted by VA/DoD programs)
- **Bupropion**: moderate efficacy for CUD abstinence in meta-analysis (NNT ~8); NIDA CTN ADAPT-2 trial (bupropion XL 450 mg + extended-release naltrexone 380 mg IM) showed significant abstinence rates for MUD — 13.6% vs 2.5% placebo at week 12; one of the strongest pharmacotherapy signals to date
- **Mirtazapine 30 mg/day**: reduced meth use in double-blind RCT among HIV+ MSM (Colfax et al.); mixed results in non-MSM populations; 2024 ASAM guideline gives conditional recommendation when co-occurring depression or sleep disorder present
- **Topiramate**: conditional recommendation; modulates glutamate/GABA; modest cocaine abstinence signal in RCTs but limited tolerability (cognitive side effects, weight loss)
- **Emerging — immunotherapy**: anti-cocaine hapten vaccines (e.g., TA-CD, COT-TT) generate IgG antibodies that bind cocaine in serum, preventing CNS entry; Phase II trials showed response only in high-antibody-titer subgroup; no Phase III success to date; meth vaccines in preclinical/early Phase I

## Key Facts
- ADAPT-2 (NEJM 2021): bupropion XL 450 mg + XR-naltrexone 380 mg → 13.6% abstinence vs 2.5% placebo (NNT ~9) for MUD over 12 weeks
- CM prize magnitude matters: $100–$200 cumulative voucher value associated with better outcomes; VA MIRECC programs achieved 40–50% abstinence rates with escalating reinforcement schedules
- OSU6162 Phase II alcohol studies: reduced priming-induced craving and improved executive function; stimulant SUD trials underway but no published Phase II results as of 2025
- Anti-cocaine vaccines: only patients achieving serum IgG >40 μg/mL showed reduced cocaine use in Phase II (Martell et al., 2009); immunogenicity variability remains the core barrier
- 2024 ASAM/AAAP guideline explicitly states: "No medications receive a strong recommendation for stimulant use disorder" — all pharmacotherapy is off-label, conditional
