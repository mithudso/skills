# Medigap (Medicare Supplement Insurance)

> **Educational only, NOT insurance advice.** Medigap premiums vary significantly
> by plan, insurer, age, location, and health. Get quotes from multiple insurers.
> Verify current plan benefits at **[medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/medigap)**.
> **verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

Medigap policies are **standardized private insurance plans** that fill ("gap-fill")
the cost-sharing holes in Original Medicare — primarily the Part A per-benefit-period
deductible, the daily hospital coinsurance, and the Part B 20% coinsurance. They
are sold by private insurers but the benefit packages are federally standardized,
so Plan G from Insurer A covers exactly the same items as Plan G from Insurer B.
**You compete insurers on price and service, not on what is covered.**

**Critical rule:** Medigap **only works with Original Medicare (Parts A+B)**. If
you join Medicare Advantage (Part C), you cannot use a Medigap policy.

## Part 1 — The coverage matrix (2026)

Plans are labeled A through N (C and F not available to new enrollees after Jan 1, 2020).

| Benefit | A | B | D | G | K | L | M | N |
|---------|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Part A coinsurance + 365 extra days | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Part B coinsurance/copay | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 50% | 75% | ✓ | ✓* |
| Part A hospice care coinsurance | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 50% | 75% | ✓ | ✓ |
| Skilled nursing facility coinsurance | — | — | ✓ | ✓ | 50% | 75% | ✓ | ✓ |
| Part A deductible | — | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | 50% | 75% | 50% | ✓ |
| Part B deductible ($283 in 2026) | — | — | — | — | — | — | — | — |
| Part B excess charges | — | — | — | ✓ | — | — | — | — |
| Foreign travel emergency (80%) | — | — | ✓ | ✓ | — | — | ✓ | ✓ |

*Plan N pays Part B coinsurance but the beneficiary pays **up to $20 copay** for
doctor visits and **up to $50 copay** for emergency room visits (waived if admitted
as inpatient).

> **Plans F and C** (not shown): cover everything Plan G covers PLUS the Part B
> deductible. Available only to those eligible for Medicare **before January 1, 2020**.
> If you became Medicare-eligible in 2020 or later, you cannot buy Plans C or F.

## Part 2 — The most popular plans explained

### Plan G — Most comprehensive for new enrollees
- Covers everything except the Part B annual deductible ($283 in 2026)
- Once you pay $283 in Part B-covered services, Medicare + Plan G covers the rest
- Pays Part A deductible (no matter how many benefit periods), all hospital
  coinsurance, all SNF coinsurance, Part B excess charges, foreign travel (80%)
- **Best predictability of costs** — after the $283 deductible, your out-of-pocket
  for covered services is essentially $0

### Plan F — Legacy comprehensive (pre-2020 enrollees only)
- Covers everything Plan G covers **plus the Part B deductible**
- No longer available to new Medicare enrollees (as of Jan 1, 2020)
- If eligible before 2020 and still enrolled, you can keep it; if not yet bought it,
  you must qualify under pre-2020 rules
- Typically higher premium than G; often not worth the premium differential
  since the Part B deductible it covers is only $283

### Plan N — Comprehensive with small copays
- Similar to Plan G but with **copays**: up to $20 for doctor visits, up to $50
  for ER visits (ER copay waived if admitted)
- Does **not** cover Part B excess charges — if a provider doesn't accept
  Medicare assignment (bills above Medicare's allowed amount), you owe the excess
- Lower premium than Plan G in exchange for these copay obligations
- Good fit if you rarely visit doctors and providers in your area all accept assignment

### Plans K and L — Partial coverage with OOP limits
- Cover 50% (K) or 75% (L) of most benefits rather than 100%
- Significantly lower premiums
- Have **annual out-of-pocket limits**: Plan K: **$8,000/year** (2026); Plan L: **$4,000/year** (2026)
  — once you reach the limit, the plan pays 100% for the rest of the year
- Can work for healthier beneficiaries who want catastrophic protection at lower premium

### Plan A — Basic minimum
- Covers Part A coinsurance only
- Minimal coverage; rarely the best value compared to slightly more expensive plans

### Plans B, D, M
- Niche use cases; B adds Part A deductible coverage; D adds SNF and foreign travel;
  M covers 50% of Part A deductible. Less commonly purchased.

## Part 3 — Guaranteed issue and the Medigap OEP

**This is the most important timing consideration for Medigap.**

### Medigap Open Enrollment Period (OEP)

When you turn 65 **and** enroll in Part B, you have a **6-month guaranteed-issue
window** during which insurers **must**:
- Sell you any Medigap plan they offer in your state
- Charge you the same "best available rate" regardless of your health conditions
- Cannot impose waiting periods for pre-existing conditions

This is your **best — and potentially only — opportunity to buy Medigap
without medical underwriting.** Outside this window (in most states), insurers
can review your health history and may:
- Charge higher premiums
- Impose waiting periods
- Deny coverage outright

> **The window opens when you have both Part A and Part B active and you are 65+.**
> If you delay Part B enrollment (e.g., you have employer coverage), the
> Medigap OEP doesn't open until you get Part B — but it still runs only 6 months.

### Guaranteed issue rights outside the OEP

You also have guaranteed issue rights in specific situations:
- Your Medicare Advantage plan leaves your area or stops covering your county
- You move and your MA plan doesn't cover your new area
- You join an MA plan for the first time and return to Original Medicare within
  12 months (the "trial period" right)
- Your employer-sponsored Medigap coverage ends

### Birthday rules (state-specific)

Some states (CA, ID, IL, KY, LA, MD, MO, NV, OR, and others) have a **birthday
rule** that gives you a guaranteed-issue window each year around your birthday
to switch Medigap plans from an insurer of your choice — without underwriting.
California's is the most expansive (a 60-day window every birthday). Verify your
state's rules at your state insurance commissioner's website.

## Part 4 — Medical underwriting outside guaranteed-issue

If you are outside your Medigap OEP and no guaranteed-issue right applies,
insurers in most states can underwrite:
- Review your medical history and conditions
- Charge more based on health
- Decline to sell you coverage at all

Common conditions that may cause problems: cancer in the last 2–5 years, recent
heart attack, stroke, organ transplant, HIV/AIDS, end-stage conditions. Rules
vary by state — a few states (CT, MA, NY) have year-round guaranteed issue.

## Part 5 — Cost considerations

Medigap premiums are **not standardized** (only benefits are). Factors affecting premium:
- **Plan type** (G is more expensive than N; K/L are cheapest)
- **Rating method:**
  - *Community-rated* — same premium for all enrollees in a plan; doesn't change as you age
  - *Issue-age-rated* — based on age when you first buy; premium fixed or rises slowly
  - *Attained-age-rated* — based on your current age; cheapest at 65, rises as you age
- **Location** (state, county)
- **Insurer** (same plan, large premium differences across companies)
- **Gender** (in states that allow gender rating)
- **Tobacco use**

**Strategy:** compare attained-age vs. issue-age carefully. An attained-age plan
may be cheaper at 65 but cost significantly more at 75–85.

---

## Part 6 — Medigap does NOT cover

- **Prescription drugs** (Part D handles this; Medigap stopped selling drug coverage)
- **Dental, vision, hearing** (not in any standardized Medigap plan)
- **Long-term custodial care** (Medicaid or private LTC insurance)
- **Medicare Advantage cost-sharing** (Medigap is incompatible with MA)
- **Private room** upgrades
- Care outside the U.S. (except the 80% foreign travel benefit in some plans)
