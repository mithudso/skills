# Medicare Enrollment Periods and Penalties

> **Educational only, NOT insurance advice.** Enrollment windows have hard
> deadlines; missed windows can result in permanent premium increases.
> Contact **1-800-MEDICARE** or your local SHIP counselor with questions.
> Verify current information at **[medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov)**.
> **verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

Medicare has a complex web of enrollment periods with different rules for
different parts. Getting the timing right matters enormously: missing the wrong
window can lock you into **permanent premium penalties** or leave you without
coverage when you need it.

---

## Part 1 — Initial Enrollment Period (IEP): the foundational window

The IEP is the primary enrollment opportunity for most people turning 65.

**Duration:** 7 months
- 3 months **before** the month you turn 65
- The month **you turn 65**
- 3 months **after** the month you turn 65

**What you can enroll in:** Medicare Parts A and B.

**When coverage starts:**
- Enroll in months 1–3 (before birthday month): coverage starts the first day of
  your birthday month
- Enroll in your birthday month: coverage starts the first day of the following month
- Enroll in months 5–7 (after birthday month): coverage starts **2 months** after
  enrollment — meaning if you wait until month 7, you could be without coverage
  for up to 3 months after your birthday

> **Practical rule:** sign up in the 3 months before your birthday for the
> smoothest coverage start date.

**Under-65 IEP:** For those who qualify via disability, the IEP runs 3 months
before the 25th month of SSDI, through the 25th month, and 3 months after.
ESRD and ALS have their own IEP rules.

---

## Part 2 — Special Enrollment Period (SEP): delaying Medicare safely

The most important SEP is for people with **employer-sponsored coverage**:

### Employer/union coverage SEP

If you or your spouse has **group health coverage from an active employer** with
**20 or more employees**, you may delay Medicare enrollment without penalty
while that coverage is active:
- You can delay Part B (and Part D) enrollment
- Once the employer coverage **ends** (retirement, job loss, etc.), you have an
  **8-month window** to enroll in Part B without penalty
- The 8-month SEP starts when the employer coverage ends **OR** the employment
  ends, **whichever comes first** — not when COBRA starts (COBRA is not employer-
  based active coverage for SEP purposes)

**Important rules:**
- Employer must have ≥20 employees; retiree coverage is not "active employer coverage"
  for SEP purposes
- COBRA, retiree health plans, individual marketplace plans, and VA coverage
  generally do NOT qualify as creditable employer coverage that delays the IEP
- Small employer coverage (<20 employees): Medicare becomes primary; you should
  usually enroll at 65 regardless

### Other SEPs

- Moving out of your Medicare Advantage plan's service area
- Your Medicare Advantage plan leaves your area
- Losing other creditable coverage (e.g., Medicaid ending)
- Qualifying for a Medicare Savings Program
- Various special circumstances (nursing home admission, PACE enrollment, etc.)

---

## Part 3 — General Enrollment Period (GEP): the catch-up window

**When:** January 1 – March 31 each year

**Who uses it:** People who missed their IEP and have no qualifying SEP

**Coverage starts:** July 1 of that year (a gap of up to 6 months after enrollment)

**Consequence:** If you missed your IEP and use the GEP, you likely owe the
Part B **late enrollment penalty** (see Part 5).

---

## Part 4 — Medicare Advantage enrollment periods

| Period | Dates | Who can use | Changes effective |
|--------|-------|-------------|------------------|
| **ICEP** (Initial Coverage Election Period) | 3 months before 65 through end of IEP | New to Medicare | Start of Part A+B coverage |
| **AEP** (Annual Election Period) | Oct 15 – Dec 7 | Anyone | January 1 of following year |
| **OEP/MA OEP** | Jan 1 – Mar 31 | Current MA enrollees only | Feb 1 or Mar 1 or Apr 1 |
| **SEP** | Varies by trigger | Those with qualifying event | Depends on event |
| **D-SNP Monthly SEP** (2025+) | Any month | Full-benefit dual eligibles | Following month |

**AEP (Annual Election Period, Oct 15–Dec 7)** is when anyone can:
- Switch from Original Medicare to Medicare Advantage
- Switch Medicare Advantage plans
- Switch from Medicare Advantage to Original Medicare
- Join, switch, or drop a Part D standalone drug plan

**MA OEP (Jan 1–Mar 31):** Current MA enrollees only can switch to a different
MA plan or switch back to Original Medicare.

> **Note:** you cannot switch from Original Medicare to Original Medicare + new
> Medigap during the MA OEP. For Medigap, you need either your Medigap OEP
> (6 months at 65 + Part B) or a guaranteed-issue right.

## Part 5 — Late enrollment penalties

### Part B penalty

**Amount:** **+10% of the standard monthly Part B premium** for **each full
12-month period** you were eligible but didn't enroll (and had no qualifying SEP)

- **Duration:** Permanent — added to your premium **for the rest of your life**
- **2026 example:** If you delayed 24 months without a valid SEP, you owe
  20% more than the standard premium — $202.90 × 1.20 = ~$243.48/month, permanently
- The penalty compounds: each year the standard premium rises, your penalty
  percentage is applied to the new, higher base

### Part D penalty

**Amount:** **1% × national base beneficiary premium × number of uncovered months**

- 2026 national base beneficiary premium: approximately **$39.06/month**
- 24 months uncovered = 24% × $39.06 = ~$9.37/month added to your Part D premium
- **Duration:** Permanent — as long as you have Part D
- The base premium can change annually, so the penalty dollar amount can rise over time

### Part A penalty (only if you must pay for Part A)

Applies only to the ~1% of beneficiaries who don't qualify for premium-free
Part A. Penalty: **up to +10%** of the Part A premium for **twice the number
of years** you delayed. (If you delayed 2 years, you pay the penalty for 4 years.)

## Part 6 — Creditable coverage: protecting yourself from Part D penalty

To avoid the Part D late penalty, you need **creditable drug coverage** —
coverage at least as good as the standard Medicare drug benefit. Sources include:
- Most employer or union drug plans
- TRICARE (military) drug coverage
- Veterans Affairs (VA) coverage
- FEHBP (federal employee) drug coverage
- State Pharmaceutical Assistance Programs (SPAPs) in some states

When you have creditable coverage, you can delay Part D enrollment without
penalty. When that coverage ends, you have **63 days** to enroll in a Part D plan.

> **Always get written proof** of creditable coverage from any employer or plan
> when you leave. You may need to document continuous creditable coverage to
> avoid a Part D penalty when you eventually enroll.

---

## Part 7 — The most common enrollment mistake

**Working past 65 with employer coverage → retiring → waiting too long**

The sequence: you're covered by an employer plan past 65 → you retire → your
employer coverage ends → you have 8 months to enroll in Part B under the SEP.

**Mistakes people make:**
1. **Electing COBRA and assuming it extends the SEP** — wrong. The SEP runs from
   when employment ends, not when COBRA ends. If you wait until COBRA's 18 months
   expire, you may be many months into — or past — your 8-month SEP.
2. **Relying on retiree health coverage as the SEP trigger** — retiree coverage
   is not "active employer coverage" and does not qualify for the employer SEP.
   The SEP runs from when active employment coverage ends.
3. **Confusing "group coverage" with "employer coverage"** — COBRA, marketplace
   plans, and other group coverage are not qualifying employer coverage for the SEP.
