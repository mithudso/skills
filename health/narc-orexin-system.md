# Orexin/Hypocretin System

## Core Concepts
- Orexins (hypocretins) are neuropeptides discovered in 1998; two forms: **orexin-A** (33 aa) and **orexin-B** (28 aa), both cleaved from prepro-orexin
- Produced exclusively by ~70,000 neurons in the **lateral hypothalamus (LH)** and perifornical area
- Signal via two G-protein-coupled receptors: **OX1R** (orexin-A selective) and **OX2R** (dual-ligand); both are Gq/Gi-coupled excitatory receptors
- Primary function: stabilize the sleep–wake switch, preventing inappropriate transitions into sleep or wake states
- System also integrates energy homeostasis, stress response, reward/motivation, and feeding behavior

## Mechanisms / Biology
- LH orexin neurons project widely to **locus coeruleus** (norepinephrine), **tuberomammillary nucleus** (histamine), **dorsal raphe** (serotonin), **ventral tegmental area** (dopamine), and **basal forebrain** (acetylcholine) — collectively sustaining arousal
- Orexin-A crosses the blood-brain barrier more readily than orexin-B; has ~10× higher affinity for OX1R; OX2R is the dominant wake-promoting receptor
- Wake-active orexin neurons are inhibited during NREM and virtually silent during REM sleep; their tonic firing during waking suppresses REM intrusion
- In **narcolepsy type 1 (NT1)**, 85–95% of orexin neurons are lost via presumed autoimmune destruction — HLA-DQB1*06:02 present in >98% of NT1 patients
- Loss is selective: neighboring melanin-concentrating hormone (MCH) neurons are spared, confirming targeted neurodegeneration rather than diffuse LH damage
- Autoimmune hypothesis: T-cell-mediated attack, possibly triggered by molecular mimicry (H1N1 influenza antigen or Pandemrix vaccine peptide similarity to prepro-orexin)

## Clinical Picture
- NT1 prevalence: ~1 in 2,000 (0.02–0.05% globally); onset typically in adolescence/young adulthood, peak age 15–25
- CSF orexin-A <110 pg/mL (vs. normal ≥200 pg/mL) is diagnostic of NT1; found in >90% of NT1 cases; specificity >99%
- Pentad: excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS), cataplexy, sleep paralysis, hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations, disrupted nocturnal sleep
- Mean diagnostic delay: 8–10 years from symptom onset
- HLA-DQB1*06:02 allele: present in ~98% of NT1 vs. ~25% of general population; necessary but not sufficient

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Sodium oxybate** (GHB): FDA-approved for EDS + cataplexy; consolidates nocturnal sleep; reduces cataplexy by ~85% in trials
- **Pitolisant** (H3 inverse agonist): approved 2019; increases histamine/norepinephrine/dopamine; MWT improvement ~6–8 min vs. placebo
- **Solriamfetol** (dopamine/norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor): approved 2019; dose-dependent MWT improvement up to 12.6 min
- **Danavorexton** (TAK-994/TAK-861, selective OX2R agonist): 2024 Phase 2b trial showed MWT improvement of 11.1 points, outperforming modafinil; first direct orexin-replacement pharmacology
- Traditional stimulants (modafinil, amphetamines) and TCAs/SNRIs for cataplexy remain standard while OX2R agonists advance through trials

## Key Facts
- Orexin neurons discovered independently by two groups in 1998: de Lecea (hypocretin) and Sakurai (orexin)
- NT1 CSF orexin-A diagnostic threshold: **<110 pg/mL**; normal ≥200 pg/mL
- Up to **95% neuronal loss** in LH orexin population at NT1 autopsy
- HLA-DQB1*06:02 OR for NT1: ~251× increased risk vs. non-carriers
- OX2R agonism (not OX1R) is the target for wake-promotion replacement therapy
