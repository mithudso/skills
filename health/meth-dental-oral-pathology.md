# Meth Mouth: Oral Pathology Mechanisms

## Core Concepts
- **Meth mouth** (methamphetamine-associated rampant caries, MARC): accelerated, severe dental destruction in chronic methamphetamine users affecting cervical and buccal smooth surfaces of posterior teeth and interproximal areas of anterior teeth
- Five converging mechanisms: xerostomia, salivary pH drop, bruxism/trismus, vasoconstriction of oral vasculature, and behavioral hygiene neglect — no single cause
- Caries pattern is distinct from other drug-related decay: cervical/buccal predominance versus occlusal-first in sugar-excess caries
- Acidic nature of smoked methamphetamine (pH ~4–5) directly erodes enamel independent of systemic effects
- Dysbiotic shift in plaque microbiome toward *Streptococcus mutans* and *Lactobacillus* spp. amplifies demineralization

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **Xerostomia**: sympathomimetic α-adrenergic stimulation causes vasoconstriction of salivary gland vasculature → reduced acinar perfusion → salivary flow drops by up to 50%, eliminating buffering (bicarbonate), antimicrobial proteins (IgA, lactoferrin), and remineralization substrate (Ca²⁺, phosphate)
- **Acidic pH environment**: saliva pH falls below the critical 5.5 threshold for hydroxyapatite dissolution; cariogenic bacteria thrive in low-pH niches that suppress acid-sensitive commensal species
- **Bruxism/trismus**: METH-driven dopaminergic and serotonergic hyperactivation in basal ganglia and brainstem produces nocturnal and waking parafunctional grinding; mechanical attrition removes enamel at up to 1 mm/year, exposes dentin tubules, and creates pulp access for bacterial invasion
- **Vasoconstriction**: systemic α₁-adrenergic activation reduces gingival and pulpal blood flow, impairing immune surveillance and tissue repair; contributes to accelerated periodontitis and avascular necrosis of alveolar bone in severe cases
- **Behavioral compounding**: prolonged binges (days without sleep) suppress oral hygiene entirely; high-sugar soft drink consumption during intoxication floods a buffering-deficient oral environment; malnutrition reduces enamel remineralization capacity

## Clinical Picture
- Largest published cohort (n = 571): 96% had cavities, 58% had untreated decay, only 23% retained all natural teeth (vs. 48% general population); 31% had ≥6 missing teeth (vs. 8.5% general population)
- Presentation severity correlates with route of administration: smoking > injection > intranasal — direct acid vapor contact magnifies enamel erosion
- Gingival enlargement, severe periodontitis, and oral mucosal ulcerations co-present; periodontal disease rate significantly exceeds matched non-users
- Forensic dental analysis (autopsy series) identifies MARC pattern as distinguishable from methamphetamine-unrelated rampant caries and eating-disorder erosion by distribution and depth
- Co-occurring HIV infection (prevalent in METH-using populations) further impairs salivary flow and immune defense, compounding severity

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Staging**: no standardized MARC staging system adopted universally; modified DMFT (Decayed/Missing/Filled Teeth) index and CAST (Caries Assessment Spectrum and Treatment) used in research; cervical and buccal smooth surface lesion count used as proxy marker
- **Immediate management**: fluoride varnish application, saliva substitutes (pilocarpine 5 mg TID for xerostomia), chlorhexidine 0.12% rinse to reduce *S. mutans* load, high-dose topical fluoride (5000 ppm toothpaste)
- **Restorative priorities**: glass-ionomer cements preferred over composites for active users due to fluoride-releasing properties; full-mouth rehabilitation under general anesthesia often required given extent of destruction
- **Treatment challenges**: active addiction impairs appointment adherence; dental anxiety elevated in METH users; vasoconstriction contraindications to epinephrine-containing local anesthetics debated (risk of hypertensive crisis with concurrent METH use)
- **Integrated care**: most effective when dental treatment is embedded in SUD treatment programs; motivational interviewing at dental appointments improves oral hygiene behavior more than didactic instruction alone

## Key Facts
- Salivary pH in active METH users: falls to as low as 5.0 (critical threshold for enamel dissolution is 5.5)
- Salivary flow reduction: sympathomimetic effects can reduce unstimulated salivary flow by 40–60%
- Bruxism incidence: estimated 30–40% of active METH users exhibit clinically significant bruxism
- Smoked methamphetamine pH: approximately 4.0–5.5, comparable to erosive soft drinks applied directly to enamel
- DMFT scores in chronic users average 18–22 compared to 3–5 in age-matched controls in published cross-sectional studies
