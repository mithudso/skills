# Autoimmune Pathogenesis of Narcolepsy

## Core Concepts
- **Narcolepsy type 1 (NT1)** results from selective destruction of ~70,000 hypothalamic lateral hypothalamus neurons that synthesize orexin (hypocretin); NT2 has intact orexin neurons with unclear etiology
- **HLA-DQB1*06:02** is present in ~98% of NT1 patients vs ~25% of the general population; confers ~251-fold increased risk of NT1
- The DQ0602 heterodimer (HLA-DQA1*01:02 + HLA-DQB1*06:02) presents orexin-derived peptides to autoreactive T cells — the core antigen-presentation step
- Destruction is highly selective: melanin-concentrating hormone (MCH) neurons in the same lateral hypothalamic region are spared, supporting targeted autoimmunity over non-specific injury
- Both humoral and cellular arms are implicated; cellular (T-cell) evidence is more consistent than autoantibody findings

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **CD4+ T cells**: Orexin/hypocretin-reactive memory CD4+ T cells detected in all NT1 patients tested; absent in healthy HLA-matched controls — strongest cellular evidence to date
- **CD8+ T cells**: HCRT-specific CD8+ cytotoxic T cells also identified in NT1 blood; can directly kill orexin neurons via MHC class I-mediated recognition
- **Antigen mimicry hypothesis**: Structural homology between orexin peptide epitopes and influenza A (H1N1) hemagglutinin or Pandemrix AS03-adjuvanted antigen may initiate cross-reactive T-cell responses
- **TCR gene polymorphisms**: GWAS identifies T-cell receptor alpha (TCRA) locus variants as secondary NT1 risk factors, reinforcing the T-cell-driven model
- **No consistent autoantibody**: Anti-Tribbles homolog 2 (TRIB2), anti-orexin, and other antibodies reported but not replicated across cohorts; T-cell autoreactivity is the more reproducible finding

## Clinical Picture
- NT1 prevalence: ~20–50/100,000 in Western populations; onset bimodal at ages 15 and 35; median delay from symptom onset to diagnosis ~10 years
- Cardinal features: excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS), cataplexy (emotion-triggered REM atonia), sleep paralysis, hypnagogic hallucinations, disrupted nocturnal sleep
- Diagnostic gold standard: CSF orexin-A ≤110 pg/mL (or ≤1/3 of mean normal values) confirms NT1; MSLT mean sleep latency ≤8 min with ≥2 SOREMPs is the polysomnographic criterion
- HLA-DQB1*06:02 testing has ~98% sensitivity for NT1 but low specificity (positive in ~25% of general population); used to exclude diagnosis if negative
- Onset within 6–12 months of H1N1 infection or vaccination strongly suggests immune trigger in genetically susceptible individuals

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Pandemrix signal**: Following the 2009–2010 H1N1 Pandemrix campaign, NT1 incidence rose 5–14× in children/adolescents and 2–7× in adults in Nordic countries; Arepanrix (same antigen, different adjuvant) showed no comparable signal — implicating AS03 adjuvant + antigen combination
- **Immunotherapy rationale**: Case series report partial orexin recovery with early IVIG, plasmapheresis, or corticosteroids if initiated within weeks of symptom onset; no RCT evidence yet; window of opportunity likely closes once neuron loss is complete
- **Symptomatic standard of care**: Sodium oxybate (GHB; once- or twice-nightly dosing) for EDS + cataplexy; modafinil/armodafinil for EDS (first-line in many guidelines); pitolisant (H3 inverse agonist) approved EU/US for EDS ± cataplexy; solriamfetol (dopamine/norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor) for EDS
- **Emerging immunotherapy**: TAK-861 (orexin-2 receptor agonist) in phase 3 trials 2024–2025 addresses downstream deficiency rather than autoimmune cause; NTRX-07 and other immune-targeted agents in early development
- AAN/AASM guidelines recommend CSF orexin measurement when MSLT is inconclusive and cataplexy is atypical

## Key Facts
- 251-fold elevated NT1 risk with HLA-DQB1*06:02 positivity
- ~70,000 orexin neurons lost in NT1; represents ~90% depletion at symptom onset
- CSF orexin-A threshold: ≤110 pg/mL diagnostic for NT1 (normal ~200–400 pg/mL)
- Pandemrix-associated NT1 risk: 1 in ~55,000 vaccinees (children); vastly exceeded natural H1N1 infection risk of NT1 in affected countries
- Symptom-to-diagnosis delay: median 8–10 years in adults, reflecting poor disease recognition
