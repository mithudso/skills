# Medicare for Working Seniors: Coordinating with Employer Coverage

Turning 65 doesn't automatically require enrolling in Medicare if you're still working and covered by employer insurance — **but it depends critically on your employer's size.**

## The Employer Size Rule

### Large Employer: 20 or more employees
- **Medicare is SECONDARY** — your employer group health plan (GHP) pays first
- You can **safely delay Part B** (and pay no penalty later) as long as employer GHP remains active
- Your employer cannot require you to sign up for Medicare at 65 — they must offer you the same benefits as younger employees (law: Section 1862(b) of the Social Security Act)
- You must still sign up for **Part A** if it's premium-free (most people qualify after 40 quarters)
- Part A enrollment doesn't affect your employer coverage for most services

### Small Employer: Fewer than 20 employees
- **Medicare is PRIMARY** — Medicare pays first; employer plan is secondary
- If you don't enroll in Medicare, your employer plan may **refuse claims**, leaving you with bills Medicare would have covered
- This is called "ghost coverage" — you appear covered but the plan won't pay what it expects Medicare to pay first
- Enrolling in Part B is **effectively mandatory** to maintain real coverage
- Failing to enroll means Part B **late enrollment penalty** accrues for every 12-month period you lacked coverage

### How to verify your employer's size
- Ask HR whether the company has 20+ employees for Medicare coordination purposes
- The count includes all employees, not just those at your location

## The 8-Month Special Enrollment Period (SEP)

When active employment ends (or the employer GHP terminates), you get an **8-month SEP** to enroll in Part B:

- Starts the month after employment ends **or** the month after GHP coverage ends (whichever comes first)
- **Applies only to active employment coverage** — retiree coverage does NOT qualify for this SEP
- You have the full 8 months, but enrolling early ensures no coverage gap

**Critical mistake to avoid:** Enrolling in COBRA after retirement and waiting for the COBRA SEP — there is no COBRA SEP. You must enroll in Part B during the 8-month employer SEP, not after COBRA ends.

## The COBRA Trap

**Rule:** COBRA terminates automatically when you enroll in Medicare — but this only runs one direction. If you're already enrolled in Medicare and then get COBRA, you can keep both.

**The trap:** You retire at 65, your employer offers COBRA, and you delay Medicare enrollment assuming COBRA is "employer coverage" that qualifies for SEP later. It doesn't work:
1. COBRA is continuation coverage, not active employer group coverage
2. It does NOT protect you from Part B late enrollment penalties
3. When you eventually enroll in Medicare, COBRA ends — but you may have already lost the 8-month SEP

**Correct sequence:** 
1. Retire → use the 8-month SEP to enroll in Part B
2. Only then decide whether to take COBRA for any coverage gap period before Part B kicks in
3. Or: Enroll in Medicare first, then choose Medigap/Medicare Advantage

## HSA Contributions: The Medicare Conflict

**Rule:** Once enrolled in ANY part of Medicare (even Part A only), you **cannot contribute** to an HSA (Health Savings Account).

- Applies to Part A, Part B, Part D — any enrollment triggers prohibition
- **The 6-month retroactive lookback:** When you turn 65 and enroll in Part A, Social Security may enroll you retroactively up to 6 months. Any HSA contributions made during those 6 months become excess contributions subject to IRS penalty (6% excise tax per year until corrected)
- To avoid this: If you plan to work and keep HSA contributions past 65, delay ALL Medicare enrollment — but only if large-employer coverage applies (see above)
- Stop HSA contributions at least 6 months before the month you want Medicare to begin (i.e., at least 6 months before planned retirement)

## Decision Guide for Working Seniors at 65

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Large employer (20+), want to keep working | Enroll in Part A (free); delay Part B; stop HSA 6 months before planned retirement |
| Large employer (20+), employer plan has high premiums | Consider enrolling in both Medicare A+B; employer plan becomes secondary |
| Small employer (<20) | Enroll in both Part A and Part B at 65; coordinate with HR |
| Retiring at/near 65 | Use 8-month SEP; enroll in Part B before SEP expires; do not rely on COBRA for SEP purposes |
| Have COBRA, considering Medicare | Enroll in Medicare during the 8-month employer SEP; COBRA will end but Part B + Medigap fills gap |

## Common Questions

**Can I have Medicare and employer coverage simultaneously?**  
Yes — they coordinate as primary/secondary based on employer size. This can reduce your out-of-pocket costs (Medicare pays what the employer plan doesn't, or vice versa).

**What if my employer covers my spouse but I turn 65?**  
Your Medicare coordination is based on whether YOUR employer has 20+ employees. If you're covered under a spouse's employer plan, the spouse's employer size determines payer order.

**What if my employer has exactly 20 employees?**  
The threshold is 20 or more — exactly 20 qualifies as large employer; your GHP is primary.
