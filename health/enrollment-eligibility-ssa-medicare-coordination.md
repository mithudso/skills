# SSA and Medicare Enrollment Coordination

## SSA's Role in Medicare

The Social Security Administration (SSA) — not CMS — is the agency that processes most Medicare Part A and Part B enrollment. SSA:
- Receives and processes Medicare enrollment applications
- Determines eligibility based on age, work history, SSDI status
- Transmits enrollment data to CMS
- Issues Medicare cards (now plastic ID cards with Medicare Beneficiary Identifier [MBI])
- Handles Medicare-related premium collection coordination

**Important:** CMS administers Medicare benefits; SSA administers Medicare enrollment and the SSI/SSDI programs that feed into Medicare eligibility.

## Auto-Enrollment Scenarios

### Age 65 + Already Receiving Social Security Benefits
Most common scenario. If already receiving Social Security retirement or SSDI benefits when turning 65:
- SSA automatically enrolls in Parts A and B
- Medicare card mailed ~3 months before 65th birthday
- Automatic enrollment in Part B; must actively opt out if not wanted (e.g., still have employer coverage)
- How to opt out: return Medicare card or contact SSA

### SSDI After 24-Month Wait
After 24 months of SSDI payment eligibility:
- SSA automatically enrolls in Parts A and B
- Card mailed ~3 months before the 25th month
- Same auto-enrollment timing as age 65 enrollees

### Working Past 65 (Not Receiving Social Security Yet)
Must ACTIVELY apply — no auto-enrollment:
- Apply for Medicare 3 months before 65th birthday (beginning of IEP)
- Apply online at ssa.gov, call 1-800-772-1213, or visit SSA office
- Can apply for Part A only (premium-free, so no cost) while deferring Part B if keeping employer coverage

## How to Enroll via SSA

### Online (Preferred)
- ssa.gov/medicare/sign-up
- Can apply for Medicare online even if not applying for Social Security benefits simultaneously
- Can apply for Part A only (to preserve employer coverage for Part B)
- Application typically takes 10-15 minutes

### Phone
- SSA national number: 1-800-772-1213
- TTY: 1-800-325-0778
- Hours: Monday-Friday 8am-7pm local time

### In-Person
- Local SSA field office (800+ nationwide)
- Appointments recommended; walk-ins accepted

### When Applying by Mail Is Necessary
- Certain special situations (complex eligibility, disability claims) may require in-person or mailed documentation

## Special Application for Part B Only

Scenario: Had Part A for years (e.g., premium-free auto-enrolled at 65) but deferred Part B while working. Now ready to enroll in Part B:
- Submit CMS Form CMS-40B (Application for Part B)
- If using employer coverage SEP: also submit CMS-L564 (Request for Employment Information — completed by employer to verify coverage)
- These forms go to local SSA office or Medicare (not CMS directly in most cases)

## SSDI-to-Medicare Transition

**Month 1-24 of SSDI:** Person has SSDI income, no Medicare yet (waiting period)
- May be eligible for Medicaid during this period (through SSI or state Medicaid)
- States may have Medicaid continuation for disabled individuals during SSDI waiting period

**Month 25 (Medicare kicks in):**
- SSA auto-enrolls in Part A and Part B
- Person now has Medicare; if also low-income → may qualify for dual eligibility
- If in a state Medicaid plan: Medicaid may coordinate with Medicare

**SSDI Medicare card:** Sent to address on file with SSA; Medicare number is the MBI (not SSN any longer, as of 2018).

## Railroad Retirement Board (RRB) Coordination

Railroad workers and their spouses/survivors receive retirement benefits through the Railroad Retirement Board, not SSA. Medicare enrollment for railroad workers is also handled by RRB:
- Same eligibility standards as SSA
- Apply through RRB: rrb.gov or call 1-877-772-5772
- Same enrollment periods and timelines as SSA-coordinated Medicare

**Key difference:** Railroad workers who are entitled to Medicare through RRB have RRB-specific Medicare numbers (prefix with letters); claims processed by CMS but enrollment coordinated by RRB.

## Common SSA-Medicare Enrollment Errors

### 1. Failure to Opt Out of Part B Auto-Enrollment
- Auto-enrolled person with employer coverage doesn't return card → paying unnecessary Part B premium
- Fix: contact SSA ASAP; retroactive disenrollment possible within IEP window

### 2. Incorrect Birth Date in SSA Records
- If SSA records have wrong birth date, Medicare card may not arrive on time
- Fix: contact SSA with documentation (birth certificate); allow processing time

### 3. Name/SSN Discrepancy
- Medicare MBI doesn't match other ID documents due to SSA record error
- Fix: SSA office visit with ID documents

### 4. Employer Coverage SEP Documentation Issues
- Employer doesn't complete CMS-L564 accurately or timely
- Result: Part B enrollment delayed or denied; penalty risk
- Fix: SHIP counselor assistance; Medicare ombudsman; SSA reconsideration

### 5. Part D Auto-Enrollment Error (Dual Eligibles)
- CMS auto-enrolls full duals into a benchmark Part D plan
- Wrong plan assigned (doesn't cover needed medications)
- Fix: Use monthly SEP to switch; contact SMP (Senior Medicare Patrol) if fraud suspected

## Medicare Beneficiary Identifier (MBI)

Since April 2018, Medicare no longer uses SSNs on Medicare cards. New alphanumeric MBI:
- 11 characters (letters and numbers)
- Unique to each beneficiary
- Protect like SSN — fraud target
