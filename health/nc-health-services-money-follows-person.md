# NC Money Follows the Person (MFP)

## Overview

**Money Follows the Person (MFP)** is NC Medicaid's demonstration program that helps Medicaid-eligible North Carolinians living in **inpatient facilities** — nursing facilities, intermediate care facilities for people with intellectual disabilities (ICFs/IID), and long-stay hospitals — move back to **their own homes and communities** with appropriate Medicaid supports.

The program's name reflects the core principle: Medicaid dollars that were paying for institutional care can "follow" the person to community settings, funding equivalent or lower-cost home and community-based services (HCBS).

## Federal Authority and Funding

MFP was established by the Deficit Reduction Act of 2005 and reauthorized multiple times, most recently through the **Consolidated Appropriations Act 2021** (extended through September 30, 2027). CMS funds a portion of transition costs and provides enhanced FMAP during demonstration participation.

NC Medicaid administers MFP in partnership with the **Center for Aging Research and Educational Services (CARES)** at the UNC-CH School of Social Work.

## Who Is Eligible

To qualify for NC MFP, a person must:
- Be eligible for NC Medicaid
- Be currently living in a qualified inpatient facility (nursing facility, ICF/IID, long-stay hospital, or psychiatric facility for persons under 21)
- Have resided in the facility for **at least 90 consecutive days**
- Want to transition to a **community-based setting** (own home, family home, assisted living, group home — not another institution)
- Have access to HCBS waiver services in the community

## What MFP Provides

MFP provides **one-time transition funding** to remove financial barriers to discharge:

| Transition Assistance | Examples |
|---|---|
| Housing deposits | Security deposit, first/last month's rent |
| Utility setup | Deposits, startup costs |
| Household furnishings | Bed, furniture, basic household items |
| Accessibility modifications | Ramps, grab bars, widened doorways |
| Moving expenses | Transport of belongings |
| Technology and safety | Medical alert systems, phone setup |

These are **one-time, non-recurring** expenses — ongoing care is funded through HCBS waivers.

## Community Support After Transition

Once transitioned, MFP participants receive long-term supports through one of four NC Medicaid programs:

1. **Community Alternatives Program for Disabled Adults (CAP-DA)** — adults with physical disabilities; approximately 13,500 total waiver slots statewide
2. **Community Alternatives Program for Children (CAP-C)** — children with complex medical needs
3. **NC Innovations Waiver** — adults and children with intellectual and developmental disabilities (I/DD)
4. **Program of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly (PACE)** — for dual-eligible adults 55+ (Medicare-Medicaid)

## Transition Coordination Structure

**Vaya Health** (one of the four LME-MCOs in NC) provides **statewide transition coordination** for MFP participants with **physical disabilities** transitioning using PACE or CAP/DA services — this arrangement began **September 18, 2024**.

Transition coordinators:
- Work with facility staff and the individual to plan the discharge
- Connect the person to HCBS waiver services and community providers
- Arrange housing, equipment, and support services before discharge
- Provide follow-up support after transition to prevent reinstitutionalization

## NC HCBS Spending Plan

NC Medicaid submits quarterly HCBS Spending Plan narratives to CMS (required under the American Rescue Plan Act Section 9817 HCBS enhanced FMAP). These reports track rebalancing progress, quality measures, and HCBS infrastructure investments. Reports are available on the NC Medicaid website (January 2025 and July 2024 quarterly reports are published).

## Scale and Rebalancing Context

NC has historically been more institutionally-oriented than some states in LTSS financing. MFP is part of broader NC efforts to rebalance toward HCBS:

- NC provides approximately **13,500 HCBS waiver slots per year** across waiver programs (as of 2025), with CAP/DA having approximately 300 slots reserved for Alzheimer's and related dementia
- NC Innovations waiver has a waiting list of thousands of individuals
- MFP helps reduce pressure on nursing facilities and supports the state's HCBS goals
