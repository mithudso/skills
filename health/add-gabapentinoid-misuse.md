# Gabapentinoid Misuse & Dependence

## Core Concepts
- Gabapentinoids (gabapentin, pregabalin) are α2δ VGCC ligands approved for neuropathic pain, epilepsy, and fibromyalgia — structurally GABA analogs that do NOT bind GABA receptors
- Misuse defined as use outside prescribed parameters: escalating doses, obtaining without prescription, using to get high, or potentiating other substances
- Physical dependence develops even at therapeutic doses; withdrawal is clinically distinct from the original indication
- Pregabalin is DEA Schedule V (US); gabapentin is unscheduled federally but scheduled in ≥15 US states as of 2024; UK rescheduled both Class C in 2019
- Rapidly expanding prescribing base (gabapentin among top 10 US prescriptions) has driven escalating diversion and misuse reports

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Bind to α2δ-1 and α2δ-2 subunits of voltage-gated calcium channels (VGCC), reducing presynaptic Ca²⁺ influx → attenuated release of glutamate, norepinephrine, substance P, and other neurotransmitters
- Do NOT directly activate GABA-A, GABA-B, or benzodiazepine receptors despite structural similarity to GABA
- Pregabalin has linear, nonsaturable intestinal absorption (bioavailability ~90%); gabapentin absorption is saturable and dose-dependent (bioavailability 27–60%), meaning pregabalin produces more predictable dose-response curves — a key abuse-liability differentiator
- Faster onset and higher CNS penetration of pregabalin explains greater euphorigenic potential at equivalent doses; gabapentin misuse rates in the US are paradoxically higher due to greater availability and less regulated prescribing
- Dependence mechanism: chronic α2δ downregulation and compensatory upregulation of excitatory neurotransmission; abrupt cessation unmasks hyperexcitability (seizure risk, anxiety, hallucinations)
- No established receptor-level reward pathway (dopamine/opioid), but gabapentinoids enhance opioid-induced euphoria by potentiating descending inhibition and CNS depression synergistically

## Clinical Picture
- Misuse prevalence: 13.8% of gabapentin users and 14.8% of pregabalin users report misuse; general population rates ~4.4% and ~2.8% respectively (Schifano et al., Drug Safety 2020)
- Highest-risk population: opioid use disorder — up to 22% of patients on opioid maintenance therapy concurrently misuse gabapentinoids; people who inject drugs show even higher rates
- Withdrawal syndrome: anxiety, insomnia, diaphoresis, tremor, nausea, palpitations, psychosis, auditory hallucinations, and seizures; onset within 12–24 hours of last dose; can persist >10 days
- 2025 FDA label update added explicit warning for withdrawal seizures, depression, suicidal ideation, and psychotic symptoms after both short- and long-term use
- Motives: euphoria/getting high, potentiating opioids or alcohol, self-treating anxiety/insomnia/pain/opioid withdrawal symptoms, substituting for controlled substances

## Evidence & Treatment
- No FDA-approved pharmacotherapy for gabapentinoid withdrawal; gradual taper (10–25% dose reduction per week) is standard of care; benzodiazepines used adjunctively for seizure prophylaxis in severe cases
- Opioid synergy lethality: gabapentinoids increase opioid overdose mortality risk by 49–60% in observational studies; mechanism is additive CNS/respiratory depression without reversal by naloxone
- Prescribing guidelines (CDC 2022, FDA 2019 safety communication) recommend caution co-prescribing with opioids/CNS depressants, particularly in patients with SUD history or respiratory compromise
- Behavioral treatments (CBT, contingency management) have minimal direct evidence specific to gabapentinoid use disorder; SUD treatment integrated with underlying pain/anxiety management is recommended
- Buprenorphine and methadone for comorbid OUD may reduce gabapentinoid co-use indirectly; no RCT data specifically targeting gabapentinoid dependence as a primary endpoint (as of 2025)

## Key Facts
- Pregabalin bioavailability: ~90% (linear); gabapentin: 27–60% (dose-dependent, saturable)
- US gabapentin prescriptions: ~70 million/year as of 2023 — among top 10 dispensed drugs
- Opioid overdose mortality risk increase with concurrent gabapentinoid: +49–60%
- Withdrawal seizures can occur after as little as 3 weeks of therapeutic use; risk increases with doses >900 mg/day gabapentin equivalent
- UK reclassified both gabapentin and pregabalin to Class C controlled substances in April 2019; US federal scheduling remains only pregabalin (Schedule V), with state-level gabapentin scheduling expanding
