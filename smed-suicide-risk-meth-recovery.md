# Suicide Risk in Methamphetamine Use Disorder Recovery

## Epidemiology

MUD carries 3.9× higher all-cause mortality vs. general population. Suicide accounts for ~15–20% of SUD deaths. Meth-specific data: suicidal ideation in 25–35% of withdrawal-presenting patients (C-SSRS-confirmed in recent case-control studies; Frontiers Public Health, 2025). Peak risk window: **days 3–14** of acute withdrawal — when neurochemical depletion is maximal and psychosocial protective factors are depleted. Anhedonia and dysphoria can persist 12–24 months (protracted abstinence syndrome), creating a secondary elevated-risk phase.

## Mechanisms

| Pathway | Effect | Suicide mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| DA depletion (D2/D3 downregulation) | Profound anhedonia, emotional flatness | Hopelessness — "nothing will ever feel good" |
| NE depletion | Motivation deficit, fatigue | Cannot activate coping skills even if present |
| 5-HT disruption | Impulsivity | Impulsive suicidality >> planned in stimulant SUD |
| Sleep dysregulation | Hypersomnia → fragmented sleep | Sleep disturbance is independent suicide risk factor |
| Cognitive impairment | Poor executive function | Cannot problem-solve acute life stressors |

**Key clinical insight:** Stimulant withdrawal suicidality is often *impulsive*, not plan-driven. Risk windows may be brief but lethal. Continuous monitoring > periodic.

## Meth-Specific Amplifiers

- **Active psychosis (intoxication phase):** Command hallucinations, paranoid ideation ("cornered") → impulsive self-harm
- **High-dose binge pattern:** More severe dysphoric crash; faster DA depletion
- **Concurrent opioid use:** Independent suicide risk multiplier; also elevates means lethality
- **Chronic use >1 year:** Visual hallucinations emerge; additional psychosis-pathway risk (2025 case-control data)

## Risk Assessment Tools

**First-line:** Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) — validated for SUD populations; captures ideation intensity and behavior history separately. Use at intake and every 48–72 h during acute withdrawal.

**Quick screen (ED/intake):** Ask Suicide-Screening Questions (ASQ) — 4-item, <2 min.

**Ongoing adjunct:** PHQ-9 Item 9 — brief but misses severity gradients; supplement with C-SSRS.

**Document systematically:**
- Ideation type: passive ("I wish I were dead") vs. active (intent, plan)
- Plan specificity and means access
- History of prior attempts (strongest single predictor)
- Current protective factors (reasons for living, social support, children, religion)
- Intoxication/withdrawal state at time of assessment

## Risk Stratification

| Level | Profile | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **High** | Active ideation + plan + means access + intent | Inpatient psychiatric stabilization |
| **Moderate** | Active ideation, no plan OR no means access | IOP or PHP with daily contact + safety plan |
| **Low** | Passive ideation, protective factors present | Outpatient + completed safety plan + 48-h follow-up |

## Safety Planning (Stanley-Brown SPI)

Complete the plan *during* the encounter — do not simply hand a hotline card. Six steps:

1. **Warning signs** — internal states signaling crisis onset (e.g., "isolating, not sleeping, craving intensifies")
2. **Internal coping strategies** — solo actions (walking, breathing, distraction) that buy time
3. **Social distractions** — people/settings that reduce rumination (not specifically for crisis disclosure)
4. **People to contact for support** — named individuals willing to be called
5. **Professionals and crisis lines** — clinician direct line, 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, nearest ED
6. **Means restriction** — document and secure or remove (see below)

## Means Restriction Counseling

- **Firearms:** Most lethal method; most associated with completion. Counsel temporary removal to trusted third party or trigger lock. Frame as temporary, not permanent.
- **Drug supply:** Large meth quantity available = means for intentional overdose. Limit take-home amounts during high-risk window; involve support person.
- **Prescription medications:** Prescribe minimum necessary quantities (opioids, benzodiazepines, stimulants) during acute withdrawal phase.
- **Lock box:** Low-barrier intervention; provide concrete referral to obtain one.

## Pharmacotherapy Notes

- **Bupropion:** FDA black box warning for suicidality in patients <24; weigh against DA/NE agonism benefit for craving/mood. Use cautiously in high-ideation adolescents/young adults.
- **SSRIs:** Not first-line for isolated meth-withdrawal dysphoria (neurochemical, not persistent MDD). If MDD confirmed at 3–4 weeks of abstinence and suicidality persists, initiate with close follow-up.
- **Mirtazapine:** Modest evidence for meth use reduction; sedating properties may help sleep dysregulation component of risk.
- **Antipsychotics:** Indicated for meth-induced psychosis contributing to suicide risk; haloperidol or olanzapine acutely.

## Documentation Checklist

- [ ] C-SSRS score and ideation category documented
- [ ] Means access assessed and counseling provided
- [ ] Safety plan completed (not just offered)
- [ ] Disposition rationale recorded
- [ ] Follow-up contact scheduled within 48–72 h for moderate risk
