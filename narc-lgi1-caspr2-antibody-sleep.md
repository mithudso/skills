# LGI1 & CASPR2 Antibody-Associated Sleep Disorders

Two autoimmune encephalitis syndromes cause prominent sleep-wake disruption and belong in the secondary narcolepsy differential, distinct from anti-Ma2.

---

## LGI1 Antibody Syndrome

**Epidemiology:** Most common autoimmune limbic encephalitis in adults; peak age 60–70; 2–3:1 male predominance; rarely paraneoplastic (<10%).

**Pathognomonic feature — faciobrachial dystonic seizures (FBDS):** Brief (<3 s), stereotyped unilateral face-and-arm jerks, up to 100×/day. Precede encephalitis by weeks to months; respond far better to immunotherapy than to AEDs. FBDS + hyponatremia in a middle-aged male is nearly diagnostic before antibody results return.

**Sleep disturbances:**
- REM sleep behavior disorder (RBD) in ~20–40%
- Hypersomnia / excessive daytime sleepiness
- Insomnia, sleep reversal, reduced total sleep time
- Reduced N3 and REM % on PSG; decreased sleep efficiency
- LGI1 is expressed in hypothalamic neurons; hypothalamic dysfunction contributes to RBD and sleep-cycle disruption

**CSF orexin:** Rarely deficient — primary damage is limbic/hippocampal, not lateral hypothalamic. This distinguishes LGI1 from type 1 narcolepsy and anti-Ma2 encephalitis (where orexin loss is common).

**Other features:** Amnesia, confusion, temporal FLAIR signal on MRI, hyponatremia (SIADH in ~60%).

**Treatment:** IV methylprednisolone ± IVIG or plasmapheresis; early immunotherapy predicts better seizure and cognitive outcomes.

---

## CASPR2 Antibody Syndrome / Morvan Syndrome

**Epidemiology:** Rare; older males; thymoma in >50% (paraneoplastic in majority).

**Core triad of Morvan syndrome:** Peripheral nerve hyperexcitability (neuromyotonia, cramps, fasciculations) + autonomic instability + agrypnia excitata.

**Agrypnia excitata:** Near-total loss of sleep — insomnia affects ~90% of CASPR2 Morvan cases. PSG shows near-complete absence of sleep architecture with superimposed oneiric (dream-enacting) behaviors, autonomic surges (tachycardia, hyperhidrosis, hyperthermia), and visual hallucinations. Mechanistically similar to fatal familial insomnia (thalamo-limbic hyperactivation).

**Key contrast with LGI1:** Insomnia (not hypersomnia) is the dominant sleep symptom. EDS / hypersomnia is atypical. This polarity alone helps triage.

**Treatment:** Immunotherapy (IVIG, steroids, plasmapheresis) + thymoma resection when present. Sleep frequently improves with immunotherapy.

## Differential Relevance: Post-Stimulant EDS Workup

| Feature | LGI1 | CASPR2 / Morvan | Anti-Ma2 | Post-Meth Secondary Narcolepsy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary sleep sx | Hypersomnia / RBD | Insomnia (agrypnia) | Hypersomnia / RBD | Hypersomnia / cataplexy-like |
| CSF orexin | Usually normal | Usually normal | Often low | Often low |
| Seizure type | FBDS (pathognomonic) | Neuromyotonia | Absent/rare | Absent |
| Autonomic | Mild (SIADH) | Prominent | Mild | Mild |
| Tumor | Rare (<10%) | Thymoma >50% | Testicular / ovarian | None |
| Age/sex | 60–70 y/o, male | Older male | Young male | Variable |

**When to test (autoimmune encephalitis panel, serum + CSF):**
- New sleep-wake disturbance + cognitive symptoms + seizures in middle-aged/elderly, especially male
- FBDS on history (even without encephalitis yet)
- Stimulant history doesn't fully explain severity or time-course
- Subacute onset over days–weeks (not the chronic gradual decline of stimulant-related damage)

**Workup:** Serum and CSF LGI1-IgG, CASPR2-IgG (VGKC-complex panel), Ma2-IgG; MRI brain with FLAIR (mesial temporal signal); PSG; CSF orexin if hypersomnia present; CT chest/abdomen-pelvis for thymoma.
