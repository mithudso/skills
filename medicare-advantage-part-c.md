# Medicare Advantage (Part C)

> **Educational only, NOT insurance, medical, or financial advice.**
> Plan availability, premiums, and networks vary by county and change annually.
> Verify at **[medicare.gov/plan-compare](https://www.medicare.gov/plan-compare)**
> during enrollment periods.
> **verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

Medicare Advantage (MA) is a **private-insurance alternative to Original Medicare**
offered by CMS-contracted insurers. Instead of getting coverage directly from
the federal government through Parts A and B, you get coverage through a private
plan that must cover at least everything Original Medicare covers — and often more.

---

## Part 1 — How Medicare Advantage works

When you enroll in a Medicare Advantage plan:
- You still have Medicare (Parts A and B) but receive coverage **through the
  private plan** rather than directly from the government.
- You **must still pay your Part B premium** ($202.90/month standard in 2026).
  The MA plan may charge an additional supplemental premium, often $0–$18/month
  on average, or may offer a Part B premium reduction.
- The federal government pays the plan a monthly **risk-adjusted capitation
  payment** on your behalf; the plan becomes responsible for your healthcare costs.
- Plans **must cover all Medicare-required services** (A+B scope); they earn
  profit by managing costs and/or offer extra benefits using their capitation.
- Most MA plans also include **Part D prescription drug coverage** (called
  MA-PD plans); if you join an MA plan without drug coverage, you may still
  need to enroll in a standalone Part D plan.

## Part 2 — Plan types

| Type | In-network only? | Referral required? | Typical premium | Notes |
|------|-----------------|-------------------|-----------------|-------|
| **HMO** | Yes (emergency excepted) | Usually yes (via PCP) | Lowest (~$12/mo avg in 2026) | Most popular; ~61% of MA enrollees |
| **PPO** | No (higher cost out-of-network) | No | Higher (~$18/mo avg in 2026) | ~38% of MA enrollees; more flexibility |
| **PFFS** (Private Fee-for-Service) | Providers must accept plan's terms | Usually not | Varies | Less common; generally higher cost |
| **SNP** (Special Needs Plan) | Varies | Varies | Varies | Targeted to specific populations (D-SNP for duals, C-SNP for chronic conditions, I-SNP for institutionalized) |
| **Regional PPO** | No | No | Varies | Covers larger geographic region; <1% of enrollees |

> **HMO key risk:** if you go out of network (except emergencies), the plan
> pays nothing. Verify your doctors are in-network before enrolling.

## Part 3 — Key differences from Original Medicare

| Feature | Original Medicare (A+B) | Medicare Advantage |
|---------|------------------------|-------------------|
| Provider freedom | Any Medicare-accepting provider nationwide | Usually within plan network |
| Prior authorization | Generally not required (fee-for-service) | Often required for procedures, specialists, DME, drugs |
| Out-of-pocket maximum | **No cap** — unlimited exposure | Legally required; max is $9,350/year in-network (2026 limit; actual plans often lower) |
| Extra benefits | None (dental/vision/hearing not covered) | Often includes dental, vision, hearing, transportation, fitness, OTC allowance |
| Medigap eligibility | Medigap can supplement A+B gaps | **Cannot use Medigap with MA** |
| Prescription drugs | Need separate Part D plan | Usually bundled (MA-PD) |
| Drug negotiation | CMS negotiates some; Part D covers rest | Plan formulary applies |

### Out-of-pocket maximum (OOP cap)
Original Medicare has **no annual out-of-pocket limit** — costs for extended
hospitalizations or costly treatments are unbounded (Medigap is needed to cap
them). Medicare Advantage is legally required to impose an OOP cap. In 2026:
- In-network max: capped at **$9,350/year** by CMS
- Combined in/out-of-network max: capped at **$14,000/year**
- Individual plans can — and often do — set lower limits

## Part 4 — Prior authorization: 2026 rules

Prior authorization (PA) is a major practical difference from Original Medicare.
MA plans require PA for many services: specialist referrals (HMOs), certain
procedures, imaging, hospital admissions, DME, and many drugs.

**2026 regulatory changes to MA prior authorization:** [^2]
- **Standard PA decisions:** must be issued within **7 calendar days**
- **Expedited (urgent) PA decisions:** within **72 hours**
- **Approved authorizations** remain valid for the **entire course of treatment**,
  even if your provider leaves the plan's network or you switch plans mid-year
- Emergency services are **always exempt** from prior authorization under
  federal law — any MA plan must cover emergency care at any U.S. hospital
  regardless of network status, without advance approval

> **Practical implication:** track all PA approvals in writing. An approved PA
> for ongoing treatment cannot be rescinded mid-course simply because your
> doctor left the network.

## Part 5 — Extra / supplemental benefits

A major MA enrollment driver is extra benefits not in Original Medicare:
- **Dental** — routine cleanings, X-rays; some plans cover major dental work
- **Vision** — routine eye exams; eyeglass/contact lens allowance
- **Hearing** — hearing aids (a major gap in Original Medicare)
- **Fitness** (SilverSneakers or equivalent)
- **Transportation** — rides to medical appointments
- **Over-the-counter (OTC) allowance** — quarterly credit for OTC items
- **Meals** after hospitalization; home safety modifications in some plans
- **Telehealth** beyond Original Medicare coverage

> Benefits vary widely by plan and county. Verify what a specific plan covers
> before enrolling; benefits that sound generous may have caps and restrictions.

## Part 6 — D-SNPs: Medicare Advantage for dual eligibles

**Dual Eligible Special Needs Plans (D-SNPs)** are a type of MA plan specifically
for people enrolled in both Medicare and Medicaid. Key features:
- Coordinates Medicare + Medicaid benefits into one plan (one ID card, shared
  care plan, aligned providers)
- Often features **$0 premium**, $0 or very low copays
- Extra benefits targeting dual-eligible needs (transportation, OTC, dental)
- **2026 change:** full-benefit dual eligibles can enroll in or switch between
  integrated D-SNPs on a **monthly** basis (not just during annual elections)
- D-SNP PPO cost-sharing limits tightened in 2026 to reduce out-of-network
  cost burden

See `medicare-medicaid-dual-eligibility` for the full dual-eligibility framework.

## Part 7 — Enrollment periods

| Period | When | Who can use it |
|--------|------|---------------|
| **ICEP** (Initial Coverage Election Period) | Begins 3 months before 65th birthday; ends last day of IEP or month before Part A+B effective | New to Medicare |
| **AEP** (Annual Election Period) | Oct 15 – Dec 7 each year | Anyone; changes effective Jan 1 |
| **OEP / MA OEP** (Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period) | Jan 1 – Mar 31 | Current MA enrollees only; switch to another MA plan or return to Original Medicare |
| **SEP** (Special Enrollment Period) | Various triggers | Losing other coverage, moving, plan termination, qualifying life events |
| **D-SNP monthly SEP** (2025+) | Any month | Full-benefit dual eligibles; enroll/switch integrated D-SNPs |

> **Medigap open enrollment interplay:** if you disenroll from MA and return to
> Original Medicare outside your Medigap OEP (the 6-month window at age 65 +
> Part B), you may face medical underwriting to buy Medigap — insurers can
> charge more or deny coverage based on health status in most states.
> See `medicare-medigap-supplement-plans`.

## Part 8 — Original Medicare vs. Medicare Advantage: the decision framework

Neither is universally "better" — the right choice depends on your situation:

| If you value… | Consider… |
|---------------|-----------|
| Freedom to see any specialist nationwide without referrals | Original Medicare + Medigap |
| Guaranteed predictable costs (no surprise OOP) | Original Medicare + Plan G Medigap |
| Lower upfront premium cost | Medicare Advantage (many $0-premium plans) |
| Extra benefits (dental/vision/hearing) | Medicare Advantage |
| Travel frequently or have providers in multiple cities | Original Medicare |
| Managing chronic conditions with coordinated care | C-SNP or D-SNP MA plan |
| Living in a rural area with limited MA plan options | Original Medicare |

> **Key risk of MA:** if you develop a serious illness requiring frequent
> specialist care or out-of-network providers, PA burdens and network limits
> can become significant. The OOP cap protects catastrophically, but the path
> to reaching it can be administratively burdensome.
