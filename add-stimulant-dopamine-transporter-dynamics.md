# Stimulant DAT/NET Mechanisms

## Core Concepts
- **DAT (dopamine transporter)** and **NET (norepinephrine transporter)** are SLC6-family reuptake proteins; primary targets of all clinically relevant stimulants
- Cocaine = **competitive reuptake blocker** (not transported); amphetamines = **transporter substrates** that drive reverse efflux
- **VMAT2** (vesicular monoamine transporter-2) is the intracellular target unique to amphetamine-class drugs; cocaine does not interact with it
- Stimulant pharmacology separates into two mechanistic classes: blockers (cocaine, methylphenidate) vs. releasers (amphetamine, methamphetamine, MDMA)
- NET blockade elevates synaptic norepinephrine, contributing to cardiovascular effects (tachycardia, hypertension) and arousal independent of DAT

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **Cocaine DAT blockade:** binds DAT at Ki ~0.2–0.6 μM; not transported across membrane; traps dopamine in synapse by preventing reuptake; chronic use triggers compensatory DAT upregulation (increased receptor density), worsening hypo-dopaminergic withdrawal
- **Amphetamine reverse transport (efflux):** enters neuron via DAT/NET; alkalinizes vesicle pH via VMAT2, collapsing the proton gradient → dopamine floods cytoplasm; elevated cytosolic dopamine then drives DAT to run in reverse, pumping DA into the synapse non-exocytotically
- **VMAT2 redistribution:** therapeutic amphetamine doses selectively redistribute VMAT2 to cytoplasm; methamphetamine at high doses inhibits VMAT2 more aggressively, leaving unsequestered cytosolic dopamine exposed to oxidative metabolism
- **Dopamine auto-oxidation and neurotoxicity (meth-specific):** excess cytosolic DA oxidizes to dopamine quinones and generates reactive oxygen species (ROS); meth also inhibits mitochondrial complex I and II, amplifying oxidative stress; D1/D2 receptor activation required for full neurotoxic cascade (D1 antagonists attenuate striatal DAT/TH loss in animal models)
- **NET parallel effects:** amphetamine and methamphetamine are equal or higher-affinity NET substrates vs. DAT; NET-mediated norepinephrine efflux drives hyperthermia, which potentiates meth neurotoxicity — temperature lowering is neuroprotective in animal models

## Clinical Picture
- Cocaine half-life ~1 hour; amphetamine ~10 hours; methamphetamine ~10–12 hours — longer duration amplifies cumulative dopamine depletion with binge use
- Post-acute meth abstinence: striatal DAT density reduced ~20–30% vs. controls by PET imaging (raclopride/[11C]WIN); correlates with anhedonia severity and relapse risk
- DAT recovery with meth abstinence: PET studies show partial normalization at 12–17 months of abstinence, suggesting functional (not purely structural) depletion in many users; TH loss may be more persistent
- Stimulant-use disorder prevalence: ~5 million cocaine users and ~2.5 million methamphetamine users in the US (NSDUH 2023); ~1.5% of US adults meet SUD criteria for stimulants
- Dopamine D2 receptor density reduced ~10–15% in chronic stimulant users (PET); reduced D2 predicts relapse at 6 months in meth-dependent individuals

## Evidence & Treatment
- **No FDA-approved pharmacotherapy** for cocaine or meth use disorder as of 2024; contrast with opioid/alcohol SUD where agonist therapies exist
- **Contingency management (CM):** highest evidence base for stimulant SUD; RCTs show ~50–60% abstinence rates during active incentive periods vs. ~20% control; strongest effect sizes in meth > cocaine
- **Methylphenidate/extended-release amphetamine:** modest benefit in cocaine SUD (reduces use frequency ~30% in meta-analyses); paradoxically, DAT occupancy from therapeutic doses may blunt cocaine's rewarding blockade
- **TAAR1 agonists (e.g., solriamfetol, ulotaront):** emerging target — TAAR1 activation dampens dopamine neuron firing without direct DAT interaction; Phase 2 trials ongoing for meth SUD
- **Bupropion + naltrexone combination:** FDA breakthrough therapy designation 2020 for meth SUD; Phase 3 iCON trial showed ~13% increase in abstinence rate vs. placebo over 8 weeks in injection meth users

## Key Facts
- Cocaine Ki at DAT ~0.2–0.6 μM; methamphetamine Ki at DAT ~0.1 μM — meth is a higher-affinity DAT substrate
- Amphetamine-induced DA efflux is blocked by cocaine (DAT inhibition) and by VMAT2 inhibitors (tetrabenazine), confirming dual vesicular + transporter mechanism
- Meth neurotoxicity requires hyperthermia: core body temperature >40°C dramatically increases striatal dopamine terminal loss; normothermia is neuroprotective
- DAT upregulation in chronic cocaine users reverses within weeks of abstinence (functional); meth-induced TH (tyrosine hydroxylase) loss may persist >1 year
- D1 receptor antagonists (SCH23390) prevent methamphetamine-induced striatal DAT and TH depletion in rodent models, implicating downstream signaling (not just direct oxidative damage) in neurotoxicity
