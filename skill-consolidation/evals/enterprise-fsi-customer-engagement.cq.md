# enterprise-fsi-customer-engagement — competency questions

Concept family: selling to, satisfying, and managing the relationship with a large FSI enterprise (JPMorgan Chase), incl. buyer psychology, vendor management, POC motion, FSI vertical/regulation, and big-bank IT. Written by concept-family-explorer (Step 1). Coverage-saturation (Step 8) requires each question to resolve to a HAVE or researched node.

- Who sits on a big bank's buying committee for a data-platform decision, what role does each play (economic buyer / champion / blocker / gatekeeper), and what does each need to say yes? → S1
- Why do enterprise buyers default to the incumbent or "no decision," and how do switching costs and vendor-lock-in fear shape a competitive displacement? → S1 (G9)
- What does a bank's third-party risk management (TPRM) process require of a vendor like MongoDB before and during an engagement — tiering, SIG/CAIQ, ongoing monitoring? → S2
- How do DORA, the OCC/FFIEC third-party guidance, and the UK critical-third-party regime change what an FSI customer must contract for around a cloud/data vendor? → S2 (G10)
- Which regulations most shape how a big bank evaluates and buys infrastructure software, and how do I speak to them credibly without overclaiming? → S3
- How should I scope a POC at a regulated bank so it converts to production — success/exit criteria, environment, data access, mutual action plan? → S4
- Where do a bank's core-banking/mainframe estate and cloud-migration posture create openings or objections for a modern data platform? → S5
- What is JPMorgan Chase's technology organization, budget, and cloud/AI strategy, and who are the relevant decision-makers? → S6
- What actually drives a CIO-level account's satisfaction and loyalty beyond a CSAT score, and how do I measure and move it (Kano, SERVQUAL, effort, expectancy-disconfirmation)? → S7
