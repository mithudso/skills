# Dopamine Neuroscience

## Core Concepts
- Dopamine (DA) is a catecholamine neurotransmitter synthesized from L-tyrosine; functions as a "teaching signal" for reward prediction, motivation, and movement initiation
- The **four major dopaminergic pathways** serve distinct functions: reward/motivation, movement, cognition, and hormonal regulation — dysfunction in each produces distinct clinical syndromes
- Dopaminergic pharmacology spans both **endogenous modulation** (DAT/VMAT2/MAO targets) and **receptor-level pharmacology** (D1-D5 agonism/antagonism)
- **Dopamine agonists** are FDA-approved for Parkinson's disease, restless legs syndrome, and hyperprolactinemia; all are under investigation for addiction
- **Dopamine depletion** underlies Parkinson's disease, drug-induced movement disorders, and the crash phase of stimulant withdrawal

## Synthesis and Life Cycle

**Biosynthesis**
1. L-Tyrosine → L-DOPA: catalyzed by **tyrosine hydroxylase (TH)** — rate-limiting step; TH requires tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4) as cofactor; inhibited by catecholamine product feedback
2. L-DOPA → Dopamine: catalyzed by **DOPA decarboxylase** (aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase, AADC); requires pyridoxal phosphate (B6)
3. Optional further conversion: DA → Norepinephrine (DBH, dopamine-β-hydroxylase, in NE neurons) → Epinephrine (PNMT, in adrenal medulla)

**Storage**
- Dopamine packaged into synaptic vesicles by **VMAT2** (vesicular monoamine transporter 2); VMAT2 is the pharmacological target of tetrabenazine/valbenazine (tardive dyskinesia treatment) and was depleted by reserpine (historical antihypertensive → depressive side effect)
- VMAT2 inhibition → redistributes DA from vesicles to cytoplasm → MAO-mediated metabolism → net DA depletion

**Release**
- Action potential → Ca²⁺-dependent exocytosis; kiss-and-run or full fusion with plasma membrane
- **Autoreceptors:** D2 receptors on presynaptic neuron (somatodendritic and terminal) — sense extracellular DA and feedback-inhibit synthesis (via TH phosphorylation) and release; a major target for antipsychotic side effects

**Reuptake**
- **DAT (dopamine transporter, SLC6A3):** primary mechanism for synaptic DA clearance; secondary active transport (coupled to Na+/Cl−); major target of cocaine (competitive inhibitor), amphetamines (reversal of transport direction, releasing DA), and methylphenidate (competitive inhibitor)
- High DAT density in striatum (nigrostriatal pathway); lower in PFC where DA diffusion and COMT metabolism predominate

**Degradation**
- **MAO-B** (intraneuronal, abundant in astrocytes): oxidative deamination of cytoplasmic DA → DOPAC (3,4-dihydroxyphenylacetic acid) → HVA; MAO-B inhibitors (selegiline, rasagiline) used in Parkinson's
- **COMT** (extracellular, especially in PFC): methylates DA → 3-MT → HVA; COMT val158met polymorphism (high-COMT "warrior" → lower DA in PFC, risk for schizophrenia/poor working memory); COMT inhibitors (entacapone, tolcapone) adjunctive in Parkinson's
- Final common metabolite: **HVA (homovanillic acid)** — measurable in CSF (reduced in Parkinson's, schizophrenia)

## Four Dopaminergic Pathways

| Pathway | Origin → Projection | Function | Dysfunction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mesolimbic** | VTA → Nucleus accumbens (NAc), amygdala, hippocampus | Reward, motivation, salience, reinforcement learning | Hyperdopaminergic: addiction, psychosis positive symptoms; Hypodopaminergic: anhedonia, depression |
| **Nigrostriatal** | Substantia nigra pars compacta (SNc) → Striatum (caudate, putamen) | Voluntary movement, motor coordination, procedural learning | Parkinson's disease (70-80% SNc neuron loss), tardive dyskinesia, drug-induced parkinsonism |
| **Mesocortical** | VTA → Prefrontal cortex (PFC), anterior cingulate | Working memory, executive function, cognitive flexibility, attention | Hypodopaminergic: negative symptoms of schizophrenia, ADHD, meth-related cognitive impairment |
| **Tuberoinfundibular** | Arcuate nucleus (hypothalamus) → Anterior pituitary | Tonic inhibition of prolactin release | D2 antagonism (antipsychotics) → hyperprolactinemia → galactorrhea, amenorrhea, sexual dysfunction |

## Dopamine Receptors (D1–D5)

**D1-like family** (D1, D5): Gs-coupled → adenylyl cyclase activation → ↑ cAMP → PKA → downstream phosphorylation
- **D1:** most abundant DA receptor; postsynaptic; striatum, PFC; role in reward salience, motor activity, working memory (optimal DA level required — inverted U function)
- **D5:** lower abundance; PFC, hippocampus; similar function to D1

**D2-like family** (D2, D3, D4): Gi/Go-coupled → adenylyl cyclase inhibition → ↓ cAMP; also activate GIRK channels and inhibit Ca²⁺ channels
- **D2:** presynaptic autoreceptor (D2S short isoform) + postsynaptic (D2L long isoform); target of all antipsychotics; mesolimbic/nigrostriatal/tuberoinfundibular distribution; D2 short variant = high-affinity autoreceptor → feedback inhibition
- **D3:** limbic distribution (NAc, olfactory tubercle); low expression; reward, impulse control; cariprazine has high D3 affinity; target for addiction pharmacotherapy research
- **D4:** low abundance; PFC, retina; D4.7 variant associated with ADHD; clozapine high affinity (vs. typical antipsychotics); lower EPS than other D2-like targets

## Dopaminergics as a Drug Class

Classes of drugs modifying the dopamine system:

| Drug class | Mechanism | Examples | Primary clinical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dopamine precursors** | Bypasses TH rate-limit | Levodopa (+ carbidopa AADC inhibitor) | Parkinson's disease |
| **Direct DA agonists** | Receptor agonism (D2/D3) | Pramipexole, ropinirole, rotigotine, cabergoline, bromocriptine, apomorphine | Parkinson's, RLS, hyperprolactinemia |
| **Reuptake inhibitors** | Block DAT | Cocaine, methylphenidate, bupropion (weak) | ADHD, SUD (cocaine); antidepressant |
| **Releasing agents** | Reverse DAT; release DA from vesicles | Amphetamines, methamphetamine, MDMA | ADHD; abuse; SUD |
| **VMAT2 inhibitors** | Block vesicular storage → DA depletion | Tetrabenazine, valbenazine, deutetrabenazine | Tardive dyskinesia, Huntington's chorea |
| **MAO-B inhibitors** | Prevent DA breakdown | Selegiline, rasagiline, safinamide | Parkinson's adjunct; some evidence for depression |
| **COMT inhibitors** | Prevent DA methylation (especially in PFC) | Entacapone, tolcapone | Parkinson's adjunct (reduce "off" fluctuations) |
| **D2 antagonists** | Block postsynaptic D2 | Haloperidol, risperidone, all antipsychotics | Schizophrenia, bipolar, antiemetic |
| **D2 partial agonists** | Partial agonism at D2 | Aripiprazole, brexpiprazole, cariprazine | Schizophrenia, bipolar, adjunct MDD |

## Dopamine Agonists — Clinical Applications

**Parkinson's disease**
- Pramipexole (Mirapex), ropinirole (Requip), rotigotine transdermal patch (Neupro): D2/D3 agonists; first-line for younger patients (delay levodopa initiation → reduce motor complications); risk of **impulse control disorders** (ICDs: gambling, hypersexuality, binge eating) via D3 mesolimbic activation — major side effect requiring vigilance
- Apomorphine: potent D1/D2 agonist; SC injection or sublingual film for acute "off" episodes

**Restless legs syndrome (RLS)**
- Pramipexole and ropinirole: FDA-approved for moderate-severe RLS; paradox — chronic use causes **augmentation** (worsening/spreading of RLS symptoms) in up to 50% at 5 years; transitioning to alpha-2-delta ligands (gabapentin enacarbil) recommended for augmentation

**Hyperprolactinemia**
- Cabergoline (preferred, twice weekly), bromocriptine (daily): D2 agonists suppress prolactin secretion from pituitary lactotrophs; treatment of prolactinoma, medication-induced hyperprolactinemia; cabergoline also used off-label for Cushing's disease (D2 on corticotroph adenomas)

**Investigational: SUD**
- Pramipexole trials in cocaine/SUD: theoretically restore hypodopaminergic state; results inconsistent, generally negative — caution given ICD risk profile
- Cabergoline: small trials in cocaine/stimulant SUD; limited evidence

## Dopamine Depletion — Mechanisms and Consequences

**Parkinson's disease**
- Loss of dopaminergic neurons in substantia nigra pars compacta (SNc) — idiopathic in most; α-synuclein aggregation (Lewy bodies); gradual progression
- Symptoms emerge when 70-80% of SNc neurons lost; striatal DA falls below threshold for normal motor function → bradykinesia, rigidity, resting tremor, postural instability
- Later stages: non-motor symptoms from non-nigrostriatal dopamine depletion (depression, cognitive decline, REM sleep behavior disorder)

**Drug-induced dopamine depletion / movement disorders**
- **VMAT2 inhibitors** (tetrabenazine, valbenazine, deutetrabenazine): intentional therapeutic depletion of monoamines for tardive dyskinesia and Huntington chorea
- **Reserpine** (historical): irreversible VMAT2 inhibition → catecholamine depletion; caused severe depression — first molecular link between monoamine depletion and depression
- **Antipsychotics (D2 antagonism):** functional dopamine "block" in nigrostriatal pathway → drug-induced parkinsonism, tardive dyskinesia (from receptor upregulation)

**Stimulant withdrawal (hypodopaminergic crash)**
- Chronic meth/cocaine use → downregulation of DAT and D2 receptors, DA vesicle depletion; acute cessation → relative DA deficiency → profound dysphoria, anhedonia, fatigue (see meth-dopaminergic-neurotoxicity for meth-specific)
- Recovery of dopaminergic tone takes weeks to months (DAT neuroimaging shows partial recovery at 14 months post-meth)

**Dietary / nutritional depletion (mild)**
- L-Tyrosine depletion (from protein-poor diet) reduces DA synthesis; tyrosine/phenylalanine depletion protocol used experimentally to study DA function
- L-Tyrosine supplementation theoretically supports synthesis but clinical evidence for therapeutic benefit is limited

**Stress-induced dopamine dysregulation**
- Acute stress activates mesolimbic DA (salience signal); chronic stress depletes DA reserves and downregulates D1 signaling in PFC → anhedonia, cognitive impairment (overlap with depressive phenotype)
- **PTSD-SUD comorbidity:** shared dopamine dysregulation mechanism — stress-induced DA depletion drives craving and relapse

## Key Facts
- TH (tyrosine hydroxylase) is rate-limiting for dopamine synthesis; it is the target of product (catecholamine) feedback inhibition
- DAT density measured by DaTscan (SPECT imaging with ioflupane) — reduced in Parkinson's, DLB; preserved in essential tremor and drug-induced parkinsonism
- D2 receptor occupancy: 60-70% → therapeutic antipsychotic; >80% → EPS onset; this window is narrower for FGAs
- Impulse control disorders (ICDs) — gambling, hypersexuality, binge eating — occur in ~14% of Parkinson's patients on DA agonists; severity dose-dependent; resolve on drug discontinuation
- HVA (homovanillic acid) in CSF: reduced in Parkinson's and schizophrenia negative symptoms; elevated in acute psychosis
- Methamphetamine causes persistent DAT downregulation measurable by neuroimaging for 12-14 months post-use
