# Medicare Part D Prescription Drug Enrollment

## Eligibility

Any Medicare beneficiary enrolled in Part A and/or Part B may enroll in a Part D stand-alone Prescription Drug Plan (PDP) OR a Medicare Advantage Prescription Drug plan (MA-PD). Part D is optional but carries late enrollment penalties if delayed without creditable coverage.

## Enrollment Periods

| Period | Timing | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Initial Enrollment Period (IEP)** | 7-month window around 65th birthday (or Medicare due to disability) | Enroll in Part D without penalty |
| **Annual Enrollment Period (AEP)** | Oct 15 – Dec 7 | Switch, join, or drop a Part D plan; effective Jan 1 |
| **Special Enrollment Period (SEP)** | Triggered by qualifying event | Losing creditable coverage, gaining/losing Extra Help, moving, other qualifying events |
| **Medicare Advantage OEP** | Jan 1 – Mar 31 | If switching from MA-PD to Original Medicare, can enroll in stand-alone PDP |

## Creditable Coverage — The Key to Avoiding Penalties

**Creditable coverage** = drug coverage with actuarial value at least equal to standard Part D. Maintaining creditable coverage during the Part D IEP and afterward prevents late enrollment penalties.

Sources of creditable coverage:
- Employer/union group health plan (must be notified annually if creditable or not)
- VA drug benefits
- TRICARE
- FEHB plans
- PACE programs
- Other government programs

**The 63-day rule:** If you go 63+ consecutive days without Part D or creditable drug coverage after your IEP ends, you incur a late enrollment penalty when you eventually enroll.

## Late Enrollment Penalty (2026)

`Penalty = 1% × $38.99 (2026 base premium) × number of uncovered months`

The penalty is **lifetime** and added to your monthly Part D premium. Example: 24 months uncovered = 24% surcharge on the base premium, added permanently.

## How to Choose a Plan

1. Go to **medicare.gov/plan-compare** — enter your ZIP code and drugs
2. Compare plans by: total annual drug cost (premium + deductible + copays), formulary tier for your drugs, preferred pharmacy network
3. Check star ratings (CMS rates plans 1–5 annually)
4. Confirm your pharmacy is in the plan's preferred network

## 2026 Part D Redesign (Inflation Reduction Act)

- **Out-of-pocket cap: $2,100** — once you reach this amount in deductibles + coinsurance + copays, all covered drugs are free for the rest of the year
- **Catastrophic phase eliminated** — no more 5% coinsurance in catastrophic phase
- Manufacturer discounts count toward your out-of-pocket cap
- Monthly payment option available for high-cost drugs (Medicare Prescription Payment Plan)

## Low Income Subsidy / Extra Help Interaction

LIS/Extra Help beneficiaries are **auto-enrolled** in a benchmark Part D plan if they do not choose one. They can switch to any plan at any time (monthly SEP). See skill: `enrollment-eligibility-extra-help-lis` for eligibility and application.

## Benchmark Plans for Auto-Enrolled LIS Beneficiaries

CMS sets regional benchmark premiums annually. LIS recipients auto-enrolled in plans above the benchmark must pay the excess premium or switch to a $0-premium benchmark plan. CMS notifies affected enrollees in October each year with a plan change option.

## Common Enrollment Mistakes

- Assuming COBRA drug coverage is creditable — it is if the underlying employer plan is creditable; verify with plan
- Not enrolling in Part D when leaving COBRA because "COBRA has drug coverage" — COBRA ends, creating an involuntary gap
- Delaying Part D because VA drug coverage is primary — VA coverage IS creditable but veterans must still enroll in Part D when VA coverage ends to avoid penalty
- Missing the IEP entirely by not knowing it starts 3 months before the 65th birthday month
