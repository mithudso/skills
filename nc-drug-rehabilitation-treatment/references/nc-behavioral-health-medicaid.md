## 1. Overview — The NC Behavioral Health Medicaid Architecture

NC Medicaid behavioral health — including substance use disorder (SUD) treatment — is administered through a **managed care structure** since 2023. Two parallel managed care tracks cover most Medicaid members:

| Track | Who it covers | Who manages it |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard Plans** | General Medicaid population (physical health + some BH) | Commercial managed care organizations |
| **Tailored Plans** | Members with severe MH, SUD, IDD, or TBI | LME-MCOs (Alliance, Partners, Trillium, Vaya) |
| **Medicaid Direct (FFS)** | Certain exempt populations | NC DHHS directly |

SUD services for **members with severe SUD** are primarily covered through **Tailored Plans** (launched July 1, 2024). Members who do not meet Tailored Plan eligibility access SUD services through their Standard Plan or Medicaid Direct.[^1]

## 2. LME-MCO System — The Four Entities

**Local Management Entities / Managed Care Organizations (LME-MCOs)** are the regional authorities that administer both Medicaid behavioral health managed care (as Tailored Plans) and state-funded behavioral health services for uninsured/underinsured individuals in NC.[^1][^2]

**verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

| LME-MCO | Counties Served (approximate) |
|---|---|
| **Alliance Health** | Wake, Durham, Johnston, Chatham, Lee, Harnett, Orange, Person, Alamance, Cabarrus, Union, Mecklenburg, Rowan |
| **Partners Health Management** | Lincoln, Gaston, Cleveland, Iredell, Catawba, Burke, McDowell, Rutherford, Polk, Henderson, Transylvania, Buncombe, Madison, Yancey, Mitchell, Avery |
| **Trillium Health Resources** | Eastern NC — 24 counties including Wayne, Pitt, Lenoir, Jones, Craven, Onslow, Duplin, Sampson, Cumberland, Columbus, Brunswick, New Hanover, Pender |
| **Vaya Health** | Western NC — mountain and foothills counties including Cherokee, Clay, Graham, Macon, Jackson, Swain, Haywood, Henderson (partial), Forsyth, Guilford, Randolph, and others |

> Note: Following LME-MCO consolidations in early 2024, the number of entities was reduced from 7 to 4. The four remaining entities are the Tailored Plan operators.[^2]

**What LME-MCOs do:**
- Administer Tailored Plan Medicaid managed care for eligible members
- Manage state-funded SUD services for uninsured/underinsured members
- Process prior authorizations for behavioral health services
- Connect members to treatment providers and recovery supports
- Interface with NC Drug Treatment Courts (see `nc-drug-treatment-courts.md`)

## 3. Tailored Plans — Launched July 1, 2024

**Tailored Plans** are a specialized NC Medicaid managed care plan for approximately **210,000 members** with complex behavioral health or developmental needs.[^1]

### Eligibility Criteria

Members are enrolled in a Tailored Plan if they have one or more of:
- **Serious Mental Illness (SMI)** or Serious Emotional Disturbance (SED)
- **Severe Substance Use Disorder (SUD)**
- **Intellectual/Developmental Disability (IDD)**
- **Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI)**

Severity criteria are defined in Appendix B of the NC Medicaid Tailored Plan contract.

### What Tailored Plans Cover

Tailored Plans provide **comprehensive, whole-person care** integrating:
- Behavioral health services (MH and SUD)
- Physical health / primary care
- Pharmacy benefits
- Long-term services and supports (LTSS)
- Health-related resource needs (social determinants of health)

This integration is a core improvement over prior LME-MCO managed care — members no longer need separate plans for physical and behavioral health.[^1]

### Four Tailored Plan Operators

Alliance Health, Partners Health Management, Trillium Health Resources, and Vaya Health each operate Tailored Plans in their respective geographic service areas.

### Launch Flexibilities

For the first 91 days after launch (through September 30, 2024):
- Pharmacy prior authorization requirements were relaxed
- Medical prior authorization requirements were relaxed
- Existing active prior authorizations from NC Medicaid Direct were honored[^1]

## 4. ASAM Levels of Care in NC Medicaid

NC Medicaid covers the full continuum of **ASAM (American Society of Addiction Medicine) levels of care** for SUD treatment, organized under Clinical Coverage Policies (CCPs).[^3]

**verified-as-of: 2026-06-22** (1115 Waiver-aligned CCPs effective January 1, 2026)

| ASAM Level | NC Medicaid Service Name | CCP | Hours/Setting |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0.5** | Early Intervention | — | Brief assessment and intervention |
| **1.0** | Outpatient Services | 8C | Standard outpatient, <9 hrs/week |
| **2.1** | Intensive Outpatient (IOP) | 8A-12 (SAIOP) | 9–19 hrs/week (adults); 6–19 hrs/week (adolescents) |
| **2.5** | Partial Hospitalization | 8A-13 (SACOT) | ≥20 hrs/week, clinically intensive |
| **3.1** | Clinically Managed Low-Intensity Residential | 8D | 24-hour residential, low-intensity clinical services |
| **3.5** | Clinically Managed High-Intensity Residential | 8D | 24-hour residential, high-intensity programming |
| **3.7** | Medically Monitored Intensive Inpatient | 8D-6 | 24-hour non-hospital with medical monitoring |
| **4.0** | Medically Managed Intensive Inpatient (hospital) | — | Hospital-based; requires medical necessity |

**Key definitions:**

**SAIOP (Substance Abuse Intensive Outpatient Program)** — ASAM 2.1. Structured program of skilled treatment with group and individual therapy. 9–19 hours per week for adults; 6–19 hours per week for adolescents. Available for ages 12 and older.[^3]

**SACOT (Substance Abuse Comprehensive Outpatient Treatment)** — ASAM 2.5. Clinically intensive partial hospitalization program. 20 or more hours per week. Available for ages 18 and older.[^3]

**Medically Monitored Intensive Inpatient** (formerly "Substance Abuse Medically Monitored Community Residential Treatment") — ASAM 3.7. For members whose biomedical and emotional, behavioral, or cognitive problems are so severe that they require subacute inpatient treatment with intensive medical monitoring.[^3]

## 5. NC 1115 SUD Demonstration Waiver — Updated Service Array (2026)

NC operates an **1115 Substance Use Disorder (SUD) Demonstration Waiver** approved by CMS, which allows NC Medicaid to:
- Cover certain SUD services in residential treatment settings (previously excluded from Medicaid as "institutions for mental disease")
- Align the NC Medicaid SUD benefit array with ASAM Criteria, 3rd edition
- Cover new service types including mobile medication units

### 2026 CCP Updates Under the Waiver

Effective January 1, 2026, NC Medicaid finalized standalone SUD Clinical Coverage Policies aligned with ASAM Criteria:[^3][^4]

- **New/updated service types:** Clinically Managed Low-Intensity Residential Treatment (ASAM 3.1), Clinically Managed High-Intensity Residential, Clinically Managed Residential Withdrawal Management, Medically Monitored Intensive Inpatient Services (ASAM 3.7), SACOT (CCP 8A-13), SAIOP (CCP 8A-12)
- **OTP services:** Mobile medication units now approved as a place of service under CCP 8A-9 (Opioid Treatment Program)
- **Withdrawal management:** New dedicated CCPs for withdrawal management at multiple ASAM levels

## 6. Prior Authorization for SUD Services

**Prior authorization (PA)** is required for most SUD services covered by NC Medicaid managed care (Tailored Plans and Standard Plans).[^4]

### MHPAEA-Compliant PA Rules (as of January 1, 2025)

Under NC Medicaid's MHPAEA compliance framework:
- For SUD/MH benefits with an applicable State CCP, the plan's PA requirements may be **no more restrictive** than what the CCP specifies.
- PA and concurrent review requirements must be applied **comparably to medical/surgical services**.

### PA Restoration Timeline

- July 1, 2024: Tailored Plans launched with relaxed PA requirements (first 91 days).
- October 1, 2024: Full PA requirements restored.
- October 1, 2025: Authorization requirements were further reviewed and confirmed compliant with MHPAEA.[^4]
- January 1, 2026: Updated NQTLs (Non-Quantitative Treatment Limitations) applied consistent with the staggered implementation plan.

### Practical Notes

- Emergent SUD services (crisis stabilization, ED-level care) generally do not require prior authorization.
- Members may appeal PA denials through the LME-MCO's grievance process and then through the NC OAH (Office of Administrative Hearings).
- Tailored Plan PA criteria are publicly available through each LME-MCO's clinical coverage policy reference tool.

## 7. MHPAEA Federal Parity — NC Medicaid Compliance (2025)

The **Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act (MHPAEA)** (federal, 2008; amended 2024) requires that Medicaid managed care plans not impose more restrictive limitations on MH/SUD benefits than on comparable medical/surgical benefits.[^4]

### NC Medicaid Compliance Milestones

**verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| Jan. 2025 | Revised CCPs promulgated (Dec. 20, 2024, effective Jan. 1, 2025) to comply with MHPAEA |
| Jan. 2025 | PA/CR requirements aligned with MHPAEA (no more restrictive than CCP for SUD/MH) |
| Oct. 2025 | All PA requirements reviewed and confirmed MHPAEA-compliant |
| Dec. 1, 2025 | Children and Families Specialty Plan achieves operational MHPAEA compliance at launch |
| Jan. 2026 | NQTLs staggered implementation completed; full MHPAEA compliance across all plans |

NC Medicaid publishes MHPAEA compliance reports and maintains a dedicated MHPAEA page at medicaid.ncdhhs.gov.[^4]

### What MHPAEA Means in Practice

- Residential SUD treatment cannot face stricter PA than residential medical treatment.
- Concurrent review intervals for SUD must be comparable to those for physical health inpatient care.
- Step therapy (requiring lower levels of care before authorizing higher levels) for SUD must be no more restrictive than comparable medical/surgical requirements.

## 8. NC Children and Families Specialty Plan

Launched **December 1, 2025**, the **NC Children and Families Specialty Plan** is a new Medicaid specialty plan for:
- Children and adolescents with complex behavioral health needs (serious emotional disturbance / SED)
- Families involved with the child welfare system
- Youth with co-occurring SUD and mental health conditions

The Children and Families Specialty Plan achieved MHPAEA operational compliance at launch (January 10, 2025 compliance date met).[^4]

For SUD services for youth in the child welfare/CPS system, the Children and Families Specialty Plan coordinates with the DSS/CPS process — see `nc-sud-family-law.md` for the family law intersection.

## 9. NCCARE360 — Statewide Resource Referral Platform

**NCCARE360** is NC's statewide digital closed-loop referral platform connecting health care providers and community-based organizations (CBOs) to address social determinants of health (SDoH).[^5]

**Relevance to SUD treatment:**
- Providers can make referrals for SUD-related social services (housing, food, transportation, employment) and receive confirmation that the referral was completed.
- Individuals can self-serve: search for SUD treatment resources, housing, recovery supports, and other services by category, location, or eligibility.
- Part of NC Medicaid's whole-person care approach integrated into Tailored Plans.

**2025 update:** New self-serve navigation tool launched in late 2025 allowing individuals, families, and providers to search for services without initiating a formal referral.

**Funding concern:** The NC General Assembly did not include Healthy Opportunities Pilots funding in the current state fiscal year; Healthy Opportunities Pilots services ended July 1, 2025. NCCARE360 as a platform continues but some social services that relied on Pilots funding have been reduced.[^5]

## 10. Anti-Patterns / Common Misunderstandings

**"All NC Medicaid members get SUD services through Tailored Plans"**
No. Tailored Plans serve members with severe MH/SUD/IDD/TBI (approximately 210,000 members). Other Medicaid members access SUD services through Standard Plans (commercial MCOs) or Medicaid Direct (fee-for-service).[^1]

**"The LME-MCO and the Tailored Plan are separate entities"**
Post-July 2024, the four remaining LME-MCOs (Alliance, Partners, Trillium, Vaya) ARE the Tailored Plan operators in their geographic areas. They serve dual roles: Tailored Plan MCO for Medicaid members + administrator of state-funded services for uninsured individuals.[^2]

**"ASAM residential treatment (3.5, 3.7) has always been covered by NC Medicaid"**
Not historically. The 1115 SUD Demonstration Waiver was required to cover residential SUD treatment settings that would otherwise be excluded as "institutions for mental disease" under federal Medicaid rules. This coverage was enabled through the waiver and expanded with the 2026 CCP updates.[^3]

**"MHPAEA means NC Medicaid must cover all behavioral health treatments without limits"**
MHPAEA requires PARITY — behavioral health limits cannot be MORE restrictive than comparable medical/surgical limits. It does not require coverage of all treatments or eliminate all PA requirements.[^4]

**"NCCARE360 funding is secure"**
As of 2025-2026, Healthy Opportunities Pilots funding was eliminated from the NC state budget. NCCARE360 as a platform continues but some services previously funded through the pilots have been reduced or discontinued.[^5]
