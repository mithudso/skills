# ED Management of Acute Methamphetamine Intoxication

*Neuroprotective lens: interventions below are ordered not only by acute life-threat priority but by their dual role in limiting LH/orexin neurotoxicity. Every degree above 39°C amplifies irreversible orexin neuron loss. See → meth-hyperthermia-neurotoxicity, meth-neuroprotection-acute-intoxication.*

---

## Presentation Spectrum

Tachycardia is universal. Presentations cluster by severity:

| Severity | Features |
|---|---|
| Mild | Agitation, diaphoresis, mild hypertension, tachycardia, mydriasis |
| Moderate | Hyperthermia (38–40°C), severe agitation/delirium, hypertensive urgency, chest pain |
| Severe | Hyperthermia >40°C, psychosis, seizures, MACE, rhabdomyolysis, end-organ damage |

Fentanyl co-contamination is now common; examine for opioid toxidrome and have naloxone ready.

---

## Priority 1 — Temperature Control (Highest-Leverage Neuroprotective Step)

**Target: core temperature <38°C. Every minute above 39°C drives LH/orexin ROS cascade.**

Methods (apply simultaneously):
- Ice packs to axillae, groin, neck (major vessel beds)
- Cooling blanket or Arctic Sun if available
- Misting fan + tepid water evaporation
- Cold IV saline (1–2 L; serves dual hydration/cooling purpose)
- Active cooling room if core temp >40°C

**Do NOT use antipyretics (acetaminophen, NSAIDs).** Drug-induced hyperthermia is driven by a central sympathomimetic mechanism, not by prostaglandin-mediated fever — antipyretics are ineffective. Acetaminophen carries hepatotoxicity risk in meth users who may have been fasting or have existing liver stress.

---

## Priority 2 — Benzodiazepines (First-Line for Agitation, Seizures, and Thermogenesis)

IV lorazepam 2–4 mg or diazepam 5–10 mg; titrate to sedation. Repeat q5–10 min PRN. No ceiling in true toxidrome if patient is monitored.

**Mechanism dual benefit:**
1. Reduces agitation-driven muscular thermogenesis — directly lowers core temperature
2. Blunts excitotoxic glutamate release implicated in LH/orexin neuron death
3. First-line seizure prophylaxis and treatment

**Avoid haloperidol and typical antipsychotics** as first-line agents:
- Lower seizure threshold
- QTc prolongation risk
- Neuroleptic malignant syndrome risk in a patient already hyperthermic
- No benefit over benzos for meth-driven agitation

---

## Priority 3 — Hypertensive Crisis Management

Meth hypertension is adrenergically mediated (excess dopamine + norepinephrine).

**Do NOT use beta-blockers alone:** unopposed alpha-adrenergic stimulation causes paradoxical worsening of hypertension and peripheral vasoconstriction.

Protocol:
1. **Benzodiazepines first** — treating agitation often resolves mild-moderate hypertension
2. **Phentolamine** (alpha-blocker) IV for refractory hypertension: 2.5–5 mg IV bolus, repeat PRN
3. **Nicardipine** IV drip (calcium channel blocker) as alternative or adjunct
4. **Hydralazine** IM/IV if nicardipine unavailable
5. **Nitroprusside** for malignant hypertension with end-organ involvement

Target: reduce MAP by ~20–25% in first hour; avoid overshoot (ischemic risk).

---

## Priority 4 — Rhabdomyolysis

Check CK, BMP, UA on every moderate-severe presentation. Rhabdo is common (agitation + hyperthermia + direct muscle toxicity) and independently amplifies neurotoxicity risk via renal failure and systemic oxidative stress.

- Aggressive IV hydration: 1–1.5 mL/kg/hr; target urine output 200–300 mL/hr
- Urine alkalinization (IV bicarbonate) is controversial; used if CK >5,000 with myoglobinuria
- Serial CK and creatinine monitoring; nephrology if AKI develops

---

## Priority 5 — Psychosis

Meth psychosis is dopamine-driven; it mimics paranoid schizophrenia and may persist 24–48h after vital signs normalize.

- Benzodiazepines reduce psychotic agitation adequately in many cases
- **If antipsychotic needed:** olanzapine 10 mg IM (atypical; lower EPS/seizure risk than haloperidol) or droperidol 2.5–5 mg IM as alternative
- Avoid haloperidol + active hyperthermia — NMS risk
- Monitor in ED until normothermic and behavioral stabilization; 23h observation common

---

## Adjunct: NAC Consideration (Neuroprotective, Off-Label)

IV N-acetylcysteine (same formulation used for acetaminophen overdose) replenishes glutathione — the primary endogenous quencher of the ROS burst responsible for LH/orexin neuron apoptosis. No RCT in meth overdose; preclinical evidence is strong (see → meth-neuroprotection-acute-intoxication). Consider in severe hyperthermia presentations as a low-risk dual-purpose intervention (protocol: 150 mg/kg IV over 1h, then maintenance). Disclose off-label status; document reasoning.

---

## Seizures

IV lorazepam first-line; levetiracetam IV if refractory. Avoid phenytoin (ineffective for toxin-induced seizures via NMDA/excitotoxic mechanism). Address hyperthermia and hyponatremia as precipitants.

---

## Cardiac (MACE)

Meth-induced chest pain: rule out STEMI/NSTEMI, Takotsubo, aortic dissection. Meth accelerates coronary artery disease and causes coronary vasospasm. ECG immediately; troponin if clinical suspicion. Avoid beta-blockers in active cocaine or meth toxidrome.

---

## Harm Reduction Opportunity

The ED visit is a high-yield intervention point. SBIRT (Screening, Brief Intervention, Referral to Treatment) initiated in the ED has RCT evidence for increasing SUD treatment entry. Offer warm handoff to SUD navigator or peer recovery coach before discharge. Prescribe naloxone given fentanyl contamination risk.

---

## Summary Priority Order

1. Cooling — target <38°C, begin immediately (neuroprotective + life-saving)
2. Benzodiazepines IV — agitation, seizures, thermogenesis, glutamate-mediated LH damage
3. IV fluids — hydration + cooling + rhabdomyolysis
4. Hypertension — benzos first, phentolamine/nicardipine for refractory
5. Antipsychotic if needed — olanzapine/droperidol, not haloperidol while hyperthermic
6. Consider NAC IV — off-label neuroprotective adjunct in severe presentations
7. SBIRT at discharge — harm reduction, treatment linkage
