# Pitolisant and H3 Receptor Antagonism

## Core Concepts
- Histamine H3 receptors (H3Rs) are presynaptic **autoreceptors and heteroreceptors** — they function as brakes on histamine, ACh, dopamine, and NE release across wake-promoting circuits
- Pitolisant is an H3R **antagonist/inverse agonist** (Ki ~0.16 nM): blocks H3R and reverses its constitutive (ligand-independent) activity, producing greater histamine tone than neutral antagonism alone
- H3R is concentrated in the tuberomammillary nucleus (TMN), cortex, striatum, and brainstem; widespread distribution explains multi-neurotransmitter enhancement profile
- Pitolisant is the **only FDA-approved narcolepsy treatment not DEA-scheduled**: no direct monoamine reuptake inhibition, no dopamine surge in nucleus accumbens, no reward-circuit engagement
- Relevant for SUD-history patients: non-scheduled status means no prior-authorization substance-abuse carve-outs, no REMS program, and no dispensing restrictions at most pharmacies

## Mechanisms / Biology
- H3 autoreceptor activation normally suppresses histamine synthesis (via reduced histidine decarboxylase induction) and release; pitolisant removes this inhibition → elevated histamine in wake-promoting posterior hypothalamic–cortical pathways
- Inverse agonism stabilizes H3R in its inactive conformation — effective even at low endogenous histamine levels (relevant in narcolepsy where orexin loss secondarily dampens TMN firing)
- Heteroreceptor blockade on cholinergic, dopaminergic, and noradrenergic terminals amplifies vigilance independently of the direct dopamine-transporter (DAT) mechanism exploited by amphetamines and methylphenidate
- No occupancy of dopamine D1/D2 receptors, no DAT binding, no serotonin reuptake inhibition — the absence of these accounts for the placebo-level Drug Liking scores in abuse potential studies
- CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 metabolism; ~10–12 h half-life; no active dopaminergic metabolites

## Clinical Picture
- Pitolisant non-inferior to modafinil on ESS in HARMONY III head-to-head; both superior to placebo
- Abuse potential RCT (Sleep 2020, n=64 recreational stimulant users): Drug Liking VAS at Tmax indistinguishable from placebo; significantly lower than phentermine — FDA used this to deny scheduling
- No published RCT specifically enrolling narcolepsy patients with comorbid SUD, but the unscheduled status and mechanism make it the **default option** when stimulants are contraindicated
- Cataplexy reduction: HARMONY-CTP showed 75% weekly cataplexy rate reduction vs 38% placebo — relevant because cataplexy triggers (strong emotion) are common in stressful SUD-recovery contexts
- Also studied in: Parkinson's disease hypersomnia, Prader-Willi syndrome, ADHD-linked EDS — all exploiting H3R's broad vigilance role

## Evidence & Treatment
- **HARMONY I** (8-week RCT, adults): ESS −5.8 (pitolisant) vs −3.4 (placebo); p<0.001 — established EDS efficacy
- **HARMONY-CTP**: cataplexy primary endpoint met; pitolisant 35.6 mg/day outperformed placebo across all cataplexy severity strata
- FDA approved Aug 2019 (adult EDS), Oct 2020 (adult cataplexy), June 2024 (pediatric ≥6 yrs EDS+cataplexy)
- Dosing: titrate over 3 weeks — week 1: 8.9 mg, week 2: 17.8 mg, week 3: 35.6 mg if needed; all once-daily morning dosing
- When stimulants are contraindicated by SUD history: pitolisant + sodium oxybate combination is used in refractory cases; oxybate carries Schedule III (mixed salts) or IV (low-sodium formulation) — coordinate with addiction medicine to weigh oxybate risk separately

## Key Facts
- Ki ~0.16 nM at H3R; >1,000-fold selectivity over H1, H2, H4 subtypes
- Only one of four FDA-approved narcolepsy drug classes without DEA scheduling (amphetamines: Sched II; sodium oxybate: Sched III/IV; modafinil/armodafinil: Sched IV; pitolisant: unscheduled)
- ESS improvement ~5 points in pivotal trials — comparable to modafinil, less than sodium oxybate (~8–9 points)
- Orphan drug designation (US, EU, Japan 2024); narcolepsy prevalence ~1 in 2,000 (0.05%) globally
- QTc prolongation risk at supratherapeutic doses; avoid with other QT-prolonging drugs — relevant in recovery patients on methadone (QT effects additive)
