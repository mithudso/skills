# Motivational Interviewing (MI) for Substance Use Disorders

## MI Spirit: PACE

Four elements define what makes MI different from advice-giving or instruction:

- **Partnership** — clinician and client as collaborators; clinician is not the expert on the client's life
- **Acceptance** — unconditional positive regard, affirmation of autonomy, accurate empathy; client is not required to accept the clinician's frame
- **Compassion** — actively promoting the client's best interests, not the clinician's agenda
- **Evocation** — drawing out the client's own motivations, values, and reasons for change rather than imposing them from outside

The "righting reflex" (also called the "fixing reflex" in MI-4) is the clinician's impulse to argue for change directly. It reliably elicits **sustain talk** via psychological reactance — the client argues against change because the clinician is arguing for it. ([NCBI Bookshelf / SAMHSA TIP-35](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK571068/))

## Four Processes

Non-linear, overlapping — not rigid phases ([NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK571068/)):

1. **Engaging** — building trust and rapport; OARS skills dominant; premature action-focus destroys alliance
2. **Focusing** — agenda mapping, identifying which change to discuss when multiple concerns exist
3. **Evoking** — eliciting intrinsic motivation, exploring discrepancy between values and use; the heart of MI
4. **Planning** — developing commitment and a change plan once readiness is demonstrated

In meth SUD, especially at first contact (ED, primary care, early outpatient), sessions often reach only Engaging and Evoking. Planning in the first session is frequently premature.

## OARS Micro-Skills

- **Open questions** — invite reflection: "What concerns you about your meth use?" vs. "Do you want to stop?" (closed question invites one-word answers, stops exploration)
- **Affirmations** — genuine recognition of specific strengths or effort: "You drove across town to get here — that tells me something about how seriously you're taking this." Not generic praise ("good job"). Affirmations counter shame without false reassurance.
- **Reflective listening** — simple reflections name content; complex reflections name meaning or feeling; **double-sided reflections** hold ambivalence without resolution: "It sounds like meth has helped you stay awake for work, AND it's costing you your relationship with your kids."
- **Summaries** — collect, link, and transition; emphasize change talk selectively

([NCBI Bookshelf / SAMHSA TIP-35](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK571068/))

## Change Talk and Sustain Talk

**Change talk** = language favoring movement toward change. DARN-CAT taxonomy:
- **DARN** (earlier motivational talk): Desire ("I want to"), Ability ("I could"), Reasons ("because..."), Need ("I have to")
- **CAT** (commitment/action): Commitment ("I will"), Activation ("I'm ready"), Taking steps ("I enrolled in...")

**Sustain talk** = language favoring continuation of use. Higher frequency of sustain talk predicts worse outcomes; more change talk predicts better outcomes.

**Key MI rule**: the *client* should be making the argument for change, not the clinician. If the clinician is arguing harder for change than the client, MI spirit has broken down. ([MDedge Psychiatry / NCBI](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK571068/))

When a client responds with sustain talk, the response is to reflect, reframe, or emphasize autonomy — not to push harder. Techniques: simple reflection (validate without agreeing), amplified reflection (overstate to prompt natural correction), double-sided reflection, reframing, autonomy emphasis ("It's completely your call").

## Stages of Change (Prochaska / DiClemente)

MI is most effective in **precontemplation** and **contemplation** — when readiness to change is low. ([SAMHSA TIP-35 Chapter 4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK571072/))

- **Precontemplation**: client doesn't recognize a problem or isn't ready to change; clinician task is engagement, not persuasion; sustain talk and anger are expected, not pathological
- **Contemplation**: client recognizes problem but is ambivalent; double-sided reflections and exploring discrepancy are central
- **Preparation / Action**: skill-building approaches (CBT, contingency management) become more relevant; MI maintains alliance but is not the primary intervention

**Meth patients at first contact** are frequently in precontemplation — particularly in ED settings and primary care. Brief MI in these settings plants seeds that can germinate after the encounter. A single MI session has demonstrated measurable effects even with precontemplation-stage clients. ([SAMHSA TIP-35](https://library.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/tip-35-pep19-02-01-003.pdf))

## Evidence Base

**Cochrane 2023 review** (93 RCTs, N=22,776): MI probably reduces substance use slightly compared to assessment and feedback at medium/long-term follow-up (SMD 0.24, moderate certainty). MI may reduce substance use vs. no intervention at short-term follow-up (SMD 0.48, low certainty). Little difference vs. other active treatments. ([PubMed / Cochrane — PMID 38084817](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38084817/))

**Smedslund et al. 2011 Cochrane review** (59 RCTs): pooled effect size g=0.28 vs. weak comparators for drug use reduction. Established MI as evidence-based for SUD. ([Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD008063.pub2/abstract))

**Meth-specific RCT** (Polcin et al., *Journal of Addictive Diseases* 2014, N=217): both 1-session and 9-session MI produced significant reductions in methamphetamine consumption and Addiction Severity Index scores. Extended MI additionally reduced the psychiatric/psychological component of addiction severity and the number of days of meth-associated psychosis. No significant difference between groups on use reduction alone. ([Promises Behavioral Health](https://www.promises.com/addiction-blog/motivational-interviewing-helps-meth-addicts-reduce-use/))

**NIJ / CrimeSolutions**: MI for substance abuse rated "Promising" practice based on aggregated RCT evidence. ([CrimeSolutions](https://crimesolutions.ojp.gov/ratedpractices/motivational-interviewing-substance-abuse))

## Meth-Specific Clinical Adaptations

**Meth psychosis**: acute psychosis impairs the cognitive engagement MI requires. Attempting MI during active psychosis is contraindicated. Wait for psychosis to clear (hours to days depending on severity) before engaging. Psychosis itself generates shame that complicates subsequent MI engagement.

**Cognitive impairment**: meth neurotoxicity impairs working memory, executive function, and abstract reasoning. Adaptations: use shorter, simpler reflections; repeat and summarize more frequently; provide more structure; avoid complex open questions requiring multi-step reasoning. Dual Diagnosis MI (DDMI) formalizes these accommodations. ([PubMed — PMC4753608](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4753608/))

**Shame**: meth use carries intense stigma; patients frequently present with profound shame about consequences (family loss, criminal history, appearance, sexual behavior). Affirmations and unconditional positive regard (Acceptance in PACE) are especially important. Avoid labeling, diagnosing, or moralizing. Double-sided reflections that include genuine strengths buffer shame without false reassurance.

**Trauma history**: high rates of trauma comorbidity in meth SUD require trauma-informed MI adaptations — slower pacing in Engaging, greater attention to safety, careful use of body-sensation questions that can activate trauma responses.

**Precontemplation as the norm**: most meth patients at first contact are not ready to change; engaging without agenda, affirming their autonomy, and reflecting ambivalence is the clinical goal — not eliciting a commitment to stop.

## Common MI Errors in SUD Practice

- **Righting reflex** (arguing for change → elicits sustain talk)
- **Question-and-answer trap** (rapid-fire closed questions destroy MI spirit)
- **Premature focus on planning** before engagement is established
- **Labeling trap** ("you're an addict") activates reactance
- **Treating resistance as client failure** rather than information about ambivalence
