## Chemsex: Definition and Drugs

Chemsex = intentional drug use before/during sex to enhance or alter the experience; term emerged from UK gay men's communities ~2010s. Core triad: **methamphetamine** (crystal/tina/slam), **GHB/GBL**, **mephedrone**. Meth dominates in North American and Asian MSM scenes; mephedrone more prevalent in Europe. Polydrug combinations are the norm, not the exception.

**Slam** = IV injection of meth during sex — highest-risk route: combines blood-borne HIV exposure (shared needles/works) with pronounced disinhibition, hypersexuality, and erection-sustaining effects that drive marathon condomless sessions.

## Prevalence

Pooled chemsex prevalence among MSM: ~22% globally (systematic review/meta-analysis); sexualized meth use specifically ~8–16% depending on region (higher in Asia/Pacific). PLWH and men engaged in sex work are overrepresented.

## HIV Risk Amplification (Syndemic Mechanics)

Four pathways converge:
1. **Condomless sex** with multiple partners during extended sessions
2. **Shared injecting equipment** (slam parties) — direct blood-borne transmission
3. **Meth-driven viral load dynamics** — meth activates latent HIV replication and reduces ART adherence
4. **Mucosal trauma** from prolonged sex while anesthetized to pain (GHB compound)

Chemsex participants are 2–4× more likely to report recent condomless anal intercourse and new STI diagnoses compared to non-chemsex MSM.

## Syndemic Theory

Singer's syndemic framework maps the interlocking epidemics: **meth use + HIV/STIs + minority stress + stigma + sexual compulsivity** mutually reinforce one another. Minority stress (internalized homophobia, discrimination, concealment) predicts negative affect → avoidance coping → meth use → sexual compulsivity → shame → amplified minority stress. Structural stigma (healthcare discrimination, criminalization of drug use) closes the loop by blocking treatment access.

**Sexual trauma and self-medication**: Elevated childhood sexual abuse prevalence in MSM populations; meth specifically reduces inhibition around sex-related shame and trauma responses. Party-and-play (PnP) scenes offer community belonging, sexual validation, and affirmation otherwise withheld by heteronormative contexts.

## Clinical Features Distinct to This Population

- **Post-chemsex crash**: dysphoria, anhedonia, suicidal ideation 24–72 h after extended use; high-acuity window
- **Sexual compulsivity**: continues as autonomous stressor even when meth use decreases; requires dual-targeted treatment
- **Community embeddedness**: apps (Grindr, Scruff) function as drug-procurement and chemsex-coordination platforms; use is socially normalized in certain scenes
- **HIV-positive identity**: some PLWH report "serosorting" within chemsex scenes, rationalizing condomless sex; ART adherence disrupted during binge cycles

## Treatment Challenges

- **Shame and disclosure fear**: patients often withhold chemsex history from HIV providers and vice versa; siloed care misses the intersection
- **Community norms**: destigmatization of drug use in LGBTQ+ spaces can inadvertently normalize harmful use
- **Sparse LGBTQ+-affirming SUD programs**: mainstream 12-step culture often homophobic or ill-equipped for sexual minority identity integration
- **Contingency management (CM)** has best evidence for meth SUD generally; acceptance in LGBTQ+ chemsex-specific contexts is promising but under-studied

## Tailored Interventions

- **EMIS (European MSM Internet Survey)** — large-scale surveillance and needs-assessment; informs harm reduction messaging
- **Chemsex harm reduction**: sterile injection kits without requiring abstinence intent, GHB dosing guidance (narrow therapeutic window), buddy systems, comedown support resources
- **LGBTQ+-affirming SUD treatment**: therapists trained in minority stress, sexual identity integration, and non-judgmental chemsex framing
- **Integrated HIV/SUD care**: co-locating chemsex support in sexual health/HIV clinics lowers disclosure barrier
- **Digital interventions**: apps (e.g., Chemsex Support, feasibility-tested 2024) for anonymous harm reduction and crisis access
- **CBT + motivational interviewing** adapted for sexual compulsivity alongside substance use; address shame as a core treatment target
