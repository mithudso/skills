# NC Domestic Violence Criminal Defense

> **Educational only — NOT legal advice.** For any specific domestic violence case, consult a licensed North Carolina attorney.

Domestic violence (DV) in North Carolina exists at the intersection of civil protective orders (GS Chapter 50B) and multiple criminal statutes. A single incident can generate both a civil DVPO proceeding and criminal charges. Defense counsel must navigate both systems simultaneously, as they are independent but interconnected.

`verified-as-of: 2026-06-22`

---

## 1. The Civil/Criminal Intersection

NC domestic violence law has two parallel tracks:

| Track | Statute | Forum | Standard |
|-------|---------|-------|----------|
| **Civil DVPO** | GS Chapter 50B | District Court (civil) | Preponderance |
| **Criminal charges** | GS 14-32.5, 14-134.3, 14-33, 50B-4.1, and others | District/Superior Court (criminal) | Beyond reasonable doubt |

A victim may pursue both tracks simultaneously. Criminal defense attorneys must be aware that:
- A 50B DVPO can be obtained ex parte (without the defendant present), resulting in immediate restraints
- Violation of a 50B order triggers separate criminal charges regardless of the underlying criminal case outcome
- Statements made in the civil proceeding may be used in the criminal proceeding

## 2. Domestic Violence Protective Orders (DVPO) — GS Chapter 50B

### Who Qualifies for a 50B

A person may seek a DVPO if they have a "personal relationship" with the perpetrator (GS 50B-1(b)):
- Current or former spouses
- Persons of opposite sex who live together or have lived together
- Parents and children (or persons acting in loco parentis)
- Grandparents and grandchildren
- Current or former household members
- Persons who have a child in common
- Current or former dating partners (opposite or same sex)

### Acts Constituting DV Under GS 50B-1(a)

- Attempting to cause bodily injury or intentionally causing bodily injury
- Placing the victim in fear of imminent serious bodily injury
- Continued harassment that rises to the level of inflicting substantial emotional distress
- Committing specified sex offenses (GS 14-27.21 through 14-27.33)

### Ex Parte Order

Available immediately without notice to the defendant when the plaintiff shows a danger of acts of domestic violence. Valid for up to 10 days; defendant must be served before the full hearing. [^1]

### Full Hearing (50B Order)

Held within 10 days of the ex parte order (or within 7 days of service, whichever is later). Both parties appear. Court may enter an order valid up to **one year**, renewable. The order may include:
- No-contact provisions
- Stay-away from home, workplace, school
- Temporary child custody
- Requirement to surrender firearms
- Requirement to attend batterer intervention program

## 3. Criminal Charges Arising from Domestic Violence

### DVPO Violation (GS 50B-4.1)

Violation of a validly issued and served 50B order is a **separate criminal offense**: [^2]
- First or second violation: **Class A1 misdemeanor**
- Third or subsequent violation: **Class H felony**
- Violation involving a **deadly weapon**: Class H felony
- Violation by entering a safe house or shelter: Class H felony

**Warrantless arrest:** Officers must make warrantless arrests when probable cause exists that someone violated exclusion or no-contact conditions of a 50B order.

### Domestic Criminal Trespass (GS 14-134.3)

- Requires trespass onto property occupied by a **present or former spouse or cohabiting partner**
- Class 1 misdemeanor (first offense); Class H felony if defendant carries a deadly weapon
- Applies only where defendant does not have a legal right to enter the premises

### Misdemeanor Crime of Domestic Violence (GS 14-32.5) — 2025 Changes

SL 2025-70 (effective July 2025) created/refined a standalone **misdemeanor crime of domestic violence** (MCDV): [^3]
- Elements: use or attempted use of physical force, or threat to use a deadly weapon, against a person in a personal relationship
- Classification: **Class A1 misdemeanor**
- **48-hour rule:** MCDV was added to offenses that automatically trigger the 48-hour hold requirement for pretrial release without a judicial finding (GS 15A-534.1)
- **Federal firearms:** Class A1 misdemeanor MCDV likely qualifies as a "misdemeanor crime of domestic violence" under 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(9) — federal firearms prohibition attaches

### Assault Offenses in DV Context

| Statute | Offense | Enhanced if DV Context? |
|---------|---------|------------------------|
| GS 14-33(c)(2) | Assault on a female by a male age 18+ | Yes — Class A1 misdemeanor regardless |
| GS 14-33(d) | Assault in presence of a minor (personal relationship) | Class A1 misdemeanor; repeat offense: 30-day minimum |
| GS 14-32 | Assault with deadly weapon | Enhanced to Class E or F felony with serious injury |
| GS 14-32.4 | Assault inflicting serious bodily injury (personal relationship) | Class F felony |

### Communicating Threats (GS 14-277.1)

Communicating a threat in a DV context is a Class 1 misdemeanor. When the victim and defendant have a personal relationship, this may also trigger 50B proceedings.

### Domestic Violence Homicide (GS 14-17(a1))

For first-degree murder charges: a prior DV conviction + current personal relationship creates a **rebuttable presumption of premeditation and deliberation** — making a first-degree murder conviction more likely.

## 4. Bail and Pretrial Release in DV Cases

### GS 15A-534.1 — Mandatory Conditions

For DV offenses, magistrates and judges must impose conditions designed to protect the victim:
- No contact with the victim
- No return to the shared residence
- May impose electronic monitoring

**48-hour hold:** Persons charged with certain DV offenses (including DVPO violations and MCDV under 2025 law) may be held up to 48 hours without bail pending a judicial hearing.

See [nc-bail-and-bond](nc-bail-and-bond.md) for the full pretrial release framework.

## 5. Federal Firearms and DV Convictions (Lautenberg Amendment)

18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(9) prohibits firearm possession by anyone convicted of a "misdemeanor crime of domestic violence" (MCDV) under federal law — regardless of whether the conviction is a misdemeanor under state law.

**For NC practitioners:**
- A Class A1 misdemeanor assault conviction in a DV context likely qualifies as federal MCDV
- The prohibition is **lifetime** and does NOT expire
- No NC expunction removes a federal MCDV firearms prohibition
- Advising clients about firearms consequences before plea negotiations is a *Padilla*-level duty for DV offenses

## 6. Defense Strategies

| Issue | Strategy |
|-------|----------|
| Victim recantation | Evaluate whether recantation is credible; prosecution may proceed without victim cooperation using prior statements (Crawford analysis) |
| Self-defense | NC recognizes self-defense; mutual combat may reduce charges |
| False allegations | Investigate history of disputes, custody battles, immigration status concerns |
| Civil/criminal coordination | Monitor 50B proceedings; statements there are admissible in criminal case |
| No-contact order compliance | Counsel client strictly; any contact — even indirect — risks new criminal charge |
| Federal firearms | Always advise before plea; MCDV conviction = lifetime federal firearms prohibition |
| Immigration | DV offenses frequently trigger deportation grounds; consult immigration counsel |
