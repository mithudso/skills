# NC Controlled Substance Schedules (GS Chapter 90, Article 5)

> **Educational only, NOT legal advice.** verified-as-of: 2026-06-22

North Carolina's Controlled Substances Act (GS Chapter 90, Article 5) establishes six controlled substance schedules (I–VI) — one more than the federal five-schedule system. The schedules determine penalty severity for possession, sale, manufacture, and trafficking offenses under GS 90-95.

---

## Overview

| Schedule | GS Section | Medical Use | Abuse Potential | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | 90-89 | None | High | Heroin, LSD, psilocybin, MDMA |
| II | 90-90 | Yes (restricted) | High | Fentanyl, oxycodone, cocaine, meth |
| III | 90-91 | Yes | Moderate/Low | Ketamine, buprenorphine, anabolic steroids |
| IV | 90-92 | Yes | Low | Xanax, Valium, tramadol, Ambien |
| V | 90-93 | Yes | Lowest | Low-dose codeine combinations, pregabalin |
| VI | 90-94 | None (NC) | Low public-health risk | Marijuana, THC |

NC's Schedule VI has no federal equivalent — marijuana is Schedule I federally but Schedule VI in NC. [^1]

---

## Scheduling Authority (GS 90-88)

The **Commission for Mental Health, Developmental Disabilities, and Substance Abuse Services** has authority to add, delete, or reschedule substances within Schedules I–VI. [^2]

**Procedures:**
- Commission may act on petition of any interested party or on its own motion
- Must provide public notice and hold a hearing under Chapter 150B before rescheduling
- Emergency exception: when DEA schedules a substance federally, the Commission has 30 days after Federal Register publication to adopt parallel NC scheduling — **no rulemaking notice or hearing required** for adoption of federal action [^2]

**Eight Factors the Commission Must Consider (GS 90-88(a1)):** [^2]
1. Actual or relative potential for abuse
2. Scientific evidence of pharmacological effect, if known
3. State of current scientific knowledge regarding the substance
4. History and current pattern of abuse
5. Scope, duration, and significance of abuse
6. Risk to public health
7. Potential to produce psychic or physiological dependence liability
8. Whether the substance is an immediate precursor of an already-controlled substance

**Federal coordination:** NC generally mirrors DEA scheduling changes but retains independent authority and may object. State and federal schedules are **not identical** — the NC Commission can refuse to adopt federal changes. [^3]

**Administrative scheduling:** In addition to the GS Chapter 90 text, the Commission may administratively add substances via 10A NCAC 26F.0102 et seq. [^3]

## Schedule I — GS 90-89

**Criteria:** High potential for abuse; no currently accepted medical use in the United States; or lack of accepted safety for use under medical supervision.

**Key substance categories:**
- **Opiates:** Heroin (diacetylmorphine), fentanyl analogs not approved for medical use, morphine analogs
- **Hallucinogens:** LSD, psilocybin, psilocin, MDMA (ecstasy), mescaline, DMT, peyote
- **Depressants:** Gamma-hydroxybutyric acid (GHB), methaqualone
- **Stimulants:** Cathinone, methcathinone, substituted cathinones (bath salts)
- **Synthetic cannabinoids** (e.g., K2/Spice compounds)
- **NBOMe compounds** and substituted phenethylamines

**Notable:** Heroin, LSD, psilocybin mushrooms, and MDMA are the most frequently prosecuted Schedule I substances in NC. [^1]

---

## Schedule II — GS 90-90

**Criteria:** High potential for abuse; currently accepted medical use in the US (possibly with severe restrictions); abuse may lead to severe psychic or physical dependence.

**Key substance categories:**
- **Opioids:** Oxycodone (OxyContin), hydrocodone (Vicodin), fentanyl (medical), morphine, methadone, codeine (pure), hydromorphone (Dilaudid)
- **Stimulants:** Amphetamine (Adderall), methamphetamine, methylphenidate (Ritalin), cocaine
- **Depressants:** Phencyclidine (PCP), amobarbital, pentobarbital
- **Other:** Nabilone (synthetic cannabinoid approved for chemotherapy nausea)

**Practice note:** Hydrocodone was rescheduled from Schedule III to Schedule II federally in 2014; NC followed suit. Cocaine's placement in Schedule II (not I) reflects its accepted medical use as a topical anesthetic. [^1]

---

## Schedule III — GS 90-91

**Criteria:** Potential for abuse less than Schedules I and II; currently accepted medical use; abuse may lead to moderate or low physical dependence or high psychological dependence.

**Key substance categories:**
- **Depressants:** Ketamine, certain barbituric acid derivatives, tiletamine-zolazepam combinations
- **Narcotics in limited quantities:** Buprenorphine, codeine combinations (up to 90 mg/dosage unit), dihydrocodeine, opium preparations
- **Anabolic steroids:** Testosterone and derivatives
- **Cannabinoids:** Dronabinol (synthetic THC) in sesame oil capsules
- **Stimulants:** Benzphetamine, phendimetrazine

---

## Schedule IV — GS 90-92

**Criteria:** Low potential for abuse relative to Schedule III; currently accepted medical use; limited physical or psychological dependence relative to Schedule III.

**Key substance categories:**
- **Benzodiazepines:** Alprazolam (Xanax), diazepam (Valium), lorazepam (Ativan), clonazepam (Klonopin), triazolam (Halcion)
- **Stimulants/diet drugs:** Phentermine, modafinil, diethylpropion
- **Other:** Tramadol, butorphanol, pentazocine, zolpidem (Ambien), carisoprodol (Soma)
- **Designer benzodiazepines:** Various newly synthesized benzodiazepine analogs

---

## Schedule V — GS 90-93

**Criteria:** Low potential for abuse relative to Schedule IV; currently accepted medical use; limited physical or psychological dependence relative to Schedule IV.

**Key substance categories:**
- **Narcotic preparations (limited quantities):** Codeine combinations (≤200 mg/100 ml), dihydrocodeine, diphenoxylate, difenoxin
- **Anticonvulsants:** Pregabalin (Lyrica), lacosamide, brivaracetam, cenobamate, ezogabine
- **Other:** Pyrovalerone

**Retail sale:** Schedule V substances may be sold without a prescription by a registered pharmacist only to persons 18+ with proper ID — but only in quantities and for purposes specified by NC law.

---

## Schedule VI — GS 90-94 (NC-Unique)

**Criteria:** No currently accepted medical use in the US, OR relatively low potential for abuse in terms of risk to public health and potential to produce psychic or physiological dependence liability.

**Substances:**
- **Marijuana** — all parts of the plant of the genus Cannabis (except sterilized seeds incapable of germination and fiber produced from stalks)
- **Tetrahydrocannabinols (THC)** — synthetic equivalents of cannabis substances

**Critical carve-out — Hemp exemption:**  Products containing **no more than 0.3% delta-9 THC by dry weight** are classified as hemp and are **excluded** from Schedule VI per GS 90-87(13a)–(13b). [^4] This mirrors the federal 2018 Farm Bill definition and has created a gray zone for delta-8 THC and similar hemp-derived intoxicants.

**NC vs. Federal comparison:**
- NC: Marijuana = Schedule VI (lowest schedule — NC-created category)
- Federal: Marijuana = Schedule I (highest restriction)
- Practical consequence: NC imposes significantly lower possession penalties than would be triggered under federal law; however, federal law still applies on federal property and for interstate transport [^4]

**Synthetic cannabinoids update:** Synthetic cannabinoids were removed from Schedule VI in 2017 and reclassified to Schedule I. Older legal resources may incorrectly list them as Schedule VI. [^4]

## Penalty Framework by Schedule (GS 90-95)

The schedule determines the **base offense class** — penalties then cascade through NC Structured Sentencing (see `nc-structured-sentencing` spoke).

### Possession Penalties

| Schedule | Base Offense | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| I | Class I felony | Exception: MDPV ≤1g = Class 1 misdemeanor |
| II, III, IV | Class 1 misdemeanor | **Elevated to Class I felony** if: methamphetamine, amphetamine, PCP, cocaine, fentanyl/carfentanil, ≥4 dosage units of hydromorphone, or >100 dosage units total |
| V | Class 2 misdemeanor | — |
| VI (marijuana) | Class 3 misdemeanor | ≤½ oz: fine or suspended sentence; ½–1½ oz: Class 1 misdemeanor; >1½ oz: Class I felony |

### Sale/Delivery/Manufacture Penalties

| Schedule | Manufacture/Delivery | Sale |
|---|---|---|
| I or II | Class H felony | Class G felony |
| III, IV, V, or VI | Class I felony | Class H felony |
| Methamphetamine (manufacture) | **Class C felony** | — |
| Meth packaging/labeling only | Class H felony | — |

**Key note on PWIMSD:** Possession With Intent to Manufacture, Sell, or Deliver (PWIMSD) is governed by the same felony class as sale/delivery — not by simple possession class. See `nc-simple-possession-and-pwimsd` spoke for full analysis.

## Practical Patterns & Anti-Patterns

**Schedule identification at charging stage:**
- The schedule of the controlled substance is an **element of the offense** and must be alleged in the indictment and proven at trial
- Lab reports must identify the specific substance; field test results alone are insufficient for prosecution in NC
- For counterfeit controlled substances, see GS 90-95.3 (separate offense)

**Common scheduling traps:**
- Hydrocodone combination products: Now Schedule II (not III) since 2014 federal rescheduling — charging as Class 1 misdemeanor is error
- Synthetic cannabinoids: Now Schedule I (not VI) since 2017 — treating as marijuana-equivalent is error
- Hemp-derived delta-8 THC: Below 0.3% delta-9 threshold means NOT Schedule VI — prosecution requires careful lab analysis distinguishing delta-8 from delta-9 THC

**Analog theory (GS 90-89.1):** NC's analog provision covers substantially similar chemical structures to Schedule I or II substances — relevant for novel designer drugs not yet explicitly scheduled.

---

## Anti-Patterns

- **Assuming NC = federal schedules:** NC has 6 schedules; federal law has 5. Marijuana's NC placement (Schedule VI) is not equivalent to federal Schedule I treatment.
- **Ignoring administrative scheduling:** Substances added by Commission rule (10A NCAC 26F) have the same legal effect as legislative schedules — practitioners must check both sources.
- **Applying outdated synthetic cannabinoid law:** Pre-2017 cases treated synthetic cannabinoids as Schedule VI (marijuana equivalent); they are now Schedule I with significantly harsher penalties.
- **Underestimating Schedule II possession triggers:** The 100-unit threshold and specific substance triggers (cocaine, meth, fentanyl) elevate what looks like a misdemeanor to a Class I felony.
