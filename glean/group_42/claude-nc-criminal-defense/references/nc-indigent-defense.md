# NC Indigent Defense Services and Right to Counsel

**Educational only, NOT legal advice.** Consult a licensed NC attorney for specific matters.

## Constitutional Foundation

**Gideon v. Wainwright**, 372 U.S. 335 (1963): The Sixth Amendment right to counsel applies to the states through the Fourteenth Amendment. Indigent defendants facing imprisonment have a constitutional right to appointed counsel. NC G.S. 7A-451 implements and expands this right for NC proceedings.

**NC Statutory Entitlement:** Every person accused of a crime is entitled to counsel. An indigent person is entitled to appointed counsel in any case where imprisonment, or a fine of $500 or more, is likely to be adjudged (G.S. 7A-451).

## NC Indigent Defense Services — Structure

### Legislative Origin
The North Carolina General Assembly passed the **Indigent Defense Services Act of 2000**, creating:
- The **Office of Indigent Defense Services (IDS)** — administrative office
- The **Commission on Indigent Defense Services** — 13-member governing body

IDS is headquartered in Durham, NC. Only three executive directors have led IDS in its 26-year history: Tye Hunter, Tom Maher, and Mary Pollard (retiring August 2026).

### Commission on Indigent Defense Services — Composition

The 13-member Commission includes appointments from:

| Appointing Authority | Member Type |
|---|---|
| Chief Justice of the NC Supreme Court | Active or former judge |
| Governor | Non-attorney |
| General Assembly | 2 attorneys |
| NC Public Defender's Association | Attorney |
| NC State Bar | Attorney |
| NC Bar Association | Attorney |
| NC Academy of Trial Lawyers | Attorney |
| NC Association of Black Lawyers | Attorney |
| NC Association of Women Lawyers | Attorney |
| Commission itself (3 seats) | 1 non-attorney; 1 active judge; 1 Native American |

**Prohibited from serving:** active prosecutors, law enforcement officials, judicial officers (with limited exceptions), full-time public defenders, and IDS employees.

### Commission Authority
- Overseeing provision of legal representation to indigent defendants
- Developing training, qualification, and performance standards
- Determining the most appropriate methods of delivering legal services in each district
- Managing fee schedules and compensation for appointed counsel

## Service Delivery Models

### Public Defender Offices
NC has expanded from 11 to **27 public defender offices** covering **60 counties** (verified 2026). These offices:
- Handle **over half of all indigent cases** in North Carolina
- Employ salaried assistant public defenders
- Cover both district court and superior court indigent cases

### Private Assigned Counsel (PAC)
In counties without a public defender office and as supplemental coverage in PD districts, **Private Assigned Counsel (PAC)** — private attorneys who:
- Agree to accept appointments for eligible clients
- Are compensated at an hourly rate set by IDS
- Are independent contractors, not IDS employees
- Must meet qualification standards for case types

### Managed Assigned Counsel (MAC)
IDS contracts with some attorneys under a **Managed Assigned Counsel** arrangement, which involves specific contract categories and performance requirements.

### Specialized Units
IDS maintains statewide specialized offices for:
- Capital defendants
- Juvenile defendants
- Parents in abuse/neglect proceedings
- Involuntary commitment respondents
- Appellate cases (non-capital)

## Roster Requirements and Qualification Standards

### Uniform Appointment Plan
IDS promulgates a **Uniform Appointment Plan** that sets required qualifications for counsel based on case type. Roster management and oversight is left to the local bar in non-public-defender districts.

### Case-Type Qualification Requirements
Attorneys must meet minimum qualifications to handle specific case categories:

| Case Type | Minimum Requirements (selected) |
|---|---|
| High-level felonies | Licensed in NC ≥ 3 years; tried ≥ 2 jury trials to verdict; competent to try criminal superior court cases |
| Capital cases | Separate, more demanding standards under IDS Rules Part 1 (lead and associate counsel) |
| Juvenile | Experience in juvenile proceedings; compliance with IDS juvenile standards |
| Appellate | Admitted to NC Court of Appeals; separate IDS appellate rules (Part 3) |

Attorneys seeking MAC contracts must certify that they meet minimum qualifications for each contract category or request a waiver.

## Scope of the Right to Counsel — G.S. 7A-451

### Covered Proceedings
G.S. 7A-451(a) entitles indigent defendants to appointed counsel in 19 categories, including:
- Criminal cases where imprisonment or fine ≥ $500 is likely
- Habeas corpus petitions
- Motions for appropriate relief (MAR)
- Probation revocation and extension hearings
- Parole revocation hearings
- Extradition proceedings
- Involuntary commitment proceedings
- Juvenile hearings where institutional commitment is possible
- Parental rights termination proceedings
- Incompetency determinations
- Sex offender registration review hearings
- Capital postconviction proceedings

### When the Right Attaches
Entitlement to counsel begins **"as soon as feasible after the indigent is taken into custody"** or served with initiating process.

Continues through all **critical stages**, including:
- Custodial interrogation
- Pretrial identification procedures (lineups, showups) after formal charges
- Bail hearings and hearings for reduction of bail
- Probable cause hearings
- Trial
- Sentencing
- Direct appeal
- Capital postconviction (separate provisions under G.S. 7A-451(c))

### Initial Appearance and Arraignment
- **Initial appearance before a magistrate** (to determine probable cause for arrest): not itself a "critical stage," but the right to counsel **attaches** at this point; counsel must be appointed within a reasonable time after
- **Arraignment**: defendant has both constitutional and statutory right to counsel; if defendant appears without an attorney, the judge must advise of rights before the defendant may be called upon to plead — even if the defendant previously waived or was denied appointed counsel

### Capital Postconviction
Defendants under death sentence may apply for postconviction counsel within 10 days after direct appeal concludes. Appointed capital postconviction counsel cannot have previously represented the defendant at trial or on direct appeal, unless the defendant waives future ineffective assistance claims.

## Appointment Process in Practice

### In Public Defender Districts
1. Defendant appears in court and claims indigency
2. Public defender's office is assigned; assistant public defender represents the defendant
3. PD office handles the case through district court, superior court, or both depending on case track

### In Non-Public-Defender Districts
1. Court determines eligibility (indigency finding)
2. Court appoints from the roster of qualified attorneys maintained by the local bar
3. Attorney files fee applications with IDS for compensation

### Indigency Determination
The court makes the indigency determination. G.S. 7A-451 does not define "indigent" with a means test in the statute itself; courts apply a practical assessment of ability to retain private counsel. Prior adjudication of indigency creates a rebuttable presumption for capital postconviction purposes.

## IDS Resources for Defense Attorneys

- Current counsel rates and payment schedules (updated by IDS)
- Fee application processing and online portal
- Trial resources including expert and investigator referrals
- Mitigation specialist support (especially in capital cases)
- Roster applications for private attorneys seeking appointment
- Annual training programs (partnership with UNC School of Government)

## Key Statutes

- G.S. 7A-451 — Scope of entitlement to counsel
- G.S. 7A-452 — Determination of indigency
- G.S. 7A-453 through 7A-458 — Appointment and compensation
- G.S. Chapter 7A, Article 30 — Uniform Appointment Plan authority
- G.S. Chapter 7A, Article 39B (G.S. 7A-498 et seq.) — IDS Act; Commission creation and authority
