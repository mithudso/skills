# Byzantine Fault Tolerance, Gossip, and CRDTs

The BFT family tolerates **arbitrary/malicious** node behavior, not just crashes. The unifying bound is **n ≥ 3f+1** replicas to tolerate **f Byzantine** faults under partial synchrony with unauthenticated messages, with **2f+1-size quorum certificates**. ## Byzantine Generals and the 3f+1 bound

Framed by Lamport, Shostak, Pease, "The Byzantine Generals Problem" (ACM TOPLAS 1982), building on "Reaching Agreement in the Presence of Faults" (JACM 1980).[^byz][^reaching] A Byzantine fault is the strongest model: a node that doesn't merely crash but actively lies and can collude. The central result for **oral (unsigned, unauthenticated) messages** is that agreement is solvable **iff n ≥ 3m+1** for m faulty processors — equivalently "more than two-thirds of the generals must be loyal," so a single traitor confounds two loyal generals and ≥4 nodes are needed to tolerate 1 fault.[^byz][^reaching] The recursive oral-message algorithm OM(m) runs in m+1 rounds with cost exponential in m, which is why practical systems avoid it.[^byz] With **signed (authenticated) messages** (assumption A4: a loyal general's signature is unforgeable and verifiable) "the problem is solvable for any number of generals and possible traitors," collapsing the bound so any majority of reachable loyal nodes suffices.[^byz] **[fact]**

The synchronous/asynchronous distinction is consequential: Dwork-Lynch-Stockmeyer ("Consensus in the Presence of Partial Synchrony," JACM 1988) prove that under **partial synchrony**, crash/omission and *authenticated* Byzantine faults need **n ≥ 2f+1**, while *unauthenticated* Byzantine faults need **n ≥ 3f+1**, and these bounds are tight.[^dls][^dt3f] (FLP impossibility is the backdrop but is covered in `foundations-theory.md`.) **[fact]**

## PBFT

"Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance" (Castro & Liskov, OSDI 1999; expanded ACM TOCS 2002) was the first BFT state-machine-replication protocol practical for asynchronous/partially-synchronous networks like the Internet, running ~3% slower than unreplicated NFS.[^pbft][^pbfttocs] It uses **3f+1 replicas to tolerate f Byzantine faults (optimal)** with a primary/backup model where primary = view-number mod n.[^pbft] Each request runs a **three-phase protocol: pre-prepare, prepare, commit** — pre-prepare and prepare totally order requests *within* a view (even with a faulty primary), prepare and commit guarantee ordering *across* views.[^pbft] The quorum threshold is **2f+1 matching messages** per phase; two quorums of 2f+1 in a system of 3f+1 intersect in ≥f+1 replicas (i.e. ≥1 honest), which is the geometric reason for safety.[^pbft][^pbftslides] Clients wait for **f+1 matching replies** in the base protocol.[^pbft] **Safety holds under any network conditions; liveness requires eventual synchrony**, and a faulty/slow primary triggers a **view-change** (the new primary collects 2f+1 view-change messages).[^pbfttocs] A key optimization: normal-case messages use **MACs/authenticators** (~1000× cheaper than public-key signatures), reserving digital signatures for rare view-change/new-view messages.[^pbfttocs] Message complexity is **O(n²) per request** in the normal case (all-to-all prepare/commit) and **O(n³) for view-changes**.[^bottlenecks] **[fact]**

## Tendermint / CometBFT

Tendermint (Buchman's 2016 MSc thesis; formalized as "The latest gossip on BFT consensus," Buchman-Kwon-Milosevic, arXiv 1807.04938) is a BFT SMR algorithm and the consensus core of the Cosmos ecosystem.[^tmthesis][^tmgossip] At each blockchain height a **round-based protocol** runs three steps — **Propose, Prevote, Precommit** — plus Commit/NewHeight; the proposer is chosen by deterministic round-robin weighted by voting power.[^tmgossip][^cometspec] Safety assumes **at most ⅓ of voting power is Byzantine**: a **commit is a set of precommits from more than ⅔ of voting power for the same block in the same round**, and a set of +2/3 prevotes is a "proof-of-lock-change" (polka).[^tmthesis][^cometspec] Validators **lock** on a block when they precommit it and may only change the lock on seeing a polka at a higher round — this locking guarantees no two validators commit different blocks at the same height, giving **instant/deterministic finality with no forks** (unlike longest-chain), at the cost that locking rules must be carefully designed not to break liveness.[^tmthesis][^cometspec] It tolerates 1 fault with a minimum of 4 validators; proposals/votes are signed and **gossiped**.[^tmthesis] As a two-phase variant it achieves **O(n) view-change communication but is *not* responsive** — it must wait Δ for proposals — whereas PBFT's view-change is O(n²)/O(n³).[^dt3f] **[fact]**

**Landscape (verified-as-of 2026-06-16):** **CometBFT** is "a fork of, and successor to, Tendermint Core," announced Feb 2023 and maintained by Informal Systems; the Cosmos SDK migrated from `tendermint/tendermint` to `cometbft/cometbft` as its default engine.[^cometfork][^cometspec] **[qualified]**

## HotStuff

HotStuff ("HotStuff: BFT Consensus with Linearity and Responsiveness," Yin, Malkhi, Reiter, Golan-Gueta, Abraham, PODC 2019; full version arXiv 1803.05069) is a leader-based BFT replication protocol for the **partial-synchrony** model and the basis of Facebook/Diem (Libra) consensus.[^hotstuff][^hotstuffpodc] Its signature achievement is **communication complexity linear in n, O(n)**: in each phase **only the leader broadcasts to all replicas and replicas respond only to the leader (star topology)**, and the leader aggregates (n−f) votes into one **quorum certificate (QC)** via a **(k,n)-threshold signature**, so a QC is a single O(1)-size authenticator.[^hotstuff] The protocol has **four message types/phases — prepare, pre-commit, commit, decide** — built on a **three-phase (three-chain) core**: collect (n−f) prepare votes → prepareQC → (n−f) pre-commit votes → precommitQC (replicas **lock** here) → (n−f) commit votes → commitQC → decide/execute.[^hotstuff] It offers **optimistic responsiveness** (after GST, a correct leader drives consensus at actual network delay) and is **pipelinable/chained** (each QC advances multiple in-flight blocks) for throughput.[^hotstuff][^hotstuffpodc] The headline contrast: HotStuff has a **linear O(n) view-change**, versus PBFT's **O(n²) view-change / O(n³) worst-case**.[^dt3f][^hotstuff] **Lineage:** decentralizedthoughts tabulates PBFT (2-phase, responsive, O(n²) view-change), Tendermint (2-phase, *not* responsive, O(n) view-change), HotStuff (**3-phase**, responsive, O(n) view-change), and **HotStuff-2** (eprint 2023/397 — back to **2-phase** with O(n) responsive view-change); **DiemBFT-v4, Jolteon/Ditto, Fast-HotStuff** are two-phase descendants that regain low latency but revert to a PBFT-style O(n²)/cascading-O(n³) view-change.[^dt3f][^fasthotstuff][^hotstuff2] **[fact]**

## Gossip / epidemic protocols

Epidemic (gossip) protocols originate with Demers et al., "Epidemic Algorithms for Replicated Database Maintenance" (PODC 1987; Xerox PARC CSL-89-1), for the Clearinghouse replicated DB.[^demers] It defines three strategies: **direct mail** (timely, unreliable); **anti-entropy** (every site periodically picks a random peer and reconciles full DB contents — extremely reliable but expensive, so it can't run too often); **rumor-mongering** (a new update is a "hot rumor" pushed to random peers until too many contacted peers already have it, then it stops — cheaper per cycle, small chance of incomplete reach).[^demers] It analyzes **push, pull, push-pull**, finding push-pull rumor-mongering robust across topologies.[^demers] The theoretical hallmark is **logarithmic dissemination**: randomized rumor spreading informs all n nodes in **Θ(log n) rounds w.h.p.**, with a matching **Ω(log n) lower bound**.[^rumorspread] For **membership and failure detection**, SWIM (Das, Gupta, Motivala, DSN 2002) replaces quadratic heartbeating: it **separates failure detection (randomized ping/ping-req probing) from membership-update dissemination (gossip piggybacked on probes)**, so both expected detection time and per-member load are **independent of group size O(1)**, plus a **suspicion mechanism** to cut false positives (deployed in HashiCorp Serf/Consul, Uber Ringpop).[^swim] **[fact]**

## CRDTs

Conflict-free Replicated Data Types were formalized by Shapiro, Preguiça, Baquero, Zawirski ("Conflict-free Replicated Data Types," SSS 2011; tech report "A Comprehensive Study of Convergent and Commutative Replicated Data Types," INRIA RR-7506, 2011).[^crdtsss][^crdttr] A CRDT's mathematical properties guarantee **Strong Eventual Consistency (SEC)** — replicas accepting updates without synchronization converge "despite any number of failures."[^crdtsss] Two equivalent styles: **state-based (Convergent / CvRDT)**, where states form a **monotonic join-semilattice** ordered by ≤, merge computes the **least upper bound (LUB)**, and state is monotonically non-decreasing; and **operation-based (Commutative / CmRDT)**, where concurrent operations **commute** and updates are delivered in causal order — the two are provably equivalent.[^crdtsss][^crdttr] Common types: **G-Counter** and **PN-Counter** (increments/decrements commute), **OR-Set** (Observed-Remove Set — tags adds with unique IDs for add-wins), **2P-Set**, **LWW-Register** (last-writer-wins).[^crdttr][^crdtsurvey] The principal limitation is **metadata/tombstone overhead**: removal-supporting sets use tombstones or unique tags that **grow state unboundedly without garbage collection**, and effective compaction requires knowing all replicas observed a state — partially undermining the coordination-free appeal.[^crdtsurvey] **[fact]**

## Disconfirming findings

- **PBFT does not scale.** O(n²) normal-case messages are fine for n=4 but break down for n in the hundreds; the **O(n³) view-change** can ping-pong when a primary is slow-but-not-failed, worst case **O(n⁴)** in cascading failure. Root cause is all-to-all broadcast + a single leader proposing O(n)-size messages; serialization CPU cost bottlenecks throughput even with ample bandwidth. The protocol is also intricate (~11 normal-path cases) and assumes a fixed replica set.[^bottlenecks][^pbftannotated]
- **HotStuff's linearity costs latency.** Its responsiveness + linear view-change require a **third phase (three-chain commit)** — three happy-path rounds vs two for PBFT/Tendermint — which lengthens confirmation. Fast-HotStuff, Jolteon/Ditto, DiemBFT-v4, and **HotStuff-2** reduce this back to two phases (HotStuff-2 claims ~33% latency improvement at no extra complexity), while the others **trade back to an O(n²)/cascading-O(n³) view-change**.[^dt3f][^fasthotstuff][^hotstuff2]
- **3f+1 is provably tight — but model-dependent.** DLS (1988) shows Byzantine Agreement is **impossible for n ≤ 3f under partial synchrony**, but this collapses to **n ≥ 2f+1 under synchrony OR with authentication** (and crash-only faults need just 2f+1) — so "3f+1" is specifically the *unauthenticated Byzantine, asynchronous/partially-synchronous* bound, not universal.[^dls][^dt3f][^synchotstuff]
- **CRDTs trade coordination for unbounded metadata.** Tombstone-based sets grow forever; sequence CRDTs carry large identifiers; GC itself often needs consensus/causal-stability that "stops becoming stable under partitions," quietly reintroducing coordination. CRDTs guarantee only convergence, **not arbitrary application invariants** (e.g. a PN-Counter can't enforce "balance ≥ 0").[^crdtsurvey]
