# NC Drug Trafficking Thresholds and Mandatory Minimums

> **Educational only, NOT legal advice.** Consult a licensed NC attorney for any specific situation. Verified-as-of: 2026-06-22.

North Carolina G.S. § 90-95(h) defines drug trafficking offenses by specifying weight or quantity thresholds. All trafficking convictions carry **mandatory active prison sentences** — the judge has no discretion to suspend or reduce below the mandatory minimum. Trafficking is not defined by intent to distribute; possessing the threshold quantity alone is sufficient.

---

## Key Principle: Mandatory vs. Discretionary Sentencing

Unlike other drug offenses governed by Structured Sentencing (GS 15A, Article 81B), trafficking sentences are **fixed by GS 90-95(h)** — prior record level does not apply, and the court cannot mitigate below the statutory minimum. All trafficking sentences require active incarceration; probation is not available.

## Trafficking Thresholds by Drug (GS 90-95(h))

### Marijuana (GS 90-95(h)(1))

| Weight | Felony Class | Min. Prison | Max. Prison | Min. Fine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| > 10 lbs, ≤ 50 lbs | H | 25 months | 39 months | $5,000 |
| > 50 lbs, ≤ 2,000 lbs | G | 35 months | 51 months | $25,000 |
| > 2,000 lbs, ≤ 10,000 lbs | F | 70 months | 93 months | $50,000 |
| > 10,000 lbs | D | 175 months | 222 months | $200,000 |

## STRONG Act of 2025 — School-Proximity Enhancements

The STRONG Act (S.L. 2025 — effective December 1, 2025) added enhanced penalties for opioid/heroin trafficking near:

- **School property or within 1,500 feet of a school** (persons 21+): offense elevated to Class B1 felony
- **Public parks, shopping malls, transit stations**: +36 months added to the mandatory minimum term

These enhancements apply to opium, opioid, or heroin trafficking specifically (GS 90-95(h)(4) and (4b)).

---

## Trafficking vs. Possession with Intent (PWIMSD)

Trafficking is triggered by **weight/quantity alone** — state need not prove intent to distribute. This distinguishes it from PWIMSD (GS 90-95(a)(1)), where intent is an element but weight thresholds are not defined. Defendants below the trafficking threshold may still face PWIMSD charges based on circumstantial evidence of intent (packaging, scales, cash, statements).

See: [nc-simple-possession-pwimsd](nc-simple-possession-pwimsd.md) for PWIMSD elements.
