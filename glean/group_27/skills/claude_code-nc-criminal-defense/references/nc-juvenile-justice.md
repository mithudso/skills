# NC Juvenile Justice

> **Educational only — NOT legal advice.** For any specific juvenile case, consult a licensed North Carolina attorney with juvenile defense experience.

North Carolina's juvenile justice system is governed by the **Juvenile Code (GS Chapter 7B)** and has undergone significant changes through the "Raise the Age" legislation and subsequent amendments. Juvenile delinquency cases are fundamentally different from adult criminal cases: the goal is rehabilitation, proceedings are confidential, adjudications are not "convictions," and an entirely separate dispositional structure applies.

`verified-as-of: 2026-06-22`

## 1. Age of Juvenile Jurisdiction (Post-Raise the Age)

### Original "Raise the Age" (Effective December 1, 2019)

Senate Law 2017-57 (the Juvenile Justice Reinvestment Act) and SL 2019-186 raised the age of juvenile court jurisdiction to include **16- and 17-year-olds**, bringing NC in line with all other states. Before 2019, NC was one of only two states that automatically prosecuted 16- and 17-year-olds as adults for all offenses. [^1]

### 2024 Amendment — Class A–E Felonies (Effective December 1, 2024)

SL 2024-17 significantly modified the Raise the Age framework: [^2]

| Age | Offense Class | Starting Court |
|-----|--------------|----------------|
| 16–17 | **Class A–E felonies** | **Adult superior court** (starts there; may be removed to juvenile court) |
| 16–17 | Class F–G felonies | Juvenile court (with mandatory transfer available) |
| 16–17 | Class H–I felonies and misdemeanors | Juvenile court |
| Under 16 | All offenses (felonies/misdemeanors) | Juvenile court |

**Key feature:** For Class A–E felonies charged to 16–17 year olds, a **joint motion by prosecutor and defense attorney** can remove the case to juvenile court. If a joint motion is filed, the superior court must remand.

The 2024 law also:
- Eliminated interlocutory appeals of transfer orders in mandatory transfer cases
- Created a unified 90-day probable cause hearing timeline across mandatory transfer categories
- Extended the remand process to ages 13–15 (joint motion can remand felonies to juvenile court)

### Pending Rollback Concerns (2025–2026)

NC legislators have proposed further narrowing juvenile jurisdiction, which could reverse some Raise the Age protections for 16–17 year olds charged with additional felony classes. [^3] Monitor NC General Assembly sessions for updates.

## 2. The Delinquency Process — From Complaint to Disposition

### Step 1: Complaint and Intake (GS 7B-1702)

A "complaint" is filed with the juvenile court counselor (intake officer). Intake is the **screening and evaluation** process to determine whether to:
- Divert (handle without a petition) — most common for first-time, lower-level offenses
- File a petition for court intervention
- Refer to law enforcement or other agencies

Intake counselors use the **Youth Level of Service/Case Management Inventory (YLS/CMI)** — an 8-domain risk/needs assessment — to assess risk to reoffend and guide diversion vs. petition decisions. The 8 domains include: prior and current offenses, family/parenting, education, peer relations, substance abuse, leisure/recreation, personality/behavior, and attitudes/orientation.

### Step 2: Petition Filed (GS 7B-1801)

A **petition** initiates formal court proceedings. The juvenile is the respondent; a guardian ad litem may be appointed. The petition must allege the specific delinquent act.

**Secure custody** (pre-adjudication detention) is available when:
- The juvenile is a danger to themselves or others
- The juvenile is likely to flee
- No appropriate alternative is available (GS 7B-1903)

Secure custody is **reviewed by a judge within 5 calendar days** and must continue to be reviewed every 10 days. [^4]

### Step 3: Adjudicatory Hearing (GS 7B-2405)

Similar to a bench trial (no jury in juvenile court). The state must prove the delinquent act **beyond a reasonable doubt**. Key procedural rights:
- Right to counsel (appointed for indigent juveniles)
- Right to confront witnesses
- Protection against self-incrimination (5th Amendment applies)
- Exclusionary rule applies (4th Amendment violations suppressible)

**Adjudication ≠ conviction.** A finding that a juvenile committed a delinquent act is NOT a criminal conviction, creates no criminal record, and the juvenile may not be referred to as a "criminal" or "convicted." [^5]

### Step 4: Dispositional Hearing (GS 7B-2501)

A separate hearing after adjudication. The court selects a disposition from three levels based on offense seriousness and prior record:

#### Level 1 — Community Dispositions (GS 7B-2506(1)–(12))
Available in every delinquent case. Options include:
- Supervision in the home (probation)
- Placement with DSS, relative, or other suitable person
- Fine (up to the adult maximum) and community service (up to 100 hours)
- Curfew, no-contact conditions, intermittent detention (up to 5 × 24-hour periods)
- Restitution

#### Level 2 — Intermediate Dispositions (GS 7B-2506(13)–(23))
- Placement in a wilderness program
- Placement in a group home or residential treatment facility
- Electronic monitoring
- Intensive probation

#### Level 3 — Commitment (GS 7B-2506(24))
The only Level 3 option: **commitment to the Division of Juvenile Justice** for placement in a **Youth Development Center (YDC)** — a secure residential facility providing treatment, education, and rehabilitation.
- Minimum commitment: 6 months
- Maximum: Until age 18 (or 20 for offenses committed at age 17, per GS 7B-2513)
- YDCs are not prisons; they provide educational programming and therapeutic services

## 3. Transfer to Adult Superior Court

Transfer moves the case from juvenile court to adult criminal court, where the juvenile faces adult prosecution and potential adult sentences.

### Mandatory Transfer (GS 7B-2200.5)

As of December 1, 2024, **automatic/mandatory transfer** applies when:
- A 16- or 17-year-old is charged with a Class A–G felony — these cases **begin** in superior court (for Class A–E) or are mandatorily transferred (for F–G) after a finding that a bill of indictment has been returned
- A probable cause hearing must be held within **90 days**

### Discretionary Transfer (GS 7B-2200)

The court may transfer any juvenile age **13 or older** charged with a felony to superior court after:
1. Probable cause hearing (or waiver by the juvenile)
2. Consideration of statutory factors (seriousness of offense, culpability, amenability to treatment, community safety)

If transferred and convicted, the juvenile is sentenced under adult structured sentencing. If acquitted or charges dismissed, the case does not revert to juvenile court.

### Removal Back to Juvenile Court

SL 2024-17 created/expanded the **joint motion removal/remand** process:
- A joint motion by prosecutor AND defense attorney can return a Class A–E felony case to juvenile court from superior court
- If a joint motion is filed, removal is mandatory (the court must grant it)
- For ages 13–15 felonies in superior court, a joint motion can similarly remand to juvenile court

## 4. DSS Crossover Youth

"Crossover youth" are juveniles involved in both the delinquency system AND the child welfare/DSS system simultaneously. NC law addresses the intersection but maintains separation between the two systems:

- DSS custody can be ordered for a juvenile adjudicated **undisciplined** or, in limited circumstances, adjudicated **delinquent** when the juvenile needs more adequate care or supervision (GS 7B-2506(1))
- DSS custody **cannot be combined** with secure custody in a detention center or YDC commitment — these are mutually exclusive placements under GS 7B-2513 [^4]
- Crossover youth are among the most vulnerable — attorneys in delinquency cases should coordinate with DSS attorneys and guardians ad litem

## 5. Juvenile Record Confidentiality and Expunction

### Confidentiality of Juvenile Records

By statute:
- Juvenile delinquency adjudications are **NOT public records**
- Court files, social records, and agency records are confidential (GS 7B-3000)
- Court may not be disclosed to the public without a court order
- Juvenile hearings are generally **closed** to the public (GS 7B-2402)
- Juvenile adjudications generally **do not appear** on background checks available to employers or landlords

### Expunction of Juvenile Records

- A juvenile may petition for expunction of records after adjudication and successful completion of a disposition (GS 7B-3200)
- No expunction available while the juvenile is under court jurisdiction
- Petition filed in the district court where adjudication occurred
- Different from adult expunction under GS 15A-145 (see [nc-expungement-record-sealing](nc-expungement-record-sealing.md))

### Transfer Cases — Adult Record

If a case is transferred to adult superior court and results in a conviction, the defendant has an **adult criminal record** (not a juvenile record). Adult expunction rules apply, not juvenile confidentiality protections.

## 6. NC Office of the Juvenile Defender (OJD)

The **NC Office of the Juvenile Defender** (OJD), housed within NC Indigent Defense Services (IDS), provides: [^5]
- Training and technical assistance to juvenile defense attorneys
- Quality standards for juvenile representation
- Advocacy for systemic reform
- A "Juvenile Jurisdiction Decision Tree" flowchart to navigate the post-Raise the Age framework
- Website: <https://www.ncjuveniledefender.org/>

**Right to counsel:** Juveniles have a statutory right to appointed counsel in NC delinquency proceedings (GS 7B-2000). Counsel must be appointed before any court proceeding. Waiver of counsel is discouraged and requires careful inquiry by the court.

## 7. Undisciplined Juveniles

A separate category from delinquent juveniles. An "undisciplined juvenile" is a child who:
- Is habitually truant from school
- Is regularly disobedient to parents/guardians in ways affecting their welfare
- Has run away from home
- Is regularly found in places the law prohibits minors from being

Undisciplined adjudication dispositional options are more limited than delinquent — placement in DSS custody is an option; secure custody at YDC is generally not available.

## 8. Juvenile Drug Offenses

Juveniles charged with drug offenses are handled in **juvenile court** unless transferred. Key distinctions:
- No adult criminal record from juvenile adjudication
- Disposition may include drug treatment as a condition
- Juvenile drug treatment courts available in some counties (part of NC Recovery Courts system)
- See [nc-drug-treatment-courts](nc-drug-treatment-courts.md) for NC Recovery Courts overview
