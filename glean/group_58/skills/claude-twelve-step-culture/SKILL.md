# Twelve-Step Meeting Culture, Rituals & Speech Conventions

Ethnographic reference on AA meeting culture (with NA/Al-Anon variants), built for
verisimilitude. Per-claim confidence: **FACT** (3+ independent sources), **QUALIFIED** (2 or
bounded), **TENTATIVE** (single-source/contested). **LOCAL** marks customs that vary by
region/group · see the portability guide at the end.

**The organizing fact (highest-value insight):** almost none of the iconic AA rituals are
"official." AA publishes *suggested* formats; chips, slogans, the "Hi, Bill!" chorus, the
no-crosstalk rule, and hand-holding circles are **folk practice** governed by group autonomy
(Tradition Four) and "group conscience." AA's own archives note the Serenity Prayer, slogans,
and circle-and-triangle "have never had official recognition"; the strict crosstalk rules
circulating since 1992 were "erroneously attributed to World Service"; nothing in
Conference-approved literature tells members to introduce themselves as alcoholics at all; and
in **1993 the General Service Conference declared sobriety chips "a matter of local autonomy"**
and barred AA World Services from producing them.[^1][^2] Real members argue about these
customs · characters can too.

## 1. Meeting types · FACT

- **Open vs closed** is the primary axis (open = anyone; closed = "a desire to stop
  drinking"). Formats: **speaker** (story told as "what we were like, what happened, and what
  we are like now," 20–45 min), **discussion/topic** (chair picks from Big Book, 12&12,
  *Daily Reflections*, Grapevine; common topics: resentment, fear, gratitude, honesty,
  humility, amends, surrender, willingness), **Big Book study**, **step/tradition study**,
  **beginners** (Steps 1–3 or Q&A), plus business and group-inventory meetings.[^3]
- **Zoom-era conventions:** host + co-hosts (attested slang: "spiritual bouncer"),
  mute-on-entry, raise-hand queues (\*9/\*6 for phone users), 3-minute timers, first-name
  renaming, waiting rooms and lock-outs after Zoom-bombing. Verisimilitude gem: **unison
  responses fall apart on Zoom lag**, so many online groups drop the "Hi, Bill!" chorus and
  stay muted through the close.[^4] · **FACT**.

## 2. The ritual arc of a typical meeting · FACT (documented across independent intergroup scripts)

1. Chair: "This is the regular meeting of the ___ group of Alcoholics Anonymous. My name is
   ___ and I am an alcoholic."
2. Moment of silence ("to do with as you wish"), then the **Serenity Prayer** in unison.
3. Readings by pre-asked members: **AA Preamble**, **"How It Works"** (Big Book ch. 5 —
   "Rarely have we seen a person fail…", includes the Twelve Steps), often the Traditions;
   sometimes the **Ninth Step Promises** (optional/local · **QUALIFIED**).
4. **Newcomer ask:** "Anyone attending their first AA meeting ever? …not to embarrass you,
   it's so we can get to know you better." Some areas invite self-ID for the whole first 30
   days · **LOCAL**.
5. Chips/announcements where practiced (see §6).
6. **Seventh Tradition basket** with near-liturgical script: "We have no dues or fees…
   entirely self-supporting, declining outside contributions." Some Western groups pass a
   second "**pink can**" for Hospitals & Institutions literature · **LOCAL**.
7. Main body: shares / speaker / study.
8. **Closing anonymity statement:** "Anonymity is the spiritual foundation of all our
   traditions…" plus the placard couplet "**Who you see here, what you hear here, when you
   leave here, let it stay here.**" (Group response "Hear, hear!" is fellowship/group-specific
   · **TENTATIVE for AA; don't default it.**)
9. **Close:** Lord's Prayer (older/traditional, strongest US South/Midwest/East), Serenity
   Prayer, or the **Responsibility Declaration** ("I am responsible. When anyone, anywhere,
   reaches out for help, I want the hand of A.A. always to be there…" · adopted Toronto 1965 (provenance: standard AA history — QUALIFIED);
   newer/urban/secular-leaning). Standing in a **hand-holding circle** is the North American
   default but NOT universal (Japanese groups mostly sit). After the prayer many US groups
   chant "**Keep coming back — it works if you work it** (so work it, you're worth it)" · folk
   practice, absent from official literature.[^3][^5][^6]

Official AA's own gloss: "just a little bit of ceremony seems to go a long, long way in
A.A." (P-16).[^2]

## 3. Speech conventions

- **Identification ritual:** "Hi, my name is ___ and I'm an alcoholic." Room: "**Hi, ___!**"
  Universal US folk practice · and *nowhere mandated*: Cleveland's archives confirm nothing in
  Conference-approved literature prescribes it; Bill W. rarely introduced himself that
  way.[^1] Variants: "grateful recovering alcoholic," "alcoholic-addict" (contested under
  singleness-of-purpose). Opting out is acceptable: "I'm ___ and I'm just listening today."
  · **FACT** (practice + unofficial status).
- **Share etiquette:** raise hand / be called on; speak to the group, not a person; "I"
  statements; one share per meeting ("no double-dipping"); 3–5 minutes, timekeeper in big
  meetings. · **FACT**.
- **No crosstalk** · **FACT** for the custom, **FACT** that it has no official definition:
  GSO's Box 4-5-9 (Holiday 1994) traced the strict formulation to a 1992 central-office
  newsletter *erroneously attributed to World Service*.[^7] Working folk definition:
  interrupting; addressing a person rather than the group; giving advice about someone's
  share; commenting on/criticizing another's share; strict groups extend it to "when you
  talked about X it reminded me…" and even mid-meeting comforting touches. Strictness varies
  wildly · a rural speaker meeting tolerates what a Manhattan meeting would gavel. The
  sanctioned substitute is the "**second story**": telling your own story to mirror the
  previous speaker's (conversation-analysis literature).[^8]
- **"Qualifying" / "a qualification"** · **NYC/East Coast dialect** for telling your story /
  leading the meeting ("Have you qualified yet?"); NY Intergroup's speaker forms use it
  officially. Elsewhere it's just "speaking."[^9] · **FACT**, regionally marked.
- **Story template:** "what it was like, what happened, and what it's like now" (Big Book
  p. 58); "experience, strength and hope" is the Preamble shorthand. "**Drunkalogue**" =
  mildly pejorative in-culture term for a war story long on drinking, short on recovery.[^10]
- **Slogan canon** (folk, none Conference-approved): One Day at a Time (ODAAT), Easy Does It,
  First Things First, Live and Let Live, Let Go and Let God, Keep It Simple, Progress Not
  Perfection, This Too Shall Pass, Keep Coming Back, It Works If You Work It, Fake It Till You
  Make It, HALT (Hungry/Angry/Lonely/Tired), HOW (Honest/Open-minded/Willing), Think Think
  Think, But For the Grace of God, "your best thinking got you here," "stick with the
  winners," "take what you need and leave the rest," "meeting makers make it." Register:
  offered as gentle redirect or comfort, hung as wall placards (ODAAT / Easy Does It / Think
  is the classic trio), never argued. Provenance: Easy Does It, First Things First, Live and
  Let Live appear in the first-edition Big Book; most others are untraceable oral
  tradition.[^1][^11] · **FACT**.
- **Slogan criticism (in-world texture):** "thought-terminating cliché" critiques from both
  ex-members and sympathetic insiders ("your best thinking got you here" as anti-intellectual;
  "it works if you work it" as blaming the member when the program fails). A realistic meeting
  has an old-timer who loves slogans and a newcomer who rolls their eyes.[^12]
  · **QUALIFIED-to-FACT** (contested framing, multiple sources).
- **"Take what you like and leave the rest"** · *official* in Al-Anon (Suggested Closing);
  folk slogan in AA.[^13] · **FACT**.

## 4. Sponsorship culture · FACT (official pamphlet P-15 + independents)

- Asking is informal: approach a compatible member after a meeting; "Most A.A.s are happy and
  grateful to receive such a request"; suggested sponsor profile = year-plus sober, has worked
  the Steps; **temporary sponsors** are a normal on-ramp; changing sponsors is acceptable.
  Relationship of equals ("just as Bill and Dr. Bob did"); core activity = working the Twelve
  Steps together; styles legitimately range from brusque tough-love to hand-holding; folk
  practice adds daily phone calls early on.[^14]
- **Same-sex sponsorship convention** with stated rationale ("we remain thoroughly human…");
  P-15 notes some gay/lesbian members choose an opposite-sex sponsor *for the same reason*;
  the 2025 UK revision reframes around attraction/safeguarding. · **FACT** (custom+rationale).
- **"13th stepping"** · recognized, named predatory pattern: a member with sobriety pursuing a
  newcomer (<1 year), defined by the power differential. Bogart & Pearce (2003, 55 women):
  ≥50% had experienced 13th-stepping behaviors; two reported rape by men met in AA. Folk
  countermeasures: "no relationships in your first year," women's meetings, same-sex
  sponsorship, old-timers running interference.[^15] · **FACT**.

## 5. Anonymity norms · FACT

- **First-names-only** is meeting-level custom; the official rule is subtler: using your own
  full name at a meeting is "your privilege"; the hard rules are never revealing *another*
  member's identity and keeping membership anonymous "at the level of press, radio and films"
  (now including social media: full name + face + "AA member" = a break). Dr. Bob's line: two
  ways to break the Tradition · splash your name publicly, or be *so* anonymous no drunk can
  find you.[^16]
- Traditions 11–12 are read aloud in many meetings; the 12&12's rationale dates to the 1941
  Saturday Evening Post boom ("100 percent anonymity" at the public level guards against
  member-messiahs and public relapses).[^16]

## 6. Sobriety time markers · FACT (system), FACT (unofficial status)

- **Sequence:** 24-hour chip ("desire chip"/"surrender chip"/white chip · for wanting to stop;
  re-taken after relapse) → 30/60/90 days → 6 and 9 months → yearly
  "birthdays"/"anniversaries" with bronze medallions and often cake.
- **Provenance:** chips are NOT in the Big Book and were never adopted by AA as a whole.
  Precursors: 1840s+ temperance medals; Sister Ignatia's Sacred Heart medallions in Akron
  ("return it before you drink again"); graduated-chip system attributed variously to
  Indianapolis 1942 (GSO archivist Nell Wing's memo) and Elmira, NY 1947; AA archives call the
  origin unknown. **1993 Conference: local autonomy; AAWS/Grapevine barred from producing
  them.**[^1][^17] · **FACT**.
- **AA chip colors are NOT standardized** · two conflicting vendor schemes circulate; safest
  details are white-for-newcomer and bronze-for-years. **NA key tags ARE standardized**
  (official NAWS product): Welcome white → 30d orange → 60d green → 90d red → 6mo blue → 9mo
  yellow → 1yr glow-in-the-dark → 18mo gray → years black. NA marks 18 months; AA generally
  doesn't.[^17][^18] · **FACT**. (The reverse of what most writers assume.)
- **Ceremony mechanics:** chair or "chip person" calls ascending increments; walk-up to
  applause and usually a hug; the 24-hour chip often offered last, framed as "the most
  important chip in the room"; medallion passed around the room to be held/"blessed" —
  **LOCAL**; monthly **cake/birthday nights** strong in the West/Southwest. Vocabulary:
  "birthday" (Midwest/West) vs "anniversary" (East Coast); natal birthday = "**belly button
  birthday**"; pre-90-days = "counting days."[^17][^19] · **FACT** general, **LOCAL** specifics.

## 7. Higher-power language · FACT

- Bedrock: Steps 3/11 "God *as we understood Him*"; Step 2 "a Power greater than ourselves";
  Big Book ch. 4 "We Agnostics." The softening has a documented history: atheist early member
  **Jim Burwell** forced the 1938–39 compromises ("as we understood Him" added; "on our knees"
  cut; "suggested as a program of recovery").[^20]
- Folk: G.O.D. = "Group Of Drunks" / "Good Orderly Direction"; the **doorknob** as proverbial
  minimal higher power (used earnestly, affectionately, and derisively); attested folk higher
  powers include the group, the ocean, Mae West.[^21]
- **Secular AA:** "We Agnostics"/"Beyond Belief" meetings; fastest-growing segment (~80→350
  listed groups worldwide during the Toronto fight); **Toronto intergroup delisted agnostic
  groups 2011 → Ontario Human Rights Tribunal complaint → 2017 settlement** (relisted; Steps
  left intact in listings). Some groups omit prayers entirely.[^22] · **FACT**.
- "Spiritual, not religious" is the self-description; some US courts treat mandated attendance
  as religious · **QUALIFIED**.

## 8. Coffee, service, and court cards · FACT

- **Coffee as sacrament** (official P-16: members "first 'felt like members' when they began
  making coffee"; 12&12 blesses it as "Twelfth Step work in the very best sense").
  **Service positions** (elected "trusted servants," rotation principle, graduated sobriety
  requirements): secretary, chair, treasurer ("prudent reserve"), GSR, literature person,
  **coffee maker** (often explicitly no sobriety requirement · the newcomer's job), greeter,
  chip person, timekeeper. "Service keeps you sober."[^2][^23]
- **Court-mandated attendees:** AA "cooperates but does not affiliate"; groups don't keep
  attendance records but the secretary usually signs a **court card** (first name/initials);
  some groups decline to sign; official literature reminds courts attendance guarantees
  nothing. Folk texture: "**slip signers**," the "**aisle of denial**," bolting after the
  signature; ~12% of members were introduced via courts/corrections (2022 survey). NA's
  parallel "chit" system rubber-stamps so no individual signs.[^24] · **FACT**.

## 9. NA / Al-Anon differences that matter · FACT

- **NA:** "I'm an **addict**" (never "addict and alcoholic" · NA WSB Bulletin #13 *officially*
  discourages mixed identification and the words "sober/sobriety/clean and sober"; NA words
  are "**clean**," "**clean time**," "recovery"); Step One = "powerless over *our addiction*"
  (not a substance); daily reader = "**Just for Today**"; Basic Text (1983) vs Big Book
  (1939); key tags vs chips; huggier register; skews younger. A character saying "clean and
  sober" in an NA meeting marks themselves as an AA crossover or rehab graduate.[^18][^25]
- **Al-Anon** (friends/family): official Suggested Closing carries three portable set-phrases
  · "Take what you liked and leave the rest"; "Whatever your problems, there are those among
  us who have had them, too"; "let there be no gossip or criticism of one another"; many
  groups add the Declaration ("…Let It Begin with Me").[^13]

## 10. Storytelling scholarship (the ethnographic canon)

- **Cain 1991** (*Ethos*): the AA personal story is a cultural genre through which members
  *acquire the identity* of "alcoholic" · learning to tell your life as an AA story is how the
  identity is internalized.[^26]
- **Humphreys 2000**: five types of AA stories; community narrative ↔ personal story interplay
  (Rappaport 1993 frames the theory).[^27]
- **Strobbe & Kurtz 2012**: Big Book stories follow a five-stage arc · early drinking →
  alcoholic regression → hitting bottom → progress in AA → stable sobriety.[^28]
- **Conversation analysis** (Arminen; Helsinki school): meetings run on extended monologic
  turns with ordinary conversation suspended; "second stories" are the sanctioned
  identification mechanism; the turn system is institutionally *symmetrical* · egalitarian by
  design, which is how conflict is managed without hierarchy.[^8]
- **Mäkelä et al. 1996** (8-country WHO-affiliated study): substantial cross-national
  variability in ritual detail · the meeting as "speech event."[^29]
- Critical strand for balance: Madsen's "crisis cult" (1974), commitment-mechanism analyses,
  cult-critique polemics · contested but part of the discourse.[^12]

## Portability guide · LOCAL vs UNIVERSAL (the writer's cheat sheet)

**Safely portable (any US meeting):** open/closed distinction; Preamble; Serenity Prayer
somewhere; "How It Works" read aloud; "Hi, I'm ___, I'm an alcoholic" + "Hi ___!";
no-crosstalk custom (strictness varies); raise-hand 3–5 min shares; 7th Tradition basket +
"no dues or fees" script; sponsors; same-sex sponsorship suggestion; "who you see here…" in
some form; coffee urn + folding chairs + slogan placards; "keep coming back"; the
what-it-was-like arc; first-name culture; court slips existing.

**Regional/local · specify with care, or use deliberately to place a scene:**
- Lord's Prayer close = older/traditional/South/Midwest/East; Responsibility Declaration or
  Serenity close = newer/urban/secular-leaning; Japan sits, no hand-holding.
- Chips at all, chip colors, cake nights, medallion-blessing pass: local autonomy (explicit
  1993 Conference action); East = "anniversaries," West/South = "birthdays."
- "Qualify/qualification" = NYC/East Coast dialect.
- "Hear, hear!" response = group-specific; don't default into an AA scene.
- Pink can (H&I) = Western US. Newcomer 30-day self-ID = local. "Alcoholic-addict" self-ID =
  tolerated some places, side-eyed elsewhere.
- Zoom: dropped unison responses, chat-fellowship, "spiritual bouncer" co-hosts.

## Anti-patterns · what writers get wrong

1. Treating chips, slogans, or the intro chorus as official AA liturgy → all folk practice;
   the 1993 Conference explicitly made chips local autonomy.
2. Standardized AA chip colors → AA colors vary by region/vendor; it's NA key tags that are
   standardized.
3. A universal strict no-crosstalk rule → custom with no official definition; strictness
   varies wildly by group.
4. Everyone must say "I'm an alcoholic" → nowhere required; "just listening today" is fine.
5. One national meeting script → group autonomy means real variance; regional dialect markers
   (qualify, birthday/anniversary, closing prayer) can place a scene geographically in one
   line.
6. NA characters saying "sober" → NA officially discourages it; "clean" is the word.
