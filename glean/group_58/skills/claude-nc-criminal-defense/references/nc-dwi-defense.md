# NC DWI Defense

> **Educational only — NOT legal advice.** For any specific DWI case, consult a licensed North Carolina attorney.

North Carolina DWI law is among the most complex misdemeanor (and sometimes felony) criminal areas in the state. The offense definition, chemical testing procedures, sentencing regime, administrative license consequences, and restoration pathway each operate under distinct statutory frameworks. This reference covers the full defense lifecycle: from the traffic stop through license restoration.

`verified-as-of: 2026-06-22`

---

## 1. The Offense: GS 20-138.1

Under **G.S. 20-138.1(a)**, a person commits impaired driving if they drive any vehicle on a highway, street, or public vehicular area (PVA) while:

1. **Under the influence** of an impairing substance — the "appreciable impairment" standard: the substance must impair the person's mental or physical faculties to an appreciable degree; **or**
2. **Per se BAC**: after consuming alcohol such that the person has **alcohol concentration (AC) of 0.08 or more** at any relevant time after driving; **or**
3. **Per se controlled substance**: any amount of a **Schedule I controlled substance** or its metabolites is present in the person's blood or urine.

**Key limitations:**
- Legal entitlement to use alcohol or a drug is **not a defense** (GS 20-138.1(c)).
- "Any vehicle" covers motor vehicles, bicycles, and mopeds operated on public highways or PVAs.
- "Relevant time after driving" typically means the time of testing; rising-blood-alcohol arguments attack whether the AC was 0.08 *at the time of driving*.

### Related Offenses
| Statute | Offense | Notes |
|---------|---------|-------|
| GS 20-138.2 | Impaired driving in commercial vehicle | CDL holders: 0.04 threshold |
| GS 20-138.3 | Driving after consuming — under 21 | 0.01 threshold |
| GS 20-138.4 | Impaired driving in school bus/child care |
| GS 20-138.5 | **Habitual impaired driving** (felony) | 3+ DWI convictions within 10 years → Class F felony, minimum 12 months active (non-suspendable), **permanent license revocation** |

## 2. Implied Consent & Chemical Testing (GS 20-16.2)

### Implied Consent Rule
Any person who drives on NC roads **is deemed to have consented** to a chemical analysis if charged with an implied consent offense. This is automatic — no separate agreement is needed. [^1]

### Pre-Test Rights (GS 20-16.2(a))
Before testing, the officer must advise the driver of:
- The right to refuse, and that refusal results in **30-day civil revocation** and **12-month willful refusal revocation**
- The right to call an attorney and to select a witness to observe the testing
- That testing may not be delayed for these purposes **longer than 30 minutes** from the time rights are notified

### Chemical Analysis Options
| Test Type | Authority | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Breath (Intox EC/IR II) | GS 20-16.2 | Evidentiary; must be performed by Licensed Chemical Analyst (LCA) |
| Blood draw | GS 20-139.1 | Requires warrant post-*Missouri v. McNeely* (2013); chain of custody critical |
| Urine | GS 20-139.1 | Primarily for controlled substances |
| Portable breathalyzer (PBT) | GS 20-16.3 | **Roadside screening only** — NOT admissible for guilt; refusal does NOT trigger license suspension |

### Willful Refusal
- Refusal of the **evidentiary** breath, blood, or urine test is "willful refusal."
- Triggers **12-month license revocation** (separate from any criminal proceeding).
- Willfulness is a factual question — medical inability to provide a breath sample is a defense.

## 3. The Intoximeter EC/IR II

North Carolina's evidentiary breath instrument is the **Intoximeter EC/IR II** (electrochemical + infrared dual-sensor technology). [^2]

### How It Works
- **Infrared (IR)** sensor measures real-time ethanol by absorption; detects mouth alcohol through a **slope detector** (negative slope = mouth-alcohol flag).
- **Electrochemical (EC) fuel cell** quantifies alcohol concentration in the breath sample.
- Result converted to blood alcohol via a **blood-breath partition ratio** of **2100:1** (the statutory ratio); actual individual ratios range 1900–2400, creating inherent variability. [^3]

### Mandatory Procedures for Admissibility
1. **15-minute observation period** — operator must observe continuously that the subject does not eat, drink, smoke, vomit, regurgitate, or put anything in the mouth. Restart the clock if any event occurs.
2. **Licensed Chemical Analyst (LCA)** — operator must hold current LCA certification from NC DHHS.
3. **Two breath samples** required; results must agree within 0.02 g/210L.
4. **Calibration checks** — instrument must be calibrated per NC DHHS standards; maintenance logs are discoverable.
5. **Simulator solution** — blank air tests and simulator calibration solution checks before and after samples.

### Common Defense Challenges to Breath Results
| Challenge | Basis |
|-----------|-------|
| Observation period violation | Mouth alcohol contamination → falsely elevated result |
| LCA certification lapsed or invalid | Operator not qualified |
| Calibration log defects | Instrument out of tolerance |
| Partition ratio defense | Individual physiology below 2100:1 |
| Rising BAC | AC below 0.08 *at time of driving* even if above at testing |
| Medical conditions | GERD, diabetes (ketones), COPD (physiological effects) |
| Mouth alcohol (undetected) | Dental appliances, recent mouthwash, acid reflux |

## 4. Civil License Revocation (GS 20-16.5)

### Immediate Civil Revocation at Initial Appearance
A **magistrate must** revoke the license immediately if all four criteria are met: [^4]
1. Officer has reasonable grounds to believe an implied consent offense occurred.
2. Person is charged with the offense.
3. Proper chemical analysis procedures were followed.
4. Person either **willfully refused** OR had AC of 0.08+, 0.04+ (commercial vehicle), or Schedule I metabolite.

**Duration:** At least 30 days (until the $100 civil revocation fee is paid to the clerk).

### Contesting Civil Revocation
- Request hearing (AOC-CVR-5) **at initial appearance or within 10 days**; missing this window forfeits the right.
- Hearing within **3 working days** (before magistrate) or **5 working days** (before district court judge).
- License remains revoked during hearing.

### Limited Driving Privilege — Pre-Trial
- Available after **10 days** of the civil revocation if the person:
  - Obtains a **substance abuse assessment** (G-CSAS or SSARS-certified provider).
  - Enrolls in recommended treatment.
  - Has no previous DWI conviction within 7 years.
  - Has a valid license.
- Restricted to essential purposes (work, medical, school, court, church) with specified hours.

### Willful Refusal — DMV Administrative Hearing
- DMV revokes license for **12 months** for willful refusal.
- Driver may request a **DMV willful refusal hearing** (separate from the criminal case and the civil revocation hearing).
- DMV hearing officer serves as both prosecutor and judge — not bound by Rules of Evidence.
- Issues decided: Was the person charged? Did proper chemical testing rights notification occur? Was the refusal willful?

## 5. DWI Sentencing (GS 20-179)

DWI sentencing is **completely separate** from the regular NC Structured Sentencing system. The DWI-specific framework has six levels. [^5]

### Step 1: Identify Grossly Aggravating Factors (GAFs)
*State proves GAFs beyond a reasonable doubt.*

| GAF | Description |
|-----|-------------|
| GAF 1 | Prior impaired driving conviction within **7 years** (before or after offense date but before sentencing; each conviction = separate GAF) |
| GAF 2 | Driving with **revoked license** under GS 20-28(a1) |
| GAF 3 | **Serious injury** to another person caused by the impaired driving |
| GAF 4 | **Child under 18**, person with child-level mental development, or person with disability preventing unaided exit, present in the vehicle |

### Step 2: Determine Mandatory Level Based on GAFs
| GAF Count/Type | Mandatory Level |
|----------------|-----------------|
| 3 or more GAFs | **Aggravated Level One** |
| GAF 4 present, OR 2 other GAFs | **Level One** |
| Exactly 1 other GAF (no GAF 4) | **Level Two** |
| **No GAFs** → proceed to factor weighing | Level Three/Four/Five |

### Step 3: Weigh Aggravating vs. Mitigating Factors (No-GAF Cases)
*State proves aggravating factors beyond reasonable doubt; defendant proves mitigating factors by preponderance.*

**Selected Aggravating Factors (GS 20-179(d)):**
- "Gross impairment" or AC ≥ **0.15** (conclusive — not modifiable)
- Especially reckless or dangerous driving
- Negligent driving causing reportable accident
- Driving with revoked license
- 2+ prior motor vehicle convictions (≥3 points or revocation) within 5 years
- Speeding to flee (GS 20-141.5) or 30 mph+ over limit (GS 20-141)
- Passing stopped school bus (GS 20-217)

**Selected Mitigating Factors (GS 20-179(e)):**
- Slight impairment; AC ≤ 0.09
- Slight impairment; no chemical analysis available
- Safe, lawful driving except for impairment
- Safe driving record (no conviction ≥4 points or revocation in 5 years)
- Impairment from lawfully prescribed drug at prescribed dosage
- Voluntary mental health facility admission and treatment
- Substance abuse assessment completion + 60+ days continuous abstinence monitoring
- Pre-trial ignition interlock use (6+ months, ≤0.02 violations)

**Result:**
- Aggravating factors substantially outweigh mitigating → **Level Three**
- Factors roughly balanced or absent → **Level Four**
- Mitigating substantially outweigh aggravating → **Level Five**

### Sentencing Level Ranges
| Level | Min Imprisonment | Max Imprisonment | Max Fine | Notes |
|-------|-----------------|-----------------|----------|-------|
| Aggravated Level One | 12 months | 36 months | $10,000 | Not parole eligible; active term |
| Level One | 30 days | 24 months | $4,000 | Special probation: ≥30 days; min reducible to 10 days with continuous monitoring |
| Level Two | 7 days | 12 months | $2,000 | Special probation: ≥7 days or 90-day abstinence monitoring |
| Level Three | 72 hours | 6 months | $1,000 | Sentence may be suspended |
| Level Four | 48 hours | 120 days | $500 | Sentence may be suspended |
| Level Five | 24 hours | 60 days | $200 | Sentence may be suspended |

**Note:** Alcoholism, drug addiction, and mental disease are **explicitly excluded** as mitigating factors (GS 20-179(e)).

## 6. Ignition Interlock Device (IID) Requirements (GS 20-17.8)

### Mandatory IID Triggers
- AC **≥ 0.15** at the time of the offense → IID required for license restoration.
- **Second DWI conviction** within 7 years → IID required.
- **Habitual DWI** (GS 20-138.5) → permanent revocation; IID not a path to restoration.

### IID Requirements
- Device calibrated and checked every **30 days**.
- Fail-point set at AC **0.02** — attempting to start the vehicle at or above this level is a violation.
- Rolling retests required at random intervals while driving.
- Data logs reviewed at each calibration visit; violations may trigger revocation extension.

### IID Duration
| Original Revocation Period | IID Duration |
|---------------------------|--------------|
| 1 year | 1 year from restoration |
| 4 years | 3 years from restoration |
| Permanent (partial restoration) | 7 years from restoration |

### Defense Issues with IID Violations
- Calibration records are discoverable; faulty calibration may explain apparent violations.
- Multiple false positives (from food, mouthwash, hand sanitizer) can be challenged with logs.
- Missed rolling retest (failure to provide sample) is treated as a violation even without AC evidence.

## 7. License Revocation & Restoration

### Administrative Revocations by Situation
| Situation | Revocation Period |
|-----------|------------------|
| Civil revocation at arrest (GS 20-16.5) | 30 days (min) |
| Willful refusal of chemical test | 12 months |
| First DWI conviction | 1 year |
| Second DWI conviction within 3 years | 4 years |
| Third+ DWI conviction within 10 years | permanent |
| Habitual DWI conviction (GS 20-138.5) | permanent |

### Restoration Requirements After DWI Conviction
1. Complete revocation period.
2. Substance abuse assessment (DHHS-approved) and completion of recommended treatment.
3. Pay $100 civil revocation fee (if applicable).
4. IID installation (if required).
5. Pay restoration fees to NC DMV.

> **Expungement note:** DWI convictions under GS 20-138.1 are **not eligible for expungement** under North Carolina law. GS 15A-145.5(c)(2) expressly excludes impaired driving offenses. Habitual DWI (GS 20-138.5) is likewise ineligible. See [nc-expungement-record-sealing](nc-expungement-record-sealing.md) for full eligibility rules.

### Limited Driving Privilege — Post-Conviction (GS 20-179.3)
- Available for Level 3/4/5 convictions and some Level 1/2 convictions after serving minimum active term.
- Must complete substance abuse assessment and any recommended treatment.
- IID required if AC ≥ 0.15.
- Restricted hours and purposes.
- **2024 changes** expanded eligibility for post-conviction limited driving privileges, creating pathways for more offenders who previously would have been ineligible. [^6]

## 8. Felony DWI: Habitual Impaired Driving (GS 20-138.5)

- **Trigger:** 3+ impaired driving convictions within **10 years** of the current offense date.
- **Classification:** Class F felony.
- **Sentence:** Minimum **12 months active** (mandatory; cannot be suspended, probated, or deferred).
- **License:** **Permanent revocation** — DMV does not restore for habitual DWI absent extraordinary circumstances.
- Habitual DWI is prosecuted in **Superior Court** (not District Court).
- Prior convictions must be proven — records from other states qualify.

---

## 9. Substance Abuse Assessment & Treatment

Under GS 20-17.6, any person convicted of DWI must:
1. Obtain a **substance abuse assessment** from a NC DHHS–certified assessment site (G-CSAS standard).
2. Complete any recommended education or treatment program (typically **ADETS** — Alcohol and Drug Education Traffic Schools, or more intensive treatment).
3. Proof of completion required before license restoration.

This assessment also applies as a **condition of limited driving privilege** (pretrial and post-conviction).

## 10. DWI and Specific Populations

### Under-21 Drivers
- **GS 20-138.3**: It is unlawful for a person under 21 to drive a vehicle with any alcohol concentration (0.01 threshold).
- No impairment need be proven — purely per se.
- Civil revocation applies; standard implied consent procedures required.

### Commercial Motor Vehicles (CDL)
- **GS 20-138.2**: 0.04 BAC threshold for CMV impaired driving.
- CDL disqualification for 1 year (first offense); lifetime for second offense or transporting hazardous materials.
- CDL disqualification is **separate** from personal license revocation.

### Drugs and Impaired Driving
- Any Schedule I controlled substance (or metabolite) in blood or urine = per se impaired (GS 20-138.1(a)(3)).
- Other drugs (Schedule II-VI, prescription, OTC) prosecuted under the "appreciable impairment" standard.
- Drug Recognition Experts (DRE) — officers with specialized training in observing drug impairment; DRE evaluations are admissible but subject to challenge on foundation and qualification.

## 11. Defense Strategy Overview

| Stage | Key Defense Actions |
|-------|---------------------|
| Traffic stop validity | Challenge reasonable suspicion (4th Amendment / Art. I §20 NC Const.) |
| Field sobriety tests (FSTs) | Challenge standardized conditions, medical factors; FSTs are voluntary |
| Implied consent rights | Verify proper advisement timing and content |
| Observation period | Document any gaps; challenge admissibility of breath result |
| Chemical test procedures | Obtain LCA certification, calibration logs, maintenance records via discovery |
| Sentencing factors | Pursue mitigating factors; pre-trial interlock; substance abuse evaluation timing |
| Civil revocation | File CVR-5 hearing request within 10 days; seek limited driving privilege |
| DMV willful refusal hearing | Challenge whether refusal was truly "willful" |
