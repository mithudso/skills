# NC Bond Forfeiture & Remission (GS 15A-544.1 through 15A-547.1)

> **Educational only, NOT legal advice.** verified-as-of: 2026-06-22

When a defendant fails to appear in NC, the court may enter a bond forfeiture — a civil action requiring the surety (bail bondsman or accommodation bondsman) to pay the bond amount to the State. GS 15A-544.1 through 15A-544.8 establish the complete forfeiture process, including notice, deadlines, grounds for relief, and civil enforcement.

---

## 1. Forfeiture Jurisdiction (GS 15A-544.1)

- The **court in which the case is pending** has jurisdiction over bond forfeiture matters
- For district court cases: the district court judge handles forfeiture proceedings
- For superior court cases: the superior court judge
- Forfeiture proceedings are civil in nature even though they arise in criminal cases

---

## 2. Bond Identification Requirements (GS 15A-544.2)

Before a bond may be subject to forfeiture, it must contain:
- Full name and address of the defendant
- Full name and address of each surety
- The amount of the bond
- The case number(s) to which it applies

Bonds missing required identifying information may have forfeiture challenged on that basis.

---

## 3. Entry of Forfeiture (GS 15A-544.3)

### When Forfeiture Is Entered
Forfeiture is entered when:
1. The defendant fails to appear at a required court date
2. The court makes a "call and fail" — calls the case, the defendant does not appear, and the court orders forfeiture

### Timing Requirement
- Forfeiture must be **entered within 150 days** of the failure to appear
- Entry of forfeiture triggers the notice clock

### What "Entry" Means
The court announces the forfeiture in open court and enters a written forfeiture order in the case record. This is distinct from a **final judgment** of forfeiture — the forfeiture becomes final only after the notice and set-aside period has run.

---

## 4. Notice of Forfeiture (GS 15A-544.4)

### Who Receives Notice
The clerk of court must send notice of forfeiture by **first-class mail** to:
- The defendant at their last known address of record
- **Each surety** who signed the bond (including the bail bondsman)

### Timing
Notice must be mailed **no later than 30 days** after the call and fail / forfeiture entry.

### Significance of Notice
The notice date starts the **150-day clock** for filing a motion to set aside forfeiture (GS 15A-544.5). Missing the notice deadline can be grounds to challenge the forfeiture.

---

## 5. Setting Aside Forfeiture (GS 15A-544.5) — The Primary Relief Vehicle

### Deadline
A written motion to set aside must be filed **within 150 days** of the date notice was mailed (not 150 days from the failure to appear).

### Who May File
- The **defendant**
- Any **surety** on the bond (typically the bail bondsman)

### Form and Service
- Motion must be **in writing**
- Must state the reason(s) for setting aside
- Must attach supporting evidence
- Service on the district attorney's office required

### Statutory Grounds (Mandatory Set-Aside)

Setting aside is **mandatory** (not discretionary) if the defendant or surety establishes any of the following:

| Ground | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Defendant surrendered to court or law enforcement** | Must occur before or during the 150-day period |
| **Defendant incarcerated in NC** | Currently in state custody |
| **Defendant incarcerated in another jurisdiction** | Federal or out-of-state custody |
| **Defendant's serious illness or incapacity** | Must prevent appearance; documentation required |
| **Defendant's death** | Death certificate required |
| **Inability to appear due to condition outside defendant's control** | Catch-all for truly involuntary non-appearance |
| **Any other reason the court finds just and proper** | Court has discretion on this catch-all ground |

**Key rule:** If a listed statutory ground is shown, the court **must** set aside the forfeiture — it has no discretion to refuse.

### Effect of Granted Motion
- Forfeiture is set aside and **may never become a final judgment**
- Bond is reinstated; defendant may be released on the same bond or new conditions set
- The forfeiture "shall not thereafter ever become a final judgment of forfeiture or be enforced or reported to the Department of Insurance"

### Hearing Procedure
- Rules of evidence do not apply
- Court may consider any competent evidence
- Burden on the movant to show statutory grounds

---

## 6. Final Judgment of Forfeiture (GS 15A-544.6)

If the forfeiture is **not set aside** within 150 days of notice:
- The forfeiture automatically becomes a **final judgment**
- The date of finality is the later of:
  - The date the 150-day period expires without a motion
  - The date the court denies a timely-filed motion

Final judgment is enforceable as a civil money judgment against the surety.

---

## 7. Civil Enforcement (GS 15A-544.7)

Once a final judgment of forfeiture exists:
- The clerk **dockets** the judgment in the civil judgment docket
- Enforcement proceeds like any other civil money judgment (liens on real property, execution on assets)
- The judgment is against the **surety** (bail bondsman or accommodation bondsman), not the defendant individually
- The **NC Department of Insurance is notified** — this can affect bondsman's license capacity

---

## 8. Relief from Final Judgment (GS 15A-544.8)

Even after a final judgment, the court **may** (in its discretion) grant relief if:
- The defendant is surrendered after judgment
- The court finds "substantial justice" requires relief

**This is discretionary relief** — unlike the mandatory set-aside under GS 15A-544.5. Courts typically consider:
- Whether the surety acted in good faith to locate the defendant
- How long after judgment the defendant was surrendered
- The nature of the original offense

---

## 9. Remission for Non-Incarceration Sentences (GS 15A-547.1)

A separate remission provision applies **after conviction:**

If a defendant is convicted and sentenced to:
- **Community punishment** (probation, community service, etc.), OR
- **Intermediate punishment** (supervised probation, house arrest, etc.)

— i.e., **not active incarceration** — the court **may** (discretionary) remit the bail bond funds to the surety. The rationale: the defendant's bail obligation has served its purpose (securing appearance through trial), and ongoing financial burden on the surety is no longer necessary.

---

## 10. Failure to Appear Criminal Charge (GS 15A-543)

Distinct from bond forfeiture (a civil consequence against the surety), a defendant who **willfully** fails to appear commits a separate **criminal offense:**

| Underlying charge | FTA offense |
|---|---|
| Misdemeanor | Class 1 misdemeanor |
| Felony | Class I felony |

Both the bond forfeiture and the FTA criminal charge may proceed simultaneously.

## Forfeiture Timeline Summary

```
DEFENDANT FAILS TO APPEAR
        │
        ▼
COURT ENTERS FORFEITURE (within 150 days of FTA)
        │
        ▼
CLERK MAILS NOTICE TO DEFENDANT & SURETY (within 30 days of forfeiture entry)
        │
        ▼ ← 150-day clock starts from notice mailing date
MOTION TO SET ASIDE (if filed within 150 days)
  ├─ Statutory ground shown → MANDATORY set-aside → bond reinstated
  └─ No ground / denied → FINAL JUDGMENT on later of:
                            (a) 150-day expiration, or
                            (b) date motion denied
                                    │
                                    ▼
                          CIVIL DOCKETING & ENFORCEMENT
                          (DOI notified; license capacity affected)
                                    │
                                    ▼
                          POST-JUDGMENT RELIEF (GS 15A-544.8)
                          — discretionary; surrender + substantial justice
```

---

## Anti-Patterns / Common Errors

- **Missing the 150-day window:** The clock runs from **notice mailing date**, not from the failure to appear — many sureties miss this distinction
- **Treating mandatory and discretionary grounds interchangeably:** If a listed statutory ground exists (death, incarceration, illness), set-aside is mandatory; if relying on the "just and proper" catch-all, it is discretionary
- **Failing to serve the DA:** A motion to set aside must be served on the district attorney; defective service can result in dismissal
- **Assuming DOI notification is automatic:** NC DOI is notified of final judgments, which can affect the bondsman's license capacity ratio — a final judgment may require the bondsman to reduce outstanding bonds or deposit additional security
- **Overlooking the criminal FTA charge:** Clients sometimes focus only on the bond forfeiture and miss that willful non-appearance is a separate criminal offense running concurrently
