# NC Drug Paraphernalia Law (GS 90-113.20–90-113.24, Article 5B)

> **Educational only, NOT legal advice.** Verified-as-of: 2026-06-22.

North Carolina's drug paraphernalia statutes are codified in **GS Chapter 90, Article 5B** (GS 90-113.20 through 90-113.24). Article 5B is separate from the main Controlled Substances Act but closely related to it. Paraphernalia charges frequently accompany drug possession or distribution charges.

---

## Definition of Drug Paraphernalia (GS 90-113.21)

"Drug paraphernalia" means all equipment, products, and materials of any kind that are used, intended for use, or designed for use in:
- Planting, propagating, cultivating, growing, harvesting, manufacturing, compounding, converting, producing, processing, preparing, testing, analyzing, packaging, repackaging, storing, containing, concealing, injecting, ingesting, inhaling, or otherwise introducing into the human body a controlled substance

**Examples (GS 90-113.21(b)):**
- Kits for planting, propagating, growing marijuana
- Kits for manufacturing, compounding or processing controlled substances
- Testing equipment for identifying strength, effectiveness, or purity of controlled substances
- Scales and balances for weighing or measuring controlled substances
- Dilutants and adulterants for cutting controlled substances
- Separation gins and sifters for marijuana
- Blenders, bowls, containers, spoons and mixing devices used for manufacturing controlled substances
- Capsules, balloons, envelopes, and other containers for packaging small quantities
- Containers and other objects for storing or concealing controlled substances
- Hypodermic syringes, needles, and other objects for parenterally injecting substances
- Pipes, screens, carburetor pipes, bongs, and smoking devices primarily intended for marijuana, hashish, or cocaine use

### Factors Courts Consider in Characterizing Paraphernalia (GS 90-113.21(c)):
1. Statements by owner or person in control
2. Proximity to controlled substances
3. Existence of residue
4. Direct or circumstantial evidence of intent
5. Instructions for use
6. Descriptive material in proximity
7. Advertising concerning use
8. Manner of display for sale
9. Local community standards

## Offenses and Penalties

### GS 90-113.22 — Possession of Drug Paraphernalia

Unlawful to use or possess with intent to use drug paraphernalia to:
- Plant, propagate, cultivate, grow, harvest
- Manufacture, compound, convert, produce, process, prepare
- Test, analyze, package, repackage, store, contain, conceal
- Inject, ingest, inhale, or otherwise introduce into the human body a controlled substance

**Penalty:** Class 1 misdemeanor (up to 120 days jail + fines)

### GS 90-113.22A — Possession of Marijuana Paraphernalia

Separate, lower-level offense specifically for marijuana paraphernalia:

**Penalty:** **Class 3 misdemeanor** (lower than GS 90-113.22)

This distinction mirrors the Schedule VI / marijuana-specific tiered approach in the main possession statute.

### GS 90-113.23 — Manufacture or Delivery of Drug Paraphernalia

Unlawful to sell, deliver, possess with intent to deliver, or manufacture with intent to deliver drug paraphernalia:

**Penalty:** Class 1 misdemeanor **except:**
- **Delivery by adult (18+) to minor (under 18) who is at least 3 years younger:** **Class I felony** (elevation applies regardless of whether the substance is marijuana or another controlled substance)

### GS 90-113.24 — Advertisement of Drug Paraphernalia

Unlawful to place in a newspaper, magazine, handbill, or other publication an advertisement, knowing that the purpose is to promote the sale of drug paraphernalia:

**Penalty:** Class 2 misdemeanor

## Penalty Summary

| Offense | Statute | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Possession (general controlled substances) | GS 90-113.22 | Class 1 misdemeanor |
| Possession (marijuana-specific) | GS 90-113.22A | Class 3 misdemeanor |
| Manufacture/delivery | GS 90-113.23 | Class 1 misdemeanor |
| Delivery by adult to minor (3+ yrs younger) | GS 90-113.23 | Class I felony |
| Advertisement | GS 90-113.24 | Class 2 misdemeanor |

## Conditional Discharge for Paraphernalia (GS 90-96)

Possession of drug paraphernalia under GS 90-113.22 and GS 90-113.22A are specifically listed as qualifying offenses for conditional discharge under GS 90-96 (first-offense program). First-time offenders with no prior drug or felony convictions may receive a deferred prosecution leading to dismissal. See [nc-conditional-discharge-90-96](nc-conditional-discharge-90-96.md).

## Syringe Exchange Program Exemption

Participants and staff of NC's authorized syringe exchange programs (SSPs) under GS 90-113.27 are **immune from prosecution** for possession of hypodermic needles, syringes, or other injection-related paraphernalia obtained through or related to the SSP. This exemption intersects with NC harm reduction law. See [nc-harm-reduction-laws](nc-harm-reduction-laws.md) in the nc-drug-rehabilitation-treatment hub for full SSP details.
