# NC Sex Crime Defense

> **Educational only — NOT legal advice.** For any specific sex offense case, consult a licensed North Carolina attorney.

Sex offense charges in North Carolina carry some of the most severe consequences in the criminal justice system: lengthy sentences, mandatory sex offender registration, residency restrictions, and lifetime collateral consequences. Defense in these cases requires knowledge of specific statutes (GS Chapter 14, Article 7B), registration requirements (GS Chapter 14, Article 27A), and constitutional limits on prosecution.

## 1. NC Sex Offenses — GS Chapter 14, Article 7B

NC reorganized its sex offense statutes, effective December 1, 2015, renumbering them as Article 7B (GS 14-27.20 through 14-27.36). Key offenses:

### Rape Offenses

| Statute | Offense | Classification |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| GS 14-27.21 | First-degree rape (vaginal intercourse by force; victim helpless; victim under 13, defendant 12+ and 4+ years older) | Class B1 felony |
| GS 14-27.22 | Second-degree rape (vaginal intercourse by force against victim's will; victim mentally disabled/incapacitated/physically helpless) | Class C felony |

### Sexual Offense (Non-Vaginal)

| Statute | Offense | Classification |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| GS 14-27.26 | First-degree sexual offense (same triggers as first-degree rape) | Class B1 felony |
| GS 14-27.27 | Second-degree sexual offense | Class C felony |

### Statutory Offenses

| Statute | Offense | Classification |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| GS 14-27.25(a) | Statutory rape (vaginal intercourse with person under 15; defendant 6+ years older) | Class B1 felony |
| GS 14-27.25(b) | Statutory rape (vaginal intercourse with person 15 or 16; defendant 12+ years older) | Class C felony |
| GS 14-27.30 | Statutory sexual offense (same age structure, non-vaginal) | Class B1 or C felony |

### Close-in-Age Exemption ("Romeo and Juliet")

NC does not have a standalone Romeo-and-Juliet statute, but GS 14-27.25(b) and GS 14-27.30(b) embed close-in-age protections: [^1]
- A **12–4 year age gap rule**: No prosecution for consensual sex between persons who are at least 12 years old and **no more than 4 years apart in age**
- Example: A 17-year-old and a 15-year-old with a 2-year age gap cannot be prosecuted for statutory rape
- The exemption does NOT apply if one party is under 12

### Other Key Offenses

| Statute | Offense | Classification |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| GS 14-27.31 | Sex act with student by teacher/school official | Class G felony |
| GS 14-27.32 | Sexual activity with a student by substitute teacher | Class I felony |
| GS 14-202.1 | Taking indecent liberties with a child | Class F felony |
| GS 14-202.3 | Solicitation of child by computer (GS 14-202.3) | Class H felony |
| GS 14-208.11A | Registered sex offender violating restrictions | Class F or H felony |

## 2. Sex Offender Registration (SORN) — GS Chapter 14, Article 27A

### Registration Requirement

Conviction of a "reportable conviction" under GS 14-208.6(4) requires **lifetime registration** with NC Sex Offender and Public Protection Registration Program, administered by NC SBI and local sheriffs. [^2]

### Who Must Register

- Any person convicted in NC of a qualifying sex offense (felonies and some misdemeanors)
- Any person who moves to NC after conviction in another jurisdiction
- Juveniles adjudicated for certain offenses who are transferred to adult court

### Registration Obligations

- Register within **3 business days** of release from incarceration, change of address, or moving to NC
- In-person registration with sheriff of county of residence
- Report changes (address, employment, vehicle, online identifiers) within 3 days
- **Annual verification** in person with sheriff
- Passport must be marked

### Residency and Employment Restrictions (GS 14-208.16)

- Registered offenders may not reside **within 1,000 feet** of a school, childcare center, or other specified location
- Employment restrictions similarly limit proximity to schools and children

### Petition to Terminate Registration

- Tier I offenders (lowest risk) may petition after 10 years; court considers factors
- Tier II offenders: 25 years; Tier III (highest risk): generally lifetime, no termination
- Not available for offenders with victims under 13 where defendant was 17 or older

### Adam Walsh Act / SORNA (Federal)

The federal Sex Offender Registration and Notification Act (SORNA) imposes minimum standards on states. NC's system is SORNA-compliant. Federal prosecutions are possible for registered offenders who travel interstate without updating registration (18 U.S.C. § 2250).

## 3. Defense Issues in Sex Offense Cases

### Consent Defense

- Consent is a defense to force-based rape/sexual offense (not statutory)
- State must prove lack of consent (force or coercion, or victim's incapacity to consent)
- Prior sexual behavior of the victim is generally **inadmissible** under NC Rape Shield Law (GS 8C-1, Rule 412), with narrow exceptions

### Expert Testimony

- DNA evidence, serology, forensic nursing testimony common
- Defense may challenge chain of custody, lab procedures, analyst qualifications
- Expert on false memory, child suggestibility, or PTSD admissibility (Rule 702)

### Child Witness Issues

- NC allows closed-circuit television testimony by child victims in certain circumstances (GS 15A-1225.1)
- Recorded forensic interviews of children (CAC/CARE interviews) are commonly offered — admissibility subject to Crawford/Confrontation Clause challenges

### Statute of Limitations

NC eliminated the statute of limitations for most Class B1 and Class C sex felonies involving victims under 18. Lesser offenses may have limitations periods — check GS 15-1 through 15-2.

### Registration as Component of Sentence

Registration is a **collateral consequence**, not punishment — courts have held it does not constitute double jeopardy. However, retroactive application of more restrictive registration requirements has generated constitutional challenges.

## 4. Collateral Consequences Specific to Sex Offenses

- **Permanent sex offender registration** (cannot be expunged under most circumstances)
- **Housing restrictions:** Cannot live within 1,000 feet of schools, childcare centers
- **Employment:** Cannot work in positions involving children; many licensing boards automatically disqualify
- **Internet/social media restrictions:** Some registration conditions prohibit certain online activity
- **No expunction:** Sex offense convictions requiring registration are **permanently ineligible** for expunction under GS 15A-145.5 — one of the absolute bars
- **Immigration:** Sex offenses frequently constitute "aggravated felonies" or "crimes of moral turpitude" under federal immigration law → deportation risk

## 5. Key Statutes

- **GS 14-27.20 through 14-27.36** — Sex offense definitions (Article 7B)
- **GS 14-27.25, 14-27.30** — Statutory rape/offense; close-in-age exemptions
- **GS 14-202.1** — Taking indecent liberties with a child
- **GS 14-208.6 through 14-208.45** — Sex offender registration requirements (Article 27A)
- **GS 14-208.16** — Residency restrictions for registered offenders
- **GS 8C-1, Rule 412** — NC Rape Shield Law
- **GS 15A-1225.1** — Remote testimony by child witnesses
