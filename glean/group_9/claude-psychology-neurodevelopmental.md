# Psychology — Neurodevelopmental (ADHD & Autism)

Research-domain reference for **ADHD** and **autism spectrum disorder (ASD)** as deep knowledge domains: mechanisms, seminal theory, and (critically) **what replicates vs. what is popular-but-fragile**. This topic is unusually full of clinically-popularized constructs that lack an empirical base (RSD, SPD-as-standalone-diagnosis, "extreme male brain"). The spine of this skill is the **solid-vs-fragile split**: never assert a headline term as established just because it's well-known.

For the operator question "**how do I deal with a neurodivergent colleague/customer?**" → `applied-psychology`. This spoke answers "**how does it work / what does the research actually show?**"

## 1. Core concepts & mechanisms

### 1.1 ADHD — what it is

DSM-5-TR: a persistent pattern of **inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity** interfering with functioning/development, onset before age 12, present in ≥2 settings, in three presentations (predominantly inattentive, predominantly hyperactive-impulsive, combined). Pooled prevalence ≈ **5.9% of youth**, **2.5–2.8% of adults**; youth sex ratio ≈ **2:1 male:female** (likely inflated by under-recognition of inattentive girls). ADHD is **highly heterogeneous**: there is **no single universal neuropsychological deficit** every individual shares.

**Genetics (solid).** ADHD is among the most heritable psychiatric conditions: twin-study heritability ≈ **74%** (Faraone consensus). It is **polygenic** — a genome-wide study of >55,000 participants found many common variants of individually tiny effect, plus rare variants and copy-number variants. The clinically useful framing: **many genetic + early-environmental risks accumulate**; environmental risks act early (fetal/perinatal). No single "ADHD gene."

**Neuroimaging (real but small; not diagnostic).** ENIGMA-ADHD mega-analyses (thousands of participants) find **slightly reduced** total cortical surface area and smaller subcortical volumes (accumbens, amygdala, caudate, hippocampus, putamen), most pronounced in childhood and attenuating with age. Effect sizes are **small (Cohen's d roughly 0.1–0.3)**, group-level only. **No brain measure can diagnose ADHD in an individual**: a load-bearing caveat against neuro-essentialism.

### 1.2 ADHD — the major theoretical models

| Model | Theorist | Core claim |
|---|---|---|
| **Behavioral inhibition / EF** | Russell **Barkley** (1997) | A primary deficit in **response inhibition** cascades into impairments of the four executive functions (working memory, self-regulation of affect/motivation/arousal, internalization of speech, reconstitution). Self-regulation = self-directed action across time. |
| **Dual-pathway model** | Edmund **Sonuga-Barke** (2002–03) | ADHD is **heterogeneous**: a *cool* executive/inhibitory pathway (dorsal frontostriatal) **and** a dissociable *hot* motivational pathway — **delay aversion** (ventral/reward circuitry, mesolimbic). Either, or both, can produce ADHD. |
| **Triple-pathway / temporal** | Sonuga-Barke, Coghill et al. | Adds **temporal/timing** processing as a third dissociable component independent of inhibition and delay. |
| **Executive-function (broad)** | Thomas **Brown** | ADHD as impaired **self-management system** (activation, focus, effort, emotion, memory, action) — emphasizes emotion as integral, not incidental. |

Meta-analyses confirm **moderate** group-level impairments across working memory, response inhibition, reaction-time variability, and planning, but EF deficits are **neither necessary nor sufficient**: a substantial minority of people with ADHD perform normally on EF tasks. The field has moved firmly from "ADHD = one deficit" to **multiple dissociable pathways**.

### 1.3 The dopamine story — write the mechanism, flag the over-claim

**What's solid:** Stimulants (methylphenidate, amphetamine) **increase synaptic dopamine and norepinephrine**. Methylphenidate **blocks the dopamine transporter (DAT)**, densest in striatum, amplifying the striatal DA signal and plausibly improving salience/attention. **Volkow's PET work** (e.g., 53 unmedicated adults with ADHD vs. 44 controls) found **lower D2/D3 receptor and DAT markers in the accumbens/midbrain** reward pathway, correlating with **low achievement motivation**, linking a reward-circuit signature to the *motivational* (delay-aversion) side of ADHD.

**What's over-claimed:** The tidy "**ADHD is a dopamine deficit**" slogan is an **oversimplification**. DAT-density findings are *mixed and confounded by prior medication exposure* (chronic stimulant use can up-regulate DAT). There is no clean unidirectional "low dopamine → ADHD" model; the system involves DA, NE, and circuit dynamics, not a single deficient chemical. Treat "dopamine dysregulation" as a **useful pointer to reward-circuit involvement, not a validated unified cause.**

### 1.4 Emotional dysregulation & "Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria" — the marquee fragility flag

- **Emotional dysregulation in ADHD = well-supported.** Barkley treats it as **near-core** (a direct consequence of EF/inhibition deficits, i.e. poor self-regulation of affect), and it predicts impairment beyond the DSM's inattention/hyperactivity dyad. It is **not** a formal DSM-5-TR core symptom but is empirically robust.
- **Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD) = popular, NOT validated.** RSD is **not in DSM-5-TR or ICD-11**, was **popularized by one clinician (William Dodson)** from clinical observation, and **lacks a research-validated construct or measure**. The handful of studies addressing it are small (n ≈ 4–43) and qualitative. **Barkley explicitly rejects RSD as a distinct disorder**, arguing it is already subsumed by ADHD-related emotional dysregulation. Do **not** present RSD as established; present it as a clinical/popular label for intense rejection-related emotional pain, distinct from the separately-researched **rejection sensitivity** construct (Downey & Feldman, 1996, in interpersonal/depression literature).

### 1.5 Autism — what it is

DSM-5 (2013) collapsed prior subtypes (autistic disorder, Asperger's, PDD-NOS) into one **spectrum** with two domains: (A) persistent deficits in **social communication and interaction**, and (B) **restricted, repetitive behaviors/interests**, which **now explicitly includes sensory hyper-/hyporeactivity**. Specified with severity levels and "with/without intellectual or language impairment." Lorna **Wing's "triad of impairments"** (social interaction, communication, imagination; Wing & Gould, 1979) was the bridge from Kanner's narrow category to the modern spectrum.

**Genetics (solid).** Twin meta-analysis (**Tick et al., 2016**): heritability ≈ **64–91%** (MZ correlation ≈ .98, DZ ≈ .53–.67); previously-reported shared-environment effects appear to be a statistical artifact. Highly **polygenic + rare high-impact variants/CNVs**. ENIGMA-ASD neuroimaging shows **small, regionally distributed** cortical/subcortical differences: again **no single biomarker, not individually diagnostic.**

### 1.6 Autism — the major cognitive theories (and their counter-frames)

| Theory | Theorist(s) | Claim | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Theory of Mind / "mindblindness"** | Baron-Cohen, Leslie & **Frith (1985)**; Sally-Anne task | Autistic difficulty representing others' mental states | **Foundational but contested**; ToM tasks have psychometric/ generalizability problems; deficit framing challenged |
| **Double empathy problem** | Damian **Milton (2012)** | Breakdown is **bidirectional**: autistic↔non-autistic mismatch in mutual understanding, *not* a one-sided autistic deficit. Autistic-autistic interaction can be effective (Crompton et al. evidence) | Influential reframe; growing empirical support — the **key counter-frame to ToM** |
| **Weak central coherence** | **Frith & Happé** | Bias toward local/detail processing over global gist | Reframed by Happé as a *cognitive style* (often a **strength**), not pure deficit |
| **Executive dysfunction** | Ozonoff et al. | Planning/flexibility difficulties | Real but non-specific (overlaps ADHD) |
| **Enhanced Perceptual Functioning** | Laurent **Mottron** | Superior, more autonomous perceptual processing — a **strengths** account | Well-regarded strengths model |
| **Monotropism** | Dinah **Murray**, Lawson, Lesser (1990s–2005) | Attention pools into few highly-aroused interests ("attention tunnel"); explains focus, overwhelm, transitions, stimming | Autistic-led unifying account; gaining research attention |
| **Empathizing-Systemizing / "Extreme Male Brain"** | Baron-Cohen | Autism = systemizing≫empathizing, "extreme male brain" | **Weak/mixed support, ethically charged** — flag as contested, do not present as settled |
| **Predictive coding / Bayesian** | Pellicano & Burr; Van de Cruys | Atypical weighting of priors vs. sensory prediction error | Active mechanistic frontier |

### 1.7 Masking / camouflaging

**Camouflaging** = consciously/unconsciously suppressing autistic features and performing neurotypical behavior (masking, compensation, assimilation). Measured by the **CAT-Q** (Camouflaging Autistic Traits Questionnaire; **Hull, Mandy, Lai et al., 2019**, 25 items; 9-item short form). Findings: camouflaging is **associated with worse mental health** (anxiety, depression, exhaustion/burnout, suicidality, and a sense of lost/false self); it is **more reported by autistic women/girls** (gendered social pressure) and contributes to **late or missed diagnosis** in women. Note: associations are largely **cross-sectional/correlational**, so causal direction (does masking cause distress, or does distress drive masking?) is not firmly established, and CAT-Q is **self-report**.

### 1.8 Sensory processing & stimming

- **Sensory differences are real and core to autism** (~90% report sensory atypicalities; now in DSM-5 autism criteria). But **"Sensory Processing Disorder" (SPD) as a standalone diagnosis is rejected** by DSM-5 and ICD-11; the **American Academy of Pediatrics (2012)** advised against diagnosing SPD independently, recommending evaluation for ASD/ADHD/anxiety instead. Sensory **integration therapy** (Ayres) has **weak efficacy evidence**. Keep the distinction: *sensory differences exist* ≠ *SPD is a validated separate disorder.*
- **Stimming** (self-stimulatory/repetitive behavior such as rocking, flapping, vocalizing) serves **self-regulatory functions**: managing sensory load, emotion regulation, focus, and expressing emotion. The neurodiversity-informed clinical shift is away from suppression toward **permitting non-harmful stims** as adaptive coping.

### 1.9 Neurodiversity — separate the science from the movement

- **Concept:** "Neurodiversity" (sociologist **Judy Singer**, ~1998, modeled on biodiversity) = natural variation in human cognition; recent scholarship (Botha et al., 2024) stresses it was **developed collectively** by the autistic community, not one author. **Jim Sinclair's** "Don't Mourn for Us" (1993) anchored autistic self-advocacy.
- **As empirical framing:** the **difference vs. deficit** debate is genuine and partly evidence-based (e.g., double empathy, enhanced perception, monotropism reframe "deficits" as differences-in-context).
- **As social/political movement:** advocacy claims (e.g., that all neurodivergence should be de-medicalized) are **normative, not empirical**. Keep these separate, and note the **intra-community tension**: families/individuals with high support needs and co-occurring intellectual disability sometimes find a pure social model under-serves them. Present neurodiversity as a **valid reframing with both empirical support and contested boundaries.**

### 1.10 ADHD–autism overlap ("AuDHD")

DSM-5 **removed the prior prohibition** on co-diagnosis: ADHD and autism **can be diagnosed together** and frequently co-occur (estimates ~ 20–50% overlap depending on sample). They share substantial **genetic correlation** and overlapping EF and emotional-dysregulation profiles, but differ in core features (social-communication + restricted interests vs. inattention/impulsivity). Profiles can pull in opposite directions (e.g., novelty-seeking vs. sameness-preference), complicating assessment.

## 3. Replication status — solid vs. fragile (read before asserting anything)

**Solid / replicates well:**
- ADHD heritability ≈ 74%; autism heritability ≈ 64–91%: robust across twin meta-analyses.
- Both conditions are **polygenic**, with no single causal gene and **no diagnostic biomarker**.
- ENIGMA structural differences are **real but small (d ≈ 0.1–0.3), group-level, non-diagnostic**.
- Stimulant *mechanism* (DAT blockade → ↑synaptic DA/NE) is established; short-term symptom-reduction efficacy is consistently found.
- Emotional dysregulation as a robust, impairing feature of ADHD.
- Camouflaging ↔ poorer mental health association (replicated, though cross-sectional).

**Mixed / contested — flag explicitly:**
- **Stimulant evidence *quality*:** the **Storebø/Cochrane** review rated most methylphenidate trials **high risk of bias** and outcomes **"very low" GRADE certainty** (large reported effect, e.g., teacher-rated SMD ≈ −0.62 to −0.77, but low confidence). The Cochrane review was itself **disputed** by other experts. Bottom line: medication helps short-term symptoms; **long-term MTA follow-up showed initial advantage diminished** by ~3 years (interpretation debated). Hold the efficacy claim with calibrated, not absolute, confidence.
- **Theory of Mind** as *the* explanation of autism: contested; **double empathy** is the major counter-frame.
- **EF deficits** in both conditions: moderate group effects but **not universal** (many individuals unimpaired).
- **"Dopamine deficit"** unified-cause framing: oversimplified; transporter findings confounded by medication.

**Fragile / NOT empirically validated — do not present as established:**
- **Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD):** not a DSM/ICD construct; tiny, qualitative evidence base; single-clinician origin; rejected by Barkley.
- **Sensory Processing Disorder (SPD)** *as a standalone diagnosis*: rejected by DSM-5/AAP (sensory *differences* are real; the *separate disorder* is not).
- **"Extreme Male Brain" / strong empathizing-systemizing:** weak, mixed support; ethically and politically charged.
- **Sensory integration (Ayres) therapy efficacy:** weak/insufficient evidence.
- Strong **neuro-essentialist** claims ("scan-confirmed," single brain marker): unsupported; reject.

## 4. Practical applications

- **Assessment literacy.** Both diagnoses are **clinical/behavioral, not lab-confirmable**: there is no blood test, gene panel, or brain scan that diagnoses ADHD or autism. Treat anyone selling one skeptically. Comorbidity is the rule (anxiety, depression, learning disorders, AuDHD overlap), so single-label thinking misleads.
- **Strengths + support framing.** Pair difficulty-based language with the difference/strengths models (monotropism, enhanced perception) where evidence supports them; avoid pure-deficit framing **and** avoid romanticizing (high-support-needs autism is real and disabling for many).
- **Environment over "fixing the person."** Double-empathy and monotropism imply many "social deficits" are **interactional mismatches**, so accommodations (clear expectations, predictability, reduced sensory load, permitting stimming, async/written communication) often beat normalization pressure.
- **Masking has a cost.** High camouflaging predicts burnout and distress; sustained "passing" is not a neutral coping strategy. Relevant to late-diagnosed adults and to women under-identified by male-normed criteria.
- **Medication: calibrated claims.** Stimulants reliably reduce *short-term* symptoms for many; evidence *quality* is weaker than the popular narrative and long-term functional gains are uncertain. Present as "helpful for many, monitored, not a cure," not as settled magic.
- **Language matters & is contested.** Identity-first ("autistic person") is preferred by much of the autistic community; person-first ("person with autism") by parts of the clinical/family world; mirror the individual. "High/low functioning" is **deprecated** in favor of support-needs language.
- **Guard against pop-psych drift.** When a user cites RSD, SPD, "dopamine detox," or "extreme male brain" as fact, **acknowledge the lived experience but correct the epistemic status**: name what's validated vs. popularized.
