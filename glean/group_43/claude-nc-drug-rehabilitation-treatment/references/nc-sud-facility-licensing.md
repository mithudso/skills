# NC Substance Use Treatment Facility Licensing
## GS 122C, DHSR MHLCS, 10A NCAC 27G, OTP Certification, SAMHSA Accreditation

## 1. Overview — NC Regulatory Framework

NC SUD treatment facilities are regulated through a **multi-layer system**:

| Layer | Agency | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **State facility license** | NC DHSR – Mental Health Licensure and Certification Section (MHLCS) | GS 122C; 10A NCAC 27G |
| **Certificate of Need (CON)** | NC DHSR – Certificate of Need Section | GS 131E (certain residential/inpatient only) |
| **OTP federal certification** | SAMHSA | 42 CFR Part 8 |
| **OTP DEA registration** | DEA | 21 CFR Part 1301 |
| **SOTA approval** | NC Division of MH/DD/SUS (SOTA) | State opioid treatment authority |
| **Medicaid enrollment** | NC Medicaid / LME-MCO | Separate from licensure |

All licensed SUD treatment providers serving 2+ adults or 1+ minor in day services (3+ hrs/day) or residential (24+ hrs) must hold an **NC DHSR MHLCS license** — regardless of Medicaid participation.[^1]

---

## 2. Statutory Basis (GS Chapter 122C)

**GS Chapter 122C** (NC Mental Health, Developmental Disabilities, and Substance Abuse Act of 1985) is the primary enabling statute.[^1]

Key provisions relevant to facility licensing:
- **GS 122C-3(14)(b)** — defines "licensable facility" as serving MH/IDD/SUD individuals: day services 3+ hours to same individual, or residential 24+ consecutive hours
- **GS 122C-22** — exclusions from licensure; addresses deemed status
- **GS 122C-81** — national accreditation benchmarks; facilities meeting certain national accreditation standards may qualify for deemed status (reduced state inspection requirements)
- **GS 122C-24.1** — license suspension/revocation authority

---

## 3. DHSR Mental Health Licensure and Certification Section (MHLCS)

The **NC Division of Health Service Regulation (DHSR)** — specifically its **Mental Health Licensure and Certification Section (MHLCS)** — is the licensing authority for all MH, IDD, and SUD facilities in NC.[^2]

**Contact/portal:** info.ncdhhs.gov/dhsr/mhlcs/

**MHLCS licensing functions:**
- Issues initial and renewal licenses
- Conducts on-site inspections (initial + periodic)
- Reviews and acts on complaints
- Maintains the public licensed facilities database
- Processes appeals of license actions

## 4. Licensed Service Categories for SUD Treatment (10A NCAC 27G)

NC Administrative Code **10A NCAC 27G** (Subchapter G — Rules for MH, IDD, and SA Facilities and Services) contains the specific standards for each licensed SUD service type.[^3]

**verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

| Service Type | NCAC Citation | ASAM Level Alignment | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Social Setting Detoxification** | 10A NCAC 27G .3200 | 3.2 (clinically managed withdrawal) | Non-medical detox; social model |
| **Outpatient Opioid Treatment (OTP)** | 10A NCAC 27G .3600 | 1.0 (OTP-based) | Requires dual-track licensing (see Section 7) |
| **Residential Treatment / Rehabilitation** | 10A NCAC 27G .3400 | 3.1–3.5 | 24-hour residential; active treatment |
| **SAIOP (Intensive Outpatient)** | 10A NCAC 27G .4400 | 2.1 | 9–19 hrs/week; age 12+ |
| **SACOT (Comprehensive Outpatient)** | 10A NCAC 27G .4500 | 2.5 | 20+ hrs/week; age 18+ |
| **Residential Recovery Programs** | 10A NCAC 27G .4100 | — | SA individuals with children |
| **Medically Monitored Community Residential** | 10A NCAC 27G .3700 | 3.7 | Medical monitoring; 24-hour non-hospital |
| **Supervised Living** | 10A NCAC 27G .5600 | — | Sober living / recovery housing (if licensed) |

**Outpatient SUD services** (ASAM 1.0 / standard outpatient) are licensed under general outpatient service standards rather than a dedicated SUD rule, depending on the service model.

## 5. Licensing Process — Step-by-Step

**verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

The general licensing process under MHLCS:[^2]

**Step 1 — Pre-Application Consultation**
Contact MHLCS at the earliest planning stage. MHLCS offers pre-application consultation to identify required license categories and applicable standards before the formal application.

**Step 2 — Application Submission**
Submit the MHLCS Initial Licensure Application Packet. Include:
- Organizational documents (articles of incorporation, bylaws, board composition)
- Proposed service site and floor plan
- Staffing plan and job descriptions
- Policies and procedures compliant with 10A NCAC 27G
- Background check authorization for staff

**Step 3 — Administrative Review**
MHLCS reviews the application for completeness. Missing items result in a Notice of Deficiency with a response deadline.

**Step 4 — On-Site Inspection**
MHLCS inspectors visit the facility before initial licensure. Inspectors assess physical environment, policies/procedures, staffing, and client records against applicable NCAC standards.

**Step 5 — License Issuance**
If compliant: **Annual license issued** authorizing the specific service types listed.

**Step 6 — Ongoing Compliance**
- Annual license renewal required
- Periodic unannounced inspections (frequency varies by service type and compliance history)
- Complaint-triggered inspections

## 6. Certificate of Need (CON) Requirements

**Not all SUD facilities require a CON.** CON is generally required for:[^2]

- Facilities adding or establishing **residential psychiatric beds** above certain thresholds
- Certain **medically monitored inpatient** (ASAM 3.7) expansions
- **Hospital-based SUD programs** subject to hospital CON rules

Outpatient SUD services (SAIOP, SACOT, standard outpatient) generally do **not** require a CON. Social setting detox and residential rehabilitation programs may or may not require CON depending on capacity and structure.

Contact the NC DHSR Certificate of Need Section for a project-specific determination before making capital commitments.

## 7. OTP (Opioid Treatment Program) Dual-Track Licensing

Opioid Treatment Programs (methadone clinics and buprenorphine OTPs) require the most complex multi-agency approval process in NC.[^4]

**NC SOTA Pre-Approval (required first)**

The **NC State Opioid Treatment Authority (SOTA)** within NC DHHS must provide **preliminary approval** before an OTP can apply for an NC DHSR license. The SOTA letter of support evaluates the community need and is required for any new OTP or expansion.

As of 2025, NC DHHS has issued guidance on the letter of support process for new OTP waiver requests and renewals.[^4]

**Required OTP Approvals — Full Sequence**

| Step | Agency | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **NC SOTA** — Letter of support / preliminary approval | NC DHHS |
| 2 | **NC DHSR MHLCS** — State facility license under 10A NCAC 27G .3600 | GS 122C |
| 3 | **DEA** — Controlled Substance Registration (Schedule II narcotics) | 21 CFR 1301 |
| 4 | **SAMHSA** — OTP Certification under 42 CFR Part 8 | 42 USC 290aa |
| 5 | **Accrediting body** — Accreditation required by federal law (42 CFR 8.11(d)(e)) | COA, CARF, JCAHO, or other SAMHSA-approved body |
| 6 | **DEA — additional** — Compliance with DEA regulations per 10A NCAC 27G .3604 | 21 CFR |

**The NC DHSR license and SAMHSA certification are parallel applications** — not sequential (except SOTA comes first). They can and typically are pursued simultaneously after SOTA approval.[^4]

For more on OTP federal regulation (42 CFR Part 8, take-home flexibility, mobile units), see `nc-mat-otp-law.md`.

## 8. SAMHSA Certification and Accreditation

**SAMHSA OTP certification** (under 42 CFR Part 8) is distinct from and in addition to NC state licensure.[^5]

**Who needs SAMHSA certification:** Any program dispensing opioid agonist medications (methadone, buprenorphine for OTP purposes) for the treatment of opioid use disorder must be a certified OTP.

**SAMHSA-approved accrediting bodies (partial list):**
- CARF International
- The Joint Commission (JCAHO)
- Commission on Accreditation of Rehabilitation Facilities
- Council on Accreditation (COA)

**Accreditation vs. certification:**
- **Accreditation** is provided by an approved private accrediting body and evaluates quality of care standards
- **Certification** is the federal status granted by SAMHSA after reviewing the accreditation and verifying 42 CFR Part 8 compliance
- Both are required for OTPs; accreditation is also the gateway to SAMHSA certification

**GS 122C-81 — Deemed Status:** NC law allows facilities that meet certain national accreditation standards (CARF, JCAHO) to receive "deemed status" — reducing certain state inspection requirements. This is distinct from SAMHSA certification and applies to the NC state license.[^1]

## 9. Ongoing Compliance — Inspections, Renewals, Violations

**License renewal:** Annual; MHLCS issues renewal notices. Failure to renew results in license expiration.

**Inspections:**
- Initial licensure inspection (required before license issuance)
- Periodic announced/unannounced inspections (frequency based on service type and compliance history)
- Complaint-triggered inspections (must be conducted within regulatory timeframes)

**Violations and enforcement actions:**
- Statement of Deficiency issued after inspection finding violations
- Plan of Correction required from facility
- If deficiencies not corrected: license conditions, provisional license, suspension, or revocation under GS 122C-24.1
- DHSR publishes inspection reports and Statement of Deficiency outcomes publicly

**OTP-specific compliance:**
- SAMHSA may conduct independent compliance visits
- DEA may inspect for controlled substance handling
- State, federal, and accrediting body surveys may occur in the same year

## 10. Medicaid Provider Enrollment (Separate from Licensure)

A DHSR MHLCS license does **NOT** automatically make a facility a NC Medicaid provider. Medicaid enrollment is a separate process.[^2]

- **NC Medicaid Direct / NC Tracks:** Providers must enroll through the NC Medicaid provider enrollment portal
- **Tailored Plan network:** Enrollment with the LME-MCO (Alliance, Partners, Trillium, or Vaya) serving the relevant geographic area is required for Medicaid managed care patients
- **LME-MCO provider contract:** Separate contract with each LME-MCO whose members the facility wants to serve

A facility may hold an MHLCS license and provide state-funded services without enrolling in Medicaid. Conversely, Medicaid enrollment requires holding the applicable MHLCS license first.

## 11. Anti-Patterns / Common Misunderstandings

**"NC DHSR license = Medicaid provider status"**
No. Licensure and Medicaid enrollment are separate processes with separate applications. Both are required to serve Medicaid patients and bill Medicaid. A licensed facility must separately enroll with NC Medicaid and any relevant LME-MCOs.[^2]

**"SAMHSA OTP certification is optional for methadone clinics"**
No. Federal law (42 USC 290aa; 42 CFR Part 8) requires SAMHSA certification for any program dispensing opioid agonist treatment medications for OUD. Operating an OTP without SAMHSA certification is a federal violation.[^5]

**"Getting SAMHSA accreditation is enough; we don't need NC state licensure"**
No. SAMHSA certification does not replace NC DHSR MHLCS licensure. Both are required. They are parallel regulatory requirements from different governments.[^4]

**"All SUD treatment facilities need a Certificate of Need"**
No. CON requirements in NC are limited to specific facility types. Most outpatient SUD services (SAIOP, SACOT, standard outpatient) do not require a CON. Residential and inpatient expansions may. Project-specific CON determination from DHSR is required.[^2]

**"After getting licensed, inspections are scheduled in advance"**
Inspections may be announced or unannounced. Complaint-triggered inspections are generally unannounced. Annual renewal inspections are typically announced. Facilities should maintain compliance readiness at all times.[^2]
