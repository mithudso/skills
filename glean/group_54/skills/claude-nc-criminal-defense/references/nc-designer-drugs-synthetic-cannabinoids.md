# NC Designer Drugs and Synthetic Cannabinoids

> **Educational only, NOT legal advice.** CURRENCY WARNING: This is an actively churning area of law (2026). Verified-as-of: 2026-06-22.

---

## Overview

NC targets synthetic designer drugs through three overlapping mechanisms:
1. **Specific scheduling** in NC Schedules I–VI
2. **Analogue provision** (GS 90-89.1) treating analogues as Schedule I
3. **Specific trafficking offenses** for synthetic cannabinoids under GS 90-95(h)(1a)

---

## Synthetic Cannabinoids (Spice, K2, etc.)

### Legal Status

NC has **banned synthetic cannabis since 2011**. Synthetic cannabinoids are classified as **Schedule VI controlled substances** — the same schedule as marijuana — under GS 90-94.

"Synthetic cannabinoids" are defined in GS 90-94 as any synthetic chemical compound that is a cannabinoid receptor agonist mimicking naturally occurring substances OR has a stimulant, depressant, or hallucinogenic effect on the central nervous system, that is **not listed as a controlled substance in Schedules I through V** and is **not an FDA-approved drug**.

**Common synthetic cannabinoids:** Spice, K2, Skunk, Black Mamba, Moon Rocks, fake weed, JWH-018, AM-2201.

### Penalties

Because they are Schedule VI (same as marijuana), synthetic cannabinoids can face:
- **Simple possession**: Class I felony (if quantity exceeds marijuana-equivalent thresholds) or Class 3 misdemeanor (small quantities) under GS 90-95(d)(4)
- **PWIMSD**: Class I felony (Schedule VI) under GS 90-95(a)(1)

### Trafficking in Synthetic Cannabinoids (GS 90-95(h)(1a))

**Dosage unit = 3 grams** for purposes of this subsection:

| Dosage Units | Felony Class | Min. Prison | Max. Prison | Min. Fine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≥ 50, < 250 | H | 25 months | 39 months | $5,000 |
| ≥ 250, < 1,250 | G | 35 months | 51 months | $25,000 |
| ≥ 1,250, < 3,750 | F | 70 months | 93 months | $50,000 |
| ≥ 3,750 | D | 175 months | 222 months | $200,000 |

## NC Analogue Statute (GS 90-89.1)

GS 90-89.1 provides: **A controlled substance analogue shall, to the extent intended for human consumption, be treated for the purposes of any State law as a controlled substance in Schedule I.**

### Definition of Analogue

A substance is a controlled substance analogue if:
1. Its chemical structure is substantially similar to a Schedule I or II controlled substance; OR
2. It has, is represented to have, or is intended to have a stimulant, depressant, or hallucinogenic effect substantially similar to a Schedule I or II controlled substance; AND
3. It is not otherwise controlled or approved for medical use

### Federal Parallel: The Federal Analogue Act (FAA)

The Federal Analogue Act (21 U.S.C. § 813, part of the Controlled Substances Analogue Enforcement Act of 1986) operates similarly at the federal level: substances substantially similar in structure or effect to a Schedule I or II controlled substance are treated as Schedule I **for purposes of federal law** when intended for human consumption. NC's GS 90-89.1 is modeled on the FAA.

**Key difference:** "Intended for human consumption" is a required element under both NC and federal analogue law — the government must prove the substance was intended for human use (not, e.g., as a research chemical or industrial solvent).

## Substituted Cathinones (Bath Salts)

Bath salts (MDPV, mephedrone, methylone, etc.) are **specifically listed in NC Schedule I** and also in NC Schedule II depending on the compound. They are also covered by NC's analogue statute.

**Trafficking in substituted cathinones** (GS 90-95(h)(3c)):

| Weight | Felony Class | Min. Prison | Max. Prison | Min. Fine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≥ 28g, < 200g | F | 70 months | 93 months | $50,000 |
| ≥ 200g, < 400g | E | 90 months | 120 months | $100,000 |
| ≥ 400g | C | 225 months | 282 months | $250,000 |

## Hemp-Derived Products: Delta-8, THCa, and the 2026 Crisis

### Current Legal Status (as of June 2026)

| Product | NC State Status | Federal Status |
|---|---|---|
| CBD (< 0.3% delta-9 THC) | Legal | Legal |
| Delta-8 THC (hemp-derived) | Legal (from compliant hemp) | Legal under 2018 Farm Bill |
| THCa flower | Legal through Nov 11, 2026 | **Illegal after Nov 12, 2026** |
| Delta-9 THC (> 0.3%) | Illegal (marijuana) | Illegal |

### November 12, 2026 Federal Change (P.L. 119-37)

Federal law P.L. 119-37 redefines "hemp" using **total THC** (delta-9 + THCa) with a **0.4 mg per container cap**. After this date:
- THCa flower and most THCa products become federally classified as marijuana
- Most Delta-8 THC products will exceed the new cap
- NC vendors selling these products face federal criminal exposure
- Whether NC state law follows is unclear — watch the NC General Assembly

### NC Legislative Activity (2025-2026)

NC HB 328 ("Regulate Hemp-Derived Consumables") would have established a new Chapter 18D regulatory framework for hemp consumables. **The bill died April 21, 2026** without passing. NC has no standalone hemp-regulation statute as of June 2026.

## NC Schedule I Synthetic Drugs (Selected)

Beyond cannabinoids, NC Schedule I (GS 90-89) includes numerous synthetic substances. The Commission for Mental Health, DD, and SAS regularly adds new synthetic drugs via the emergency scheduling process. Examples include:
- Substituted tryptamines (4-AcO-DMT, 5-MeO-DMT)
- Substituted phenethylamines (2C-series, NBOMe series)
- Synthetic opioids (fentanyl analogues not separately scheduled)
- Psilocybin, psilocin (natural but scheduled)
- Kratom is **not currently scheduled** in NC (as of June 2026) but legislation has been proposed
