## Nakamoto / longest-chain Proof of Work

Nakamoto consensus (Bitcoin whitepaper, 2008) reaches agreement on transaction *ordering* without a trusted party by combining hash-based PoW leader election with a longest-chain rule.[^btc][^nakamotoconsensus] PoW is "essentially one-CPU-one-vote": miners scan for a nonce making a block's hash fall below a difficulty target, and "the majority decision is represented by the longest chain, which has the greatest proof-of-work effort invested in it."[^btc] This gives **Sybil resistance via cost** — votes are weighted by computation, not identity, so an attacker "able to allocate many IPs" cannot subvert majority decisions; they must out-compute the honest majority.[^btc] The security assumption is an **honest majority of hashpower**: "as long as a majority of CPU power is controlled by nodes that are not cooperating to attack the network, they'll generate the longest chain and outpace attackers."[^btc] Finality is **probabilistic**: §11 models the honest-vs-attacker race as a Gambler's Ruin where catch-up probability "diminishes exponentially as subsequent blocks are added" (for q=0.1 of hashpower, reversal probability after z=6 confirmations ≈ 0.0002).[^btc] A **51% attack** lets a majority-hashpower adversary reorder/censor transactions and double-spend via a longer alternative chain, but cannot forge invalid transactions (honest nodes reject invalid blocks).[^btc][^51pct] Caveat: strictly the rule is "most-work / heaviest," not literally "longest," since per-block work varies with difficulty.[^nakamotoconsensus] **[fact]**

## Proof of Stake fundamentals

In PoS, the right to append blocks is assigned in proportion to **staked/bonded coins** rather than computation, removing mining's hardware/energy cost.[^casper][^ethpos] Validators lock capital ("bond") that can be **slashed** (partly or wholly destroyed) for provable misbehavior; security derives from the *penalty size*, which can exceed rewards.[^casper] PoS faces three signature problems. **(a) Nothing-at-stake:** with only rewards and no penalties, a rational validator is "equally happy to attest to any, or even multiple, forks" because signing every fork is costless;[^posfaq][^ethfaqs] solved by **slashing/finality conditions** that delete the deposit of validators signing conflicting blocks.[^posfaq][^casper] **(b) Long-range attacks:** validators who have *withdrawn* their stake can use old keys to fabricate an alternative history "without fear of getting slashed";[^casper] mitigated by a never-revert-finalized rule + **weak subjectivity**.[^posfaq][^weaksubj] **(c) Weak subjectivity:** new or long-offline nodes cannot objectively distinguish the canonical chain from a long-range fork, so they bootstrap from a recent trusted "weak subjectivity checkpoint" (a state root obtained socially/out-of-band) and verify objectively forward.[^weaksubj][^ethfaqs] The **PoS-vs-PoW tradeoff**: PoS is dramatically more energy-efficient but introduces costlessness-of-simulation problems (nothing-at-stake, long-range) that PoW avoids because PoW work is externally costly and non-reusable.[^posfaq][^ethpos] **[fact]**

## Ethereum's Gasper = Casper FFG + LMD-GHOST

Since The Merge (Sep 2022, verified-as-of 2026-06-16), Ethereum's PoS consensus is **Gasper**, formalized in "Combining GHOST and Casper" (Buterin et al., arXiv 2003.03052, 2020) — "a combination of Casper FFG, a finality tool, with LMD GHOST, a fork-choice rule."[^gasper][^gasperdoc] **[fact]**

**Casper FFG** (Buterin & Griffith, arXiv 1710.09437) is a **finality gadget** — an overlay that finalizes blocks atop a proposal mechanism, "agnostic to whether the provided chain were proof-of-work or proof-of-stake."[^casper] It operates on **checkpoints** at epoch boundaries (Ethereum: epoch = 32 slots × 12 s).[^casper][^eth2bookffg] Finalization is a **two-phase commit** weighted by deposit, requiring a **2/3 supermajority** at each phase: (1) a **supermajority link** s→t exists when ≥2/3 of validators (by stake) vote source s → target t; a checkpoint becomes **justified** via a supermajority link from a justified checkpoint; (2) it becomes **finalized** when justified *and* there is a supermajority link to its *direct child*.[^casper][^eth2bookffg] The **two Casper Commandments** (slashing conditions) protect safety: **no double vote** (at most one vote per target epoch) and **no surround vote** (no votes s₁→t₁ and s₂→t₂ with h(s₁) < h(s₂) < h(t₂) < h(t₁)).[^casper][^eth2bookffg] FFG's guarantees are **accountable safety** (two conflicting checkpoints cannot both finalize unless ≥1/3 of stake is provably slashable — "economic finality") and **plausible liveness** (if ≥2/3 follow the protocol, a new checkpoint can always be finalized).[^casper][^eth2bookffg] FFG also adds the **never-revert-finalized** rule and the **inactivity leak** — if the chain fails to finalize for several epochs, non-voting validators' stakes are gradually drained until the active set regains a 2/3 supermajority, restoring liveness.[^casper][^ethattack] **[fact]**

**LMD-GHOST** (Latest Message Driven Greediest Heaviest Observed SubTree) is the fork-choice rule selecting the chain head between finalizations: at each fork it picks the subtree with the **greatest accumulated weight of validator attestations**, counting only each validator's **latest** message.[^gasper][^gasperdoc] It descends from GHOST (see fork-choice section), adapted from PoW to PoS.[^gasper] **How the gadget layers over the fork-choice:** Casper FFG *constrains* LMD-GHOST — the combined rule runs LMD-GHOST "starting from the highest justified checkpoint as root, ignoring blocks that do not descend from it."[^gasper][^eth2bookforkchoice] Finalized history is a pruned linear chain; LMD-GHOST only operates on the tree *after* the last finalized checkpoint.[^eth2bookforkchoice] **Evolving (verified-as-of 2026-06-16):** Ethereum-specifics here change actively — proposer boosting and edge-case patches were added because "the interface between Casper FFG finalization and LMD-GHOST fork choice" has been a recurring source of attacks (see Consensus-layer attacks).[^eth2bookforkchoice][^ethattack] **[qualified]**

## Other PoS variants: Ouroboros, Tower BFT

**Cardano Ouroboros** (Kiayias, Russell, David, Oliynykov; ePrint 2016/889, CRYPTO 2017) is "the first blockchain protocol based on proof of stake with rigorous security guarantees" and "the first to be based on peer-reviewed research."[^ouroboros][^cardanoouro] Time is divided into **epochs** subdivided into **slots**; one **slot leader** per slot produces a block, with election probability **proportional to stake**.[^ouroboros][^cardanoouro] In **Praos** (ePrint 2017/573) leader election uses a **VRF** evaluated *privately and locally* — a party leads a slot if its VRF output falls below a stake-derived threshold — hiding leaders until they act (defeating adaptive corruption); Praos is **adaptively secure in the semi-synchronous setting** under honest-majority-of-stake.[^praos] Ouroboros is proven secure against an adversary holding <50% of stake, via three named combinatorial properties: **Common Prefix**, **Chain Quality**, **Chain Growth** (math internals out of scope).[^ouroboros][^praos] **Ouroboros Genesis** (ePrint 2018/378) adds a chain-selection rule enabling **bootstrap-from-genesis** (safe rejoin without trusted checkpoints), addressing Praos's weak-subjectivity reliance.[^genesis] **[fact]**

**Solana Tower BFT** is a PBFT-derived voting protocol assisted by **Proof of History (PoH)** as a pre-consensus clock.[^towerbft][^solanawp] PoH is a sequential, pre-image-resistant hash chain (a Verifiable Delay Function) producing a verifiable, ordered passage of time before consensus.[^poh][^shoup] Tower BFT is "a derivation of PBFT" whose key difference is that PoH provides "a global source of time before consensus," so PBFT's exponentially-increasing **timeouts/lockouts** are computed and enforced *in the ledger itself*, cutting peer-to-peer messaging.[^towerbft][^shoup] Validators vote (stake-weighted) on PoH hashes; a vote commits the validator not to vote for a non-descendant for a lockout period that doubles with each subsequent vote, and a validator only votes on observing a **supermajority** on the same fork.[^towerbft][^solanawp] **[fact for PoH/PBFT-derivation; qualified for finality-threshold specifics]**

## Finality: probabilistic vs deterministic/economic

Finality falls into two camps.[^finalityqn][^finalitydagstuhl] **Probabilistic (eventual) finality** (PoW/longest-chain — Bitcoin): blocks are never strictly final but become exponentially harder to revert with each confirmation; "there is always a nonzero chance of reversal… it never reaches absolute zero."[^btc][^finalityqn][^finalitytob] A formal treatment frames this as the **availability-finality dilemma** — by CAP-style reasoning, "most blockchains only offer probabilistic eventual finality" to favor availability under partition.[^finalitydagstuhl] **Deterministic / economic finality** (BFT-style gadgets — Ethereum post-Merge, Tendermint-family, Algorand): once a finality threshold is reached, the block "cannot be reverted unless an attacker destroys" a large slashable stake.[^finalityqn][^gasperdoc] On Ethereum this is specifically **economic finality**: reversal is theoretically possible but requires burning >1/3 of staked ETH.[^gasperdoc] A **finality gadget** differs from a **pure fork-choice rule**: the fork-choice picks the *current* head from a growing tree (always revisable), the gadget declares certain checkpoints *irreversible* and constrains the fork-choice to descend from them.[^gasper][^eth2bookforkchoice] **Disconfirming nuance:** even "deterministic" finality is ultimately economic/conditional — it holds only while <1/3 of stake is adversarial, and Ethereum incidents have shown finality can stall (lose liveness) without losing safety.[^casper][^finalitytob][^ethattack] Practitioners are warned not to conflate *confirmation* (blocks built on top) with *finality* (protocol-level irreversibility).[^finalitytob] **[fact]**

## Fork-choice rules

A **fork-choice rule** is the deterministic function each node applies to its view of the block tree to pick the canonical head when forks exist; it is distinct from a **finality rule**, which permanently commits blocks.[^eth2bookforkchoice][^gasper] **Longest-chain** (Nakamoto): pick the chain with the most cumulative work, ties by first-seen.[^btc] **GHOST** (Greedy Heaviest-Observed Sub-Tree; Sompolinsky & Zohar, 2013, ePrint 2013/881) inverts this: start at genesis and at *each fork* descend into the **heaviest subtree** (most blocks/work rooted at that child), so off-chain ("uncle") blocks still contribute weight to their ancestor; the purpose was security at high throughput, where naive longest-chain degrades below the 50% threshold.[^ghost] **LMD-GHOST** is the PoS adaptation in Gasper: weight is the **stake of attesting validators** (not work), counting only each validator's **latest** message.[^gasper][^gasperdoc] Historical note: Ethereum 1.0 (PoW) never ran GHOST in production (it used an uncle-reward variant); GHOST's descendant LMD-GHOST is what post-Merge PoS Ethereum uses.[^ghostbook] **[fact]**

## Sybil resistance

The **Sybil attack** was formalized by Douceur ("The Sybil Attack," IPTPS 2002): in a P2P system relying on redundancy across "independent" remote entities, "if a single faulty entity can present multiple identities, it can control a substantial fraction of the system."[^douceur] Douceur's impossibility result: "without a logically central, trusted authority… Sybil attacks are always possible except under extreme and unrealistic assumptions of resource parity and coordination."[^douceur] Its constructive escape hatch — "issue resource-demanding challenges to validate identities" — anticipates PoW/PoS.[^douceur] **This is why open/permissionless consensus needs Sybil resistance that classical BFT does not:** PBFT-family protocols assume a *known, fixed, permissioned* validator set (≤1/3 faulty), so identity is given; permissionless systems have no gatekeeper and must make identity *costly*.[^casper] The three main mechanisms tie voting power to a scarce resource: **PoW → cost** (computation/electricity);[^btc] **PoS → stake** (bonded, slashable capital);[^casper] **PoA and others → vetted identity/reputation**, which reintroduces a trusted authority and is used mainly in permissioned/consortium settings.[^trilemmachainlink] Sybil resistance controls *who can vote*; the consensus rule still governs *agreement*.[^casper] **[fact]**

## The scalability-security-decentralization trilemma

The **trilemma** (popularized by Buterin) claims a blockchain using "simple" techniques can achieve only two of three.[^trilemmavitalik][^trilemmachainlink] Buterin's own definitions:[^trilemmavitalik] **Scalability** — "the chain can process more transactions than a single regular node (a consumer laptop) can verify"; **Decentralization** — "the chain can run without any trust dependencies on a small group of large centralized actors… no honest-majority assumption of a set of nodes you cannot join with a consumer laptop"; **Security** — "the chain can resist a large percentage of participating nodes trying to attack it (ideally 50%; anything above 25% is fine, 5% is definitely not fine)" — note this qualifies the naive ">50%" framing. Buterin argues **sharding (with data-availability sampling + fraud/ZK proofs)** can approach all three.[^trilemmavitalik] **Disconfirming:** most sources stress the trilemma "is not a physical law… not a theorem… a reliable heuristic."[^trilemmachainlink] A formal arXiv rebuttal (2507.21111) argues it "rests on semantic equivocation, misuse of distributed systems theory, and a failure to define operational metrics," calling it "a narrative that survives through repetition rather than rigour" — though this rebuttal is itself a single adversarial source, flagged as contested.[^trilemmarebuttal] **[fact that critiques exist; their correctness contested]**

## Consensus-layer attacks

- **51% / majority attack** (PoW): majority hashpower enables reordering, censorship, double-spends via a longer secret chain; cannot mint invalid coins.[^btc][^51pct] Engineers are advised to size confirmations against a **25% attacker**, not 51%, for margin.[^finalitytob]
- **Selfish mining** (Eyal & Sirer, "Majority Is Not Enough," arXiv 1311.0243, 2013): a pool keeps discovered blocks private, mining a secret lead and releasing strategically to orphan honest blocks, earning **revenue exceeding its fair share**.[^selfish][^selfishcacm] Implication: "Bitcoin's mining protocol is not incentive-compatible"; the profitability threshold can be "as low as zero," the safe upper bound is 1/3, so "at least 2/3 of the network needs to be honest" — "a simple majority is not enough."[^selfishcacm][^selfish] Their fix raises the threshold to 1/4.[^selfish]
- **Nothing-at-stake** (PoS): rational validators attest to every fork because it's costless; solved by slashing/finality conditions.[^posfaq][^ethfaqs] *Disconfirming note:* some analyses hold it's overstated in practice because correlated-slashing makes multi-fork voting irrational — but it remains a real design constraint.[^posfaq]
- **Long-range attacks** (PoS): withdrawn-stake validators rewrite deep history with no slashing exposure; neutralized by never-revert-finalized + weak-subjectivity checkpoints.[^casper][^ethattack][^weaksubj]
- **Grinding / randomness manipulation** (PoS): an adversary controlling leader-election randomness "resamples it and picks the most desirable outcome." On Ethereum's **RANDAO**, validators bias selection by *withholding* blocks at epoch tails (ePrint 2025/037; one analysis claims ~33% stake can secure a target slot with high success) — though other studies find mainnet biasability negligible, so magnitudes are contested.[^randao][^randaoslot] Ouroboros Praos's beacon is provably grinding-resistant up to a bounded adversarial stake.[^praos]
- **Balancing / bouncing attacks on Gasper** (Neu, Tas & Tse, "Ebb-and-Flow Protocols," ePrint 2020/1091, S&P 2021): the authors found attacks "in the standard synchronous network model, highlighting a general difficulty with existing finality-gadget-based designs" — a **bouncing attack** keeps the FFG justification point oscillating to stall finality; a **balancing attack** exploits LMD's latest-message handling, the adversary equivocating on two chains and timing vote release to split honest validators and keep them balanced.[^ebbflow][^balancing] A later balancing variant **defeats proposer boosting**, the patch intended to stop earlier balancing attacks.[^balancing] *Disconfirming/landscape balance:* Ethereum maintainers note these were demonstrated only under idealized network conditions and have shipped patches (proposer boosting, RLMD-GHOST research), but researchers counter LMD-GHOST still does not fully achieve dynamic availability — an open, evolving area (verified-as-of 2026-06-16).[^ethattack][^rlmdghost]

## Disconfirming findings

1. **The naive ">50% honest" PoW assumption is materially weaker than advertised** — selfish mining proves Bitcoin isn't incentive-compatible with a profitability threshold potentially as low as 0 and a safe upper bound of 1/3; Buterin's own security definition treats 25%+ resistance (not 50%) as the bar.[^selfishcacm][^selfish][^trilemmavitalik]
2. **"Deterministic" finality is really economic and conditional** — it holds only while <1/3 stake is adversarial and can degrade to a liveness failure (chain stalls without safety loss); probabilistic vs deterministic is a spectrum, not a binary.[^finalitytob][^casper][^finalitydagstuhl]
3. **The trilemma is widely repeated but "not a law, not a theorem"**; a formal arXiv rebuttal calls it semantic equivocation — but that rebuttal is itself single-source/contested.[^trilemmarebuttal][^trilemmachainlink]
4. **PoS-is-more-decentralized is genuinely disputed** — the compounding/"rich-get-richer" effect is real under some reward schemes, while others argue PoW centralizes more via economies of scale; verdict unsettled.[^posfaq][^ethpos]
5. **Gasper's LMD-GHOST does not fully achieve dynamic availability** — balancing/bouncing attacks break liveness even with proposer boosting; mitigations (RLMD-GHOST) are research-stage, demonstrated only under idealized synchronous timing.[^ebbflow][^balancing][^rlmdghost]
