# Exhaustive Guide to Survey Question Types

This document provides a comprehensive overview of survey question types, ranging from fundamental formats to advanced research methodologies. Choosing the right question type is critical for data quality, respondent engagement, and the depth of analysis possible.

---

## 1. Categorical (Closed-Ended) Questions
These questions provide respondents with a pre-defined set of options. They are easy to analyze and ideal for demographic data or simple preferences.

### A. Dichotomous Questions
Questions that offer only two possible answers (e.g., Yes/No, True/False, Agree/Disagree).
* **Example:** "Have you purchased a product from our website in the last 30 days?"
    * ( ) Yes
    * ( ) No

### B. Multiple Choice (Single Select)
Respondents choose one option from a list.
* **Example:** "What is your primary mode of transportation?"
    * ( ) Car
    * ( ) Public Transit
    * ( ) Bicycle
    * ( ) Walking
    * ( ) Other

### C. Multiple Answer Selection (Multi-Select / Checkbox)
Respondents can select more than one option.
* **Example:** "Which of the following social media platforms do you use daily? (Select all that apply)"
    * [ ] Instagram
    * [ ] LinkedIn
    * [ ] X (Twitter)
    * [ ] TikTok
    * [ ] Facebook

### D. Dropdown Menu
A variation of single-select multiple choice, used to save space when the list of options is long (e.g., selecting a Country or State).
* **Example:** "Select your country of residence: [Dropdown Menu]"

---

## 2. Scaled Questions
Scaled questions measure the intensity of a respondent's feelings or attitudes.

### A. Likert Scale
Measures agreement or disagreement with a statement, usually on a 5 or 7-point scale.
* **Example:** "To what extent do you agree with the following: 'The software is easy to navigate.'"
    1. Strongly Disagree
    2. Disagree
    3. Neither Agree nor Disagree
    4. Agree
    5. Strongly Agree

### B. Semantic Differential (Bipolar)
Respondents rate an object between two polar opposite adjectives.
* **Example:** "How would you describe our customer service?"
    * Efficient [ 1 ] [ 2 ] [ 3 ] [ 4 ] [ 5 ] Inefficient
    * Friendly [ 1 ] [ 2 ] [ 3 ] [ 4 ] [ 5 ] Unfriendly

### C. Unipolar Scale
Measures the presence or absence of a single item/trait, usually ranging from "None" to "Maximum."
* **Example:** "How helpful was the tutorial?"
    1. Not at all helpful
    2. Slightly helpful
    3. Moderately helpful
    4. Very helpful
    5. Extremely helpful

### D. Numeric Rating Scale (NRS)
Respondents choose a number to represent their sentiment.
* **Example:** "On a scale of 0 to 10, how likely are you to recommend us to a friend?" (Net Promoter Score / NPS)

---

## 3. Matrix and Grid Questions
Used to ask multiple questions that share the same response options.

### A. Standard Matrix
A grid where rows represent different items and columns represent the scale.
* **Example:** "Please rate your satisfaction with the following aspects of your stay:"
    * (Rows: Cleanliness, Staff Courtesy, Room Comfort)
    * (Columns: Very Dissatisfied, Dissatisfied, Neutral, Satisfied, Very Satisfied)

### B. Side-by-Side Matrix
Allows respondents to evaluate two different dimensions for the same set of items.
* **Example:** "Rate the **Importance** of these features vs. your **Satisfaction** with them."
    * (Item: Battery Life) -> [Importance Scale 1-5] | [Satisfaction Scale 1-5]

---

## 4. Ranking and Comparative Questions
These questions ask respondents to evaluate items relative to one another.

### A. Rank Order
Respondents arrange a list of items based on preference or importance.
* **Example:** "Rank the following features from 1 (Most Important) to 4 (Least Important):"
    * [ ] Price
    * [ ] Quality
    * [ ] Brand Reputation
    * [ ] Customer Support

### B. Constant Sum
Respondents allocate a total number of points (usually 100) across different options.
* **Example:** "Distribute 100 points among these features based on their value to you:"
    * Speed: ____
    * Design: ____
    * Security: ____
    * Total: 100

### C. MaxDiff (Best-Worst Scaling)
Respondents are shown a subset of items and asked to identify the best and worst (or most and least important) among them.
* **Example:** "Out of the following four features, which is the **Most Important** and which is the **Least Important**?"
    * [Most] | [Feature] | [Least]

### D. Paired Comparison
Respondents are presented with two options and asked to choose the preferred one.
* **Example:** "Which do you prefer: ( ) Remote Work or ( ) Office Work?"

---

## 5. Open-Ended Questions
Used to gather qualitative data where respondents answer in their own words.

### A. Single-Line Text
For short answers like names or email addresses.
* **Example:** "What is your job title?"

### B. Multi-Line Text (Essay/Comment Box)
For detailed feedback or explanations.
* **Example:** "Please describe any challenges you faced during the checkout process."

---

## 6. Visual and Interactive Questions
Modern survey tools use interactive elements to reduce "survey fatigue."

### A. Star Rating
A visual representation of a numeric scale.
* **Example:** "Rate this movie: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐"

### B. Visual Analog Scale (Slider)
Respondents move a pointer along a continuous line.
* **Example:** "Drag the slider to indicate your level of stress (0 = Low, 100 = High)."

### C. Image Choice
Respondents select an image instead of text.
* **Example:** "Which of these logo designs do you prefer? [Image A] [Image B] [Image C]"

### D. Heatmap
Respondents click on specific areas of an image (like a website mockup) to show what they like or dislike.

---

## 7. Advanced Research Questions

### A. Conjoint Analysis
A sophisticated technique where respondents choose between different product "profiles" with varying attributes to determine which attributes drive decision-making.
* **Example:** "Which Laptop would you buy?"
    * Option A: $800, 8GB RAM, 13-inch screen.
    * Option B: $1200, 16GB RAM, 15-inch screen.

### B. Demographic / Profiling Questions
Standardized questions to segment data.
* **Example:** "What is your annual household income range?"

### C. Drill-Down (Hierarchical)
A multi-step question where the second choice depends on the first.
* **Example:** Select Car Make -> [Toyota] -> Select Model -> [Camry].

---

## Summary Table of Question Use Cases

| Question Type | Best Used For... | Data Type |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dichotomous** | Quick filtering / Screening | Nominal |
| **Likert Scale** | Measuring attitudes/opinions | Ordinal |
| **NPS (0-10)** | Customer loyalty/advocacy | Interval |
| **Constant Sum** | Determining weight/budget priority | Ratio |
| **Open-Ended** | "Why" questions and deep insights | Qualitative |
| **MaxDiff** | Prioritizing long lists of features | Comparative |
