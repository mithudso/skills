# Situational Interview Questions — Worked Examples

Full worked Q&A for the six most common hospitality behavioral interview scenarios. Use STAR method (Situation → Task → Action → Result). All examples sourced from major hospitality interview guides (see `sources.md`).

---

## Difficult or Dissatisfied Guest

**Question form:** "Tell me about a time you handled a dissatisfied customer." / "A guest complains their drink doesn't taste right — what do you do?"

**What's being probed:** De-escalation instincts, whether you comp without authorization, guest recovery vs. doormat behavior.

**Strong answer elements:** Acknowledge without invalidating; offer a specific resolution (remake, adjustment, manager escalation if needed); follow up. Don't lead with "I comped it" — comp authority varies by venue and unauthorized comps flag you as a liability.

---

## Intoxicated Patron

**Question form:** "How do you handle a guest who appears intoxicated and keeps ordering?"

**What's being probed:** RSA (Responsible Service of Alcohol) instincts, conflict-aversion vs. policy compliance, manager escalation.

**Strong answer elements:** Switch to water/food proactively before a direct refusal; offer the decline calmly ("for your safety, I can't pour another — can I get you some water?"); document and notify manager per house policy. Do not be hostile; do not let the table pressure you.

---

## Being in the Weeds (Rush Management)

**Question form:** "Describe how you manage your section when you're slammed." / "How do you prioritize when you have 10 tickets open?"

**What's being probed:** Triage instincts (ticket age, drink complexity, guest urgency), communication with barbacks/expo, composure signaling.

**Strong answer elements:** Acknowledge and name the triage system (oldest tickets first, then complexity-weighted, then simple pours for waiting guests to buy time); call barbacks proactively; brief the expo on delays before guests escalate.

## 86'd Item

**Question form:** "A guest orders something we're out of — how do you handle it?"

**What's being probed:** Menu knowledge, upsell reflex, whether the candidate makes the guest feel they chose an alternative vs. got downgraded.

**Strong answer elements:** Know three alternatives for the item before you arrive at the table ("we're out of that tonight — but if you liked it, I can suggest X which has a similar profile, or Y which is different but very popular with our regulars"). Frame the substitute as a recommendation, not a fallback.

---

## FOH/BOH Conflict

**Question form:** "Describe a conflict between you and kitchen staff / a cook / the expeditor."

**What's being probed:** Whether you take sides publicly, empathy for BOH pressure, appropriate escalation.

**Red-flag behaviors:** Bashing former coworkers by name; framing the result as "I won"; claiming there was never any conflict (reads as naive or dishonest).

**Strong answer elements:** Name the specific tension (ticket timing, special-request miscommunication), describe the de-escalation you initiated, describe the systemic fix (new communication signal, a conversation with the manager, a workflow change). Result is a better system, not a vindicated ego.

---

## Upselling Approach

**Question form:** "Walk me through how you suggest upgrades or additions to a guest's order." / "What's your approach to upselling?"

**What's being probed:** Whether the upsell feels authentic vs. pushy, product knowledge depth, whether candidate can cite a result.

**Strong answer elements:** Lead with product knowledge as the driver ("I upsell because I genuinely know what pairs well, not because I have a quota"). Quantify where possible: "I averaged $X/ticket in add-ons" or "my check average ran 15% above the floor average." Describe the technique without sounding scripted.
