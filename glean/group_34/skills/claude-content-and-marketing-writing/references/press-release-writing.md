# Press Release Writing

## Overview

A press release is a short, journalist-facing news document with a single goal: make it so easy to publish your news that a busy reporter (or a wire-service automated feed) can run it nearly verbatim. The form is old, conservative, and intentionally boring — every deviation from convention is a friction point that costs pickup.

This skill is for **external, wire-style press releases** — the kind that go on PR Newswire, BusinessWire, GlobeNewswire, Cision, or a company's "News" page that journalists scrape. It is **not** for owned-blog launch posts (those have a different voice and structure — see `release-blog-and-launch-narrative`), and not for internal/employee announcements (see `executive-comms`).

The defining traits of a press release:

- Third person, past or present tense, never "we"
- Inverted-pyramid structure: most important fact in sentence one, everything else in decreasing importance
- AP Style dateline at the top of the body
- Stable boilerplate at the bottom (the "About" paragraph) that gets reused release after release
- Quote attribution that is publication-ready (a journalist should be able to copy-paste the quote)
- End mark: `###` centered on its own line (or the older `-30-`)
- 400–600 words is the sweet spot; under 300 looks thin, over 800 rarely gets read

## Core concepts

### 1. The headline (and the subhead)

The headline is the first — and often only — thing a journalist reads. Treat it like a news article headline, not a marketing headline.

**Rules**:

- Under ~100 characters. Wire services truncate longer ones in newsroom feeds.
- Active verbs. Present tense for news happening now; future tense for upcoming.
- Title Case (most wire services) or Sentence case (some modern releases). Pick one and stick to it.
- No marketing puffery: avoid "industry-leading," "revolutionary," "best-in-class," "game-changing."
- State the news, not the spin. "Acme Acquires Beta Corp for $400M" beats "Acme Cements Future-Forward Leadership Position."

**The subhead** (optional but recommended) is one line under the headline that adds the second-most-important fact. Use it to land the "so what" — the consequence, the size of the deal, the differentiator.

```text
Acme Corp Acquires Beta Analytics for $400 Million
Deal Doubles Acme's Enterprise Data Footprint and Adds 1,200 Engineers
```

### 2. Release-type tag

Always present, always at the top, always in ALL CAPS:

- `FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE` — the default; journalists may publish on receipt
- `EMBARGOED UNTIL: [Day, Month Day, Year, HH:MM TIME ZONE]` — journalists may **not** publish before the listed time
- `MEDIA ADVISORY` — for an upcoming event/availability, not the news itself
- `HOLD FOR RELEASE` — older convention; rarely used today

For embargoes, **always include the time zone** and a 24-hour or "a.m./p.m." indicator. Embargo breaches are a chronic problem; ambiguous time strings cause them.

### 3. The dateline (AP Style)

The dateline is the very first element of the body. AP format:

```text
CITY, State Abbreviation, Month Day, Year — <lede starts here>
```

**Specifics**:

- City name in ALL CAPS
- Comma, then the AP state abbreviation (not USPS!). AP abbreviates most states (e.g., Calif., N.Y., Mass., Wash.) but **never abbreviates** Alaska, Hawaii, Idaho, Iowa, Maine, Ohio, Texas, or Utah.
- Comma, then date written out: `Nov. 12, 2026` (AP uses abbreviated months for Jan., Feb., Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., Dec. — never March, April, May, June, July).
- Em dash (`—`) before the lede.
- For non-US releases: city in caps, country if not obvious. e.g., `LONDON, Nov. 12, 2026 —`
- For multi-city news: list both. e.g., `NEW YORK and LONDON, Nov. 12, 2026 —`

### 4. The lede (and the five Ws)

The first sentence must contain the five Ws — **Who, What, When, Where, Why** — and ideally a sixth (**How**). If a reader stops after sentence one, they should know the entire news.

**Bad lede** (delays the news):

> Acme Corp, a leading provider of analytics solutions for enterprise customers worldwide, is excited to announce today an important new milestone in its ongoing growth journey.

**Good lede** (five Ws in one sentence):

> Acme Corp today announced it has acquired Beta Analytics, a Boston-based real-time data platform, for $400 million in cash and stock, expanding Acme's enterprise data footprint and adding 1,200 engineers.

Notes:

- "Today" or the explicit date — never "recently" or "this week"
- The company name and the news verb come early
- One sentence is fine, even if long; clarity beats brevity in the lede

### 5. The body (inverted pyramid)

After the lede, descend in importance:

1. **Paragraph 2**: amplification — the size, the scope, the differentiator. Add the second-most-important context.
2. **Paragraph 3**: the executive quote (see below).
3. **Paragraph 4–5**: supporting detail — product/feature specifics, timeline, terms.
4. **Paragraph 6**: optional second quote — partner, customer, board member.
5. **Paragraph 7**: practical info — availability, pricing, link, event details.
6. **Boilerplate**: stable "About" paragraph(s).
7. **Media contact** block.
8. **End mark**: `###`.

Keep paragraphs 2–3 sentences each. Mobile-first journalists scan; long paragraphs get cut.

### 6. Quote attribution

Quotes are the most-cited content in a release; reporters copy-paste them. Get them right.

**Standard pattern**:

> "We chose Beta because their real-time architecture is the strongest in the market," said Jane Doe, CEO of Acme Corp. "This acquisition lets us deliver sub-second insights to every enterprise customer by Q2."

**Rules**:

- Attribution after the first sentence of the quote, not before
- `said` is the AP-standard verb. Avoid `stated`, `commented`, `noted`, `exclaimed`.
- Full name + title + company on first reference; last name only on subsequent references
- One quote per speaker is plenty; two quotes max in a single release
- The quote should sound like a person spoke it. If the CEO would never say "leverage synergies," neither should the release.
- Quotes are the only place opinion belongs. Everything else is fact.

### 7. The boilerplate

The boilerplate is the "About [Company]" paragraph at the bottom. It is a stable, third-person, factual paragraph reused across releases. Treat it like a versioned asset.

**Rules**:

- Third person only. Never "we," "us," "our."
- 60–120 words. Long enough to convey scope; short enough to skim.
- Updated when a hard fact changes (employee count, customer count, headquarters, public/private status).
- One per legal entity. If you have subsidiaries, each gets its own.

**Skeleton**:

```text
About Acme Corp
Acme Corp is a [category] company that helps [audience] [job-to-be-done]. Founded in [year] and headquartered in [city], Acme serves [customer count or named marquee customers]. The company is publicly traded on [exchange: ticker]. For more information, visit www.acme.com.
```

### 8. Media contact block

Always present, always last (before the end mark). Journalists need a single point of contact.

```text
Media Contact:
Jordan Smith
Director of Communications, Acme Corp
+1 (415) 555-0142
press@acme.com
```

Include: name, title, company (if not obvious), phone (with country code for international wire), email. Optional: PR-agency contact as a second block.

### 9. The end mark

The release ends with one of:

- `###` (most common today; centered on its own line)
- `-30-` (older newspaper convention; still acceptable, especially in print contexts)

The end mark exists so wire-service editors know nothing was truncated mid-transmission. Always include it.

### 10. Photo and asset captions

If the release includes images (most wire submissions do), each image gets a caption. Captions are short, third person, and identify everyone left-to-right.

```text
Caption: Acme CEO Jane Doe (left) and Beta CEO Sam Wei (right) sign the acquisition agreement at Acme's San Francisco headquarters on Nov. 12, 2026. Photo credit: Acme Corp.
```

Photo credit is mandatory. Multimedia News Releases (MNRs) on PR Newswire/BusinessWire require alt text for accessibility.

## Templates and examples

### Full press release skeleton

```text
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

Acme Corp Acquires Beta Analytics for $400 Million
Deal Doubles Acme's Enterprise Data Footprint and Adds 1,200 Engineers

SAN FRANCISCO, Nov. 12, 2026 — Acme Corp (NASDAQ: ACME) today announced it has acquired Beta Analytics, a Boston-based real-time data platform, for $400 million in cash and stock, expanding Acme's enterprise data footprint and adding 1,200 engineers to its global workforce.

The acquisition is the largest in Acme's 15-year history and the company's third in 2026. Combined, the two organizations will serve more than 8,000 enterprise customers across 47 countries and process an estimated 2.3 trillion data events per day. The deal is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, subject to customary regulatory approvals.

"We chose Beta because their real-time architecture is the strongest in the market," said Jane Doe, chief executive officer of Acme Corp. "This acquisition lets us deliver sub-second insights to every enterprise customer by Q2 and brings on a world-class engineering team led by Sam Wei."

Beta Analytics, founded in 2018, has grown to $90 million in annual recurring revenue and serves customers including three of the five largest U.S. banks. As part of Acme, the Beta product line will continue to operate under its existing brand and leadership team for at least 24 months following close.

"Joining Acme gives our team the scale to bring real-time data to a much larger customer base," said Sam Wei, chief executive officer of Beta Analytics. "We share Acme's belief that enterprise data should be instant, accurate, and operational, not delayed and analytical."

The combined company will continue to maintain headquarters in San Francisco, with Beta's Boston office becoming Acme's East Coast engineering hub. No layoffs are planned in connection with the transaction.

Goldman Sachs served as financial advisor and Wilson Sonsini served as legal advisor to Acme Corp. Morgan Stanley and Cooley advised Beta Analytics.

About Acme Corp
Acme Corp is an enterprise analytics company that helps Fortune 500 organizations turn operational data into real-time decisions. Founded in 2011 and headquartered in San Francisco, Acme serves more than 6,800 customers in 42 countries and is publicly traded on the NASDAQ Global Select Market under the ticker ACME. For more information, visit www.acme.com.

About Beta Analytics
Beta Analytics is a real-time data platform company that processes streaming data for financial-services, retail, and healthcare customers. Founded in 2018 and headquartered in Boston, Beta serves more than 1,200 enterprise customers. For more information, visit www.betaanalytics.com.

Media Contact:
Jordan Smith
Director of Communications, Acme Corp
+1 (415) 555-0142
press@acme.com

###
```

### Embargoed-release variant

Replace the release tag and add a clear time zone:

```text
EMBARGOED UNTIL: Wednesday, Nov. 12, 2026, 9:00 a.m. ET

<rest of release identical>
```

For embargoes lifted in coordination with an executive's keynote or a regulatory filing, mention the trigger in the cover email — not in the release itself.

### Media advisory variant (event, not news)

Media advisories are shorter (200–300 words) and structured around logistics:

```text
MEDIA ADVISORY

What: Acme Corp Q4 Product Launch and Press Q&A
When: Tuesday, Dec. 9, 2026, 10:00 a.m. PT
Where: Acme HQ, 100 Market St., San Francisco, CA — Auditorium A
Who: Jane Doe, CEO; Priya Patel, CTO; and select customers
Why: Acme will demonstrate the launch of <product line> and take questions from press

RSVP: press@acme.com by Friday, Dec. 5
Press passes required at door. Livestream link available on request.

Media Contact:
Jordan Smith
+1 (415) 555-0142
press@acme.com

###
```

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing-style headline ("Acme Unleashes the Future of Data") | Journalists reject it as spin | State the news plainly. "Acme Launches Real-Time Data Platform for Enterprises" |
| First person ("we are pleased to announce") | Press releases are third-person reportage, not memos | "Acme Corp today announced..." |
| Vague dateline ("San Francisco, November 2026") | AP requires CITY, STATE, full date | "SAN FRANCISCO, Nov. 12, 2026 —" |
| Two paragraphs of background before the news | Inverted pyramid violated; journalists bail | Move news to sentence one; background to paragraph 4 |
| Quotes stuffed with jargon ("leverage paradigm-shifting synergies") | Journalists won't run unrunnable quotes | Rewrite quotes as something a human would actually say |
| Boilerplate that reads like a sales page | Press releases are factual, not promotional | Strip the boilerplate to verifiable facts: HQ, founding year, customer count, exchange/ticker |
| No media contact | Journalists can't follow up; release dies | Always include a named human with phone + email |
| Missing `###` end mark | Wire editors don't know if content was truncated | Always close with `###` centered |
| Embargo with no time zone ("embargoed until Nov. 12") | Embargo will break early in some time zone | "EMBARGOED UNTIL: Nov. 12, 2026, 9:00 a.m. ET" |
| Two pages of fine print at the end before the boilerplate | Disclaimers belong in a separate forward-looking-statements block, not interleaved | Place SEC-style safe-harbor language as a labeled block between the last quote and the boilerplate |
| Distributing through three wires simultaneously | Duplicate news kills SEO and confuses journalists | Pick one primary wire; cross-post on owned channels |

## Decision heuristics

**When to write a press release at all**

- Is this newsworthy to a journalist who doesn't work for you? If not, it's a blog post or a customer email — not a release.
- Tests: a funding round, an M&A deal, a major product launch, a C-level executive change, a regulatory milestone, a notable customer win, a research finding, an industry-first event.
- Not tests: a feature update (blog), a webinar (advisory), a hiring milestone (owned channels), a quarterly internal update (intranet).

**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE vs EMBARGOED**

- Use IMMEDIATE when there's no reason to coordinate timing.
- Use EMBARGOED when the news ties to a keynote, an SEC filing window, a paywalled research drop, or a coordinated multi-region launch. Embargoes are a tool for getting deeper coverage — reporters get time to research, interview, and write before the bell.
- Do not embargo trivially. Reporters resent it.

**Wire-service selection**

- **PR Newswire** (Cision): broad, journalist-facing, most credible for general/B2C and tech news.
- **BusinessWire**: required for many public-company disclosures; strong on investor-relations distribution; broader industry targeting (200+ categories vs PR Newswire's ~100).
- **GlobeNewswire**: cost-effective for public-company filings; strong in life sciences and small/mid-cap finance.
- **Owned-channel only**: acceptable when the news is for existing customers/community; not a substitute when you need third-party pickup.

**Multimedia attachments**

- Always attach at least one image — wire services rank multimedia releases higher in their feeds, and journalists are more likely to publish releases with usable art.
- Provide images at 1200×675 minimum, in JPEG or PNG, with alt text.
- Include a logo (transparent PNG), an executive headshot, and a product/contextual image when possible.

**Length budget**

- 400–600 words for a standard news release.
- 200–300 words for a media advisory.
- 800+ words is acceptable only for major M&A, IPO, or regulatory releases where the news genuinely requires the room.

**Tone calibration**

- Default: neutral, third person, news-of-the-day. Imagine a wire reporter wrote it.
- For research releases: add a "Methodology" paragraph between the quote and boilerplate.
- For product launches: tightly bound the news to availability, pricing, and target customer; do not let it slide into a feature catalog.
