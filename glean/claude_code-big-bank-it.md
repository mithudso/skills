## The 60-second orientation (BLUF)

1. **Ledger didn't move, mostly still hasn't.** As of 2026, analyst/vendor sources put **~43–45 of world's top 50 banks** still on mainframe for mission-critical core, with large COBOL estate (most-cited figure ~220B lines in production, credible estimates range to ~800B). Large banks reportedly spend **~70%** of IT budget maintaining legacy. Core replacement rare — risk/ROI math brutal: McKinsey's repeatedly-cited figure is **~30% of core transformations succeed** at full migration; IBM's 2025 survey of ~700 banking leaders (banks >$10B assets) found **fewer than half reported meaningful gains** after modernizing. Dominant strategy: **wrap, don't replace** — keep core as strong-consistency **system of record (SoR)**, build **system of engagement (SoE)** and data layer around it. *That data/engagement layer around core is where new architecture (and new datastores) actually land.* → `core-banking-and-sor-soe.md`

2. **Cloud broad but shallow; hybrid is steady state — not transition.** ~90%+ of banks "have some cloud," but only **~10% of core workloads** moved (Accenture). Hybrid + multi-cloud is durable end-state. Constraints bank architects raise are not "is it secure" but four supervisory pressures: **concentration risk, documented-and-tested exit strategy, portability/interoperability, data residency/sovereignty.** Product that **runs anywhere** (on-prem, bank's own cloud account, sovereign region) with credible **exit/egress story** de-risks buyer's resilience exposure. Don't pitch cloud-transformation thesis; pitch *workload's placement decision*. → `cloud-journey-and-constraints.md`

3. **Bank runs several latency/availability regimes in parallel.** Capital-markets trading is outlier (microsecond determinism, co-location, FPGA, kernel-bypass, kdb+-class time-series) — **most desks not microsecond-sensitive**. Payments span windowed high-value RTGS (Fedwire, CHAPS, T2), 24x7 instant rails (FedNow, RTP, SEPA Instant), batch ACH — orchestrated behind **payment hub**. **ISO 20022** is dominant migration (Fedwire cut over July 2025; SWIFT MT→ISO coexistence ended Nov 22 2025); "translate-at-the-gateway without modernizing downstream" is recognized failure mode. Always-on real at *instant-rail edge*; general channels still tolerate windows. → `latency-payments-and-247.md`

4. **Modernization incremental; big-bang feared.** "Rs" (rehost/replatform/refactor/replace) frame portfolio; **strangler-fig** and **encapsulation** (wrap legacy behind APIs) are low-risk patterns banks actually use. **TSB's 2018 big-bang core migration** — outage, £48.65m regulatory fine, ~£370m total cost — is canonical cautionary tale that pushed industry to coexistence/progressive modernization, now reinforced by operational-resilience "must be able to revert" expectations. **Batch window** (overnight EOD posting, accrual, statements, settlement files) shrinking under 24x7 pressure; itself hard modernization constraint. → `batch-and-mainframe-modernization.md`

5. **Change gated by design; cadence slow on purpose.** Every production change is ticketed, risk-assessed, approved by someone other than author, scheduled, tested, **evidenced for examiners**. **Segregation of duties** (developers can't deploy to prod; maker-checker; PAM; logged break-glass) is SOX IT general control, not preference. **Change-freeze windows** lock production around quarter/year-end and peak periods. Live tension: high deployment frequency (*DevOps Research & Assessment* "DORA" metrics — distinct from EU DORA regulation) collides with bank change control; industry resolution: **enforce separation in pipeline, not by separate teams** (peer review + automated gates generating stronger audit evidence than CAB meeting). → `change-governance-and-regulated-sdlc.md`

6. **Resilience regulated, tested property — SRE arrives adapted.** Mission-critical bank systems target near-zero **RPO** and minutes **RTO**; architecture is **metro-distance synchronous replica (RPO≈0) plus geo-distance asynchronous replica (DR)** — resolution of genuine tension between regulators wanting *distance* and engineers needing *proximity* for synchronous replication. Shift from "disaster recovery" to **operational resilience** (assume disruption happens; stay within impact tolerances); **recovery testing** ("untested DR plan is hypothesis") now supervisory expectation. **SRE** (SLO/error budgets) adopted but adapted — "embrace risk" sits uneasily where every failed request is denied payment; error budgets reframed as learning/governance mechanism. → `resiliency-dr-and-sre.md`

7. **Data and integration estate layered and build-heavy.** Analytics moved EDW (Teradata/Exadata) → Hadoop data lake (largely disappointment) → **lakehouse** (Databricks/Delta, Snowflake, open table formats like Iceberg). **Apache Kafka** is de-facto event backbone; **CDC** off core/mainframe is dominant "modernize without ripping out core" move. Integration shifted from **ESB/SOA** era (TIBCO, IBM MQ — still message-queue workhorse) to **API-led** connectivity and gateways, driven (often as compliance exercise) by open banking. Big banks run **tens of thousands of engineers** (JPMorgan ~65k technologists / ~$18B+ tech budget as of 2026; Capital One as "technology company that does banking") and institutionalize open source via **FINOS** — honest build-vs-buy reality is hybrid, in-house build fails more often than champions admit. → `data-platform-and-integration.md`

## How to use this with a customer (the seller's frame)

- **Lead with workload, not transformation.** Estate is patchwork of SoR (don't touch ledger), SoE/data layer (real opportunity), trading (specialized), payments (rail-specific), analytics (lakehouse). Identify *which* layer before proposing anything.
- **Expect "can you leave?" inside technical evaluation.** Exit/portability/concentration questions are now architecture questions, not just procurement ones. Documentable run-anywhere/egress story is *compliance asset*.
- **Respect control plane.** Slow cadence, change freezes, segregation of duties are non-negotiable controls. Credible posture: "our deployment model fits your regulated SDLC and produces audit evidence" — not "we'll help you move faster."
- **Resilience evidenced, not asserted.** RTO/RPO, tested failover, and (increasingly) burn-rate/SLO telemetry-as-audit-evidence are how bank proves operational resilience to examiners. Position to *feed* that evidence, not bypass it.
