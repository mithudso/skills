# Venture Marketing Strategy, Local SEO & Marketing Compliance

The general for-profit marketing-strategy layer for a solo/lean North Carolina founder who does
their own marketing. Strategy first (who you serve and why they pick you), then the channels
that compound for a local business (**local SEO is the single highest-leverage channel**), then
the compliance rules a DIY marketer must not get wrong.

**Scope and routing.** This is the for-profit/strategy general. For a charitable cause/nonprofit
campaign, donor journey, cause-storytelling ethics, or Google Ad Grants → `venture-cause-nonprofit-marketing`.
For design tooling, brand assets, and Canva → `venture-canva-founder-brand-stack`. For
sentence-level copy (landing-page lines, ad headlines, email body) → `content-and-marketing-writing`.
For marketing-mix modeling / incrementality / attribution math → `da-22` (in `da-applied-and-communication`).
This skill tells you *what* to do and *why*; those skills tell you *how to write it*, *how to design it*,
and *how to measure it precisely*.

**How to use this skill.** Work top-down: nail strategy (STP + positioning) before touching tactics;
pick 2-3 channels and go deep rather than spreading thin; treat the compliance section as a
pre-send checklist, not background reading. Compliance figures below are cited to the regulator
with an as-of date — re-verify before relying on them (see Disclaimer).

## 1. Marketing Strategy Fundamentals

Strategy is the set of choices about **who** you serve, **what** you offer them, and **why** they
choose you over alternatives. Tactics (posts, ads, emails) only work when these choices are clear.

### STP: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning
STP is the backbone framework: divide the market, pick where to compete, then own a place in the
customer's mind. It is *more* important for a small business, not less: with a limited budget you
win by focusing on a niche you can serve better than larger competitors rather than competing for
everyone. [Smart Insights, STP model; HubSpot STP guide]

- **Segmentation**: divide the broad market into subgroups by shared traits: demographic (age,
  income), geographic (neighborhood, county, central for a local business), psychographic
  (values, lifestyle), and behavioral (usage, loyalty, buying occasion). Behavioral and
  geographic segments are usually the most actionable for a local solo founder.
- **Targeting**: pick the segment(s) worth serving. Score each on size, growth, reachability, and
  fit with your strengths. A solo founder should typically pick **one** primary segment to start.
- **Positioning**: decide the distinct, valuable place you want to hold in that segment's mind
  relative to competitors (e.g., "the only Saturday-available plumber in north Durham").

### Value proposition
A clear statement of the benefit a customer gets, for whom, and why it is better/different than
alternatives. A useful test: *"For [target customer] who [need], [your offering] provides [key
benefit], unlike [main alternative]."* Keep it about the customer's outcome, not your features.

### Positioning statement (internal compass)
A one-paragraph internal articulation that keeps all marketing consistent:

> For **[target segment]** who **[need/occasion]**, **[brand]** is the **[category/frame of
> reference]** that **[single most compelling benefit]** because **[reason to believe / proof]**.

You rarely publish this verbatim; it governs your messaging, channel choices, and copy.

### The marketing mix — 4Ps / 7Ps
The classic **4Ps** are **Product, Price, Place, Promotion**. For service businesses (most solo
ventures), extend to **7Ps** by adding **People, Process, Physical Evidence**: because in services
the people, the steps of delivery, and tangible cues (reviews, photos, a clean storefront or site)
*are* the product experience. [Mind Tools / Smart Insights; HubSpot]

- **Product**: the offer, packaging, service tiers, guarantee.
- **Price**: pricing model and level; signals quality and positions you (see `venture-small-business-planning` for unit economics/pricing math).
- **Place**: where/how it's bought and delivered (in-person, service-area, online).
- **Promotion**: the channels in this skill (SEO, GBP, social, email, ads).
- **People / Process / Physical Evidence**: service quality, repeatable delivery, and trust signals.

### Brand strategy basics
Brand is the consistent promise and personality a customer associates with you. For a solo founder,
the minimum viable brand is: a clear positioning, a consistent name/logo/colors/voice (build assets
in `venture-canva-founder-brand-stack`), and disciplined consistency across every touchpoint
(GBP, site, social, email). Consistency beats cleverness; it compounds recognition and trust.

## 2. Go-To-Market (GTM) for a Solo/Lean Founder

GTM is the plan to reach your first/next customers with limited time and money.

1. **Validate the segment and offer first**: confirm a real, reachable group has the problem and
   will pay (customer discovery, product-market fit — see `venture-small-business-planning`).
2. **Pick a beachhead**: one narrow segment + one geography you can dominate before expanding.
3. **Choose 1-2 primary channels**, not all of them (Section 4). For a local business the default
   beachhead channels are **local SEO/GBP + word-of-mouth/reviews**.
4. **Set up measurement early**: a way to know where leads come from (call tracking, "how did you
   hear about us?", GBP insights, simple UTM links). You can't optimize what you can't see.
5. **Define a minimum viable marketing stack**: 3-4 core channels mastered beats 8 done poorly;
   master one before adding the next. [StartupOwl; micro-founder playbooks, 2026]

Realistic timelines: organic channels (SEO, content, social) typically take **3-9 months** to show
meaningful traction; paid delivers immediate visibility but stops when spend stops. [HubSpot;
multiple 2026 organic-vs-paid analyses]

## 3. The Marketing Funnel, Customer Journey & Lifecycle

Model the path from stranger to advocate so you can find and fix the leakiest stage.

**Classic funnel / journey:** **Awareness → Consideration → Conversion → Retention → Advocacy.**
- *Awareness*: they discover you exist (search, social, referral, GBP).
- *Consideration*: they evaluate you (site, reviews, comparisons, content).
- *Conversion*: they buy / book / call.
- *Retention*: they come back, you keep them engaged (email, service).
- *Advocacy*: they refer others and leave reviews (your cheapest growth).

**AARRR ("Pirate Metrics," Dave McClure):** **Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral,
Revenue** (often shown with **Awareness** prepended, AAARRR). It reframes the journey as a
measurable funnel so you can spot the bottleneck stage. [Medium/Mertz; UserGuiding] Map a metric to
each stage (e.g., Acquisition = GBP views/site sessions; Activation = first call/booking; Retention
= repeat purchase; Referral = reviews/NPS; Revenue = LTV).

**Practical rule:** find the single stage with the biggest drop-off and fix that before optimizing
anything else. For most local businesses the highest-leverage levers are Awareness (local search
visibility) and Advocacy (reviews), which feed each other.

## 4. Channel Selection on a Lean Budget

Pick channels by where your *target segment* already looks for what you sell, your budget (time vs
money), and how fast you need results.

**Organic-first is the default for a lean/solo founder**: organic channels cost time, not money,
and compound. Reserve paid for speed or to amplify what already converts. A common hybrid: ~70-80%
effort organic, ~20-30% small paid behind your best-performing organic content. [HubSpot organic
marketing; multiple 2026 organic-vs-paid guides] *(Note: some sources advise new local
service businesses to lean more heavily on paid in Year 1 to get clients fast; weigh against your
cash runway and how quickly you need revenue.)*

Channel shortlist for a local for-profit, roughly by leverage:

| Channel | Cost | Speed | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Local SEO / Google Business Profile** | Time | Medium (30-60 days for GBP changes) | Local intent buyers searching now — **highest leverage for a local org** |
| **Reviews / word-of-mouth** | Time | Medium | Trust + map-pack ranking; feeds every other channel |
| **Website + content/SEO** | Time | Slow (6-9 mo) | Compounding organic traffic, answering buyer questions |
| **Email** | Low | Medium | Retention, repeat business, owned audience (you own the list) |
| **Organic social** | Time | Slow-Med | Awareness, personality, local relevance |
| **Paid search (Google Ads)** | Money | Fast | High-intent "near me / service + city" queries when organic isn't ranking yet |
| **Paid social (Meta)** | Money | Fast | Awareness/demand-gen, retargeting site visitors |

Master one or two before adding more. Track source of every lead so you can cut what doesn't pay.

## 5. Content Marketing & SEO Fundamentals

SEO is often the most cost-effective channel for a small business: it captures people *actively
searching* for what you offer. The fundamentals (keywords, intent, quality, on-page) still drive
results in 2026. [Search Engine Land 2026; multiple 2026 SEO guides]

**Keyword research.** Identify the terms your buyers actually type. Use Google autocomplete,
"People also ask," and Google Keyword Planner (with location filters for local). Favor **long-tail**
phrases (3-5 words): lower competition, higher conversion. Balance search volume against
competition.

**Search intent (match the page to the goal).** Classify each query and serve what the searcher
wants:
- *Informational* ("how to unclog a drain") — guides, FAQs.
- *Navigational* ("Joe's Plumbing Durham") — your homepage/GBP.
- *Commercial* ("best plumber near me") — comparison/service pages with proof.
- *Transactional* ("emergency plumber Durham book") — booking/contact pages.

Content that satisfies intent outperforms thin or keyword-stuffed pages. [2026 SEO guides]

**On-page SEO (what you control on each page).** A descriptive title tag with the target keyword;
one clear H1 and logical H2/H3 headings; a compelling meta description; keyword in the URL slug;
descriptive image `alt` text; internal links between related pages; fast, mobile-friendly pages.
Write for humans first, then make it crawlable.

**Plan content + SEO together**: decide target keywords/intent before writing, then write the
asset. (For the actual writing craft → `content-and-marketing-writing`.) Increasingly, ranking also
means being citable by AI answer engines; clear, well-structured, genuinely useful content is the
through-line. [Search Engine Land 2026]

## 6. LOCAL SEO (Deep Section — Highest-Leverage Local Channel)

For a local business, local SEO is usually the #1 channel: it puts you in front of people searching
with local intent ("near me", "service + city") and in the **local map pack** (the 3 business
listings shown with the map above organic results). Per Whitespark's 2026 local-ranking data cited
across the industry, **the majority of top local-pack signals come from your Google Business
Profile** — so GBP is where to start. [Whitespark 2026, as reported by fuelonline.com and others, 2026 — *secondary citation, see Disclaimer*]

### 6a. Google Business Profile (GBP) — set up & optimize
GBP (formerly Google My Business) is your free listing that powers Maps and the local pack. Claim
and verify it, then fully complete and actively manage it. Core levers reported for 2026:

- **Categories**: choose the most accurate primary category, then relevant secondary categories.
  Category is one of the strongest relevance signals.
- **NAP**: exact, consistent Name, Address, Phone (see 6b).
- **Service area / address**: set a storefront address or service-area radius accurately.
- **Description & attributes**: clear, keyword-relevant description; fill all attributes.
- **Photos**: add new photos regularly; richer photo sets are associated with better performance
  in competitive markets. [fuelonline.com 2026]
- **Posts**: publish GBP posts regularly with relevant content to maintain an "active" signal.
- **Q&A**: seed and answer common questions.
- **Reviews**: quantity, recency, rating, and your responses (see 6c).

Most GBP optimizations show measurable movement within ~30-60 days; category and NAP fixes tend to
act fastest (~2-3 weeks). [fuelonline.com 2026 — *secondary; verify*]

### 6b. NAP consistency & citations
**NAP = Name, Address, Phone.** Google cross-references your business details across the web;
mismatches (even "St." vs "Street") erode trust and can hurt rankings. Best practice: pick **one**
canonical version of name, address format, and local phone number and use it **identically**
everywhere. [LSEO 2026; 12AM Agency 2026]

**Citations** are online mentions of your NAP:
- **Structured**: listings on formal directories with standardized fields: GBP, Bing Places,
  Apple Business Connect, Yelp, YellowPages, Foursquare, and industry/local directories. Strong
  direct ranking impact via precise NAP validation.
- **Unstructured**: natural mentions in local news, blogs, event/sponsorship pages, social. Build
  contextual trust and authority.

Action: build/claim the major structured citations, then audit your top sources periodically for
NAP mismatches (tools like BrightLocal or Whitespark automate this). [EWR Digital; Southtown 2026]

### 6c. Review generation & management
Reviews drive both conversion (trust) and map-pack ranking (quantity, rating, recency, response).
**Respond to all reviews — positive and negative — promptly (ideally within ~24 hours)**; replies
signal an actively managed, real business. [Birdeye 2026; multiple 2026 guides]

**Stay inside platform rules — Google tightened review policy in early 2026:**
- **Do NOT incentivize reviews** (no discount/gift/perk in exchange for a review or a 5-star
  rating) — now prohibited.
- **Do NOT use on-site review kiosks/tablets** to solicit reviews — now prohibited.
- **Do NOT ask customers to mention a specific employee by name** or hit a rating quota tied to a
  reward.
- **DO** invite *all* customers equally to share an *honest* experience, with an open-ended ask.
[Birdeye 2026; RealTimeFeedback 2026; multiple 2026 GBP-policy summaries — *secondary; confirm current Google policy*]

⚠️ "Review gating" (selectively routing only happy customers to public reviews while diverting
unhappy ones) violates platform policies and risks the **FTC fake-reviews rule** (Section 9d).
Never buy reviews, write fake ones, post employee/family reviews without disclosure, or suppress
honest negatives.

### 6d. Local keywords & service-area pages
- Target **service + location** terms ("plumber Durham NC", "emergency electrician Cary"). Use
  Keyword Planner with location filters and competitor analysis.
- If you serve multiple towns/areas, create a distinct, genuinely useful **location/service-area
  page** per area (not thin duplicates) and reflect those areas in GBP and citations.
- Put your city/region naturally in titles, H1s, and copy.

### 6e. Local link building
Local backlinks signal local relevance/authority: get coverage in **local news**, **sponsor local
events**, **join the chamber/business associations**, get listed in **local guides/resource pages**,
and partner with complementary local businesses. Quality and local relevance beat volume. [MJW Media
2026; EWR Digital]

## 7. Social-Media Marketing Basics

Pick **2-3 platforms** where your segment actually is and do them well; don't spread thin. A
restaurant/retailer may win on Instagram/Facebook; a B2B service may win on LinkedIn. For
brick-and-mortar, tag your location consistently to surface in local/map features. [Sprout Social
2026; DigiSphere 2026]

- **Content pillars**: define 3-5 recurring topic buckets so planning is fast and the account stays
  focused. A common service-business set: **Proof** (reviews/testimonials), **Education** (FAQs/tips),
  **Local relevance** (neighborhood/events), **Personality** (behind-the-scenes).
- **Content mix**: a workable ratio: ~40% educational, ~30% entertaining, ~20% promotional, ~10%
  community. [RecurPost 2026]
- **Consistency > volume**: a sustainable cadence you can actually keep beats sporadic bursts;
  running out of ideas is the #1 cause of quitting, which is what content pillars solve.
- Complete your profile with NAP/hours for local discovery; repurpose one idea across formats.

*(Drafting the actual posts/captions → `content-and-marketing-writing`. Designing the graphics →
`venture-canva-founder-brand-stack`.)*

## 8. Email-Marketing Fundamentals

Email is an **owned** channel (you control the list, unlike rented social audiences) and is strong
for retention and repeat business. Core moves:

- **List building (permission-based)**: offer a **lead magnet** (useful guide, template, checklist,
  first-visit discount) on a clear landing page to earn opt-ins. "Good lead magnet + simple welcome
  + steady cadence" can build a meaningful list from zero over ~12 months without paid promotion.
  [AWeber 2026; MediumTalk 2026]
- **Welcome sequence**: a 3-email series: deliver the lead magnet + introduce the brand
  (immediately) → share your best content/offer (day 2-3) → soft offer or "what are you interested
  in?" (day 5-7). Welcome emails earn far higher open/click rates than routine sends. [AWeber 2026]
- **Segmentation**: even basic splits (new subscriber vs customer; by lead magnet downloaded; by
  behavior) make content relevant and lift results.
- **Automation concepts**: beyond the welcome series: lead-nurture sequences and re-engagement
  (win-back) campaigns, triggerable in any standard email platform without a developer.

⚠️ **Email is regulated — every commercial email must satisfy CAN-SPAM (Section 9a).** Build opt-out
handling and your physical postal address into your template from day one.

## 9. Marketing Compliance — Don't Get This Wrong

This is the accuracy-critical section. Penalties are real and per-violation. **All figures cited to
the regulator with an as-of date; re-verify before relying (see Disclaimer).**

### 9a. CAN-SPAM Act — commercial email
Federal law (enforced by the **FTC**) for commercial email; **no exception for B2B**, and it applies
to *any* email whose primary purpose is commercial. The FTC's seven main requirements:
[FTC, "CAN-SPAM Act: A Compliance Guide for Business," ftc.gov/business-guidance — as of 2026]

1. **Don't use false or misleading header information** (From/To/routing must be accurate).
2. **Don't use deceptive subject lines** (must reflect the content).
3. **Identify the message as an ad** (clearly, if it is one).
4. **Tell recipients where you're located**: include a **valid physical postal address** (street
   address, a USPS-registered P.O. box, or equivalent).
5. **Tell recipients how to opt out** of future email (clear, conspicuous).
6. **Honor opt-out requests promptly**: within **10 business days**; the opt-out mechanism must
   stay functional for **at least 30 days** after you send; you can't charge a fee or require info
   beyond an email address. [FTC CAN-SPAM guide — as of 2026]
7. **Monitor what others do on your behalf**: you're responsible even if a vendor/contractor sends
   for you.

**Penalty:** up to **$53,088 per email** in civil penalties, per the FTC's inflation adjustment
effective **Jan 17, 2025** (no cap on total). [FTC inflation-adjustment / penalty figure reported
across 2025 sources — *figure is secondary-sourced; confirm current amount with the FTC*] Criminal
penalties exist for aggravated violations (e.g., harvesting, false registrations).

### 9b. TCPA — SMS texts & automated/robocalls (HIGH RISK)
The **Telephone Consumer Protection Act** (FCC-administered, plus a private right of action) governs
marketing texts and automated/prerecorded calls. **High litigation risk**: there's a private right
of action with statutory damages, so plaintiffs sue.

- **Marketing SMS / robotexts and robocalls require *prior express written consent (PEWC)*** —
  a clear written agreement to receive marketing messages, **not** a condition of purchase.
  Informational (non-marketing, transactional) texts require a lower standard (prior express
  consent, which can be oral/contextual). [ActiveProspect 2026; Bloomreach; FCC]
- **Penalties:** **$500 per message/call**, trebled to **$1,500 for willful/knowing** violations;
  **no cap** on total damages. [ActiveProspect 2026; multiple TCPA guides — statutory amounts per 47 U.S.C. §227]
- **Quiet hours:** generally **no marketing before 8:00 a.m. or after 9:00 p.m.** in the
  *recipient's* time zone. [ActiveProspect 2026]
- **Opt-out / revocation (effective April 2025):** honor opt-outs within **10 business days**;
  common keywords (e.g., **STOP, CANCEL, UNSUBSCRIBE, QUIT, END, OPTOUT, REVOKE**) are treated as
  per-se revocation. [ActiveProspect 2026; FCC revocation rules — *secondary; confirm*]
- **One-to-one consent rule — VACATED.** The FCC rule that would have banned bundled lead-gen
  consent (and required topic-related, seller-by-seller consent) was scheduled for Jan 27, 2025 but
  was **struck down/vacated by the 11th Circuit on Jan 24, 2025** in *Insurance Marketing Coalition
  Ltd. v. FCC*; the FCC subsequently moved to eliminate it. **Bundled consent remains permissible**
  and prior TCPA consent requirements remain in effect. [Goodwin; Reed Smith; Venable; Womble Bond
  Dickinson — Jan 2025 client alerts on *IMC v. FCC*, 11th Cir.] *Practical takeaway: still get clear,
  documented written consent for every marketing-SMS program.*

Also obey **CTIA / carrier messaging guidelines** (industry, not law) — they govern message
deliverability and can get a number blocked independent of legal rules.

### 9c. FTC Endorsement & "Material Connection" Disclosure
Under the FTC **Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255**, updated June 2023), if there's a **material
connection** between an endorser and the brand that consumers wouldn't reasonably expect, it must be
disclosed **clearly and conspicuously**. [FTC, "FTC's Endorsement Guides: What People Are Asking,"
ftc.gov/business-guidance; 16 CFR Part 255 — as of 2026]

- **Material connection** = any relationship that could affect the endorsement's weight/credibility:
  payment, free or discounted product, gifts, employment, or a personal/family relationship —
  including for an *unsolicited* gift or an employee posting about their employer. [FTC endorsement FAQ; legal analyses 2024]
- **"Clear and conspicuous"** = the disclosure is **difficult to miss (easily noticeable) and easily
  understood**, and **unavoidable** on interactive media. A disclosure buried at the end of a long
  post, mixed into a clump of hashtags, or **placed only in the comments is *not* adequate**.
  Platform built-in "paid partnership" tools alone may not suffice. [FTC; Hall Render 2024; Perkins Coie]
- Applies to **employees and the founder** endorsing their own business on social, to **gifted
  product** reviews, and to **AI-generated / virtual-influencer** content — if a virtual influencer
  or synthetic media promotes you, consumers must be able to tell it's commercial and not a real
  person's independent opinion. [FTC; DisclosureFacts 2024]
- Brands can be liable for an endorser's deceptive claims/inadequate disclosures — instruct anyone
  endorsing you (incl. happy customers you compensate) to disclose with a plain tag like **#ad** /
  "paid partnership" placed up front.

### 9d. FTC Rule Banning Fake Reviews & Testimonials
A separate FTC **final rule (16 CFR Part 465)**: announced **Aug 14, 2024**, **effective Oct 21,
2024** — prohibits, with **civil penalties**: [FTC press release, "FTC Announces Final Rule Banning
Fake Reviews and Testimonials," ftc.gov, Aug 14 2024; Federal Register 89 FR (Aug 22 2024) — as of 2026]

- Creating, buying, selling, or disseminating **fake or AI-generated reviews/testimonials** or fake
  celebrity testimonials (misrepresenting a reviewer's identity, experience, or existence).
- **Insider reviews** (by employees/relatives) without clear disclosure of the relationship.
- **Review suppression**: using unfounded legal threats/intimidation to prevent or remove honest
  negative reviews; misrepresenting that a review site shows all reviews when negatives are
  suppressed.
- **Company-controlled review websites** posing as independent.
- Buying **fake social-media indicators** (bot followers/views) to misrepresent influence.

This is exactly why **review gating, buying reviews, or seeding employee/family reviews is off-limits.**

### 9e. North Carolina Telemarketing / Do-Not-Call
**N.C. Gen. Stat. § 75-102** ("Restrictions on telephone solicitations") layers on top of federal
rules. [N.C.G.S. § 75-102, ncleg.gov / verified text via FindLaw codes.findlaw.com — current as of the 2023 statutory edition, accessed 2026]

- **Do-Not-Call Registry**: no telephone solicitation to a number on the latest "Do Not Call"
  Registry. (§ 75-102(a))
- **Calling hours**: no telephone solicitation **before 8:00 a.m. or after 9:00 p.m.** (§ 75-102(f))
- **Internal do-not-call procedures**: must implement **systems and written procedures** to stop
  calling anyone who asks not to be called again, and train/monitor/enforce employee and contractor
  compliance. (§ 75-102(d))
- **Caller ID**: must not transmit misleading caller-ID info or block/misrepresent the call's
  origin (may display the seller on whose behalf the call is made). (§ 75-102(i))
- **Federal TSR incorporated**: must comply with **Telemarketing Sales Rule §§ 310.3, 310.4,
  310.5** (deceptive/abusive practices and recordkeeping); complying with the TSR's timing for
  updated DNC editions counts as NC compliance. (§ 75-102(e))

NC also enforces its broader **Unfair and Deceptive Trade Practices Act (Ch. 75)** and the NC DOJ /
Attorney General handles consumer complaints. *Practical takeaway: scrub against the National DNC
Registry, keep an internal DNC list with written procedures, and never call outside 8 a.m.-9 p.m.*

### 9f. DIY-marketer compliance checklist
- [ ] Every commercial email has: truthful headers/subject, ad identification if applicable, a valid
      physical postal address, a working opt-out — and you honor opt-outs ≤10 business days
      (mechanism live ≥30 days). (CAN-SPAM)
- [ ] You send marketing SMS/robocalls **only** with documented prior express written consent,
      include "Reply STOP," honor opt-outs ≤10 business days, and never message before 8 a.m. /
      after 9 p.m. recipient time. (TCPA)
- [ ] Anyone endorsing you (you, employees, paid/ gifted customers, AI personas) discloses the
      material connection **clearly, conspicuously, up front** (e.g., #ad). (FTC Endorsement Guides)
- [ ] You never write, buy, incentivize, gate, or suppress reviews; no employee/family reviews
      without disclosure. (FTC fake-reviews rule + Google policy)
- [ ] Your GBP review asks are open-ended, to all customers, with no incentive, no kiosk, no
      employee-name requests. (Google 2026 policy)
- [ ] Telemarketing scrubbed against the National DNC Registry, internal DNC list maintained with
