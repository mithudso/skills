# NC Collateral Consequences of Criminal Conviction

> **Educational only — NOT legal advice.** For any specific situation, consult a licensed North Carolina attorney.

A criminal conviction in North Carolina carries consequences far beyond the sentence imposed in court. These "collateral consequences" are civil penalties, disabilities, and disqualifications imposed by operation of law — automatic, often invisible, and lasting. This reference maps the major categories. For exhaustive statutory lookup, use **C-CAT** (Collateral Consequences Assessment Tool), maintained by UNC School of Government, current through March 1, 2026.

`verified-as-of: 2026-06-22`

---

## 1. The C-CAT Tool

The **Collateral Consequences Assessment Tool** (C-CAT) at <https://ccat.sog.unc.edu/> is the authoritative NC resource. It catalogues NC statutory collateral consequences organized into 10 categories: [^1]

1. Civic rights (voting, holding office)
2. Domestic rights (adoption, custody, visitation)
3. Education rights (admission, state benefits)
4. Employment and professional licensure
5. Firearm rights
6. Housing (public and subsidized)
7. Judicial rights (jury service, court-appointed positions)
8. Property rights (ownership, inheritance, pensions)
9. Public benefits (TANF, SNAP, unemployment)
10. Wildlife (hunting and fishing licenses)

**C-CAT excludes:** federal consequences, immigration effects, motor vehicle licensing, sex offender registration, local government policies, and private employer policies — these must be assessed separately.

## 2. Firearms Rights

**State:** Felony conviction — any felony — triggers the prohibition on firearm possession under GS 14-415.1. There is no exception for nonviolent felonies at the time of conviction.

**Restoration of firearm rights:** A person may petition the Superior Court of their county of residence for restoration of firearm rights if:
- NC resident for at least one year
- Single nonviolent felony conviction with no subsequent convictions
- No violent misdemeanors
- Civil rights restored
- At least **20 years** have elapsed since civil rights restoration [^2]

**Domestic violence:** Active DVPO (50B order) prohibits firearm possession during its term. A DV misdemeanor conviction may trigger federal firearms prohibition (18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(9)) — applies even to NC Class A1 misdemeanors that qualify as "misdemeanor crimes of domestic violence" under the Lautenberg Amendment.

## 3. Employment & Professional Licensure

### Public Employment
Some state positions are categorically barred to persons with felony convictions. Others require case-by-case review. NC has adopted "ban the box" policies in some state agencies (inquiry into criminal history delayed until later in hiring process).

### Professional Licensing Boards
Many NC licensing boards (nursing, law, teaching, social work, contractor, real estate, etc.) may deny licenses based on felony convictions and some misdemeanor convictions. Most require "direct relationship" between the offense and the licensed profession.

**Certificate of Relief (GS 15A-173.2):** For those with up to 3 Class H/I felony convictions, a Certificate of Relief compels licensing boards to consider it favorably and bars civil liability for employers who hire COR holders. See [nc-expungement-record-sealing](nc-expungement-record-sealing.md).

### Private Employment
Private employers may conduct background checks. NC does not prohibit most private employers from considering convictions. However, EEOC guidance limits use of criminal history for federal contractors and those with disparate impact concerns.

## 4. Housing

### Public/HUD-Subsidized Housing
Federal rules permit — and often require — housing authorities to deny tenancy based on certain convictions:
- **Mandatory denial:** Lifetime sex offender registration; drug-related convictions while in federally assisted housing; methamphetamine production convictions on federally assisted premises
- **Discretionary denial:** Most other felony and misdemeanor convictions (housing authority policy dependent)

### Private Rental Market
Landlords may conduct background checks. NC does not restrict private landlords from denying housing based on conviction history. However, blanket exclusion policies may face fair housing scrutiny (HUD guidance on disparate impact).

---

## 5. Public Benefits

| Benefit | NC Rule |
|---------|---------|
| **SNAP (food stamps)** | Drug felony convictions: NC has opted OUT of the federal lifetime ban — persons with drug convictions may receive SNAP after completion of sentence |
| **TANF** | Drug felony drug conviction: NC applies the federal ban — persons convicted of drug felonies are permanently ineligible unless enrolled in a drug treatment program |
| **Unemployment Insurance** | Discharge for cause (including criminal conduct at work) may disqualify; incarceration suspends benefits |
| **SSI/SSDI (federal)** | Suspended during incarceration of 30+ days; resumes after release |

## 6. Civic Rights

### Voting Rights
- **During incarceration, probation, or parole:** NC law (amended by SL 2023-119, effective Jan 2024) restored voting rights upon release from incarceration — probationers and parolees **may vote**. [^3]
- Rights are restored automatically; no petition required.

### Jury Service
- NC: Felony conviction disqualifies from jury service until civil rights are restored (GS 9-3). Civil rights restoration occurs automatically after completion of sentence (including probation) for most offenses.

### Holding Office
- Certain offenses disqualify from holding elective office under the NC Constitution.

## 7. Immigration Consequences

Immigration consequences of criminal convictions are **federal**, not state — C-CAT does not cover them. Key principles:

- **Deportable offenses:** "Crimes of moral turpitude," "aggravated felonies" (as defined by federal immigration law, not NC classification), drug offenses, and domestic violence offenses can trigger removal proceedings.
- **NC classification misleads:** What NC calls a misdemeanor may qualify as a federal "aggravated felony" based on sentence length (1+ year) — this can have catastrophic immigration consequences.
- ***Padilla v. Kentucky* (2010):** Defense attorneys must advise non-citizen clients of immigration consequences of a plea — failure is IAC.
- **Always consult an immigration attorney** in addition to criminal defense counsel when the defendant is a non-citizen.

---

## 8. Education

- **Federal student aid (FAFSA):** Drug convictions while receiving federal aid can temporarily suspend eligibility. Completion of drug rehabilitation program restores eligibility.
- **State educational benefits:** Some NC scholarships and programs have conviction-related restrictions.
- **Professional school admissions (law, medicine):** Each licensing body has its own character and fitness review process — criminal history is routinely reported and reviewed.

---

## 9. Driver's License

NC DMV may suspend, revoke, or limit driving privileges for:
- DWI convictions (see [nc-dwi-defense](nc-dwi-defense.md))
- Drug conviction (automatic 1-year suspension under federal mandate, though NC has limited implementation)
- Habitual offender status
