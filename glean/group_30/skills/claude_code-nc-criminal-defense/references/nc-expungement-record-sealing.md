# NC Expungement & Record Sealing

> **Educational only — NOT legal advice.** For any specific expungement matter, consult a licensed North Carolina attorney.

North Carolina does not use the term "record sealing" — the state's relief mechanism is called **expunction** (sometimes spelled "expungement"). An expunction erases the arrest and conviction from official records and allows the person to lawfully deny the charge or conviction ever occurred in most contexts. This reference covers all major NC expunction pathways, automatic expunctions, the Certificate of Relief alternative, and the 2025/2026 statutory changes.

`verified-as-of: 2026-06-22`

---

## 1. Overview of NC Expunction Law

NC expunction law is codified at **GS Chapter 15A, Article 5** (GS 15A-145 through 15A-149.5). Each statute addresses a distinct category — there is no single catch-all expunction. The Second Chance Act (SL 2020-35, Senate Bill 562) enacted in July 2020 dramatically expanded eligibility, allowing for the first time expunctions of multiple convictions and eliminating the lifetime one-expunction limit. [^1]

Key structural features:
- No "record sealing" exists in NC — expunction physically destroys the records.
- After expunction, the person may **lawfully deny** the arrest/charge/conviction occurred, except in certain proceedings. [^2]
- Each expunction petition is filed in the **county of conviction or charge**.
- Most petitions require a filing fee of $175; dismissals/acquittals are free.

## 2. Automatic Expunctions (No Petition Required)

### GS 15A-146(a4) — Automatic Expunction of Dismissals and Acquittals

Effective **December 1, 2021** (SL 2021-118), cases in which **all charges** ended in a dismissal or acquittal became subject to automatic expunction. Effective **July 8, 2024** (SL 2024-35), the timeline was set at **180–210 days** after final disposition. [^3]

**Eligible for automatic expunction:**
- All charges dismissed by the prosecutor without leave (nolle prosequi)
- All charges dismissed by the court (judge)
- All charges resulting in a finding of not guilty or not responsible

**NOT eligible for automatic expunction:**
- Dismissals of felony charges pursuant to a **plea agreement** (these require a petition)
- Dismissals of charges against a defendant found **incapable to proceed** (effective December 1, 2025, per Iryna's Law / SL 2025-93, GS 15A-1008)

**Benefits of automatic expunction:**
- No petition required, no filing fee, no waiting period
- No limit on number of automatic expunctions
- No prior conviction bars

**Practical limitation:** Agencies may not be notified automatically. The person should obtain verification using form **AOC-G-260** and can request a "certification that no record exists" using **AOC-CR-370**. [^4]

## 3. Petition-Based Expunctions — Map by Category

### 3A. Dismissed Charges and Acquittals (Manual Petition Path)
**Statute:** GS 15A-146(a), (a1)  
**Forms:** AOC-CR-287 (dismissal), AOC-CR-288 (acquittal)  
**Fee:** $0 (free for acquittals and dismissals not after deferred prosecution); $175 if dismissal was after a deferred prosecution agreement  
**No waiting period; no prior conviction bar; no limit on number**

Use the manual petition when the automatic expunction has not yet processed or agencies have not updated records.

---

### 3B. First-Offense Convictions Under Age 18 (Misdemeanor)
**Statute:** GS 15A-145(a)  
**Eligibility:** Non-traffic misdemeanor conviction occurring **before age 18**  
**Waiting period:** 2 years after conviction or sentence completion  
**Prior conviction bar:** No felony or misdemeanor conviction (other than traffic) after the expungeable conviction  
**Court discretion:** Yes — court may deny  

---

### 3C. First-Offense Convictions Under Age 22 (Drug Offense)
**Statute:** GS 15A-145.2  
**Eligibility:** Drug offense conviction (GS Ch.90) before age 22, conditional discharge under GS 90-96  
**Waiting period:** 2 years after discharge  

---

### 3D. Older Nonviolent Misdemeanor and Felony Convictions (Primary Adult Pathway)
**Statute:** GS 15A-145.5 (as amended by SL 2020-35, SL 2021-118, SL 2023-103, SL 2025-71)

This is the **primary adult expunction pathway** added and expanded by the Second Chance Act.

#### Waiting Periods (updated 2025–2026)

| Category | Waiting Period | Effective Date of Change |
|----------|---------------|-------------------------|
| **1 nonviolent misdemeanor** | **3 years** after conviction OR sentence completion (whichever later) | July 9, 2025 (SL 2025-71 §12) — reduced from 5 years |
| **Multiple nonviolent misdemeanors** | 7 years after last conviction AND sentence completion | SL 2020-35 |
| **1 nonviolent felony** | 10 years after conviction AND sentence completion | SL 2020-35 |
| **Felony B&E (GS 14-54(a))** | 15 years after conviction AND sentence completion | SL 2023-103 |
| **2–3 nonviolent felonies** | 20 years after conviction AND sentence completion (convictions must fall within 24-month window) | SL 2021-118 |

**Note on "whichever is later":** For single misdemeanor petitions, the 3-year clock runs from the conviction date OR sentence completion date, whichever is later. The AOC form (AOC-CR-298) reflects the more restrictive interpretation. [^5]

#### Eligible Offenses — What Counts as "Nonviolent"

A conviction is **ineligible** if it is:
- A **Class A–G felony** (only Class H and I felonies are eligible)
- A **Class A1 misdemeanor**
- Any offense requiring **sex offender registration** under GS Ch. 14, Art. 27A
- An **impaired driving** conviction (GS 20-138.1) — permanently ineligible since 2015 [^6]
- An assault-based offense
- Certain drug trafficking felonies (methamphetamine, heroin, cocaine)

#### Prior Conviction Bars

- **Any violent conviction** on the applicant's record = complete bar (even for otherwise eligible nonviolent offenses)
- For felony petitions: no prior felony convictions allowed
- No felony convictions during the waiting period
- No misdemeanor convictions (other than traffic) during the 5 years before filing

#### Prior Expunction Limit

GS 15A-145.5 allows only **one expunction** under this statute per person, with limited exceptions:
- Filing in multiple counties within 120 days of each other
- The new offense predated the previous expunction
- Prior expunction was under a **different** statutory authority

#### Court Discretion

- **Misdemeanor petitions:** Mandatory approval if all criteria met — court has no discretion to deny.
- **Felony petitions:** Court has discretion to deny even if criteria are satisfied.

## 4. Offenses That Can NEVER Be Expunged in NC

The following conviction categories are **permanently ineligible** for expunction regardless of time elapsed or rehabilitation: [^6]

- **DWI convictions** (GS 20-138.1 and related) — dismissed DWI charges *can* be expunged
- **Sex offense convictions** requiring registration on NC Sex Offender Registry
- **Class A–G felonies** and Class A1 misdemeanors (unless charged under age-specific statutes)
- **Any violent conviction** (bars expunction of even nonviolent offenses on the same record)
- Convictions where the person has a prior expunction under GS 15A-145.5

## 5. Expunction Process — Step by Step

### Step 1: Identify the Correct Statute and Form
- Determine which GS 15A-145.x pathway applies (age, offense type, number of convictions)
- Obtain the correct AOC form from NC Courts website

### Step 2: Gather Required Documents
- Certified copy of the judgment/disposition
- SBI criminal history check (required for most conviction expunctions)
- AOC criminal history check
- Affidavit of no prior convictions (required for many statutes)

### Step 3: File in the County of Conviction
- File petition + required attachments with the **Clerk of Superior Court** in the county where convicted
- Pay filing fee ($175 for most conviction expunctions; $0 for dismissals/acquittals)
- Fee waived for **indigent petitioners**

### Step 4: Service on the District Attorney
Required for most conviction-based petitions (GS 15A-145, 15A-145.4, 15A-145.5):
- DA has **10 days** to object (standard convictions)
- DA has **30 days** to object (older nonviolent convictions under 15A-145.5, prostitution, human trafficking)

NOT required for dismissal/acquittal expunctions (GS 15A-146).

### Step 5: Court Hearing
- Court may grant petition **without a hearing** if no objection is filed
- Request a hearing if your district doesn't routinely schedule them
- For felony expunctions, court applies discretion even if all criteria are met

### Step 6: Order Issued and Records Destroyed
- Clerk transmits order to: SBI, FBI, NC DMV, AOC, and other named agencies
- Agencies must **destroy** or purge the records
- Petitioner receives a **certified copy** of the order

### Processing Time
- No statutory deadline for courts
- Automatic expunctions: 180–210 days post-disposition
- Manual petitions: typically 6–10 months from filing

## 6. Certificate of Relief (GS 15A-173.2) — Alternative to Expunction

The **Certificate of Relief** (COR) is a distinct remedy for people who are **not eligible for expunction** — primarily those with Class H or I felony convictions above the expunction threshold.

### Eligibility (GS 15A-173.2)
- No more than **3 Class H or I felony convictions** (multiple convictions in the same court session = 1 conviction)
- No Class A–G felony convictions
- **12 months** have elapsed since completion of all active time, probation, post-release supervision, and parole
- No pending criminal charges
- Engaged in or seeking lawful employment, education, or rehabilitation
- Poses no unreasonable risk to public safety

### What a COR Relieves
- **Collateral sanctions**: mandatory penalties, disabilities, or disqualifications imposed by law upon conviction
- Employment disqualifications that aren't specifically exempted
- Occupational licensing restrictions (boards must consider the COR favorably)
- **Civil liability protection**: entities who hire/do business with a COR holder (knowing of the COR) are shielded from civil liability

### What a COR Does NOT Relieve
- Sex offender registration requirements
- **Firearm disqualifications** under NC GS Ch. 14, Art. 54A/54B (state) or federal law
- Motor vehicle license suspensions/revocations under GS Ch. 20
- Constitutional or federal bars (e.g., eligibility for public office)
- Discretionary disqualifications (agencies retain discretion on these)

### COR Process
- **Fee:** $50 (waived for indigent petitioners)
- **Where filed:** Superior Court in the county of conviction
- **Notice:** Court must notify district attorney at least 3 weeks before hearing
- **Public record:** The certificate is **publicly available** (unlike an expunction, which is sealed)
- **Reapplication:** If denied, may re-petition after 12 months

### COR vs. Expunction — Key Distinction
| Feature | Expunction | Certificate of Relief |
|---------|-----------|----------------------|
| Record destroyed | Yes — physically purged | No — conviction remains on record |
| Can deny conviction occurred | Yes (in most contexts) | No |
| Max felony class eligible | Class H–I only (GS 15A-145.5) | Class H–I (up to 3) |
| DWI convictions | Not eligible | Not applicable |
| Firearm rights | Not restored by expunction | Not restored by COR |
| Public record | No (sealed) | Yes (COR issuance is public) |

## 7. Effect of Expunction

After a valid NC expunction: [^2]
- The person may **lawfully deny** in most contexts that the arrest, charge, or conviction occurred
- Expunged records are **not accessible** to employers, landlords, or the public
- Law enforcement and courts **retain limited access** for certain purposes (e.g., determining sentencing in future cases)
- The NC DMV may retain driving records notwithstanding expunction of criminal records
- Federal agencies (FBI) purge records upon notification from SBI
- **Private background check companies** are notified via AOC licensing agreements but may have delays

---

## 8. Iryna's Law Impact on Expunctions (2025)

SL 2025-93 (H 307), effective December 1, 2025, amended GS 15A-1008 to exclude from automatic expunction dismissals entered because a defendant was found **incapable to proceed** (incompetent to stand trial). This affects victims of serious crimes who wish to ensure the case remains on the defendant's record for dangerousness assessments. [^7]
