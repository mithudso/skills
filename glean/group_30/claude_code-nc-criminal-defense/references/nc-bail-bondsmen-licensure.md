# NC Bail Bondsmen Licensure & Regulation (GS Chapter 58, Article 71)

> **Educational only, NOT legal advice.** verified-as-of: 2026-06-22

North Carolina regulates bail bondsmen through the **Bail Bond Regulatory Division (BBRD)** of the NC Department of Insurance (NC DOI), under **GS Chapter 58, Article 71**. This is a separate regulatory regime from the pretrial release framework (GS 15A-531 et seq.) — it governs who may act as a commercial surety, not who sets bail.

---

## 1. License Types (GS 58-71-1 Definitions)

NC recognizes four distinct roles:

### Surety Bail Bondsman
- Licensed by NC DOI and **appointed by a licensed insurance company** via power of attorney
- The insurance company provides the financial guarantee; the bondsman is the insurer's agent
- Most common commercial bail bondsman type in NC
- Must maintain a valid appointment from a licensed surety insurer

### Professional Bondsman
- Licensed by NC DOI and **pledges their own cash or approved securities** with the NC Commissioner of Insurance as collateral
- Acts as a principal (not an agent) — their own deposited assets back the bonds they write
- Minimum security deposit: **$15,000** with the Commissioner [^1]
- Bond capacity limits: may write bonds totaling up to **12x** their security deposit outstanding; the **single largest bond** they may write is **1/4 of their security deposit** [^1]
- Example: $15,000 deposit → max single bond = $3,750; max total outstanding = $180,000

### Accommodation Bondsman
- **Not licensed** — operates outside the commercial licensing framework
- A NC resident (18+) who posts bail as surety for a defendant based on personal relationship
- **May not charge any fee or receive any consideration** — purely gratuitous
- Must provide satisfactory evidence of ownership, value, and marketability of real or personal property sufficient to cover the bond amount
- Distinguished from professional/surety bondsmen by the absence of compensation and the personal relationship requirement

### Bail Runner
- An employee of a licensed bail bondsman
- Licensed separately by NC DOI
- Authorized to: assist in presenting defendant in court; assist in apprehension and surrender of defendant; keep defendant under surveillance; execute bonds on behalf of the supervising bondsman (with recorded power of attorney)
- Must be employed by **only one professional bondsman** who supervises their work and is responsible for their conduct
- Attorneys and law enforcement officers acting in their official capacity are excluded from the runner definition

## 2. Qualification Requirements (GS 58-71-40, 58-71-50)

All applicants for a bail bondsman or runner license must meet:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Age | 21 or older |
| Education | High school diploma or equivalent (diploma, GED, or certified transcript) |
| Residency | NC resident for at least **6 consecutive months** before application |
| ID | Valid NC driver's license or NC DMV-issued identification card |
| Citizenship | US legal resident or documented citizen |
| Criminal history | No outstanding bail bond obligations; no prior bail bond law violations; no disqualifying criminal history |
| Financial | No pending bankruptcy without notifying Commissioner within 3 business days |
| Knowledge | Sufficient knowledge, training, or experience to fulfill licensee responsibilities |

### Pre-Licensing Education
- **12 hours** of pre-licensing education with a NC DOI-approved provider required before application
- Content must be pertinent to bail bond duties
- Certificate of completion submitted with application

### Application Process
1. Complete 12-hour pre-licensing course
2. Submit application electronically through the **National Insurance Producer Registry (NIPR)** at nipr.com
3. Upload required documents to NIPR Warehouse
4. Undergo criminal history record check via fingerprints
5. Pass the NC licensing **examination** (administered through Pearson VUE testing centers)
6. Receive NC DOI approval and license issuance (typically 10–12 business days post-exam)
7. For surety bondsmen: obtain appointment from a licensed insurance company

**Required documents at application:**
- Valid NC driver's license or DMV ID card
- Fingerprint release forms (electronic and standard)
- Pre-licensing education completion certificate
- Two proof-of-residency documents (pay stub, utility bill, written lease, tax receipt, or financial institution statement)
- High school diploma, GED, or certified transcript

### First-Year Licensee Restrictions (GS 58-71-41)
A **first-year licensee** (any person licensed for less than 12 months) must:
- Operate **only under the supervision** of a licensed supervising bail bondsman
- Work from the supervising bondsman's official business address
- This restriction automatically expires after 12 months of licensure

## 3. Prohibited Persons (GS 58-71-105)

The following **may not act as surety or agent** for any bail bond in NC:
- Sheriffs, deputy sheriffs, and law enforcement officers
- Judicial officials (judges, magistrates, clerks)
- Attorneys (while acting in that capacity)
- Parole officers and probation officers
- Jailors and detention facility employees
- Other public employees assigned to criminal justice duties
- **Spouses** of the above (while the person holds the disqualifying position)
- Any person convicted of a felony
- Any person convicted of a misdemeanor drug violation within the previous **24 months**

## 4. Premium Regulation (GS 58-71-95)

### Premium Cap
The maximum premium a bail bondsman may charge is **15% of the face amount of the bond** [^2]

Example: $10,000 bond → maximum premium = $1,500

### What "Premium" Covers
The non-refundable fee paid to the bondsman for executing the bond. It is earned when the bond is written and is not returned if:
- The defendant fails to appear
- The case is dismissed or the defendant is acquitted
- The defendant is found not guilty

### Premium Refund Rules (GS 58-71-20)
The **full premium must be returned within 72 hours** of surrender if the bondsman surrenders the defendant **before any court date** — unless:
1. Defendant fails to pay the agreed premium
2. Defendant hides, conceals themselves, or leaves the state
3. Defendant violates conditions of the bond
4. Defendant provides false information to the bondsman

---

## 5. Prohibited Practices (GS 58-71-95)

Bail bondsmen and runners are prohibited from:
- Charging or receiving premiums exceeding the 15% cap
- **Soliciting business in or around courthouses or detention facilities** (the primary venue prohibition)
- Paying any fee, rebate, or consideration to a jailer, law enforcement officer, or court employee to reduce bond forfeitures or secure business referrals
- Impersonating a law enforcement officer
- Falsely claiming government affiliation
- Advising a principal to forfeit a bond
- Participating as an attorney in any case involving a defendant on whose bond they are surety
- Requiring collateral exceeding a reasonable relationship to the bond amount
- Conditioning bond execution on the defendant hiring a specific attorney
- Sharing or splitting premiums with prohibited persons

## 6. Disciplinary Authority (GS 58-71-80, 58-71-82)

The NC Commissioner of Insurance may **deny, place on probation, suspend, revoke, or refuse to renew** any license for:

| Ground | Notes |
|---|---|
| Felony conviction | Automatic denial/revocation |
| Misdemeanor conviction in course of bail bond business | Discretionary |
| Material misstatement or fraud in obtaining license | |
| Misappropriation of client funds | |
| Financial irresponsibility or incompetence | |
| Charging premiums exceeding statutory cap | |
| Violating prohibited practices (GS 58-71-95) | |
| Failure to maintain required security deposit | Professional bondsmen |
| Cheating on licensure examination | |
| Filing for bankruptcy without timely notification | Commissioner must be notified within 3 business days; failure = suspension of 60 days to 3 years |

### Administrative Process
- Hearings conducted under NC Administrative Procedure Act
- Right to appeal adverse decisions through the courts
- BBRD conducts routine compliance audits

---

## 7. Defendant Surrender Rights (GS 58-71-20, 58-71-25)

A licensed bondsman (or their runner, with recorded power of attorney) has the right to surrender a defendant to the sheriff **at any time before bond forfeiture**:
- Surrender cancels the bondsman's liability on the bond
- No court order required for pre-forfeiture surrender
- Full premium must be returned within 72 hours (see Premium Refund Rules above)
- After surrender, defendant is booked and new bond conditions may be set
- Bondsmen do **not** have law enforcement authority — they may not use force beyond what is necessary and lawful

## 8. Continuing Education (GS 58-71-71)

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Pre-licensing | 12 hours (one-time, before initial license) |
| Annual CE | **3 hours** per year in subjects pertinent to bail bond duties |
| Renewal cycle | Even-numbered years (biennial renewal); all requirements due by **June 30** |
| Exemption | Licensees **65 or older with 15+ years of experience** are exempt from CE |
| Administration | Through NIPR / Pearson VUE system |

---

## 9. Bond Capacity Summary (Professional Bondsman Only)

| Metric | Rule |
|---|---|
| Minimum security deposit | $15,000 with NC Commissioner |
| Maximum total outstanding bonds | 12x security deposit |
| Maximum single bond | 1/4 of security deposit |
| Deposit form | Cash or NC DOI-approved securities |

**Example:** A professional bondsman with $60,000 on deposit may write bonds totaling up to $720,000 outstanding, with no single bond exceeding $15,000.

Surety bondsmen (insurance-backed) are not subject to these individual capacity limits — their capacity is governed by the insurance company's filing and the appointed insurer's financial strength.

---

## 10. BBRD Oversight Structure

- **NC DOI Bail Bond Regulatory Division (BBRD)** administers all licensing, renewal, disciplinary, and audit functions
- Application and renewal are processed electronically through **NIPR** (National Insurance Producer Registry)
- Examinations administered through **Pearson VUE** testing centers statewide
- **NC Bail Agents Association (NCBAA)** is the primary industry association; provides approved pre-licensing education courses
- Annual audit program: BBRD conducts routine audits for compliance with Article 71 and Article 26 (bail statutes)

## Anti-Patterns / Common Errors

- **Confusing the three bondsman types:** Only surety and professional bondsmen are licensed; accommodation bondsmen are unlicensed family/friend sureties who may not be paid
- **Assuming premium is always 10%:** NC law caps at 15%; there is no statutory minimum. The actual rate is negotiated within the 15% ceiling
- **Missing the courthouse solicitation ban:** Bondsmen cannot solicit business at or near courthouses or detention facilities — a common disciplinary trigger
- **Overlooking the first-year supervision requirement:** New licensees must work under supervision for the full first 12 months
- **Conflating surrender and apprehension:** Bondsmen have civil (not criminal) arrest authority; use of force rules differ from law enforcement. NC does not give bondsmen the broad "bounty hunter" authority some other states recognize
- **Missing bankruptcy notice:** Failing to notify the Commissioner within 3 business days of a bankruptcy filing triggers mandatory license suspension
