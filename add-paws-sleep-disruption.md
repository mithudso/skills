# PAWS Sleep Disruption: Hypersomnia, Cycling, and Relapse Risk

## Core Concepts
- **PAWS** (Post-Acute Withdrawal Syndrome): neurobiological symptoms persisting weeks–months after acute detox ends; distinct from acute withdrawal by timeline and cyclic character
- **Biphasic sleep trajectory**: early PAWS (weeks 1–4) dominated by hypersomnia and fatigue; mid-PAWS (months 1–6) shifts to insomnia-hypersomnia cycling; late PAWS (months 6–24) resolves toward insomnia phenotype
- **Insomnia-hypersomnia cycling**: hallmark PAWS feature — good days/bad days alternation; sleep disruptions compound other PAWS symptoms (mood lability, craving, cognitive fog) in a bidirectional loop
- **Substance specificity**: meth/stimulant PAWS leads with hypersomnia → insomnia; alcohol/opioid PAWS leads with insomnia → normalizes; meth produces longest PAWS sleep duration (months vs weeks)
- **Protracted anhedonia-fatigue complex**: low energy, amotivation, and hypersomnia co-occur in stimulant PAWS as a unified DA-deficiency syndrome, not separate symptoms

## Mechanisms
- **DA depletion-driven hypersomnia**: meth exhausts vesicular monoamine stores and downregulates DAT ~20% at 1 month; reduced mesolimbic DA tone removes wake-promoting drive — nucleus accumbens glucose metabolism remains reduced weeks to months
- **D2/D3 receptor downregulation**: striatal D2/D3 receptor density recovers slowly; reduced receptor availability impairs motivated wakefulness and hedonic processing, sustaining the fatigue-anhedonia-hypersomnia triad
- **Adenosine rebound**: chronic stimulant use suppresses adenosine-mediated sleep pressure; cessation triggers adenosine accumulation disproportionate to true sleep need → hypersomnolence despite prolonged sleep time
- **HPA axis dysregulation**: CRF hypersensitivity established during dependence drives stress-reactive insomnia during PAWS; blunted cortisol awakening response correlates with fatigue severity
- **Opioid/alcohol contrast**: opioid PAWS insomnia mediated by upregulated NE (locus coeruleus rebound) and reduced inhibitory tone; alcohol PAWS insomnia from GABA-A downregulation and NMDA sensitization — both are hyperarousal-driven vs. stimulant PAWS hypoarousal-driven

## Clinical Picture
- **Weeks 1–4 (hypersomnic phase)**: 11–18 h/day sleep common; increases of 2–3 h/night over controls with poor quality (light, fragmented, unrefreshing); vivid dreams/nightmares peak then subside by week 2
- **Months 1–6 (cycling phase)**: symptom clusters episodically worsen and remit; "the wall" (3–6 month mark) is peak relapse-risk window when post-crash euphoria fades and depression/anhedonia intensify
- **Post-acute insomnia**: by months 6–12 hypersomnia recedes; sleep efficiency deficits (≈75.7% vs ≥85% normal) and elevated N1% persist even at 81-day abstinence per PSG data
- **Meth vs. cocaine**: meth PAWS lasts weeks to months; cocaine crash shorter (hours–days) with faster resolution; meth produces more severe and prolonged hypersomnic phase due to greater DAT toxicity
- **Relapse window**: ~59% of people with meth use disorder relapse within 12 months; anhedonia and sleep disruption at discharge are significant independent predictors

## Evidence
- **Cochrane review (PMC7138250, 2020)**: amphetamine withdrawal Phase 2 (protracted) symptoms — including mild hypersomnia or insomnia — generally resolve by week 3 in uncomplicated cases; no medication proven effective for sleep-specific PAWS
- **PSG abstinence study (PMC8776271, 2022)**: meth-dependent patients at mean 81 days abstinence: REM latency 116±68 min, sleep efficiency 75.7%, N1 elevated at 12.7% — demonstrates clinically significant sleep disruption well into remission
- **NCBI Bookshelf NBK576550**: post-crash "wall" phenomenon at 3–6 months post-cessation identified as high-risk relapse period; hypersomnolence and fatigue precede dysphoric return-to-use
- **Behavioral intervention**: CBT-I adapted for PAWS context is first-line for insomnia component; sleep hygiene + stimulus control + relaxation; pharmacotherapy (trazodone, mirtazapine) has clinical use despite limited RCT data specific to PAWS
- **DA recovery timeline (London et al., PET)**: striatal DAT normalizes to near-control levels by 12–14 months sustained abstinence — directly predicts when PAWS sleep/energy symptoms should substantially resolve

## Key Facts
- **2–3 extra hours/night** sleep in stimulant PAWS weeks 1–4, but quality is poor — light, fragmented, unrefreshing (Cochrane, PMC7138250)
- **3–6 month mark**: highest relapse-risk window in meth PAWS; corresponds to DA system still in deep deficit before partial DAT recovery
- **12–14 months**: timeline for striatal DAT recovery toward normalization (PET data); approximate ceiling for PAWS sleep symptom resolution
- **75.7% sleep efficiency** at ~81 days meth abstinence vs. ≥85% normal — significant persistent insomnia even after hypersomnic phase resolves
- **Alcohol/opioid PAWS insomnia** is hyperarousal-driven (NE/GABA rebound); stimulant PAWS hypersomnia is hypoarousal-driven (DA/adenosine depletion) — mechanistically opposite, requiring different management
