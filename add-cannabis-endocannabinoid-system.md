# Endocannabinoid System & Cannabis Use Disorder

## Core Concepts
- **Endocannabinoid system (ECS):** Lipid-based retrograde neuromodulatory system comprising CB1/CB2 receptors, endogenous ligands (AEA/anandamide, 2-AG), and degradative enzymes (FAAH, MAGL)
- **CB1 receptors:** Most abundant GPCR in the CNS; concentrated in basal ganglia, hippocampus, prefrontal cortex, cerebellum; Gi/o-coupled — activation inhibits adenylyl cyclase and reduces presynaptic neurotransmitter release
- **CB2 receptors:** Predominantly peripheral (immune cells, spleen); also expressed in CNS microglia and astrocytes; role in neuroinflammation increasingly recognized
- **Cannabis use disorder (CUD):** DSM-5 diagnosis requiring ≥2 of 11 criteria within 12 months; severity: mild (2–3), moderate (4–5), severe (≥6)
- **THC** is a partial CB1/CB2 agonist (Ki ≈ 35–80 nM at CB1); unlike AEA/2-AG it is not rapidly degraded, producing sustained receptor activation

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **Retrograde signaling:** Postsynaptic depolarization triggers 2-AG/AEA synthesis and release; endocannabinoids travel retrogradely to activate presynaptic CB1, suppressing further neurotransmitter release (DSI/DSE — depolarization-induced suppression of inhibition/excitation)
- **THC-induced synaptic suppression:** THC hijacks this retrograde mechanism, tonically activating CB1 on GABAergic interneurons in the VTA → disinhibition of dopamine neurons → increased mesolimbic dopamine; parallels in corticostriatal glutamate circuits drive drug-seeking
- **Chronic exposure & neuroadaptation:** Repeated THC causes CB1 receptor downregulation (~50% reduction in striatum and cortex) and desensitization via β-arrestin recruitment; functional hypodopaminergia and HPA axis dysregulation persist weeks into abstinence
- **Withdrawal neurobiology:** CB1 downregulation unmasks baseline anxiety/dysphoria; CRF hyperactivity (amygdala) and reduced OFC signaling drive negative reinforcement loop; AEA, OEA, DHEA levels are 31–40% higher in overnight-abstinent CUD patients vs. controls, suggesting compensatory upregulation
- **Adolescent vulnerability:** CB1 expression peaks during adolescence; THC interference with synaptic pruning in PFC impairs executive function and approximately doubles lifetime CUD risk when initiation occurs before age 15

## Clinical Picture
- **Prevalence:** ~9% of all cannabis users develop CUD; 20–30% of regular/daily users meet criteria; 6.8% of Americans aged 12+ (19.2 million) met past-year CUD criteria per 2023 NSDUH; highest rate in ages 18–25 (16.6%)
- **Withdrawal syndrome (Cannabis Withdrawal Disorder, DSM-5):** Onset within 24 hours of cessation; peak days 2–3; resolution ~1–2 weeks. Core symptoms: irritability, anxiety, insomnia, decreased appetite, restlessness, depressed mood; somatic: headache, sweating, GI distress, tremor
- **Comorbidity:** High overlap with anxiety disorders (~40%), major depression (~25%), and psychosis risk (dose-dependent; high-potency/daily use ORs 1.4–4.8× for psychotic disorders)
- **Potency trend:** Mean delta-9-THC concentration in US flower increased from ~4% (1990s) to ~12–15% (2020s); concentrates reach 60–90%; higher potency correlates with faster CUD progression and more severe withdrawal

## Evidence & Treatment
- **No FDA-approved pharmacotherapy exists** (as of 2026); nabiximols (THC:CBD 1:1 oromucosal spray) reduces withdrawal severity in trials but is not US-approved for CUD
- **Investigational agents:** N-acetylcysteine (glutamate modulation) showed benefit in adolescents (RCT, Gray 2012) but failed in adults; gabapentin reduced withdrawal symptoms in one RCT; CB1 positive allosteric modulators and FAAH inhibitors in early-phase development
- **First-line psychosocial:** Cognitive-behavioral therapy (6+ sessions), motivational enhancement therapy, and contingency management (strongest evidence across multiple RCTs); combined MET+CBT outperforms either alone in adults
- **Family-based therapy** (FFT, ACRA) recommended for adolescents; adolescent CM trials show 40–50% abstinence rates at end of treatment
- **CBD:** Preclinical evidence suggests attenuates THC-induced CB1 overstimulation (negative allosteric modulation); human CBD trials for CUD show mixed results; not currently recommended as standalone treatment

## Key Facts
- CB1 is expressed at ~10× higher density than most other GPCRs; ~1 million CB1 receptors per neuron in some striatal cells
- THC half-life in plasma: 1–2 days; in adipose tissue: 10–13 days; urine positive up to 30 days in heavy users
- Withdrawal onset: ≤24 hours; peak: 48–72 hours; duration: 1–2 weeks (sleep disturbance may persist 4+ weeks)
- Risk of CUD given any use: ~9%; given daily use: ~33–50%
- CUD relapse rates: ~70–80% within 12 months without treatment; comparable to other SUD categories
