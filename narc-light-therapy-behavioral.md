# Light Therapy & Behavioral Interventions in Narcolepsy / Secondary EDS

## Scope

Non-pharmacological adjuncts for excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) in NT1, NT2, and secondary narcolepsy — including meth-withdrawal-related EDS where circadian disruption compounds orexin-mediated or dopaminergic deficits. These interventions do not replace pharmacotherapy but meaningfully reduce EDS burden when applied correctly.

---

## 1. Scheduled Naps (Gold Standard Adjunct)

**Protocol:** 2–3 planned naps/day, 15–20 min each, timed to anticipated EDS windows (commonly post-lunch and mid-afternoon). Derive optimal timing from a 1–2 week sleep diary — individual EDS peaks vary.

**Evidence:** Three controlled studies show consistent, significant ESS reductions regardless of exact schedule. A single longer afternoon nap offers the greatest reaction-time benefit when multiple naps are impractical. Evidence is modest (small samples, mostly 1990s–2000s), but clinical consensus is strong.

**Key distinction:** Planned naps in narcolepsy are therapeutic, not an accommodation failure. They reduce EDS severity without displacing pharmacotherapy.

---

## 2. Bright Light Therapy

**In primary NT1:** Direct evidence is weak. A controlled trial (Springer 1994) found no objective or subjective improvement in narcoleptic symptoms with bright white light. Do not present as first-line or evidence-based for NT1 specifically.

**In secondary narcolepsy / meth-withdrawal EDS:** More rationale exists. Methamphetamine disrupts circadian entrainment independently of orexin status; prolonged use blunts melatonin rhythms and phase-shifts the clock. Bright light therapy (10,000 lux, 30 min within 30 min of desired wake time) advances circadian phase, reinforces morning alertness signaling, and may partially restore suppressed melatonin amplitude at night. Evidence is extrapolated from circadian disorder literature, not meth-withdrawal RCTs — disclose this.

**Practical use:** Position light box at eye level ~18 inches; do not stare directly. Avoid evening use (delays phase).

---

## 3. Sleep Hygiene — Narcolepsy-Specific Modifications

Standard sleep hygiene guidelines do NOT apply unmodified. Key differences:

| Standard Rec | Narcolepsy Modification |
|---|---|
| Avoid daytime naps | Schedule 2–3 planned naps (therapeutic) |
| Restrict time in bed | Contraindicated — see §5 |
| Flexible wake time on weekends | Rigid wake time — non-negotiable anchor |
| "Sleep when tired" | Structure overrides subjective sleepiness signals |

**Additional:** Dark bedroom at night supports melatonin secretion; total nighttime sleep 7.5–8 h target; avoid alcohol (fragments already unstable sleep architecture).

---

## 4. Circadian Zeitgebers / Social Schedule

Regular meal timing, consistent exercise timing, and social engagement entrain the circadian clock via non-photic pathways. Particularly important in meth recovery, where circadian chaos may persist months post-cessation (disrupted dopaminergic clock-gene feedback loops).

Practical anchors: breakfast within 30 min of wake; main exercise window consistent daily; social contact at predictable times. These do not replace pharmacotherapy but reduce the amplitude of EDS fluctuation.

---

## 5. Sleep Restriction Therapy — CONTRAINDICATION

Standard CBT-I sleep restriction is **contraindicated in narcolepsy**. Sleep deprivation in narcolepsy:
- Worsens EDS severity
- Increases cataplexy frequency/severity in NT1
- Destabilizes already fragmented nighttime sleep architecture

This is a common clinical error when sleep-medicine providers unfamiliar with narcolepsy apply standard insomnia protocols. Flag any CBT-I referral: confirm the therapist is adapting the protocol for hypersomnia, not using restriction.

---

## 6. Exercise

**Evidence:** Small RCTs show moderate aerobic exercise at consistent timing improves sleep quality and reduces ESS scores in narcolepsy. Effect size modest; mechanism likely via circadian reinforcement + adenosine clearance.

**Protocol:** Moderate intensity (not high-intensity); consistent daily timing; avoid within 2–3 h of intended sleep onset.

**Meth-recovery caveat:** Cardiovascular deconditioning, cardiomyopathy risk, and autonomic dysregulation are common post-meth. Clear with PCP before initiating structured aerobic program; start low and progress slowly.

---

## 7. Cognitive / Alerting Strategies

During pharmacotherapy gaps or trough periods: alerting activities (standing desk, walking meetings), cold air/water exposure, active engagement (conversation, complex tasks), chewing gum (trigeminal arousal pathway). Not curative — functional bridges to reduce dangerous microsleeps during high-stakes periods (driving, operating machinery).

---

## 8. Combined Protocol for Meth-Withdrawal Secondary Narcolepsy

Optimal multimodal approach:
1. **Pharmacotherapy** (wake-promoting agent ± sodium oxybate if applicable)
2. **Rigid wake time** daily anchor
3. **Bright light therapy** 10,000 lux, 30 min at wake
4. **2–3 scheduled naps** timed to diary-identified EDS peaks
5. **Circadian zeitgebers** (meals, exercise, social)
6. **No sleep restriction** — explicitly contraindicated
