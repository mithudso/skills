# Medicaid LTSS Functional Eligibility (Level of Care)

## Two-Gate Eligibility for Long-Term Services and Supports

Qualifying for Medicaid LTSS (home and community-based services, nursing facility care, etc.) requires passing **two separate eligibility gates**:

1. **Financial eligibility**: Income and asset tests (for non-MAGI Medicaid groups — nursing facility and HCBS waiver participants)
2. **Functional eligibility** (Level of Care / LOC): Demonstrated need for the level of care the program provides

This spoke covers the **functional eligibility** gate — the assessment of physical/cognitive/behavioral need for services.

## What Is Level of Care (LOC)?

Level of care (LOC) refers to the assessed intensity of personal care, medical oversight, and support services a person requires. The LOC determination matches the individual to the appropriate Medicaid benefit:

- **Nursing facility level of care**: Needed for institutionalized care (SNF/NF) AND most HCBS waivers (person could need NF care but wishes to live in community)
- **Intermediate care facility for individuals with intellectual disabilities (ICF/IID)**: For people with I/DD needing residential habilitation
- **State-specific HCBS waiver criteria**: Some waivers use program-specific LOC standards

## The Functional Assessment Process

**Step 1 — Application for LTSS**
Individual (or representative) applies for LTSS through Medicaid, typically identifying a need for home-based services (waiver) or nursing facility placement.

**Step 2 — Functional Assessment**
A state assessor (RN, social worker, or trained assessor) conducts an in-person assessment covering:

| Domain | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Activities of Daily Living (ADLs)** | Bathing, dressing, eating, toileting, transferring, continence |
| **Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADLs)** | Meal preparation, medication management, finances, transportation, housekeeping |
| **Cognitive function** | Memory, orientation, judgment, decision-making capacity |
| **Behavioral and psychiatric** | Wandering, agitation, depression, psychosis |
| **Medical complexity** | Wound care, tube feeding, ventilator dependence, IV medications |
| **Functional health** | Vision, hearing, communication, mobility |

**Step 3 — LOC Determination**
Assessor or state agency determines whether the individual meets the applicable LOC standard. Written determination issued with findings.

**Step 4 — Combined with Financial Eligibility**
Both financial and functional eligibility must be met to receive benefits. In practice, these assessments may occur simultaneously or sequentially depending on state process.

## Assessment Tools

States use various standardized instruments; common ones:
- **interRAI suite**: Nationally recognized; includes Home Care (HC), Long-Term Care Facilities (LTCF), Contact Assessment, Palliative Care, and Pediatric versions. NC is implementing interRAI tools
- **Minimum Data Set (MDS)**: For nursing facility residents — completed by facility
- **State-specific tools**: Many states have developed proprietary LOC screening instruments

**NC 2025 update**: FSSA (Indiana) and NC have been implementing interRAI PEDS HC (5–18 years) and Early Years (0–4) tools for pediatric populations starting July 2025.

## Nursing Facility Level of Care Standard

To meet "nursing facility level of care," an individual typically must require:
- Skilled nursing or rehabilitative services on a daily basis that can only be provided in an NF setting, OR
- Substantial assistance with multiple ADLs due to physical or cognitive impairment, AND the condition is expected to be long-term

States define their specific NF LOC standard; most align with "unable to live safely in community without significant supervision and assistance."

## HCBS Waiver LOC

Most HCBS waivers (home and community-based services under 1915(c)) serve individuals who **would otherwise qualify for nursing facility or ICF care but choose to live in the community**. The LOC standard is usually the same NF level — meeting the clinical criteria is required even if the person is not actually in a nursing facility.

**HCBS waivers in NC (examples):**
- **CAP/DA (Community Alternatives Program for Disabled Adults)**: Age 18+, NF-level LOC required
- **CAP/C (Community Alternatives Program for Children)**: Under 18, hospital or NF level of care
- **NC Innovations**: I/DD population; ICF/IID level of care

## Appeals

If LOC is denied:
- You have the right to a **fair hearing** (Medicaid due process)
- Request within timeframe stated on the denial notice (typically 90 days)
- Coverage continues at current level during the appeal period if appeal is filed timely
- Consider clinical documentation from treating physicians, specialists, and therapists to support appeal
- SHIP counselors and legal aid organizations can assist with appeals

## Interaction with Financial Eligibility

For institutional (nursing facility) Medicaid:
- Income rules: Most income must be applied to cost of care (patient liability); a personal needs allowance is kept
- Asset rules: Must pass asset test; 5-year look-back applies (see `enrollment-eligibility-medicaid-ltc-lookback`)
- Married couple rules: Community spouse resource allowance (CSRA) and minimum monthly maintenance needs allowance (MMMNA) protect the community spouse

For HCBS waivers:
- Same non-MAGI income/asset rules but asset limit may be higher in some waivers (e.g., some states allow higher resource limits for HCBS to incentivize community care)
- Spousal protections apply similarly

## Person-Centered Service Plans

Once LOC and financial eligibility are established, a **person-centered service plan** is developed with the individual and their chosen representatives — specifying which HCBS services will be provided, by whom, and at what frequency. This plan drives actual services and is separate from the eligibility determination.
