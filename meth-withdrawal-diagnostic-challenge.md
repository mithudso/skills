# Methamphetamine Cessation: Differential Diagnosis of EDS and Hypersomnia

## Core Concepts

- **Five diagnostic categories** compete after meth cessation: (1) acute withdrawal rebound hypersomnia, (2) PAWS hypersomnia (post-acute withdrawal syndrome), (3) unmasked pre-existing narcolepsy (stimulant use was self-treatment), (4) secondary/symptomatic narcolepsy from orexin neurotoxicity, (5) idiopathic hypersomnia (IH)
- **Narcolepsy Type 1 (NT1)** requires mean sleep latency ≤8 min + ≥2 SOREMs on MSLT, or CSF orexin-A <110 pg/mL; NT2 requires MSLT criteria without orexin deficiency or cataplexy
- **Idiopathic hypersomnia** features mean sleep latency ≤8 min, <2 SOREMs, no cataplexy, often long (>10 h) overnight sleep with severe sleep inertia
- **Secondary narcolepsy** from orexin neurotoxicity is biologically distinct from autoimmune NT1 — orexin neuron damage rather than immune destruction; HLA-DQB1\*06:02 likely absent
- Withdrawal hypersomnia and PAWS are diagnoses of exclusion; persistent EDS beyond 3 months warrants formal evaluation regardless of attribution

## Mechanisms

- Meth acutely triples c-Fos expression in orexin neurons; chronic use causes downstream orexin receptor downregulation and potential axon terminal damage
- Dopamine transporter (DAT) density is reduced 20–30% in striatum of chronic meth users by PET; DAT mediates arousal tone — loss contributes to post-cessation EDS
- Serotonin transporter (SERT) loss in multiple brain regions post-meth use disrupts sleep architecture (reduced REM latency, fragmented NREM)
- Acute withdrawal hypersomnia: norepinephrine/dopamine/orexin systems crash simultaneously after meth-mediated hyperactivation — rebound overshoots baseline by days
- PAWS: persisting dopaminergic and serotonergic deficit state with hypersomnia, anhedonia, cognitive slowing; duration 6 weeks to >12 months, severity tracks cumulative use

## Clinical Picture

- **Acute crash**: hypersomnia 14–20 h/day begins within 12–24 h of last dose; peaks at days 5–7; resolves most hypersomnia by end of week 2 in acute-only phase
- **Red flags for underlying sleep disorder**: EDS persisting >3 months post-cessation; cataplexy-like events; sleep paralysis; hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations; or severe sleep inertia exceeding crash period
- **PAWS hypersomnia**: typically 10–12 h/day sleep with non-restorative quality; fluctuates; improves over 3–12 months; no clear MSLT mean latency cutoff established
- MSLT in active withdrawal or early cessation is unreliable: false-positive SOREMs from REM rebound and cholinergic supersensitivity mimic narcolepsy pattern
- CSF orexin-A <110 pg/mL confirms NT1 regardless of drug status; levels 110–200 pg/mL (gray zone) require clinical correlation

## Evidence

- AASM guidelines (2021): at minimum 2-week washout of stimulants required before MSLT; longer washout (4–6 weeks) preferred for heavy chronic meth users given prolonged neurochemical disruption
- Urine drug screen on day of MSLT is mandatory per AASM; positive amphetamine invalidates SOREM count for diagnostic purposes (JCSM 2015, PMC4298782)
- Postmortem meth-user brain tissue shows significant decreases in striatal dopamine concentration, tyrosine hydroxylase, and DAT protein — consistent with secondary sleep-wake dysregulation (not reversible in all users)
- HLA-DQB1\*06:02 typing helps distinguish: >90% of NT1 patients carry the allele; secondary narcolepsy from neurotoxicity and PAWS hypersomnia are not HLA-associated — allele absence in symptomatic patient weakens NT1, supports IH or PAWS
- No RCT-level evidence establishes optimal washout duration specifically for meth before MSLT; 2-week minimum is extrapolated from amphetamine half-life data and clinical consensus

## Key Facts

- Meth half-life 10–12 h (active metabolite amphetamine 12–34 h); 5× half-lives ≈ 5–7 days for pharmacokinetic clearance, but neurochemical normalization takes weeks to months
- CSF orexin-A <110 pg/mL: 87% sensitivity, 99% specificity for NT1 — most valuable single test when MSLT is unreliable due to inadequate washout
- Mean sleep latency: ≤8 min is threshold for NT1/NT2/IH; 8–10 min is borderline; >10 min makes primary hypersomnia diagnosis unlikely
- Acute withdrawal hypersomnia resolves in ≥80% of patients by 4 weeks; EDS persisting beyond 3 months should prompt formal PSG/MSLT after adequate washout
- Recommended decision algorithm: (1) confirm ≥2-week (ideally ≥4-week) abstinence with UDS; (2) obtain PSG night before MSLT; (3) if MSLT positive, check CSF orexin-A and HLA-DQB1\*06:02; (4) orexin <110 confirms NT1; normal orexin + MSLT criteria = NT2 or IH; negative MSLT = PAWS or IH by clinical judgment
