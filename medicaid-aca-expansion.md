# Medicaid ACA Expansion

> **Educational only — NOT health or legal advice.** Coverage rules and eligibility thresholds change; verify with your state Medicaid agency or healthcare.gov navigator.

**verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

---

## 1. What ACA Expansion Is

The Affordable Care Act (2010) added a new mandatory Medicaid coverage group: **non-elderly adults ages 19–64 with income at or below 138% of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL)**, regardless of disability, pregnancy, or parental status. (138% = 133% statutory threshold + 5% income disregard.)

**NFIB v. Sebelius (2012)**: The Supreme Court ruled that this expansion could not be imposed on states as a mandatory condition — states had to lose ALL Medicaid funding if they refused. The Court made expansion effectively **optional**. As of 2026, 41 states + DC have expanded; ~10 states have not.

---

## 2. Who Expansion Covers

**Pre-ACA Medicaid** covered adults mainly through categorical pathways: pregnant women, children, parents of dependent children, elderly/disabled (SSI-linked). Childless adults with low incomes had no federal Medicaid pathway regardless of income.

**ACA expansion added**: Childless adults, non-custodial parents, and low-income adults who did not qualify through categorical pathways — the **single largest coverage group** added to Medicaid since its 1965 creation.

### 138% FPL in 2026 (approximate)
| Household Size | Annual Income |
|---|---|
| 1 person | ~$20,800 |
| 2 people | ~$28,200 |
| 4 people | ~$43,000 |

## 3. Federal Matching Under Expansion

Congress created a special enhanced FMAP specifically for expansion enrollees:

| Phase | Federal Match (FMAP) | State Share |
|---|---|---|
| 2014–2016 | 100% | 0% |
| 2017 | 95% | 5% |
| 2018 | 94% | 6% |
| 2019 | 93% | 7% |
| 2020 onward | 90% | 10% |

This **90/10 split** (permanent) is far more generous than regular Medicaid's average ~57% federal match (FMAP), giving states strong fiscal incentive to expand. Late-expanding states received a **5 percentage point FMAP bump** for their first two years of expansion (ARP Act 2021 incentive).

---

## 4. The Coverage Gap

In **non-expansion states**, adults with income below the ACA Marketplace minimum (~100% FPL) fall into a **coverage gap**:
- Too poor for Marketplace subsidies (which start at 100% FPL)
- Not eligible for Medicaid (which covers adults only through categorical pathways below pre-ACA thresholds — often 0–50% FPL for parents, nothing for childless adults)

**Result**: Millions of low-income adults in holdout states have no affordable insurance option. As of 2026, ~1.5–2.5 million adults nationwide remain in the coverage gap in non-expansion states.

---

## 5. NC Expansion (December 1, 2023)

**North Carolina** expanded Medicaid effective **December 1, 2023** — the last major Southern state to do so. NC was unique in that bipartisan legislation (SB 355, signed August 16, 2023) passed with a trigger mechanism: expansion went live only after the NC state budget was enacted.

### Enrollment Impact
- Approximately **600,000 adults** gained Medicaid coverage in the first year
- NC uninsured rate fell significantly in 2024
- Standard Plans (commercial managed care) absorbed most new enrollees

### Key NC Expansion Rules
- Income: ≤138% FPL, ages 19–64
- MAGI-based income counting (no asset test)
- NC uses Standard Plans (managed care) — enrollees assigned to commercial MCOs
- Members with serious behavioral health needs who meet criteria enroll in Tailored Plans instead

---

## 7. Post-Expansion Research Findings

High-quality research consistently shows expansion states vs. non-expansion states have:
- Lower uninsured rates (10–15 percentage point gap)
- Reduced hospital uncompensated care costs
- Improved detection of chronic conditions (diabetes, hypertension, cancer)
- Lower mortality rates among near-elderly low-income adults
- Rural hospital financial stability improvements

---

## 8. Anti-Patterns

**"ACA expansion replaced regular Medicaid"** — No. Expansion is an additional coverage group layered onto existing Medicaid. Pre-ACA mandatory groups (children, pregnant women, elderly, disabled) remain with their own FMAP rates.

**"Expansion enrollees get inferior coverage"** — No. Expansion enrollees receive the same Medicaid benefit package as other adults in the state. NC's expansion enrollees receive full Medicaid benefits through Standard Plans.

**"The coverage gap no longer exists"** — Partially false. As of 2026, ~10 holdout states still have a coverage gap; only expansion eliminated it in those states.
