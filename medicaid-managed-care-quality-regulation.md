# Medicaid Managed Care Quality and Rate Regulation

> **Educational only — NOT legal or healthcare compliance advice.** CMS regulations are updated frequently; verify with CMS or state Medicaid agency.

**verified-as-of: 2026-06-22** *(compiled from training knowledge; CMS managed care rules update frequently; verify current MLR rebate contract requirements and timely access standards with CMS)*

---

## 1. Overview: Why Oversight Matters in Medicaid Managed Care

Over 70% of Medicaid beneficiaries are enrolled in managed care organizations (MCOs) — health plans paid a per-member-per-month capitation rate to provide comprehensive care. Unlike FFS where every service triggers a payment review, managed care concentrates risk and control in the plan. Federal and state oversight addresses:
- **Actuarial soundness** of capitation rates (are plans paid enough to provide required services?)
- **Quality measurement** (are plans actually delivering good outcomes?)
- **Access** (can members get appointments, reach specialists?)
- **Medical loss ratio** (are plans spending on care vs. administration?)

---

## 2. Regulatory Framework

### CMS Managed Care Final Rules
CMS regulations at **42 CFR Part 438** govern Medicaid managed care. Major rule updates:
- **2016 Final Rule:** Comprehensive overhaul — actuarial soundness standards, MLR requirements, directed/pass-through payments, network adequacy, quality strategy, grievances/appeals
- **2020 Rule:** Modifications on network adequacy, prior authorization, quality rating system
- **2023–2024 Rule:** Strengthened network adequacy, timely access standards (quantitative standards for appointment wait times), prior authorization reforms, access reporting requirements

## 3. Actuarial Soundness and Capitation Rate Setting

### What Actuarial Soundness Means
Per 42 CFR § 438.4, capitation rates must be **actuarially sound** — i.e., sufficient to:
- Cover expected costs of covered services
- Allow for reasonable administrative costs
- Allow reasonable profit/surplus for the MCO

Rates must be developed by a **qualified actuary** using accepted actuarial principles and certified to CMS.

### Rate Development
States develop capitation rates by:
1. Projecting FFS utilization/cost experience by eligibility group
2. Adjusting for managed care utilization management effects
3. Calculating risk-adjusted base capitation rates by demographic/health status
4. Adding administrative load and profit margin

### Risk Adjustment in Medicaid
- Most Medicaid MCO contracts use **risk adjustment** or **risk corridors** to prevent adverse selection (plans avoiding high-cost members) and protect plans from catastrophic outliers
- States increasingly use **risk corridor/MLR corridor** arrangements as a stopgap while more sophisticated risk adjustment is developed
- PACE programs (fully integrated duals) use separate payment structures

### Rate Certification
Before implementation, states submit capitation rates to CMS for review. CMS can reject rates that are not actuarially sound (too low = access risk; too high = waste of federal dollars).

## 4. Medical Loss Ratio (MLR)

### Definition
The **medical loss ratio** measures what fraction of premium (capitation) revenue a plan spends on medical care and quality improvement activities vs. administrative overhead and profit.

**Federal minimum MLR for Medicaid MCOs:** 85% (42 CFR § 438.8)
- Plans must spend ≥85 cents of each capitation dollar on direct medical care or quality improvement
- Plans below 85% MLR: states must require the plan to remit the shortfall or take other corrective action per their contract — federal regulations establish the standard and require states to address shortfalls, but specific remedies (rebate, rate adjustment, sanctions) are set in state contracts

### MLR Calculations for Medicaid
More complex than commercial MLR because:
- Medicaid plans often include administrative functions states mandate (care coordination, care management, reporting)
- Behavioral health carve-outs affect what counts as medical spending
- States define "quality improvement" activities eligible to count toward the numerator

### NC MLR
NC Medicaid managed care contracts specify MLR requirements consistent with federal minimums; plans submitting annual MLR reports. Tailored Plans (behavioral health/IDD) face scrutiny given the high care management cost load.

## 5. External Quality Review (EQR)

### What EQR Is
Under **42 CFR Part 438, Subpart E**, states must contract with an independent **External Quality Review Organization (EQRO)** to evaluate whether Medicaid MCOs are providing quality care. EQROs must be NCQA-accredited or otherwise qualified; they cannot be financially affiliated with the MCO being reviewed.

### Mandatory EQR Activities
1. **Validation of performance improvement projects (PIPs)** — reviewing MCO improvement projects for methodological soundness
2. **Validation of performance measures** — confirming HEDIS and other measures are calculated correctly
3. **Validation of network adequacy** — confirming plans have adequate provider networks

### Optional EQR Activities
- Consumer surveys (CAHPS Medicaid Adult, Child)
- Administrative record review
- Case management and care coordination review
- Focused clinical studies

### NC EQRO
NC contracts with an EQRO to review both Standard Plan MCOs and Tailored Plans (LME-MCOs). EQRO reports are published annually and inform NC DHHS contract oversight.

## 6. HEDIS Quality Measures in Medicaid

### HEDIS Overview
The **Healthcare Effectiveness Data and Information Set (HEDIS)**, maintained by NCQA, is the dominant quality measure set used in Medicaid managed care. States require MCOs to report HEDIS measures annually.

### Key Medicaid HEDIS Measures
| Domain | Measure examples |
|---|---|
| Preventive care | Well-child visits, adolescent preventive care, cervical cancer screening |
| Behavioral health | Follow-up after hospitalization for mental illness (7-day, 30-day), antidepressant medication management |
| Chronic disease | Comprehensive diabetes care, blood pressure control, asthma medication ratio |
| Access | Adults' access to preventive/ambulatory care, children's access to primary care |
| Maternity | Prenatal and postpartum care, cesarean section rate |
| SUD | Initiation/engagement of SUD treatment (IET), follow-up after SUD hospitalization |

### NCQA Accreditation
Many Medicaid MCOs seek **NCQA Health Plan Accreditation** (Medicaid-specific accreditation tier). NC requires NCQA accreditation or equivalent for Standard Plan MCOs.

## 7. Network Adequacy and Access Standards

### Network Adequacy Standards (42 CFR § 438.68)
States must establish and enforce standards for:
- **Time and distance** (maximum miles to nearest in-network provider by specialty and geographic area — urban vs rural vs frontier)
- **Appointment wait times** (e.g., primary care routine: 10 business days; urgent: 24–48 hours; behavioral health urgent: 6 hours)
- **After-hours access**
- **Non-English language access**
- **Disability access** (physical and communication accessibility)

### 2024 CMS Timely Access Rule
CMS finalized quantitative appointment wait time standards for Medicaid managed care in 2024:
- Routine primary care: within 15 business days
- Behavioral health (urgent): within 6 hours
- Non-urgent outpatient mental health: within 10 business days
- These represent the first federal numerical standards; states may adopt stricter requirements

### Consequence for Non-Compliance
MCOs failing network adequacy may be required to:
- Cover out-of-network care at in-network cost-sharing
- Expand network (recruitment incentives, rate increases)
- Face financial penalties or contract termination

## 8. NC Managed Care Quality Oversight

**NC DHHS Performance Monitoring:** NC uses a Performance Oversight Dashboard tracking HEDIS measures, consumer complaints, access metrics, and quality improvement project completion rates for all Standard Plan MCOs.

**Tailored Plans (LME-MCOs):** Alliance, Partners, Trillium, Vaya face quality monitoring with BH-specific measures including EQRO review, 988 crisis response rates, and SUD treatment initiation/engagement rates.

**Contract sanctions:** NC has authority to impose sanctions, financial penalties, or contract termination for persistent quality or access failures.
