# Methamphetamine Withdrawal Hypersomnia

## Core Concepts

- **Hypersomnia** = excessive sleep duration (commonly 14–20 h/day) and daytime sleepiness in the 1–3 weeks following meth cessation; peaks in the first 72–96 h
- Distinct from **narcolepsy**: no cataplexy, no sleep-onset REM periods (SOREMPs) on MSLT, no orexin deficiency; resolves with sustained abstinence
- Two sleep-phase phenomena overlap: **REM rebound** (elevated REM % and density) in early withdrawal, then **NREM/SWS rebound** (slow-wave recovery) dominating days 3–14
- **Crash phase** (0–72 h) vs **protracted withdrawal** (weeks–months): hypersomnia is predominantly a crash-phase phenomenon; later abstinence shifts to insomnia pattern
- Classified under DSM-5 **Stimulant Use Disorder** withdrawal; ICD-11 6C46.1; sleep disruption is a B-criterion symptom
- **NREM slow-wave sleep (SWS)** is the primary restorative stage; its rebound correlates with subjective refreshment and marks the end of acute hypersomnia

## Mechanisms

- **Dopamine depletion**: meth causes massive DA release via VMAT2 reversal and DAT-mediated efflux; prolonged use depletes vesicular DA stores and down-regulates D2 receptors; DA tone in the mesolimbic/mesocortical circuits is the primary arousal-promoting signal — its loss drives profound sleepiness
- **Norepinephrine depletion**: meth releases NE via NET reversal; locus coeruleus NE is essential for maintaining arousal and suppressing REM; NE depletion during withdrawal reduces wakefulness pressure and removes REM inhibition simultaneously
- **REM rebound**: meth (like all stimulants) suppresses REM via NE and 5-HT overactivation; abrupt cessation triggers compensatory REM pressure; REM % rises from ~20–25% normal to 30–40%+ in the first few nights, with increased REM density (eye-movement frequency)
- **SWS compensation**: adenosine accumulates during extended meth-driven wakefulness; cessation releases adenosinergic sleep pressure, driving elevated Stage N3 (SWS) % — the primary driver of long sleep duration
- **Orexin system intact but under-activated**: unlike narcolepsy, orexin neurons are structurally present; low DA/NE tone fails to activate orexin-mediated arousal circuits (orexin requires monoaminergic co-activation), producing functional hypersomnia without orexin deficiency
- **Hypothalamic-pituitary axis dysregulation**: meth suppresses CRF-driven cortisol awakening response; withdrawal reveals blunted morning cortisol, removing a key circadian arousal signal

## Clinical Picture

- **Acute crash** (0–72 h): 14–20 h sleep/day, profound fatigue, hyperphagia; daytime alertness near-absent between sleep episodes; onset within 12–24 h of last dose
- **Subacute phase** (days 3–14): total sleep time normalizes toward 9–12 h/day; patients report unrefreshing sleep, vivid/disturbing dreams (REM rebound manifestation), mood dysphoria; subjective energy remains low even when PSG shows adequate SWS
- **Transition to insomnia** (~weeks 2–4): sleep pattern often reverses — prolonged sleep latency, increased WASO, reduced sleep efficiency; this is distinct from and follows the hypersomnia phase
- Narcolepsy mimicry concern: post-meth patients may score ≤8 min mean sleep latency on MSLT during acute withdrawal — **MSLT is contraindicated within 2–3 weeks of stimulant cessation** as results are uninterpretable (false-positive narcolepsy pattern)
- Comorbid depression amplifies hypersomnia; post-meth anhedonia (DA receptor down-regulation) shares the same neurobiological substrate as hypersomnic depression

## Evidence

- **Sleep architecture PSG studies**: methamphetamine-dependent subjects in early abstinence show ↑ total sleep time, ↑ REM %, ↑ REM density, and ↑ SWS % vs controls in nights 1–4 of withdrawal (Schierenbeck et al., comprehensive stimulant-sleep review)
- **REM rebound confirmation**: 1977 Hartmann/Stern rat studies demonstrated amphetamine withdrawal produces significant REM rebound; blocked by chloramphenicol (protein synthesis inhibitor), suggesting REM rebound requires new receptor synthesis — foundational mechanism data
- **DA depletion → arousal impairment**: VMAT2 knockout and DAT-blockade models confirm dopamine transporter occupancy and vesicular DA levels correlate with locomotor arousal; 96-h meth self-administration in rats produces measurable striatal DA depletion (NCBI PMC12881116, 2025)
- **MSLT timing validity**: AASM guidelines and narcolepsy literature (Stanford Sleep Clinic, Mignot group) specify ≥2 weeks washout from stimulants before valid MSLT; clinically relevant for avoiding false narcolepsy diagnosis in early-recovery meth patients
- **Recovery trajectory**: polysomnographic normalization of sleep architecture typically occurs by weeks 3–5 of abstinence in uncomplicated cases; subjective sleep quality lags objective measures by ~2 weeks

## Key Facts

- Sleep duration in acute meth crash: **14–20 hours/day**, lasting 48–96 h in typical cases
- REM % during early withdrawal: **30–40%+** vs ~20–25% baseline — REM rebound is measurable on PSG from night 1
- MSLT washout requirement: **≥14 days** off stimulants before result is interpretable for narcolepsy diagnosis
- DA/NE system recovery: receptor sensitivity (D2 up-regulation) begins within 1–2 weeks of abstinence but **full monoaminergic normalization** takes months — explains protracted fatigue beyond acute hypersomnia
- Hypersomnia → insomnia transition typically occurs at **~2 weeks** abstinence; the shift is a positive prognostic sign (sleep pressure normalizing)
