# Biomarker-Guided Workup: Post-Meth Hypersomnia

## Why Mechanism Identification Matters

Three mechanisms produce EDS after meth cessation; treatment differs by mechanism:
- **Mechanism 1 (Rebound)**: DA/NE/orexin functional depletion — self-limiting, weeks to months
- **Mechanism 2 (Neurotoxicity)**: structural DAT loss + orexin terminal damage — partial recovery over 12–17 months; may need pharmacologic bridging
- **Mechanism 3 (Unmasked NT1)**: pre-existing narcolepsy type 1 previously masked by stimulant self-medication — lifelong condition requiring sodium oxybate/modafinil

## Step 1 — Timeline-Based Stratification

| Abstinence duration | Most likely mechanism | Action |
|---|---|---|
| < 2 weeks | Mechanism 1 (rebound) — near certainty | Supportive only; no diagnostic testing yet |
| 2–6 weeks | Any; rebound resolving, others emerging | Monitor; confirm abstinence; retest at 6 weeks |
| > 3 months persistent EDS | Mechanism 2 or 3; Mechanism 1 unlikely | Initiate formal biomarker workup |

**Rule**: if EDS resolves fully by week 4–6, no further workup required. Persistent or worsening EDS after week 6 warrants evaluation.

## Step 2 — MSLT Timing Rules

**Absolute contraindications to MSLT:**
- < 2 weeks abstinence (AASM minimum; results uninterpretable — false-positive SOREMs from REM rebound mimic NT1)
- Any current stimulant use (positive UDS invalidates SOREM count per AASM/JCSM 2015)
- Antidepressants within 2 weeks (suppress/induce REM independently)

**Preferred washout for chronic heavy meth users:** ≥ 4–6 weeks. The AASM 2-week minimum is based on amphetamine pharmacokinetics (5× half-life ≈ 5–7 days), but neurochemical normalization of REM architecture in heavy chronic users takes substantially longer. No meth-specific RCT-level washout data exist; 4–6 week recommendation is clinical consensus extrapolated from neurotoxicity literature.

**Mandatory before MSLT:** UDS on the morning of testing. Document on report.

## Step 3 — CSF Orexin-A (Hypocretin-1)

**Order when MSLT is contraindicated** (recent meth use, inadequate washout) or **when MSLT is positive but mechanism is unclear**.

| CSF Orexin-A | Interpretation | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| < 110 pg/mL | Diagnostic for NT1-level deficiency | Mechanism 2 or 3 — structural loss or unmasked NT1 |
| 110–200 pg/mL | Borderline / gray zone | Likely Mechanism 2 partial damage; repeat at 3 months |
| > 200 pg/mL | Normal (typically ~300–400 pg/mL in healthy adults) | Mechanism 1 pharmacological rebound — supports expectant management |

**Note**: lumbar puncture adds procedural burden. In Mechanism 1-suspected patients with adequate washout, MSLT is preferred. CSF orexin is most actionable when MSLT cannot be validly performed.

## Step 4 — HLA-DQB1*06:02 Typing

- Order alongside CSF orexin when < 110 pg/mL or when clinical suspicion for NT1 is high (cataplexy, sleep paralysis, hypnagogic hallucinations predating meth use)
- **Positive HLA + CSF orexin < 110 pg/mL = unmasked NT1 (Mechanism 3)** → treat as lifelong narcolepsy type 1
- **Negative HLA + CSF orexin < 110 pg/mL** = Mechanism 2 (neurotoxic secondary narcolepsy) — orexin deficiency without autoimmune signature; prognosis for partial recovery more likely than NT1
- HLA-DQB1*06:02 prevalence: ~25% of general population; >90% of NT1 patients carry allele — high NPV for NT1 exclusion when absent

## Step 5 — DAT-SPECT or PET (Optional; Research-Adjacent Clinically)

- **Reduced striatal DAT binding** at ≥ 6 months abstinence → confirms ongoing dopaminergic deficiency consistent with Mechanism 2 neurotoxicity; supports pharmacologic arousal support (bupropion, armodafinil)
- **Normal DAT at 6 months** → suggests Mechanism 1 rebound-only; dopaminergic terminals largely recovered
- **Persistent DAT reduction at 12–14 months** → suggests slower or incomplete neurotoxic recovery; recalibrate treatment expectations
- DAT-SPECT (DaTscan, ioflupane I-123) is FDA-cleared for parkinsonism; off-label use for meth EDS has no established protocol. Clinically available but specialist-ordered; most practical in academic sleep/neurology settings

## Step 6 — MRI Lateral Hypothalamus

- Rarely available clinically for this indication; **hypointense T2/FLAIR signal in the lateral hypothalamus** suggests structural orexin neuron damage (Mechanism 2)
- Normal MRI does not exclude Mechanism 2 — sensitivity is low for focal LH damage at clinical field strengths
- Most useful when CSF orexin is borderline (110–200 pg/mL) to adjudicate structural vs functional etiology

## Decision Tree

```
EDS after meth cessation
        │
        ├─ < 2 weeks abstinence ──────────────► Supportive care; no testing
        │
        ├─ 2–6 weeks, EDS improving ──────────► Observe; re-evaluate at 6 weeks
        │
        └─ EDS persistent > 6 weeks (or > 3 months for definitive workup)
                │
                ├─ Adequate washout (≥ 4–6 wk) + UDS negative?
                │       YES → PSG + MSLT
                │               │
                │               ├─ MSLT negative (MSL > 8 min, < 2 SOREMs)
                │               │       → PAWS/Mechanism 1; expectant management
                │               │
                │               └─ MSLT positive (MSL ≤ 8 min, ≥ 2 SOREMs)
                │                       → CSF orexin-A + HLA-DQB1*06:02
                │                               < 110 + HLA+ → Unmasked NT1 (Mech 3)
                │                               < 110 + HLA− → Neurotoxic (Mech 2)
                │                               > 200 → IH pattern or Mech 1 residual
                │
                └─ Inadequate washout or active use
                        → CSF orexin-A directly
                                < 110 → add HLA → NT1 vs Mech 2
                                110–200 → repeat in 3 months
                                > 200 → Mechanism 1; await washout, then MSLT
```

## Key Numbers

- MSLT minimum washout: **14 days** (AASM); **4–6 weeks** preferred for heavy chronic users
- CSF orexin NT1 threshold: **< 110 pg/mL** (87% sensitivity, 99% specificity for NT1)
- DAT near-normalization: **~14 months** abstinence (moderate users; Volkow et al.)
- HLA-DQB1*06:02 in NT1: **> 90%** prevalence; general population ~25%
- EDS resolution in Mechanism 1: **≥ 80%** by week 4; persistent beyond 3 months → formal workup
