# Novel Non-Stimulant Wake-Promoting Agents

Investigational and off-label agents targeting EDS in narcolepsy and idiopathic hypersomnia (IH) via non-monoamine pathways. Distinct from approved pharmacotherapy (modafinil, pitolisant, sodium oxybate, orexin receptor agonists).

---

## Endogenous GABA Potentiator Hypothesis

Core mechanistic framework: patients with IH (and some NT2) exhibit an endogenous CSF factor — proposed as a GABA-A–modulating somnogen (GABA-AMP) — that tonically potentiates GABA-A receptor activity. This drives EDS through excessive inhibitory tone rather than orexin deficiency. Agents below exploit this by antagonizing or reversing that potentiation.

---

## Clarithromycin (Off-Label)

**Class:** Macrolide antibiotic; acts as GABA-A negative allosteric modulator at CNS concentrations — mechanism is **not** antibiotic.

**Evidence:**
- Trotti et al. 2014 (J Psychopharmacol): 64% of patients showed clinical improvement; 38% continued long-term
- RCT data: 500 mg BID produced significant ESS, FOSQ, and SF-36 improvement vs placebo
- AASM 2021: conditional recommendation for IH; French guidelines do not endorse
- Ongoing large placebo-controlled mechanistic trial (NCT04026958) investigating GABA-A modulation, neuroinflammation, microbiome, and fMRI connectivity changes

**Dose:** 500 mg BID (narcolepsy/IH use)

**Key caveat:** CYP3A4 inhibitor — raises levels of many co-administered drugs (statins, benzodiazepines, some antiepileptics). Not FDA-approved for IH.

---

## Flumazenil (Benzodiazepine Receptor Antagonist)

**Class:** Competitive antagonist at benzodiazepine site of GABA-A receptor; blocks the somnogenic effect of the endogenous potentiator.

**Evidence:**
- Trotti et al. 2016: transcutaneous flumazenil patch produced significant ESS reduction in IH — proof-of-concept for GABA-AMP hypothesis
- Clinical experience with 153 patients (Rye et al. 2016): benefit in refractory hypersomnolence including some female narcoleptics unresponsive to stimulants
- IV and intranasal formulations also studied

**Status:** Not commercially available for this use; requires compounding or research access. Important as mechanistic validation, not a practical first-line option.

---

## BTD-001 (Pentylenetetrazol — Low Dose)

**Class:** GABA-A non-competitive antagonist; low-dose oral PTZ distinct from the convulsant doses used historically.

**Evidence:**
- Phase 2 trial (NCT02512588, NCT03542851): evaluated in IH and NT2; results inconclusive on primary endpoints but mechanistically coherent
- ARISE trial (Balance Therapeutics): signal in subjective sleepiness measures

**Status:** Development stalled; no current Phase 3. Establishes PTZ class as worth revisiting with better dose-finding.

---

## Mazindol / NLS-2 (Mazindol ER)

**Class:** DA/NE/5-HT reuptake inhibitor with partial OX2R agonist activity; Schedule III; preclinical TAAR1 interactions reported but OX2R partial agonism is the more validated differentiated mechanism.

**Evidence:**
- Used extensively in Japan for narcolepsy (Sanorex); small RCTs positive for EDS and cataplexy
- NLS-2 (Neurobiological Systems): Phase 3 RCT (NCT05914194) evaluating mazindol ER in NT1 — assessing EDS and cataplexy endpoints; low-dose 0.5–1 mg ER formulation
- Springer 2025 review: DA release profile resembles methylphenidate > amphetamine; OX2R partial agonism and 5-HT1A activity distinguish it from classic stimulants

**Status:** Phase 3 ongoing; not FDA-approved for narcolepsy. Available off-label in Japan.

---

## TRH Analogs — Taltirelin (Ceredist)

**Class:** Synthetic thyrotropin-releasing hormone analog; wake-promoting via histaminergic and noradrenergic activation independent of monoamine reuptake.

**Evidence:**
- Approved in Japan for spinocerebellar degeneration
- Animal models: robust wake-promoting effects at doses that avoid peripheral TRH thyroid effects
- Human narcolepsy data: limited; mechanistically interesting as entirely non-dopaminergic pathway

**Status:** Not available in the US. Investigational for narcolepsy; no current US trials. Proof-of-concept for histamine/NE wake-promotion without stimulant liability.
