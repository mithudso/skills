# NC Healthy Opportunities Pilots (HOP)

## Overview

The **NC Healthy Opportunities Pilots (HOP)** were a first-in-nation effort to use Medicaid dollars to directly address **health-related social needs (HRSN)** — social determinants of health — at scale. Launched in 2022, the pilots operated in three pilot regions until suspended in July 2025.

**Legal authority**: Section 1115 Medicaid demonstration waiver, with $650 million in federal funding and $100 million reserved for capacity building.

## The Four Service Domains

HOP addressed four social factors linked to health outcomes:

1. **Safe and stable housing** — rental assistance, housing navigation, emergency hotel stays, home repairs to address health hazards
2. **Healthy food** — food boxes (medically tailored meals), nutrition counseling, food pantry navigation
3. **Accessible transportation** — non-emergency medical transportation beyond standard Medicaid benefits, transit passes
4. **Violence remediation** — safety planning, counseling for survivors, community violence intervention

## How It Worked

- **Community-based organizations (CBOs)** provided the services — not traditional health providers
- CBOs were paid by **Prepaid Health Plans (Standard Plans and Tailored Plans)** as if they were health care providers
- 29 approved service types available
- A Medicaid member's health plan authorized services based on documented social needs
- **Network Leads** (regional intermediary organizations) contracted with CBOs, handled payment processing, and provided technical assistance

## Evidence of Effectiveness

Peer-reviewed research (PMC 2025) on HOP found:
- Medicaid members receiving HOP services had **lower Medicaid spending** than matched controls
- Especially strong results for housing and food interventions
- Evidence that addressing social needs reduces downstream medical costs

Harvard Public Health (2023) coverage: "NC Medicaid's social determinants of health efforts are working."

CMS Rapid Cycle Assessment (2023) found promising early implementation but noted challenges in CBO capacity and authorization processes.

## Suspension — July 2025

**Critical update**: Due to lack of funding in NC General Assembly budget bills (neither the Senate nor House budgets included continuation funding):

- **No new HOP service authorizations** after July 1, 2025
- **Existing HOP services ceased delivery** after July 1, 2025
- The pilots effectively **ended** in July 2025

This suspension is a major policy development — the Milbank Memorial Fund and other policy organizations have analyzed what this means for other states considering SDOH Medicaid programs.

## Legacy and National Significance

Despite suspension, NC HOP was the most ambitious Medicaid SDOH initiative in US history and produced the first large-scale evidence that Medicaid can fund non-clinical social services. Its model influenced:
- CMS's enhanced SDOH waiver framework
- Other states' 1115 waiver applications (California CalAIM, Massachusetts, etc.)
- Federal discussions about codifying HRSN benefits in Medicaid
