# Orexin Receptor Agonists in Development

## Core Concepts
- OX2R agonists target the root cause of NT1 (orexin neuron loss) rather than downstream symptoms — paradigm shift from symptomatic management
- Two receptor subtypes: OX1R (reward, stress, autonomic) and OX2R (wakefulness promotion, REM suppression); OX2R selectivity is the therapeutic goal
- Endogenous orexin peptides (OxA/OxB) cannot cross the BBB efficiently — small-molecule oral agonists required
- TAK-994 (first oral OX2R agonist) proved the concept but was discontinued; TAK-861 (oveporexton) is the lead successor with FDA NDA accepted Feb 2026
- Class differentiated from stimulants/oxybates by mechanism: restores physiological OX2R signaling rather than compensating with unrelated pathways

## Mechanisms / Biology
- OX2R is a Gq/Gi-coupled GPCR expressed at locus coeruleus, tuberomammillary nucleus, raphe nuclei, and basal forebrain — activation of these nodes sustains wakefulness independent of functioning orexin neurons
- OX1R engagement drives blood pressure elevation and anxiety-like behavior in rodents; OX2R selectivity preserves cardiovascular safety
- Small-molecule CNS penetrance requires low molecular weight and favorable topological polar surface area (TPSA) — key design constraint distinguishing TAK-861 from TAK-994
- TAK-994's hepatotoxicity was dose-dependent and attributed to structural features not shared by TAK-861's redesigned scaffold
- TAK-861 retains OX2R selectivity while demonstrating markedly reduced hepatic signal in Phase 2 and Phase 3 — structural optimization preserved efficacy while eliminating the primary safety liability

## Clinical Picture
- NT1 affects ~1 in 2,000 people; virtually all cases involve ≥90% loss of orexin-producing hypothalamic neurons via autoimmune destruction
- Current standard-of-care (modafinil, sodium oxybate, pitolisant) addresses EDS and/or cataplexy symptomatically; none restores orexin signaling
- Phase 2 TAK-994 data: significant cataplexy and EDS reduction vs. placebo; trial halted 2022 due to dose-dependent transaminase elevation and weight loss/tachycardia
- TAK-861 Phase 3 (FirstLight, n=168; RadiantLight, n=105): both studies met all primary and secondary endpoints at week 12 (p<0.001 across all doses)
- Most common adverse events with TAK-861: insomnia, urinary urgency/frequency — no serious treatment-related AEs reported

## Evidence & Treatment
- TAK-861 doses: 1 mg twice daily (low) and 2 mg twice daily (high); high dose achieved normative wakefulness (MWT ≥20 min) in the majority of participants
- ~85% of participants on 2 mg BID achieved ESS ≤10 (comparable to healthy controls) vs. typically <20% on placebo
- Weekly cataplexy rate reduced >80% median from baseline; median cataplexy-free days improved from 0 to 4–5 days/week
- FDA granted Breakthrough Therapy designation for EDS in NT1; NDA accepted Feb 2026 with Priority Review and PDUFA target Q3 2026
- Broader pipeline: Centessa Pharmaceuticals (ORX750 series), other undisclosed OX2R agonist programs in preclinical/Phase 1 — competitive validation of target

## Key Facts
- TAK-994 halted 2022: hepatotoxicity signal in Phase 2 despite best-in-class efficacy at the time
- TAK-861 Phase 3 enrollment across 19 countries completed within 6 months — reflects unmet need urgency
- PDUFA date: Q3 2026 (Takeda); if approved, first OX2R agonist and first disease-mechanism-targeting therapy for NT1
- Orexin neurons number ~70,000 in humans; NT1 patients lose >90%; replacement therapy bypasses neurons entirely via direct receptor agonism
- OX2R agonism achieves full wakefulness promotion; OX1R co-activation adds cardiovascular risk without incremental sleep-wake benefit
