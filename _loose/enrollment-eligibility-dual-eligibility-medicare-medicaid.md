# Dual Eligibility: Medicare and Medicaid

## Overview

Approximately 12 million Americans — called "dual eligibles" or "duals" — qualify for both Medicare and Medicaid. They tend to be the most clinically complex and highest-cost population in both programs. Managing their enrollment and benefit coordination is a major policy focus.

## Types of Dual Eligibility

### Full Dual Eligibles (True Duals)

Both Medicare and full Medicaid coverage.

**Typical profile:** Low-income elderly (65+) or non-elderly disabled individuals on both SSDI and SSI; nursing home residents; individuals with complex chronic conditions.

**Benefits they receive:**
- Medicare: hospital (A), medical (B), often Part D; all standard Medicare benefits
- Medicaid: covers Medicare cost-sharing (premiums, deductibles, copays); additional services Medicare doesn't cover (dental, vision, long-term services and supports/LTSS, personal care)

**Coordination:** Medicaid pays last — "payer of last resort." Medicare pays first; Medicaid covers remaining cost-sharing.

### Partial Dual Eligibles — Medicare Savings Programs (MSPs)

Individuals who qualify for Medicaid assistance with Medicare costs but may not qualify for full Medicaid benefits. Four programs:

| MSP | Income Limit (2025 approx.) | What It Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Qualified Medicare Beneficiary (QMB)** | ≤100% FPL (~$1,255/mo individual) | Medicare Part A & B premiums, deductibles, coinsurance, copays |
| **Specified Low-Income Medicare Beneficiary (SLMB)** | 100–120% FPL (~$1,255–$1,507/mo) | Part B premium only (~$185/mo) |
| **Qualifying Individual (QI)** | 120–135% FPL (~$1,507–$1,695/mo) | Part B premium only (limited slots; first-come basis) |
| **Qualified Disabled Working Individual (QDWI)** | ≤200% FPL, disabled, lost Medicare due to work | Part A premium only |

**QMB protection:** Providers CANNOT bill QMB beneficiaries for Medicare cost-sharing. This is a CMS rule; providers who bill QMBs can be sanctioned. Important: even if provider is non-participating Medicare provider, they cannot collect Medicare cost-sharing from QMBs.

**Applying for MSPs:** Apply through state Medicaid agency (county DSS in NC); same application as Medicaid; many states auto-identify MSP eligibility when processing Medicare beneficiary data.

## Extra Help / Low-Income Subsidy (LIS) for Part D

**What it is:** Federal subsidy reducing Part D prescription drug costs for low-income Medicare beneficiaries.

**Who qualifies:**
- Full dual eligibles: automatically fully subsidized (benchmark plan premium paid, minimal or no copays)
- MSP recipients (QMB, SLMB, QI): automatically partially subsidized
- Others at/below 150% FPL with limited resources

**Resource limits (2025 approximate):**
- Individual: ≤$17,220 in assets
- Couple: ≤$34,360 in assets
- (Excludes home, one car, burial funds)

**Application:**
- SSA Form SSA-1020 — Application for Extra Help
- Online: ssa.gov/medicare/part-d-extra-help
- Auto-enrollment for full duals and MSP recipients; no separate application needed

**Benefit:** Full subsidy covers benchmark premium; ~$1–$12 copays for generic/brand. Partial subsidy covers portion of premium and reduces cost-sharing.

## D-SNPs (Dual Eligible Special Needs Plans)

Medicare Advantage plans specifically designed for dual eligibles. Required to have a Medicaid coverage relationship.

**What D-SNPs offer:**
- Integrated Medicare and Medicaid benefits in one plan
- Care coordination services
- May include Medicaid benefits (varies by state contract)
- Supplemental benefits (OTC allowances, dental, vision, meals, transportation)

**Types (as of 2023+, SSBCI changes):**
- Fully Integrated Dual Eligible SNPs (FIDE-SNPs): integrate all Medicare and Medicaid benefits
- Highly Integrated Dual Eligible SNPs (HIDE-SNPs): intermediate integration
- Other D-SNPs: Medicare benefits only; Medicaid coverage separate

**NC:** NC has approved D-SNP plans operating in most counties through the NC Medicaid Managed Care structure.

**Enrollment:** Must have both Medicare (Parts A+B) and Medicaid. Can enroll during IEP, AEP, or using monthly SEP available to dual eligibles (eligible for Special Enrollment Period every month).

## Monthly SEP for Dual Eligibles

Dual-eligible individuals have a continuous monthly Special Enrollment Period to switch Medicare plans. This allows ongoing optimization of coverage as needs change.

## Coordination of Benefits for Duals

**Payment order:**
1. Medicare pays (primary)
2. Secondary insurance (if any)
3. Medicaid pays (last resort — fills remaining gaps)

**Challenges:** When providers don't bill Medicare first (billing only Medicaid), it creates improper payment risks. CMS has coordination of benefits contractor to detect and correct.

**Balance billing protections:** Full duals with QMB status cannot be balance billed by providers for Medicare covered services.

## Enrollment in NC for Dual Eligibles

- Full duals: enrolled in NC Medicaid Managed Care through Tailored Plans (if BH complexity) or Standard Plans; D-SNPs available
- MSP recipients: apply through county DSS; NC Medicaid auto-identifies many via data matches
- Extra Help: apply through SSA; NC automatically screens Medicaid applicants
