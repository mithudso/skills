# Medicare Home Health Benefit

Medicare covers home health care when specific conditions are met — and it's covered at **$0 cost-sharing** (except durable medical equipment). Many beneficiaries don't know they qualify.

## The Four Qualifying Criteria

All four must be met:

### 1. Homebound Status
**Definition:** Leaving home requires considerable effort because of illness or injury. Specifically, you must meet BOTH conditions:
- Leaving home is not recommended because of your condition, **OR** you have trouble leaving without help (cane, walker, wheelchair, special transport, another person's assistance)
- You are normally unable to leave home, and leaving takes a lot of effort

**What homebound does NOT mean:**
- Bedridden — you can still move around your home
- Never allowed to leave — occasional short absences are allowed for:
  - Medical appointments
  - Religious services
  - Adult day care
  - Occasional unique events (family milestones, etc.)
- Leaving for these purposes does not disqualify you as homebound

**Documentation:** Your physician must certify homebound status. The agency's OASIS assessment captures functional status.

### 2. Skilled Care Need
You must need one or more of these skilled services:

| Skilled Service | Examples |
|----------------|---------|
| Skilled nursing (RN or LPN) | Wound care, IV infusions, catheter management, diabetes management, post-surgical monitoring |
| Physical therapy (PT) | Rehabilitation after stroke, hip replacement, or injury |
| Occupational therapy (OT) | Training in activities of daily living, adaptive equipment |
| Speech-language pathology | Swallowing disorders, aphasia treatment |
| Medical social services | When skilled care is also needed |
| Home health aide | ONLY when skilled care is being provided concurrently |

**"Intermittent" requirement:** Care must be part-time or intermittent — not 24-hour daily care. Typical home health is a few visits per week.

**Custodial care alone does NOT qualify:** If you only need help with bathing, dressing, housekeeping, cooking, or companionship — with no skilled care need — Medicare does not cover home health.

### 3. Physician Order
A physician or, since the 2026 CY Final Rule expansion, a **nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or clinical nurse specialist** (who has had a face-to-face encounter with you) must certify your plan of care. The certifying clinician must have seen you in person within 90 days before starting home health or within 30 days of starting care.

**2026 change (CMS-1828-F):** CMS expanded who can perform the face-to-face encounter required to certify home health, removing a restriction that had sometimes delayed care starts.

### 4. Medicare-Certified Home Health Agency (HHA)
The agency providing care must be certified by Medicare. Verify certification at: medicare.gov/care-compare (Home Health Compare).

## What Medicare Covers Under Home Health

**At $0 cost-sharing:**
- Skilled nursing visits
- Physical, occupational, speech therapy visits
- Medical social services
- Home health aide visits (when skilled care is also ongoing)
- Medical supplies provided by the HHA as part of your plan of care

**At 20% coinsurance after deductible:**
- Durable medical equipment (DME) ordered as part of home health plan

## What Medicare Does NOT Cover Under Home Health

- 24-hour (custodial or live-in) care
- Homemaker services (cleaning, laundry, meal preparation)
- Personal care aide services when that is the ONLY type of care needed
- Transportation to medical appointments (this is separate)
- Prescriptions (covered under Part D)

## Payment and Episode Structure

**30-day payment periods:** Medicare pays HHAs a **base rate per 30-day period** (~$2,038.22 for CY2026), adjusted for patient acuity using the Patient-Driven Groupings Model (PDGM). The patient pays $0 for the home health visits themselves.

**60-day certification:** Your physician certifies your plan of care in 60-day periods. If you still need home health after 60 days, the physician recertifies.

**No limit on number of episodes:** As long as you continue to meet the eligibility criteria, Medicare will continue to cover home health — there is no fixed number of days or episodes limit.

## Home Health vs Other Medicare Benefits

| Feature | Home Health | SNF | Hospice |
|---------|------------|-----|---------|
| Homebound required? | Yes | No | No |
| Skilled care required? | Yes | Yes | No (comfort-focused) |
| Curative treatment? | Yes | Yes | No (waived for terminal illness) |
| Cost-sharing for visits? | $0 | Yes (Days 1-20 free; copay Day 21+) | ~$0 most services |
| Part A or B? | Both (B for OT; A for nursing if entered via hospital) | Part A | Part A |

## Common Questions

**"My doctor ordered home health after surgery — is there a waiting period?"**  
No waiting period. Coverage starts when you meet the criteria and are admitted by a certified HHA.

**"Can I get home health while also going to outpatient therapy?"**  
Potentially — but Medicare will scrutinize whether you truly need home-based care if you can leave for outpatient therapy. If you go to an outpatient clinic regularly, you may not qualify as homebound.

**"The home health agency said Medicare will only cover 8 visits — is that true?"**  
No fixed per-episode visit limit exists under Medicare. If you continue to need and qualify for skilled care, visits continue. If coverage is denied, you have appeal rights.

**"My condition is permanent — can I get home health indefinitely?"**  
Yes, if you continue to meet homebound and skilled care criteria. Some beneficiaries with chronic conditions receive home health for months or years with ongoing physician certification.
