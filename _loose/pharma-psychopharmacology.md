# Psychopharmacology

## Core Concepts
- Psychopharmacology studies drugs that alter CNS function to affect mood, perception, cognition, and behavior — both therapeutic and recreational/abuse contexts
- CNS drug development is complicated by blood-brain barrier (BBB) permeability requirements, receptor target complexity, and difficulty translating animal behavior to human psychiatric outcomes
- **Monoamine hypothesis** of depression: deficiency of serotonin (5-HT), norepinephrine (NE), and/or dopamine (DA) underlies depression; most current antidepressants increase monoamine availability (though this is an oversimplification — synaptic changes occur in hours; clinical effect takes weeks, implying downstream neuroplasticity)
- Most psychiatric drugs act via one of: receptor agonism/antagonism, reuptake inhibition, enzymatic inhibition, or ion channel modulation
- **BBB pharmacokinetics:** CNS drugs typically require lipophilicity, molecular weight <500 Da, low plasma protein binding of free fraction, and evasion of P-glycoprotein (P-gp/MDR1) efflux to achieve brain penetration

## CNS Neurotransmitter Targets

| System | Key receptors | Drugs targeting system |
|---|---|---|
| **Serotonin (5-HT)** | 5-HT1A (autoreceptor, anxiolytic), 5-HT2A (hallucination, antipsychotic), 5-HT3 (nausea), 5-HT reuptake transporter (SERT) | SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, MAOIs, buspirone (5-HT1A partial agonist), psilocybin/LSD (5-HT2A agonist), ondansetron (5-HT3 antagonist) |
| **Dopamine (DA)** | D1/D5 (Gs), D2/D3/D4 (Gi), DAT | Antipsychotics (D2 antagonist), bupropion (DAT inhibitor), ADHD stimulants (DAT/NET inhibitor), L-DOPA, dopamine agonists |
| **Norepinephrine (NE)** | α1/α2 (adrenergic), NET (reuptake transporter) | SNRIs, TCAs, atomoxetine, clonidine (α2 agonist), prazosin (α1 antagonist for PTSD) |
| **GABA** | GABA-A (Cl− channel), GABA-B (K+ channel) | Benzodiazepines (GABA-A PAM), barbiturates (GABA-A), Z-drugs (GABA-A), baclofen (GABA-B agonist), gabapentinoids |
| **Glutamate** | NMDA, AMPA, mGluR | Ketamine/esketamine (NMDA antagonist), memantine (NMDA antagonist for dementia), D-cycloserine (NMDA co-agonist) |
| **Acetylcholine** | Muscarinic (M1-M5), nicotinic (nAChR) | Anticholinergics (M antagonists), varenicline (nAChR partial agonist), galantamine/donepezil (AChE inhibitors) |
| **Opioid** | μ, δ, κ | Opioid analgesics, buprenorphine (μ partial agonist/κ antagonist), naltrexone (antagonist) |
| **Cannabinoid** | CB1 (CNS), CB2 (immune) | Dronabinol/nabilone (CB1 agonist), rimonabant (CB1 antagonist), CBD (indirect) |

## Antidepressants

**SSRIs (Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors)**
- Fluoxetine, sertraline, escitalopram, citalopram, paroxetine, fluvoxamine
- Block SERT → increased synaptic 5-HT; therapeutic lag (~2-4 weeks) reflects downstream neuroplasticity (BDNF, neurogenesis, synaptic remodeling)
- Generally first-line for MDD, anxiety disorders, OCD, PTSD; well-tolerated; sexual dysfunction and GI side effects common
- CYP interactions: fluoxetine/paroxetine potent CYP2D6 inhibitors; fluvoxamine inhibits CYP1A2/3A4/2C19

**SNRIs (Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors)**
- Venlafaxine, duloxetine, desvenlafaxine, levomilnacipran
- Dual SERT + NET inhibition; additional noradrenergic effects provide utility in pain (neuropathy, fibromyalgia) and some anxiety disorders
- Duloxetine: FDA-approved for MDD, GAD, DPNP, fibromyalgia, MSK pain

**TCAs (Tricyclic Antidepressants)**
- Amitriptyline, nortriptyline, imipramine, clomipramine, desipramine
- Inhibit SERT and NET + antihistamine (H1), anticholinergic (M), and α1 blocking; cardiac toxicity (QTc prolongation, AV block) limits use; narrow therapeutic index
- Clomipramine: most serotonergic TCA; first-line for OCD

**MAOIs (Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors)**
- Phenelzine, tranylcypromine (irreversible, non-selective); selegiline (selective MAO-B at low dose)
- Irreversible blockade of MAO-A (5-HT, NE, DA degradation) and MAO-B (DA, phenethylamine); significant dietary tyramine restriction required (hypertensive crisis — "cheese effect")
- Selegiline transdermal patch at low dose bypasses gut MAO — reduced dietary restriction required

**Atypical antidepressants**
- Bupropion: NE + DA reuptake inhibitor (NDRI); no serotonergic activity; also FDA-approved for smoking cessation; reduces seizure threshold
- Mirtazapine: α2 antagonist (increases 5-HT/NE release) + H1/5-HT2/3 antagonist; sedating, appetite-stimulating; useful in SUD withdrawal
- Trazodone: 5-HT2A antagonist + SERT inhibitor; sedating; used off-label for insomnia in SUD
- Vortioxetine: SERT inhibitor + 5-HT1A partial agonist + 5-HT3/7 antagonist; cognitive benefit signal

## Antipsychotics

**First-generation (FGA / typical):** Haloperidol, chlorpromazine, fluphenazine, perphenazine
- Primary mechanism: D2 antagonism in mesolimbic pathway (reduces positive symptoms)
- High EPS risk (tardive dyskinesia, parkinsonism, akathisia, dystonia) from nigrostriatal D2 blockade; elevated prolactin from tuberoinfundibular blockade
- Haloperidol: high potency (low dose, high EPS); chlorpromazine: low potency (higher dose, more sedation/anticholinergic)

**Second-generation (SGA / atypical):** Clozapine, risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine, aripiprazole, ziprasidone, lurasidone, asenapine
- D2 + 5-HT2A antagonism (or partial agonism) → reduced EPS, activity against negative/cognitive symptoms
- **Clozapine:** gold standard for treatment-resistant schizophrenia; agranulocytosis risk (1-2%) requires REMS monitoring; metabolic syndrome
- **Aripiprazole, brexpiprazole, cariprazine:** D2 partial agonists — lower metabolic risk, less sedation; cariprazine also D3 agonist (negative symptoms)
- Metabolic syndrome risk (weight gain, dyslipidemia, T2DM) worst with olanzapine/clozapine; quetiapine/risperidone intermediate; aripiprazole/ziprasidone least

## Mood Stabilizers
- **Lithium:** mechanism unclear — inositol monophosphatase inhibition, Wnt/β-catenin, neuroprotection; narrow TI (0.6-1.2 mEq/L therapeutic); renal clearance — monitor in NSAID/diuretic use; tremor, polyuria, hypothyroidism
- **Valproate (VPA):** GABA enhancement + Na+ channel blockade + histone deacetylase inhibition; weight gain, teratogenicity (neural tube defects), hepatotoxicity; broad-spectrum anticonvulsant + BD
- **Lamotrigine:** Na+ channel blockade + glutamate release inhibition; excellent BD depression; slow titration (SJS risk — especially with VPA); minimal cognitive effects; preferred in pregnancy
- **Carbamazepine/oxcarbazepine:** Na+ channel blockers; potent CYP3A4 inducers — significant DDIs

## Anxiolytics
- **Benzodiazepines (BZDs):** GABA-A positive allosteric modulators; increase Cl− conductance without activating GABA-A directly; effective for acute anxiety, alcohol withdrawal, seizures; high abuse liability, tolerance, physical dependence, dangerous respiratory depression in combination with opioids/alcohol; long-acting (diazepam, clonazepam) vs short-acting (lorazepam, oxazepam, temazepam)
- **Z-drugs:** zolpidem, zaleplon, eszopiclone — GABA-A PAM with sleep-specific indication; BZD1 receptor preference; still carry dependence/abuse risk
- **Buspirone:** 5-HT1A partial agonist; non-sedating, non-habit-forming; 2-4 week onset; first-line for GAD when abuse risk present
- **Gabapentinoids:** gabapentin, pregabalin — α2δ subunit voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channel blocker; reduce excitatory neurotransmitter release; off-label anxiolysis; abuse potential increasing (esp. gabapentin in opioid SUD)

## CNS Stimulants
- **Amphetamines** (amphetamine, mixed amphetamine salts, methamphetamine): reverse DAT/NET (release DA/NE) + inhibit reuptake; robust ADHD efficacy; Schedule II; cardiovascular and psychiatric risks; high abuse liability
- **Methylphenidate:** DAT/NET reuptake inhibitor (no release); robust ADHD efficacy; lower abuse liability than amphetamines in therapeutic use; Schedule II
- **Atomoxetine:** selective NET inhibitor (no DA effect); non-stimulant ADHD; no controlled substance scheduling; slower onset; useful in SUD patients requiring ADHD treatment
- **Modafinil/armodafinil:** wake-promoting; weak DAT inhibitor + histamine/orexin modulation; Schedule IV; lower abuse liability; used for narcolepsy, shift work, SUD crash symptoms

## Hypnotics/Sleep
- Z-drugs, BZDs (temazepam, triazolam), doxepin (H1 antagonist, FDA-approved 3-6mg for sleep), ramelteon (MT1/MT2 agonist, no abuse liability), suvorexant/lemborexant (dual orexin receptor antagonist — DORA)

## Psychedelics (Clinical Applications)
- **Psilocybin:** 5-HT2A agonist; FDA Breakthrough Therapy for TRD and SUD (alcohol, smoking); profound default-mode network (DMN) disruption; non-addictive; set/setting critical; MAPS/Johns Hopkins trials ongoing
- **MDMA-assisted therapy:** 5-HT release + direct 5-HT1A agonism; FDA review for PTSD; pro-social oxytocin release; not traditional pharmacotherapy — used as adjunct to psychotherapy sessions
- **Esketamine (Spravato):** NMDA antagonist; intranasal; FDA-approved for TRD (with oral antidepressant); REMS program (dissociation/abuse risk); rapid onset (hours vs weeks)
- **Ketamine infusion:** off-label IV; rapid antidepressant/anti-suicidal; AMPA receptor activation downstream mechanism; limited durability of single infusion

## Key Facts
- Therapeutic lag of antidepressants (~2-4 weeks) despite immediate receptor occupancy → argues for neuroplasticity (BDNF, synaptogenesis) as true antidepressant mechanism
- D2 occupancy 60-70% needed for antipsychotic effect; >80% → EPS risk; 90-100% → not more effective
- Benzodiazepines safe alone (respiratory depression rare); lethal in combination with opioids or alcohol — a major driver of OD deaths
- Clozapine requires REMS (Clozapine Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy) due to agranulocytosis — ANC monitoring mandatory
- Mood stabilizers in pregnancy: lithium (Ebstein's anomaly risk, low absolute risk), VPA (contraindicated — 10x neural tube defect risk), lamotrigine (relatively safer)
