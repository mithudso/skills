# Low-Intensity Focused Ultrasound (LIFU) Neuromodulation

## Core Concepts
- **LIFU** applies acoustic energy (typically 0.25–3 MHz) transcranially to reversibly modulate neural activity in both superficial and deep structures without surgical access
- **Two modes**: low-intensity pulsed FUS = reversible neuromodulation (milliseconds–minutes); high-intensity focused FUS (HIFU) = thermal ablation (irreversible lesioning)
- **Spatial precision**: focal spot as small as 0.5 mm diameter; penetration depth 2–12 cm — far exceeding TMS (1–3.4 cm) and allowing subcortical targeting without implants
- **Transcranial FUS** uses phased-array transducers with MRI or neuronavigation guidance to compensate for skull distortion and confirm targeting accuracy
- LIFU can also transiently open the blood-brain barrier (BBB) when combined with microbubbles, enabling focal drug delivery independent of direct neuromodulation

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **Primary mechanism**: acoustic radiation force and cavitation alter membrane mechanosensitive ion channels (TREK-1, Piezo1), modulating neuronal excitability bidirectionally (excitatory or inhibitory depending on pulse parameters)
- **Sonication parameters** govern effect direction: low duty cycles (<10%) and lower intensities (~0.5–1 W/cm² ISPTA) tend to inhibit; higher duty cycles and intensities tend to excite
- **Reward circuit targets**: NAc (nucleus accumbens) shell modulates dopaminergic reward salience; anterior insula encodes interoceptive craving and salience attribution — both are implicated in cue-induced relapse
- **Synaptic plasticity**: sustained post-sonication effects (up to 90 days in early trials) suggest LTP/LTD-like mechanisms and possible GABAergic/glutamatergic rebalancing rather than purely transient membrane effects
- No ionizing radiation; thermal effects at LIFU intensities are negligible (<0.1°C rise), distinguishing it from ablative FUS protocols used in ET/essential tremor thalamotomy

## Clinical Picture
- **Stage of development**: Phase 0–I; no FDA-approved LIFU indication for psychiatric/addiction use as of 2025; active trials at WVU Rockefeller Neuroscience Institute and others
- **Addiction feasibility trial (2023)**: N=6 SUD patients, bilateral NAc targets; 90 W active vs. sham; no serious adverse events; most common side effects — sleepiness, mild headache, scalp tingling (transient); MRI at 0 min/24 h/1 week showed no structural changes
- **Craving outcomes**: In the two participants receiving 90 W enhanced dose, Visual Analog Scale cue-induced craving decreased acutely during sonication and remained reduced at 90-day follow-up across multiple substances
- **Broader indications under study**: treatment-resistant depression, OCD, pain (insula), disorders of consciousness, Alzheimer's disease, essential tremor (ablative mode)
- **Regulatory gap**: no standardized LIFU protocol exists; parameter space (frequency, duty cycle, pressure amplitude, pulse duration) is large and under-mapped for psychiatric applications

## Evidence & Treatment
- **vs. TMS**: TMS reaches max ~3 cm depth, cannot target NAc or insula; LIFU achieves 2–12 cm with sub-mm precision; both are noninvasive but LIFU offers far superior deep-target access
- **vs. DBS**: DBS requires stereotactic neurosurgery, implanted leads, and ongoing hardware management; LIFU achieves comparable subcortical targets noninvasively and reversibly, with no infection or hardware risk
- **vs. transcranial electrical stimulation (tDCS/tACS)**: electrical methods lack spatial focality (<1 cm³ resolution); LIFU achieves focal modulation with anatomically verified targeting
- **Key 2023 pilot** (Frontiers in Psychiatry, Rezai et al.): first-in-world LIFU-NAc trial for SUD; demonstrated safety and acute craving reduction — landmark proof-of-concept establishing the translational pathway
- **Next steps**: RCT designs needed; open questions include optimal dose, session frequency, durability of effect, and whether craving reduction translates to abstinence or reduced use outcomes

## Key Facts
- LIFU focal spot: as small as **0.5 mm diameter**, penetration up to **12 cm** — vs. TMS maximum ~3 cm
- Pilot trial doses: **60 W and 90 W**; enhanced-dose (90 W) group showed sustained craving reduction at **90-day follow-up**
- No structural brain changes on MRI at any post-sonication timepoint (0 min, 24 h, 1 week) in the 2023 feasibility trial
- LIFU operates at **~0.5–3 W/cm² ISPTA** for neuromodulation vs. **>1000 W/cm²** for thermal ablation — a 3-log-scale intensity difference
- Frequency range for transcranial neuromodulation: **220 kHz – 1 MHz** (lower frequencies penetrate skull with less attenuation)
