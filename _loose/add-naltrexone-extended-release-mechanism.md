# Extended-Release Naltrexone (XR-NTX) Mechanism & Evidence

## Core Concepts
- XR-NTX (Vivitrol®): 380 mg naltrexone microspheres in poly-(d,l-lactide-co-glycolide) polymer matrix, administered by IM gluteal injection every 4 weeks
- FDA-approved for AUD (2006) and OUD relapse prevention (2010)
- Pure competitive antagonist — no agonist activity, no tolerance, no physical dependence, no diversion potential
- Overcomes the primary failure mode of oral naltrexone (50 mg/day): ~70% non-adherence rates within the first month
- Classified as an opioid antagonist (not a controlled substance); no DEA registration required to prescribe

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Competitive, high-affinity antagonist at mu-opioid receptors (Ki ~0.2 nM); also blocks kappa and delta receptors with lower affinity
- Blocks euphoric and analgesic effects of exogenous opioids; removes positive reinforcement signal driving compulsive use
- In AUD: attenuates alcohol-stimulated endogenous opioid release in the mesolimbic dopamine system, blunting cue-triggered craving
- Pharmacokinetic profile: transient peak ~2 hours post-injection, second peak at 2–3 days (~25 ng/mL), plateau ~12 ng/mL through day 7, gradual decline to 1–2 ng/mL by day 30; plasma naltrexone + 6-beta-naltrexol detectable ≥30 days
- Requires full opioid detoxification before initiation — opioid-dependent patients will experience precipitated withdrawal (minimum 7–10 days opioid-free for full agonists, 14+ days for methadone)
- 6-beta-naltrexol (primary metabolite) retains pharmacological activity, contributing to sustained receptor blockade

## Clinical Picture
- OUD: ~2.7 million Americans meet DSM-5 criteria; oral naltrexone historically underutilized due to adherence failure — monthly injection removes daily decision barrier
- AUD: ~29 million Americans affected; XR-NTX most effective in motivated, abstinent patients with strong family support
- Key barrier to XR-NTX initiation in OUD: mandatory opioid-free period creates induction gap, with high dropout and overdose risk during detox phase
- Injection site reactions in ~69% of patients (mostly mild); serious reactions requiring surgical debridement reported rarely (<1%)
- Contraindications: acute hepatitis or liver failure, current opioid dependence without detox, anticipated opioid analgesic need

## Evidence & Treatment
- **OUD pivotal trial (Krupitsky et al., 2011, Lancet):** RCT of 250 opioid-dependent adults in Russia; XR-NTX vs. placebo — confirmed opioid-free weeks 90% vs. 35%, confirmed abstinence 36% vs. 23% at 24 weeks
- **X:BOT trial (Lee et al., 2018, Lancet):** Head-to-head XR-NTX vs. buprenorphine-naloxone in 570 US adults — comparable relapse rates at 24 weeks among those successfully inducted; XR-NTX showed significantly higher induction failure (28% vs. 6%) due to detox gap
- **AUD pivotal trial (Garbutt et al., 2005, JAMA):** 627 patients — XR-NTX 380 mg reduced heavy drinking days by 25% vs. placebo; response rate 35.5% vs. 23% placebo
- SAMHSA guidelines include XR-NTX as a first-line MOUD alongside buprenorphine/naloxone and methadone
- Criminal justice populations benefit disproportionately: XR-NTX during incarceration-to-community transition reduces post-release overdose mortality (Lee et al., 2016)

## Key Facts
- Dose: 380 mg IM every 4 weeks (no dose adjustment for mild–moderate hepatic impairment; avoid in acute hepatitis)
- Liver enzyme elevations (AST/ALT): doses ≥6× standard oral doses caused hepatotoxicity in early trials; 380 mg IM is within safe range, but monitor LFTs
- Precipitated withdrawal risk window: avoid if any opioid use within 7 days (short-acting) or 14+ days (methadone)
- Overdose risk surge: opioid tolerance lost during abstinence; patients who relapse after stopping XR-NTX face dramatically elevated overdose lethality
- Cost: ~$1,800–$2,000/injection in US; Medicaid and most commercial plans cover with prior authorization
