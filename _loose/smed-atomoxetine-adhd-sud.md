# Atomoxetine in ADHD with Comorbid Substance Use Disorder

## Core Concepts
- Atomoxetine (Strattera) is the first FDA-approved non-stimulant for ADHD; approved 2002 for children, adolescents, and adults
- Classified as a selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (NRI/SNRI) — not a controlled substance, no DEA scheduling
- Key clinical advantage in SUD populations: zero misuse/diversion liability vs. Schedule II stimulants
- Delayed therapeutic onset of 4–6 weeks distinguishes it from stimulants (days); patients must be counseled on this
- Treats ADHD symptoms reliably; evidence for reducing substance use itself is modest and substance-specific

## Mechanisms / Neurobiology
- Selectively inhibits the norepinephrine transporter (NET) in the prefrontal cortex, increasing synaptic NE
- Downstream dopamine increase is limited to PFC (via NET co-transport), not nucleus accumbens — explains absent reinforcement/reward signal
- No meaningful dopamine transporter (DAT) blockade: this is the structural basis for no abuse potential
- Unlike stimulants, does not trigger mesolimbic dopamine surges; animal self-administration studies confirm no reinforcing properties
- Improves prefrontal executive function (working memory, impulse control) deficient in both ADHD and addiction
- Steady-state plasma levels require ~2 weeks; full ADHD symptom response typically at 4–6 weeks

## Clinical Picture
- ADHD and SUD co-occur at 2–3× the population rate; ~25–50% of adults in SUD treatment meet ADHD criteria
- ADHD onset precedes SUD in most cases; untreated ADHD accelerates initiation and severity of substance use
- In active SUD, ADHD symptoms and substance intoxication/withdrawal symptoms overlap (inattention, impulsivity), complicating diagnosis
- Stimulant prescribing in active SUD raises prescriber concern: diversion, misuse, interaction with stimulant-class drugs (cocaine, methamphetamine)
- Atomoxetine fills the gap when stimulants are contraindicated or clinically inadvisable

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Alcohol use disorder (AUD):** 3-month double-blind RCT (n=147) found atomoxetine superior to placebo for ADHD symptoms; inconsistent effects on drinking reduction; heavy-drinking days trended lower but did not reach significance
- **Cocaine use disorder:** Open-label 12-week pilot (n=20) showed ADHD symptom improvement; no significant reduction in cocaine use; useful for ADHD-targeted outcomes even in active use
- **Adolescent SUD:** RCT comparing atomoxetine+MI/CBT vs. placebo+MI/CBT found no significant advantage on ADHD or substance use outcomes — evidence base weakest in adolescents with active polysubstance use
- **General:** Atomoxetine consistently improves ADHD symptoms across SUD comorbidity; reducing addictive behavior itself is not reliably demonstrated — combination with SUD-specific therapy (CBT, motivational interviewing, MAT) is required
- CANMAT and WFSBP guidelines list atomoxetine as preferred first-line when SUD comorbidity is present and stimulants are judged risky; stimulant XR preparations show stronger ADHD efficacy magnitude in non-SUD populations

## Key Facts
- Onset: 4–6 weeks to full therapeutic effect (vs. days for stimulants)
- DEA Schedule: Not controlled (vs. Schedule II for amphetamines/methylphenidate)
- ADHD+SUD co-occurrence: ~25–50% of SUD treatment patients screen positive for ADHD
- AUD RCT: 147-patient trial; ADHD response significant, alcohol reduction inconsistent
- Stimulant XR efficacy in ADHD is modestly superior to atomoxetine in head-to-head trials (effect size ~0.8 vs. ~0.6), but the risk-benefit calculation shifts in active SUD
