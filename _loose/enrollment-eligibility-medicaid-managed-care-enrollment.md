# Medicaid Managed Care Enrollment

## How Medicaid Managed Care Works

Most Medicaid enrollees receive coverage through **managed care organizations (MCOs)** — private health plans that contract with states to deliver Medicaid benefits in exchange for a per-member per-month capitation payment.

**Plan types:**
- **MCOs (Managed Care Organizations)**: Full comprehensive care (most common)
- **PIHPs (Prepaid Inpatient Health Plans)**: Specialized carved-out services (often behavioral health)
- **PAHPs (Prepaid Ambulatory Health Plans)**: Ambulatory services only
- **PCCMs (Primary Care Case Management)**: Fee-for-service with care coordination

**Mandatory vs. voluntary enrollment**: Most states mandate managed care for most Medicaid populations; some populations (special needs, complex medical conditions) may have voluntary enrollment options.

## The Enrollment Process

### Choice Period

When a new enrollee is determined eligible for Medicaid in a state with managed care, they typically receive:
1. A **plan selection packet** (paper or online) with information on available plans in their area
2. A **choice period** to select a plan (varies by state; commonly 30–60 days)
3. Information on each plan's provider network, covered services, and quality ratings

During the choice period, the enrollee can:
- Select any available plan in their service area
- Contact an **independent choice counselor** (required by federal law since July 2018)
- Ask for help via phone, online portal, or in-person enrollment broker

### Auto-Assignment (If No Choice Is Made)

If an enrollee does not select a plan during the choice period, the state **auto-assigns** them to a plan. Federal regulations require auto-assignment to consider:
- Continuity of care (existing provider relationships)
- Geographic access
- Plan quality/performance data
- Distributing enrollment equitably among plans

**NC-specific:** NC Medicaid auto-assigns enrollees based on existing PCP relationships when possible.

## Switching Plans — Federal Rights

### 90-Day Without-Cause Change

Federal law (42 CFR 438.56) grants enrollees in **mandatory managed care** the right to change plans **without cause** (no reason needed) within **90 days of enrollment**. After the 90-day window, enrollees may change:
- **For cause** at any time (see below)
- During the annual open enrollment period if the state offers one (typically every 12 months)

**Exception — Florida**: Florida's choice period is **120 days** without cause.

### Good Cause Changes (Anytime)

Enrollees can request a plan change **at any time** for good cause, including:
- Their primary care provider leaves the plan
- They develop a health condition requiring specialists not in the plan's network
- Plan provides inadequate access to necessary services
- Poor quality of care
- Enrollment in the plan was the result of marketing violations

States must have a process for reviewing good cause requests; coverage must not lapse during review.

### Independent Choice Counseling

States must provide **free, independent choice counseling** to all new managed care enrollees and to enrollees switching plans. Choice counselors are neutral — they do not work for any managed care plan and cannot recommend specific plans for enrollment.

## NC Medicaid Managed Care: Standard and Tailored Plans

North Carolina launched Medicaid managed care in **July 2021** (Standard Plans) and **December 2022** (Tailored Plans).

### Standard Plans (for most Medicaid enrollees):
- Cover physical health, behavioral health, pharmacy, and long-term services and supports
- Plans operating in NC: Aetna Better Health, AmeriHealth Caritas NC, Blue Cross NC, Healthy Blue, UnitedHealthcare Community Plan, WellCare

### Tailored Plans (for members with complex behavioral health needs):
- For individuals with serious mental illness, intellectual/developmental disabilities, traumatic brain injury, or substance use disorders with complex needs
- Operated by former LME-MCOs:
  - **Alliance Health** (Durham, Wake, Cumberland, Johnston counties)
  - **Partners Health Management** (west-central NC)
  - **Trillium Health Resources** (eastern NC)
  - **Vaya Health** (western NC)
- Provide integrated physical + behavioral health + LTSS coordination

### NC Enrollment Process:
- Determined eligible through NC FAST
- Choose a Standard Plan (or be auto-assigned if in Tailored Plan territory based on clinical eligibility)
- NC offers a 90-day change window; contact BCBSNC or plan enrollment at 1-833-870-5500

## Populations Often Excluded from Managed Care Enrollment

- Medically complex children with special health care needs (may be exempt or have special enrollment options)
- Individuals in certain waiver programs
- American Indians/Alaska Natives (right to opt out of managed care in most states)
- Institutionalized individuals (may be excluded from standard MCO enrollment)

## Quality Considerations in Plan Selection

- Ask: Is my current doctor in the plan's network?
- Review the formulary for your medications
- Check behavioral health and specialty access if needed
- CMS star ratings and state HEDIS quality data are publicly available
- NC Medicaid publishes performance dashboards at medicaid.ncdhhs.gov
