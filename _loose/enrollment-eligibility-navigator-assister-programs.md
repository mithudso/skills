# Navigator and Assister Programs

## Why These Programs Exist

Health insurance enrollment — across Medicare, Medicaid, CHIP, and the ACA marketplace — is complex. Navigators and assisters provide free, impartial help to consumers navigating enrollment decisions, application processes, and eligibility questions.

## ACA Marketplace Navigators

**Legal basis:** ACA §1311(i); 45 CFR Part 155, Subpart F

**Who they are:** Community-based organizations (not insurance agents) funded through federal grants administered by CMS. Required in every state with a federally-facilitated marketplace (FFM).

**What they do:**
- Provide unbiased information about health coverage options (marketplace, Medicaid, CHIP)
- Assist with marketplace enrollment (HealthCare.gov)
- Screen for Medicaid/CHIP eligibility
- Help with plan comparisons, premium tax credit calculations
- Assist with appeals and enrollment errors
- Serve underserved and hard-to-reach populations (non-English speakers, rural, homeless)

**What they CANNOT do:**
- Recommend specific health plans (unbiased, not agents)
- Receive compensation from health insurers
- Make binding eligibility determinations (Medicaid determination stays with state agency)

**Training requirements:** Must complete CMS-approved Navigator training (typically 20–30 hours) annually; state-specific training required in some states.

**Funding:** Navigator grants available for states using HealthCare.gov. State-based marketplaces fund their own Navigator programs. Trump administration dramatically cut Navigator funding 2017–2020; Biden administration restored and expanded funding 2021–2024.

**Finding a Navigator:** localhelp.healthcare.gov → search by ZIP code

## Certified Application Counselors (CACs)

**What they are:** Individuals or organizations certified by a state or the Marketplace to assist with applications; typically embedded in hospitals, clinics, FQHCs, community organizations.

**CAC vs Navigator:** Both are free and impartial; CACs often work within health care delivery sites. Multiple types of certifying entities (states, Marketplace, some Medicaid agencies).

**Hospital CACs:** Hospital financial counselors often hold CAC certification — they assist uninsured patients with Medicaid and marketplace applications on-site.

## In-Person Assistance (IPA) / Enrollment Assisters

Umbrella term for various assister types in federally-facilitated marketplace states. Includes Navigators and CACs. May also include:
- **Community Health Workers (CHWs):** Medicaid/marketplace outreach in community settings
- **Federally Qualified Health Center (FQHC) enrollment staff:** FQHCs required to assist uninsured patients with enrollment

## Agents and Brokers (Different from Assisters)

**Important distinction:** Agents and brokers are LICENSED insurance intermediaries who can sell specific plans. They:
- Receive commissions from insurers
- Can help with marketplace enrollment but may be biased toward plans they sell
- Registered with marketplace and must complete training
- Appropriate when consumer wants plan recommendations + help enrolling

Navigators provide impartial options overview; agents/brokers help execute plan selection. Both free to consumer for marketplace enrollment.

## State Health Insurance Assistance Programs (SHIP)

**What SHIP is:** Federally funded (through Administration for Community Living) program in every state providing free, unbiased Medicare counseling. Every state has a SHIP program.

**SHIP counselors help with:**
- Explaining Medicare Parts A, B, C, D
- Comparing Medicare Advantage plans vs Original Medicare
- Comparing Part D prescription drug plans
- Identifying Extra Help/LIS eligibility
- Helping with Medicare Savings Programs (MSP)
- Reviewing Medicare bills (MSNs) for errors
- Filing appeals

**SHIP counselors do NOT:**
- Sell insurance
- Receive commissions
- Make eligibility determinations

**Funding:** State Medicaid/Medicare agencies + ACL grants. Counselors are typically volunteers with required training.

**Finding SHIP:**
- shiphelp.org → state-by-state directory
- Call Medicare: 1-800-MEDICARE → ask for SHIP referral
- NC SHIIP (Seniors' Health Insurance Information Program): 1-855-408-1212

## NC-Specific Resources

**NC Navigator Organizations (selected):**
- NC Navigator Consortium (consortium of community organizations)
- Community Care of NC (CCNC)
- NC Navigators at county health departments
- FQHCs statewide (Community Health Centers)

**NC SHIIP** (Medicare counseling): operated by NC Department of Insurance
- Free Medicare counseling statewide
- Phone, in-person at county senior centers and libraries

**NC Medicaid Outreach:**
- NC DHHS Medicaid outreach staff
- County DSS workers conduct outreach in community settings
- Application assisters at hospitals (required to assist uninsured patients in applying)

## Medicaid Presumptive Eligibility Workers

Specially trained workers at hospitals, FQHCs, and some other sites who can make same-day "presumptive eligibility" determinations — providing temporary Medicaid coverage immediately while full application is processed. Different from Navigators; they have more authority.

## Special Populations

**Non-English speakers:** Navigators required to provide services in non-English languages where there are significant populations. Language access is a federal civil rights requirement (Title VI).

**People with SUD/behavioral health:** Some CACs and outreach workers are embedded in SUD treatment facilities; NC Tailored Plan enrollment assistance available through LME-MCOs.

**Incarcerated individuals pre-release:** Some states have jail/prison-based Medicaid enrollment programs; NC has piloted pre-release Medicaid enrollment for incarcerated individuals nearing release.
