Treatment tracks directly from the mechanism identified at workup. SUD history cuts across all three as a medication-selection constraint.

## Mechanism 1 — Pharmacological Rebound (DA/NE Depletion)

**Timeline:** Days to 4–8 weeks post-cessation; self-limiting as monoamine stores recover.

**Primary:** Watchful waiting, sleep hygiene, fixed schedule. Most recover without pharmacotherapy by 2–4 weeks.

**If symptomatic:** Modafinil 100–200 mg AM or armodafinil 75–150 mg AM × 4–6 weeks. DAT inhibition supports wakefulness without relapse-relevant dopamine surges; no acute rebound hypersomnolence unlike amphetamines.

**Avoid:** Sodium oxybate (adds CNS depression; no benefit during natural DA recovery). Amphetamine-class: relapse risk + re-injury to recovering DA terminals. If hypersomnia persists beyond 6–8 weeks, reassign mechanism.

## Mechanism 2 — Orexin Neurotoxicity (Secondary Narcolepsy-Like)

**Timeline:** Partial recovery possible over 3–12 months if neurotoxicity was incomplete; longitudinal CSF orexin and neuroimaging may help gauge trajectory.

**First-line — EDS:** Modafinil 200 mg AM or armodafinil 150–250 mg AM. Effective for excessive daytime sleepiness; does not address the underlying orexin deficiency but provides symptomatic benefit with a tolerable safety profile in SUD patients.

**Preferred in SUD context — EDS + cataplexy-like symptoms:** Pitolisant 17.8–35.6 mg QAM. Histamine-3 inverse agonist; does not increase striatal dopamine; FDA-approved without DEA scheduling; drug-liking profile indistinguishable from placebo in stimulant-user studies. Lack of DA mechanism makes it the lowest-misuse-risk option across all three mechanisms. Addresses both EDS and cataplexy.

**For severe cataplexy or refractory EDS with SUD history:** Sodium oxybate requires individual benefit-risk discussion. Published case series support use in secondary narcolepsy (including TBI-related orexin deficiency); Schedule III, GHB pharmacology, rapid CNS onset — all increase misuse risk in SUD patients. If used: enroll in manufacturer REMS program, confirm stable recovery, establish close monitoring, prefer low-sodium oxybate (Xywav) to reduce total sodium load. Shared decision-making with patient is non-negotiable.

**Emerging — orexin replacement (investigational):** OX2R agonists (danavorexton IV, oveporexton oral, ALKS 2680) restore the sleep-wake circuit at receptor level — mechanistically ideal for orexin-neurotoxicity mechanism. Danavorexton outperformed modafinil on MWT (+11.1 points). No trials yet in post-meth secondary narcolepsy; oral prodrug TAK-994 discontinued for hepatotoxicity. Monitor pipeline.

**Avoid:** Amphetamine-class stimulants — relapse risk and potential re-injury to surviving orexin-adjacent circuits.

## Mechanism 3 — Unmasked Primary NT1/NT2

**Approach:** Full narcolepsy pharmacotherapy is appropriate; the meth history is context, not a contraindication to evidence-based treatment.

**First-line in SUD context:** Pitolisant (non-scheduled, no DA mechanism) — preferred as initial agent given SUD history. Modafinil/armodafinil remain effective alternatives with low but non-zero misuse potential.

**Sodium oxybate:** Evidence base is strongest here (primary NT1 with cataplexy). SUD history complicates but does not prohibit use. Prefer low-sodium formulation (Xywav). Requires REMS enrollment, stable SUD recovery, shared decision-making, and ongoing monitoring. Sodium oxybate abuse in narcolepsy patients without psychiatric comorbidity or polydrug use is extremely rare — but meth history elevates the risk profile.

**Amphetamine-class stimulants:** Avoid in meth SUD history regardless of mechanism.

## Cross-Cutting Considerations

**Misuse risk hierarchy (lowest → highest):** Pitolisant (unscheduled, no DA) → modafinil/armodafinil (Schedule IV) → solriamfetol (Schedule IV, use cautiously) → sodium oxybate (Schedule III, REMS required) → amphetamine-class (avoid in meth SUD).

**Coordination:** Narcolepsy pharmacotherapy must be coordinated with the SUD treatment team, documented in a shared care plan. Urine drug screens appropriate; medication-seeking escalation warrants mechanism reassignment and relapse evaluation.

**Sleep hygiene:** Fixed schedule and strategic napping (1–2 × 15–20 min) are first-order across all mechanisms; they reduce pharmacological burden and improve response.

**Diagnostic reassignment:** Mechanism assignment is not permanent. Hypersomnia persisting beyond 8 weeks or new cataplexy warrants reassessment and possible CSF orexin measurement.

> Educational reference only. Treatment decisions require licensed clinician evaluation. Sodium oxybate prescribing requires REMS enrollment.
