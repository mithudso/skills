# EBCI Tribal Option: NC's First-in-Nation Indian Managed Care Entity

## Overview

The **EBCI Tribal Option** is the nation's first **Indian Managed Care Entity (IMCE)** — a tribally operated Medicaid managed care plan created through a partnership between the NC Department of Health and Human Services (NCDHHS) and the **Cherokee Indian Hospital Authority (CIHA)**, the health system of the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians (EBCI).

Operational since **July 1, 2021** (beneficiary open enrollment ran March 15–May 14, 2021), the EBCI Tribal Option launched alongside NC Medicaid's broader statewide managed care transition. No other tribe in the United States had previously established a tribe-operated managed care entity within a state Medicaid program.

## The Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians

- **Enrollment**: Approximately **15,000 enrolled members**
- **Location**: Most members reside in the **Qualla Boundary** — a land trust area spanning five counties in western NC
- **Geographic scope**: Cherokee, Graham, Haywood, Jackson, and Swain counties
- **Governance**: EBCI is a federally recognized sovereign nation; CIHA is the tribe's health system, having taken over governance from the federal government in **2002** through the **Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act (ISDEAA)** Title V compact

## Cherokee Indian Hospital Authority (CIHA)

CIHA is the primary health system for EBCI members:
- Provides medical care for **13,000+ members**
- Approximately **4,000 Medicaid beneficiaries** in the service area
- Operates Cherokee Indian Hospital and associated clinics
- Recognized nationally for culturally competent, tribally-integrated care
- NCHA honored CIHA for innovative, culturally grounded health initiatives

## How the EBCI Tribal Option Works

### Eligibility
The EBCI Tribal Option is available to:
- Federally recognized **members of the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians**
- **Individuals eligible to receive Indian Health Services (IHS)** — including members of other federally recognized tribes residing in the service area
- Tribal Medicaid beneficiaries in the 5-county Qualla Boundary region

### Services Covered
The EBCI Tribal Option manages:
- **Primary care**: Immunizations, well-visits, sick visits, preventive care
- **Chronic disease management**: Diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and other chronic conditions prevalent in the EBCI population
- **Care management**: For high-need and high-risk members
- **Preventive health**: Wellness programs, health education
- **Added benefits**: Education vouchers and culturally specific wellness programming

### Behavioral Health and SUD — Vaya Health Role
Mental health and substance use disorder (BH/SUD) services are coordinated through **Vaya Health** (the LME-MCO serving western NC), which provides care coordination for BH/SUD services for EBCI Tribal Option members. This arrangement maintains tribal cultural alignment while leveraging the specialized BH/SUD network Vaya has built.

## Cultural Foundation

The EBCI Tribal Option is built on Cherokee cultural values, specifically the principle of **_U wa shv u da nv te lv_** (giving from the heart). Leadership describes the approach as treating patients "like my own family" — a reflection of the tribe's integrated view of health, community, and spirituality.

This cultural framing distinguishes the IMCE from standard managed care entities, which are typically corporate health plans optimizing for financial and clinical metrics without cultural specificity.

## Tribal Sovereignty and Medicaid Policy Significance

The EBCI Tribal Option operates at the intersection of:

- **Federal Indian health law**: Tribes have rights under ISDEAA to compact federal health programs, including IHS
- **State Medicaid law**: NC's managed care transition required a separate track for tribal members
- **Tribal sovereignty**: The EBCI insisted on tribal self-governance over health care delivery for their members, not assignment to a commercial plan

The model has attracted national attention as a template for other tribes seeking to establish tribal managed care entities within state Medicaid programs.

| Population | General Medicaid enrollees | EBCI members and IHS-eligible individuals |
