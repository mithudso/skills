# NC FQHCs and Community Health Centers

## Overview

North Carolina's **42 Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs)** and Look-Alikes (LAs) form a core component of the state's safety-net health system. They are operated by non-profit, consumer-governed organizations and collectively:

- Serve nearly **500,000 patients** annually
- Operate **350+ clinical sites**
- Cover **64–85 of NC's 100 counties**
- Provide care regardless of ability to pay (sliding fee scale)

The **NC Community Health Center Association (NCCHCA)** serves as the collective voice for these centers.

## What FQHCs Are

FQHCs are federally designated health centers that:
- Receive **enhanced federal funding** under Section 330 of the Public Health Service Act (HRSA grants)
- Must meet federal requirements: serve underserved areas/populations, offer sliding-fee scale, have a patient-majority governing board, offer comprehensive primary care
- Receive **cost-based Medicaid reimbursement** (see below)

## Services Offered

NC FQHCs provide **integrated care** in one location:
- Primary medical care (adult, pediatric, OB/GYN)
- Dental services
- Mental health counseling
- Substance abuse treatment / MAT (many sites offer buprenorphine)
- Pharmacy services (often 340B drug pricing)
- Case management and care coordination
- Patient education
- Enabling services: transportation assistance, translation, health literacy support

## Medicaid Reimbursement: PPS-APM

FQHCs receive a special Medicaid reimbursement rate — **Prospective Payment System Alternate Payment Methodology (PPS-APM)**:

- Set at **113% of Medicaid Allowable Costs** based on each provider's 2021 cost report
- Provider-specific rate — each FQHC has its own APM rate
- **Standard Plans**: reimburse APM rate for dates of service on/after **August 1, 2024**
- **NC Medicaid Direct (FFS)**: New methodology operationalized **May 1, 2024**
- **Tailored Plans**: APM wrap payment methodology updated July 2025

This ensures FQHCs are not financially penalized for serving high-cost, complex patients.

## 340B Drug Pricing

Most NC FQHCs participate in the **340B program**, allowing them to purchase outpatient drugs at significantly discounted prices. This cross-subsidizes care for uninsured patients and extends organizational capacity.

## Rural Health Clinics (RHCs)

Similar to FQHCs but serving rural underserved areas; receive cost-based Medicaid reimbursement under the same APM framework. Many rural NC counties are served by RHCs where FQHCs are not present.

## Role in NC Medicaid Managed Care

Under managed care:
- FQHCs participate in **Standard Plan** networks as primary care sites
- FQHCs can be designated as **patient-centered medical homes**
- Their APM rate is a "wrap payment" — Standard Plan pays standard rate, NC Medicaid pays the difference up to the APM rate
- FQHCs are critical access points in rural and underserved counties where private providers are scarce

## Access Challenges

- **Geographic gaps**: Not all rural counties have an FQHC or RHC — 36 counties have limited FQHC presence
- **Workforce shortages**: Vacancies in physicians, dentists, mental health providers
- **Dental access**: Dentist shortages at FQHCs contribute to NC's oral health disparities (see Medicaid dental expansion)
