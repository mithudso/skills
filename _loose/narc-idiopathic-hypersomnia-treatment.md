# Idiopathic Hypersomnia Treatment

## FDA-Approved Agent: Low-Sodium Oxybate (Xywav)
- **Approval**: August 2021 — first and only FDA-approved treatment specifically for IH in adults; approval was via priority review
- **Formulation**: Calcium, magnesium, potassium, and sodium oxybates (92% less sodium than Xyrem); low-sodium formulation developed to reduce cardiovascular sodium burden in patients using nightly dosing
- **Mechanism**: GHB/GABA-B agonist; consolidates nighttime sleep and reduces sleep pressure on subsequent day; addresses sleep inertia directly — mechanism distinct from wake-promoting stimulants
- **Phase 3 REST-IH trial**: Randomized-withdrawal design (12-week open-label optimization → 2-week double-blind withdrawal); primary endpoint ESS improved ~4.7 points vs placebo (p<0.0001); secondary endpoints Patient Global Impression of Change and IH Severity Scale both significant (p<0.0001); sleep inertia domain specifically improved
- **Dosing**: Once- or twice-nightly; titrated from 6 g/night; REMS program required (same as Xyrem)
- **IH-specific note**: No cataplexy to treat; nightly oxybate targets the next-day sleep inertia and EDS burden rather than REM-instability as in narcolepsy

## Off-Label GABA-Targeted Agents
**Clarithromycin** (Trotti et al. 2014, JCSM)
- Dose: 500 mg BID
- Mechanism: Negative allosteric modulator of GABA-A receptor; counters the endogenous CSF somnogen (small peptide that allosterically potentiates GABA-A, enhancing inhibitory tone in arousal circuits); antibiotic effect is pharmacological, not antimicrobial
- Evidence: Small RCT + observational data; ~60–70% subjective improvement in GABA-somnogen-positive patients; primary RCT outcome not statistically significant at group level; individual responders can be striking
- Limitation: Long-term antibiotic use carries microbiome/resistance concerns; reserved for patients with confirmed GABA-pathway phenotype

**Flumazenil** (Rye et al. 2012, *Science Translational Medicine*)
- Mechanism: GABA-A competitive antagonist; reverses the somnogen-enhanced inhibitory tone acutely
- IV formulation: Dramatic but transient awakening (minutes to hours); used diagnostically to confirm GABA-pathway hypersomnia
- Sublingual compounded formulation: Off-label, used for longer-duration effect; bioavailability variable; no FDA approval for hypersomnia
- Evidence base: 7-patient pilot (Rye 2012); 153-patient clinical experience series (Trotti/Rye 2016, JCSM); no randomized controlled trial; considered experimental

## First-Line Wake-Promoting Agents (No IH-Specific Approval)
**Modafinil / Armodafinil**
- AASM guideline recommended for IH EDS (Level B); most prescribed agents despite no IH-specific indication
- Dose: Modafinil 200–400 mg/day; armodafinil 150–250 mg/day
- Treats EDS but does NOT address sleep inertia — a critical gap given sleep inertia is a defining IH symptom
- Well-tolerated; preferred over stimulants for initial therapy due to lower abuse potential

## Second-Line Stimulants
- Methylphenidate (20–60 mg/day), mixed amphetamine salts, dextroamphetamine — used off-label
- Modestly effective for EDS; minimal evidence for sleep inertia benefit
- Schedule II; addiction/tolerance considerations apply
- Useful when modafinil inadequate or not tolerated

## Pitolisant (Wakix)
- H3 receptor inverse agonist/antagonist; enhances histaminergic, noradrenergic, and dopaminergic wake-promoting signaling
- EU approval 2021 for IH; US status: off-label for IH (FDA-approved for narcolepsy EDS/cataplexy only)
- Evidence: Positive open-label IH data; phase 3 IH-specific trial data emerging
- Non-scheduled; no abuse potential; useful when stimulants problematic

## Pipeline
- **TAK-861**: Orexin-2 receptor agonist in IH trials — rationale is unclear since IH lacks orexin deficiency; hypothesis is pharmacological orexin signaling may still amplify arousal
- **Serdexmethylphenidate** (Azstarys): prodrug stimulant; IH trials ongoing
- **ALKS 2680, ORX-750**: Additional orexin-2 agonists in pipeline across central hypersomnias

## IH vs Narcolepsy: Treatment Distinctions
| Factor | IH | Narcolepsy |
|---|---|---|
| Cataplexy to treat | None | NT1: sodium oxybate, pitolisant, venlafaxine |
| Orexin replacement | No benefit (orexin normal) | Experimental in NT1 |
| Sleep inertia | **Primary target** — Xywav addresses this | Less central |
| Nap strategy | Limited benefit; often unrefreshing | NT1/NT2: scheduled short naps often refreshing |
| Sodium oxybate | FDA-approved via Xywav | FDA-approved via Xyrem/Lumryz |

## Sleep Hygiene & Behavioral Management
- Consistent wake time; light therapy on waking to reduce sleep inertia
- Scheduled naps provide limited relief vs narcolepsy (unrefreshing quality); not a cornerstone as in NT management
- Gradual wake protocols (alarm staging) help sleep inertia severity but do not substitute for pharmacotherapy
- Caffeine adjunct: common, modest effect on EDS

## Key Facts
- Xywav is the only FDA-approved IH-specific treatment (2021); all others are off-label
- REST-IH used randomized withdrawal (not parallel) design — ethical given prior open-label responders
- Sleep inertia is the IH symptom least addressed by stimulants/modafinil; oxybate is the only agent with evidence for it
- Clarithromycin and flumazenil are GABA-pathway probes as much as treatments; response confirms endozepine/somnogen phenotype
- IH lacks orexin deficiency, so orexin-pathway drugs (suvorexant, lemborexant) are not therapeutic targets
