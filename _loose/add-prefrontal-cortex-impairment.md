# Prefrontal Cortex Impairment in SUD

## Core Concepts
- **Top-down inhibitory control**: PFC (especially dlPFC and OFC) exerts "executive brake" over limbic/striatal drug-seeking circuits; SUD progressively erodes this brake
- **Hypofunction**: Chronic substance exposure reduces baseline metabolic activity and gray matter volume in PFC, measurable via fMRI/PET even during abstinence
- **OFC vs dlPFC roles**: OFC encodes outcome value and cost-benefit weighting; dlPFC drives working memory, impulse inhibition, and rule-based decision-making — both are degraded in SUD
- **Allostatic shift**: Repeated drug cycles recalibrate the PFC set-point, making drug cues hyper-salient while suppressing non-drug rewards (opponent process)
- **Compulsivity threshold**: OFC hypofunction specifically predicts continued use despite adverse consequences — the defining feature of SUD (DSM-5 criterion)

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **Dopamine depletion in PFC**: Chronic stimulant/opioid/alcohol exposure down-regulates D1/D2 receptors in PFC and reduces dopaminergic tone, impairing working memory and response inhibition
- **GABAergic microcircuit disruption**: Parvalbumin interneurons in mPFC lose perineuronal net integrity under chronic alcohol, weakening local inhibitory gating of pyramidal output
- **Glutamatergic dysregulation**: Reduced mPFC→nucleus accumbens glutamate release disrupts goal-directed over habitual behavior; reinstatement of drug-seeking maps onto this pathway
- **HDAC5 epigenetics**: Histone deacetylase 5 in prelimbic PFC suppresses context-associated drug-seeking; chronic cocaine blunts this epigenetic brake, prolonging reinstatement vulnerability
- **Network-level effects**: PFC hypofunction propagates to default mode, salience, and executive control networks; resting-state fMRI shows reduced PFC-striatal functional connectivity proportional to severity
- **Insula-PFC dysregulation**: During craving/anticipation stage, insula hyperactivation combined with PFC hypoactivation produces the interoceptive urgency that overrides deliberative control

## Clinical Picture
- **Neuroimaging signature**: PET studies show 15–20% lower glucose metabolism in OFC and PFC of cocaine-, opioid-, and alcohol-dependent individuals vs. controls; persists 3–4 months into abstinence
- **Cognitive deficits**: Impaired Iowa Gambling Task performance (OFC), reduced Go/No-Go inhibition (dlPFC), and deficient reversal learning are robust cross-substance findings
- **Impulsivity correlation**: Trait impulsivity scores (BIS-11) correlate inversely with dlPFC gray matter volume; pre-existing impulsivity is also a vulnerability marker, not only a consequence
- **Opioid use disorder specifics**: OUD patients show PFC-linked social impairment tied to childhood trauma history; impulsivity mediates OFC→social dysfunction pathway
- **Partial recovery**: Structural and functional PFC deficits show partial recovery after 12–18 months of sustained abstinence; recovery is substance-dependent (alcohol > stimulants > cannabis)

## Evidence & Treatment
- **rTMS/tDCS targeting dlPFC**: High-frequency rTMS (10 Hz) to left dlPFC reduces craving and use in tobacco, stimulant, and opioid SUD; meta-analyses report moderate effect sizes (d ≈ 0.4–0.6); tDCS shows similar network-level connectivity improvements
- **Naltrexone**: Restores PFC-mediated cue-reactivity suppression in AUD; fMRI studies show normalized OFC activation to alcohol cues at 4 weeks
- **N-acetylcysteine (NAC)**: Restores mPFC→accumbens glutamate homeostasis in animal models; Phase II trials in cannabis and cocaine SUD show reduced craving; Phase III results mixed
- **Cognitive training**: Working memory and inhibitory control training modestly improves dlPFC recruitment on task fMRI and reduces relapse rates in early abstinence (12-week programs)
- **Psilocybin**: Increases parvalbumin interneuron activity in mPFC and reduces reward-seeking behavior in rodent models; Phase II trials in AUD/tobacco show sustained abstinence gains potentially linked to PFC normalization

## Key Facts
- Baseline PFC glucose metabolism is ~15–20% lower in SUD vs. controls on PET (Volkow et al.)
- OFC hypofunction predicts compulsive use (continued use despite consequences) specifically; dlPFC hypofunction predicts impulsivity and cue-reactivity
- PFC structural deficits (gray matter loss) are detectable after ~2 years of heavy use for alcohol; earlier for methamphetamine
- Left dlPFC rTMS is the most-studied neuromodulation target; 10 Hz preferred over 1 Hz for SUD applications
- Partial PFC recovery begins at ~3 months abstinence; functionally meaningful recovery requires 12–18 months sustained sobriety
