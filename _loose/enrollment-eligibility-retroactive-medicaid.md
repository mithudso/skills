# Retroactive Medicaid Eligibility

## What Is Retroactive Medicaid?

Federal law has traditionally required states to provide Medicaid coverage for up to **3 months prior** to the month of application — as long as the individual was eligible during that retroactive period. This is commonly called the "3-month retroactive period" or "3-month lookback."

**Legal basis:** 42 U.S.C. §1396a(a)(34); 42 CFR §435.915

## How the Retroactive Period Works

**Application month:** If you apply in June 2025 and are approved:
- Coverage begins: up to March 2025 (3 months before June)
- Condition: you must have been Medicaid-eligible during those prior months (income, citizenship, etc.)
- Bills incurred March–May 2025 may be covered if the provider is enrolled in Medicaid

**Retroactive period eligibility is determined separately** from prospective eligibility — income in March may differ from income in June.

**Process:**
1. Applicant or hospital social worker requests retroactive coverage consideration
2. Agency reviews eligibility for each retroactive month
3. If approved, state notifies providers; providers must submit claims within applicable timely filing limit
4. Providers cannot balance-bill the patient for covered services during retroactive period

## When Retroactive Medicaid Matters Most

### Hospital Emergencies
- Person presents to ER without insurance; large inpatient bill accumulates
- Social worker initiates Medicaid application during hospitalization
- If approved, retroactive coverage can cover the entire hospital stay
- Estimated 15–20% of Medicaid approvals involve retroactive hospital coverage

### SUD Treatment Episodes
- Person in crisis enters detox or residential treatment
- Enters treatment uninsured, treatment facility initiates Medicaid application
- If retroactive coverage approved, the treatment episode is covered
- Particularly important for residential SUD treatment in NC (Tailored Plans)

### Pregnancy and Childbirth
- Pregnant woman applies late in pregnancy or postpartum
- Retroactive coverage can cover prenatal care and delivery
- Pregnancy Medicaid often processed on expedited basis

### Long-Term Care / Nursing Home
- Family applies for Medicaid after elder has been in nursing home for months
- Retroactive period can cover up to 3 months of nursing home costs
- Critical for nursing home Medicaid planning (though assets/spend-down also relevant)

## State Waivers of Retroactivity

States may request CMS approval to waive or limit retroactive eligibility via:
- 1115 demonstration waivers
- State plan amendments (for some expansion groups)

**States that have eliminated or limited retroactivity (as of 2024-25):**
- Several states waived retroactivity for the Medicaid expansion group (adults 19–64)
- States with 1115 waivers limiting retroactivity include: Arizona, Arkansas, Indiana, Iowa, Montana, and others
- Most states retain retroactivity for children, pregnant women, and SSI-related groups even when waived for expansion adults

**NC status:** North Carolina has NOT waived retroactivity for the expansion group as of 2026.

## Retroactivity and Spend-Down

For non-MAGI (medically needy) groups, retroactive coverage interacts with spend-down:
- Individual may be eligible for retroactive coverage only after spending down income during the retroactive months
- Incurred medical bills (including those being retroactively covered) can help meet the spend-down
- Complex calculations; often requires caseworker assistance

## Presumptive Eligibility and Retroactivity

Presumptive eligibility (hospital-based temporary determination) provides immediate coverage but does NOT create retroactive Medicaid coverage for services before the presumptive eligibility determination date. Full retroactive eligibility requires the complete application process.

## Practical Steps to Claim Retroactive Medicaid

1. **Apply promptly** — retroactive window runs from application month back; longer delays reduce the retroactive window
2. **Request retroactive consideration explicitly** on the application or at intake interview
3. **Document income for prior months** — separate from current income if circumstances changed
4. **Alert providers** — notify hospital or treatment facility to hold claims pending Medicaid determination
5. **Providers:** verify timely filing rules — each state Medicaid program has claims filing deadlines; retroactive approval doesn't override timely filing clock (though many states provide extensions for retroactive approvals)

## Key Limitation

Retroactive Medicaid only covers services from **Medicaid-enrolled providers**. If the emergency room or treatment facility is not enrolled in the state's Medicaid program, retroactive coverage does not apply even if the individual was eligible. Out-of-network non-enrolled providers cannot receive retroactive Medicaid payment.
