# Methamphetamine in Pregnancy and Neonatal Outcomes

## Core Concepts
Meth is the fastest-growing obstetric drug problem in the US. Prevalence is underestimated due to underreporting, but hospital discharge data show sharp increases since 2010, with meth now surpassing opioids as the predominant illicit stimulant in prenatal toxicology screens in western states. Meth crosses the placenta and BBB freely; its sympathomimetic and dopaminergic actions are the mechanistic root of almost every adverse outcome.

## Mechanisms — Placental and Fetal
- **Vasoconstriction**: catecholamine surge → uteroplacental insufficiency → IUGR, reduced fetal O₂/glucose delivery
- **Placental abruption**: hypertensive surges + vascular fragility → ~2–3× increased risk
- **Preterm labor**: uterine irritability from sympathetic activation; preterm birth rates 2× background
- **Cardiac teratogenesis**: first-trimester exposure associated with ventricular septal defects and conotruncal anomalies (biological plausibility: dopamine/adrenergic receptor expression in embryonic heart)
- **Brain development**: meth alters monoamine systems during critical windows → reduced striatal and frontal volumes; microstructural white-matter changes on DTI; limbic system and thalamus disproportionately affected

## Clinical Picture — Neonatal Withdrawal
Neonatal meth withdrawal is **distinct from opioid NAS**: mediated by dopamine/norepinephrine dysregulation rather than opioid receptor down-regulation.
- Onset: typically within 24–48 h (vs. 48–72 h for opioid NAS)
- Symptoms: tremors, excessive crying, poor feeding, sleep disturbance, hypertonia, diaphoresis
- Course: **shorter** than opioid NAS — usually resolves within 1–2 weeks without pharmacotherapy
- Finnegan/MOTHER NAS tools are validated for opioids, not meth; no standardized meth-specific scoring tool exists
- Pharmacotherapy rarely required; non-pharmacological care (swaddling, low-stimulation, kangaroo care) is first-line

## Evidence & Outcomes
**Neonatal**: Low birth weight, low Apgar scores, NICU admission, stillbirth risk elevated; neonatal mortality increased in adjusted analyses (AJOG 2024 cohort).

**Neurodevelopmental (IDEAL study and others)**:
- 3.5 years: poorer visual-motor integration, reduced sustained attention
- 5.5 years: abnormal attention processing, modestly increased ADHD risk
- 7.5 years: deficits in delayed verbal memory, delayed spatial memory, math and language achievement; autism spectrum traits reported
- Neuroimaging: reduced striatal/frontal/thalamic volumes; altered neurometabolites on MRS
- Effects persist after controlling for postnatal environment, though adversity compounds risk

**Breastfeeding**: Meth concentrates in breast milk (milk:plasma ratio ~3.5); **not recommended** regardless of dose or timing of last use.

## Treatment of MUD in Pregnancy
- **No FDA-approved pharmacotherapy** for methamphetamine use disorder (MUD)
- **Contingency management (CM)**: strongest evidence — abstinence-contingent incentives achieve 2–3× higher urine-negative rates; endorsed by SAMHSA for pregnant patients
- **MOUD caution**: buprenorphine/methadone are for opioid co-use only; prescribing for meth alone is off-label with no efficacy data
- **Bupropion + naltrexone** (ADAPT-2 trial): modest benefit in non-pregnant adults; safety data in pregnancy absent
- Integrated OB + SUD care models (co-located prenatal/addiction services) improve retention and neonatal outcomes

## Key Facts
| Parameter | Meth NAS | Opioid NAS |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| Mechanism | DA/NE dysregulation | Opioid receptor down-regulation |
| Onset | 24–48 h | 48–72 h |
| Duration | 1–2 weeks | 2–6+ weeks |
| Pharmacotherapy | Rarely needed | Often needed (morphine, methadone, buprenorphine) |
| Scoring tool | None validated | Finnegan/MOTHER |
