# Pitolisant H3-Antagonist Mechanism

## Core Concepts
- Pitolisant (Wakix) is a first-in-class, selective histamine H3 receptor (H3R) **antagonist/inverse agonist** — it both blocks H3R and reverses its constitutive activity
- H3R is a **presynaptic autoreceptor** on histaminergic neurons in the tuberomammillary nucleus (TMN) of the posterior hypothalamus; tonic activation suppresses histamine synthesis and release
- By blocking H3R, pitolisant disinhibits histaminergic neurons → increases endogenous histamine in cortical and subcortical wake-promoting circuits
- H3Rs also serve as **heteroreceptors** on non-histaminergic neurons; H3R blockade secondarily enhances release of acetylcholine, dopamine, and norepinephrine
- Only FDA-approved narcolepsy treatment that is **not DEA-scheduled** (non-controlled substance) — no direct monoamine reuptake inhibition or receptor agonism driving abuse liability

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Histamine neurons fire tonically during wakefulness and are silent during NREM/REM sleep; H3 autoreceptor activation is a brake on this system — pitolisant releases that brake
- Inverse agonism stabilizes H3R in its inactive state, producing greater sustained histamine tone than neutral antagonism alone
- Secondary ACh enhancement via heteroreceptor blockade contributes to vigilance and attention effects distinct from pure histamine promotion
- Pitolisant does not directly bind hypocretin/orexin receptors; it compensates downstream for orexin deficiency seen in Type 1 narcolepsy
- Abuse potential study (Sleep 2020) found Drug Liking scores for pitolisant statistically indistinguishable from placebo; significantly lower than phentermine comparator — attributable to absence of direct dopamine reuptake inhibition

## Clinical Picture
- Approved indications (adults): excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) in narcolepsy; cataplexy in narcolepsy
- Pediatric indication: FDA approved June 2024 for EDS and cataplexy in patients ≥6 years
- Narcolepsy Type 1 patients have 85–95% loss of orexin neurons; pitolisant provides wake-promotion without requiring intact orexin signaling
- Clinical response seen across narcolepsy with and without cataplexy; also under investigation in idiopathic hypersomnia and Prader-Willi syndrome

## Evidence & Treatment
- **HARMONY I** (pivotal EU trial, 8 weeks): pitolisant 10–40 mg reduced ESS by −5.8 vs −3.4 placebo; met primary EDS endpoint
- **HARMONY-CTP**: weekly cataplexy rate fell 75% with pitolisant vs 38% placebo in patients with ≥3 cataplexy attacks/week
- EMA approval: March 2016 (adults, narcolepsy ± cataplexy) — first regulatory approval globally, ~3 years before FDA
- FDA approval: August 2019 for adult EDS; October 2020 for adult cataplexy; June 2024 extended to pediatric (≥6 years)
- Dosing: titrated over 3 weeks; target dose 17.8 mg or 35.6 mg once daily in morning; max 35.6 mg/day (US labeling)

## Key Facts
- H3R constitutive activity means pitolisant's inverse agonism provides histamine enhancement even in the absence of endogenous histamine ligand
- ESS improvement of ~5 points in pivotal trials; comparable to modafinil in head-to-head HARMONY III
- Only non-scheduled wake-promoting agent in narcolepsy (no DEA control); all others — modafinil/armodafinil (Sched IV), sodium oxybate (Sched III/IV), amphetamine salts (Sched II)
- EMA granted orphan designation based on narcolepsy prevalence of ~0.02–0.05% (20–50 per 100,000) in Europe
- Half-life ~10–12 hours; metabolized predominantly by CYP2D6 and CYP3A4; dose adjustment required in hepatic impairment
