# Medicare Secondary Payer (MSP): Who Pays First

The Medicare Secondary Payer statute (42 U.S.C. § 1395y(b)) prohibits Medicare from paying when another payer is primary. Understanding payer order prevents claim denials and protects Medicare's right to reimbursement.

## Core Principle

**Primary payer:** Must pay first; Medicare cannot pay until the primary payer has paid or refuses to pay  
**Medicare:** Pays only the remainder (secondary), or makes a **conditional payment** if the primary payer hasn't paid yet — then recovers later

## Payer-Order Chart by Coverage Type

| Coverage Type | Who Pays First | When Medicare Pays |
|---|---|---|
| Workers' Compensation (job injury/illness) | Workers' Comp | Only for non-WC-related care, or if WC denies/disputes |
| No-fault insurance (auto, medical pay) | No-fault insurer | After insurer's limit exhausted, or after 120-day prompt payment window |
| Liability insurance (personal injury, premises) | Liability insurer | Conditional payment; recovery from settlement |
| Employer GHP — large employer (20+), active employee | Employer GHP | Secondary after GHP pays |
| Employer GHP — small employer (<20), age 65+ | Medicare | Employer GHP secondary |
| COBRA continuation coverage | Medicare (once enrolled) | COBRA secondary |
| Retiree health coverage | Medicare | Retiree plan secondary |
| TRICARE (military retirees) | Medicare | TRICARE secondary |
| CHAMPVA (veterans dependents) | Medicare | CHAMPVA secondary |
| Veterans Affairs (VA) | Separate system | Medicare does NOT coordinate with VA; treated as separate benefit |
| Black lung program | Federal black lung | Only for unrelated conditions |
| End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) — first 30 months with employer GHP | Employer GHP | Secondary during 30-month coordination period |

## Workers' Compensation

**Rule:** If an injury or illness is work-related and covered by workers' comp, **workers' comp pays primary** for all related care.

- Medicare will **not pay** for care covered by workers' comp, even if WC delays payment
- Exception: Medicare makes a **conditional payment** if WC hasn't paid within a reasonable time (120 days for prompt payment standard); CMS then seeks reimbursement from WC settlement
- **Medicare Set-Aside Arrangements (WCMSAs):** When a WC settlement closes out future medical treatment, CMS requires setting aside a portion of the settlement to pay for future Medicare-covered treatment related to the injury — to protect Medicare from having to pay for care the settlement was meant to cover
- WCMSA amounts require CMS approval for settlements above certain thresholds; CMS reviews ensure Medicare's interests are protected

## No-Fault and Liability Insurance

**No-fault insurance (auto medical payments, personal injury protection/PIP):**
- Insurer pays primary regardless of fault
- Prompt payment rule: if insurer doesn't pay within 120 days, Medicare may pay conditionally and recover later

**Liability insurance (personal injury settlements):**
- Liability insurer pays primary
- Medicare makes conditional payments to ensure care is covered while liability is disputed
- **Medicare must be reimbursed** from any liability settlement or judgment that includes medical expenses — beneficiaries cannot simply keep the settlement without repaying Medicare
- CMS's coordination of benefits contractor (Conduent) tracks conditional payments; beneficiaries receive a conditional payment notice

**Practical impact for beneficiaries involved in personal injury cases:**
1. Inform your attorney that Medicare has a potential lien
2. CMS will send a "Rights and Responsibilities" letter
3. At settlement, Medicare lien must be resolved — failure to repay triggers liability under MSP
4. CMS may waive recovery in hardship cases; can appeal conditional payment amounts

## Conditional Payments: The Recovery Process

When Medicare pays first (conditional payment) and another payer is determined to be primary:

1. CMS issues a **Demand Letter** stating the amount owed
2. Beneficiary/insurer has 60 days to pay or appeal
3. Interest accrues on unpaid amounts after the 60-day window
4. Appeals: dispute via CMS's coordination of benefits contractor; escalate to ALJ if needed

## Section 111 Mandatory Insurer Reporting

Under the MMSEA (Medicare, Medicaid and SCHIP Extension Act), liability insurers, no-fault insurers, and workers' comp entities must **report settlements and payments** to CMS through the Section 111 reporting system. This allows CMS to identify potential MSP situations and pursue recovery. Non-compliance penalties: up to $1,000/day per failure.

## TRICARE and Military Coverage

**TRICARE (military retirees and family):**
- Medicare must be enrolled to keep TRICARE; TRICARE is secondary to Medicare
- If you're eligible for Medicare but don't enroll, **TRICARE coverage terminates**
- TRICARE for Life (TFL): wraps around Medicare as secondary payer — beneficiaries often have $0 cost-sharing for Medicare-covered services

**Active duty:** Medicare is secondary to TRICARE for active-duty military members.

## ESRD Special 30-Month Coordination Period

Beneficiaries who gain Medicare due to ESRD (dialysis) and are also covered by an employer GHP (20+ employees) face a **30-month coordination period**:
- For first 30 months: Employer GHP pays primary
- After 30 months: Medicare pays primary
- Failure to enroll in Part B during ESRD-based eligibility can still result in late penalty

## 2026 MSP Compliance Priorities

CMS has increased enforcement scrutiny on:
- Liability insurers that fail Section 111 reporting
- Settlements that don't properly account for Medicare's interest
- Workers' comp claims where MSAs are underfunded
- Medicare Advantage plans' coordination with primary payers
