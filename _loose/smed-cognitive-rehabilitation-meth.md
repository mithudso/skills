Meth causes measurable deficits in executive function, working memory, processing speed, verbal learning, and attention — all of which directly impair SUD treatment engagement and functional recovery. Cognitive rehabilitation targets these deficits through five main modalities.

## Deficits to Target (Priority Order)

1. **Executive function** (planning, cognitive flexibility, inhibitory control) — most severely affected; impairs relapse prevention, decision-making, CBT homework
2. **Working memory** (verbal + visuospatial) — impairs treatment adherence, session recall, daily functioning
3. **Processing speed** — slowed in heavy users; affects occupational/functional recovery
4. **Verbal learning and episodic memory** — impacts group therapy retention and skill generalization
5. **Attention/vigilance** — disrupted sustained attention undermines all other interventions

## Computerized Cognitive Training (CCT)

**BrainHQ** (Posit Science) and **CogMed** are the most-studied platforms in SUD populations.

- Protocol: 20–30 min sessions, 4–5×/week, 8–12 weeks
- RCTs in meth SUD show improvements in working memory, attention, and processing speed (Pływaczewski et al., PMC5486910; systematic review PMC8418081 — 18/26 studies showed cognitive gains)
- Secondary benefit: improved treatment engagement — better working memory → better CBT adherence, homework completion, counseling retention
- CogMed targets working memory specifically; BrainHQ covers broader attention/memory domains
- Virtual reality-based CCT (VRainSUD-VR) showed significant memory and executive function gains in SUD in 2025 (Frontiers Behav Neurosci 2025)
- **Timing**: most effective at 2–6 months abstinence — enough substrate recovered for neuroplastic gain, window still open

## Cognitive Remediation Therapy (CRT)

Adapted from schizophrenia rehabilitation for SUD settings:

- **Scaffolding**: external supports for currently impaired functions while building capacity (written summaries, visual prompts, step checklists)
- **Errorless learning**: minimize errors during skill acquisition → reduces shame, builds success experience, especially important given meth users' elevated shame/defeat affect
- Structured, predictable learning environments improve outcomes in meth patients
- Manual-based programs available (e.g., CRT for SUD, Medalia's Neuropsychological Educational Approach to Remediation)
- Delphi consensus (PMC10073279): CRT should be integrated into residential and intensive outpatient SUD settings as adjunct treatment

## Aerobic Exercise (Most Reliably Effective Modality)

Exercise is the strongest single cognitive rehabilitation intervention in meth SUD:

- **Mechanism**: BDNF upregulation in PFC and hippocampus → synaptogenesis, dendritic branching in circuits most damaged by meth
- **Protocol**: 30-min moderate-intensity aerobic exercise, 3–5×/week
- Brown et al. RCT: 8-week exercise program improved meth abstinence AND cognitive performance simultaneously — the only intervention showing dual outcome improvement
- Targets attention, working memory, and executive function (cross-referenced: meth-neuroplasticity-recovery skill for mechanism detail)

## Neurofeedback

EEG neurofeedback targeting frontal theta/alpha ratio:

- Meth users show theta excess in frontal cortex associated with impaired executive function and impulse control
- Neurofeedback protocols reduce theta excess → improved executive function, reduced impulsivity
- Real-time fMRI neurofeedback (see nat-real-time-fmri-neurofeedback) targets same circuits with higher spatial precision
- Evidence: small RCTs, promising but not yet standard of care — appropriate as augmentation, not primary modality

## Cognitive Scaffolding in Treatment Settings

These are NOT rehabilitation but reduce barriers cognitive impairment creates for treatment engagement:

- Written session summaries provided after every counseling session
- Simple language; no multi-step verbal instructions
- Reduced working memory load in CBT (external tracking tools, simplified homework)
- Visual cue cards for relapse prevention chains
- Repetition across sessions rather than assuming retention
- "Low-cogload" CBT adaptations: materials written ≤6th-grade reading level, single-concept worksheets

## Screening and Timing

**Screen at 2-month baseline**: MoCA, Trail Making Test A/B, Digit Symbol Substitution Test (DSST) — quick, reliable in clinical settings.

**Follow-up at 6 months**: reassess to track trajectory and adjust intervention intensity.

**Trajectory**: most cognitive recovery occurs in first 6–12 months; heavy long-term users may have persistent executive function deficits. Rehabilitation is most efficient at 2–6 months — the neuroplasticity window is open and enough dopaminergic substrate has recovered to support learning.
