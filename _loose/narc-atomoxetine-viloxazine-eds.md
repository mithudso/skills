# Atomoxetine and Viloxazine as NRIs for EDS in Meth Recovery

## Core Concept

Atomoxetine (Strattera) and viloxazine XR (Qelbree) are **selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (NRIs)** — FDA-approved for ADHD, non-DEA-scheduled, and carrying mechanistic rationale for partial EDS relief in meth recovery. They address the **NE-depletion component** of stimulant-withdrawal hypersomnia without touching dopamine reuptake. This makes them distinct from bupropion (NDRI, adds DA component) and appropriate when DA reuptake enhancement is not desired — e.g., early recovery with bipolar risk, DA dysregulation concerns, or psychosis vulnerability.

## Why NE Matters for Wakefulness in Meth Recovery

Methamphetamine is a substrate for both DAT (dopamine transporter) and NET (norepinephrine transporter) — it floods synapses via both transporters during use and depletes both systems during withdrawal. The **locus coeruleus (LC) → cortical arousal pathway** is NE-dependent: LC noradrenergic neurons project broadly to cortex, thalamus, and hypothalamus and are critical for maintaining wakefulness and alerting. NE depletion in withdrawal therefore contributes to EDS independently of DA depletion. Selective NET inhibition can partially restore arousal tone via this pathway even without direct DA support.

## Atomoxetine (Strattera)

- **Mechanism:** Selective NET inhibitor; raises synaptic NE in prefrontal cortex and arousal circuits; minor serotonergic activity
- **Pharmacokinetics:** t½ ~5h (extensive CYP2D6 metabolizers); ~22h in poor metabolizers — screen for CYP2D6 status if side effects are pronounced
- **EDS/narcolepsy evidence:** Off-label use documented; a 2005 case series (Furet & Schwartz; PubMed 16268387) reported EDS and cataplexy improvement in narcolepsy. Listed by the Hypersomnia Foundation as an NRI class agent with wake-promoting properties. Sleep medicine guidelines (Maski et al.; PMC 3480574) acknowledge adrenergic reuptake inhibitors including atomoxetine as part of the therapeutic arsenal for narcolepsy and hypersomnia — "less effective than modafinil in adults but a viable non-scheduled option."
- **Cataplexy relevance:** NE reuptake inhibition suppresses cataplexy via the same noradrenergic muscle-tone mechanism as older antidepressants. If EDS + cataplexy co-present (pointing toward Mechanism 2/3 — structural orexin injury), atomoxetine may address both symptoms with one non-scheduled agent.
- **Abuse potential:** No meaningful DAT binding; no reinforcing dopamine surge; not scheduled

## Viloxazine XR (Qelbree)

- **FDA approvals:** Children 6–17 (2021); adults ≥18 (2024, first novel non-stimulant ADHD approval for adults in ~20 years). DEA confirmed not a controlled substance.
- **Mechanism — differentiated from atomoxetine:** NET inhibition **plus** 5-HT2B antagonism and 5-HT2C agonism. The 5-HT2C agonism is the key differentiator: 5-HT2C receptor activation in PFC disinhibits NE and DA release → viloxazine raises extracellular NE, DA, and serotonin in prefrontal cortex at therapeutic doses (PMC 10799649). This makes it mechanistically closer to a modest NE/DA promoter than a pure NRI, without directly blocking DAT.
- **Wake-promoting rationale:** The additional PFC DA disinhibition via 5-HT2C gives viloxazine a marginally broader catecholamine profile than atomoxetine — potentially more wake-promoting for EDS — while maintaining non-scheduled status.
- **SUD/EDS evidence:** No published meth-recovery-specific or narcolepsy-specific RCT data as of 2026. Evidence is mechanistic and pharmacodynamic. Clinical use is off-label for EDS.
- **Dosing:** 200–600 mg XR QAM (adults); start 200 mg × 2 weeks, titrate by 200 mg increments

## Comparison: NRIs vs. Bupropion for EDS in Meth Recovery

| Feature | Atomoxetine | Viloxazine XR | Bupropion (NDRI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Pure NRI | NRI + 5-HT2C agonism | NRI + DAT inhibition |
| PFC DA elevation | Indirect (NE only) | Indirect (NE + serotonin-gated DA) | Direct (DAT block) |
| Craving benefit | None established | None established | Yes (ADAPT-2) |
| Seizure risk | Low | Low | Moderate (dose-dependent) |
| DEA schedule | Unscheduled | Unscheduled | Unscheduled |
| Prefer when | Cataplexy present; CYP2D6 known; pure NE approach wanted | DA reuptake concern; want broader PFC coverage without NDRI | Concurrent craving; dual EDS+craving target |

**When to choose NRIs over bupropion:** Patient has bipolar history (bupropion can precipitate mania), prior seizures, or the clinician specifically wants to avoid DA reuptake inhibition in early recovery. Atomoxetine is also preferred if cataplexy is a feature, leveraging its established cataplexy-suppression data.

## Practical Prescribing

**Atomoxetine:**
- Start 25 mg QAM × 1 week → 40 mg QAM → target 40–80 mg QAM
- Morning dosing; onset 2–4 weeks
- Monitor LFTs periodically (rare hepatotoxicity — black box warning)
- Monitor HR/BP (NE elevation can raise both)

**Viloxazine XR:**
- Start 200 mg QAM × 2 weeks → 400 mg QAM → max 600 mg QAM
- Open capsule and sprinkle on food if swallowing is difficult
- Monitor BP; nausea is most common early side effect

**Both:** Avoid in narrow-angle glaucoma; caution with MAOIs (2-week washout); caution if urinary retention (alpha-adrenergic effects on bladder).

## Position in EDS Management Ladder

1. **First line, non-scheduled:** Bupropion (if craving co-target) **or** atomoxetine/viloxazine (if pure NE approach preferred or bupropion contraindicated)
2. **Second line, non-scheduled:** Pitolisant (H3 antagonist, orthogonal mechanism)
3. **Third line, Schedule IV:** Modafinil/armodafinil
4. **Fourth line, Schedule IV:** Solriamfetol (higher DAT affinity — more caution in SUD)
5. **Avoid in stimulant SUD:** Amphetamine-class agents (Schedule II)
