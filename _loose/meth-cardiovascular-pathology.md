# Meth Cardiovascular Pathology

## Core Concepts
- **Methamphetamine-associated cardiomyopathy (MACM)**: dilated cardiomyopathy pattern; major and growing cause of non-ischemic heart failure, especially in younger adults
- **Mechanism class**: catecholamine excess + direct cellular toxicity (distinct from pure sympathomimetic toxicity)
- **Spectrum**: MACM, malignant arrhythmia, hypertensive crisis, coronary vasospasm/ACS, pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH)
- **Reversibility**: partial reverse remodeling documented with abstinence; not universally reversible — degree depends on fibrotic burden and duration of use

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **Catecholamine surge**: meth forces vesicular release and blocks reuptake of dopamine/norepinephrine/serotonin → sustained sympathetic overdrive → increased HR, BP, afterload, myocardial O₂ demand
- **Oxidative stress**: elevated reactive oxygen species (ROS) from catecholamine auto-oxidation and mitochondrial dysfunction → lipid peroxidation, protein oxidation, myocyte apoptosis
- **Sigmar1 downregulation**: Sigma-1 receptor expression is significantly reduced in hearts of chronic meth users; Sigmar1 loss impairs mitochondrial dynamics (fusion/fission balance) → contractile dysfunction; identified as a therapeutic target
- **TAAR1 activation**: trace amine-associated receptor 1 (TAAR1) engagement contributes to intracellular calcium dysregulation and ROS generation
- **Structural remodeling**: myocytolysis, contraction band necrosis, interstitial fibrosis, vacuolization → impaired conduction and contractility; QT prolongation drives arrhythmia susceptibility
- **PAH mechanism**: chronic pulmonary vasoconstriction + vascular remodeling (intimal proliferation, medial hypertrophy) from sustained catecholamine/serotonin excess

## Clinical Picture
- **MACM demographics**: predominantly younger males (mean age ~40); MACM rose from 1.8% of new cardiomyopathy cases in southern California (2009) to 5.6% (2014) — trend continuing upward
- **Cardiovascular disease is the #2 cause of death** in meth abusers, behind accidental overdose only
- **Arrhythmias**: AF, VT, VF; QTc prolongation on ECG; risk highest with acute intoxication and in established MACM
- **Hypertensive crisis**: acute surges to >180/120 mmHg common with intoxication; risk of hemorrhagic stroke, aortic dissection
- **ACS without obstructive CAD**: coronary vasospasm (often in young patients with no atherosclerosis), accelerated atherosclerosis with chronic use, and in-situ thrombosis
- **Histology hallmarks**: contraction band necrosis, spotty fibrosis, atrophied/hypertrophied myocytes, disrupted mitochondria — distinguishable from ischemic patterns

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Abstinence-first**: partial or full LVEF recovery documented with sustained abstinence + standard HF therapy (ACEi/ARB, beta-blocker, diuretic); recovery trajectory over 3–12 months
- **Beta-blockers**: indicated for MACM-HF; also blunt acute catecholamine surges — use carvedilol or metoprolol succinate per standard HFrEF protocols
- **Acute hypertensive crisis**: IV labetalol or nicardipine preferred; avoid pure beta-blockade without alpha-blockade (risk of unopposed alpha → BP spike)
- **PAH**: standard PAH therapy (PDE5 inhibitors, ERA) used; abstinence is critical — ongoing meth use blunts treatment response
- **No FDA-approved pharmacotherapy** for meth use disorder itself; contingency management has the strongest behavioral-evidence base and is guideline-supported (SAMHSA 2023)
- **Sigmar1 agonists** (e.g., fluvoxamine) under investigation as potential MACM-targeted therapy based on preclinical data

## Key Facts
- MACM prevalence among new cardiomyopathy cases: ~1.8% (2009) → ~5.6% (2014) in high-prevalence regions; estimated higher today
- QTc prolongation present in a significant proportion of chronic meth users — precise population rate varies by study (range 10–30%)
- Cardiovascular mortality = 2nd leading cause of meth-related death (after overdose)
- Reverse remodeling with abstinence: LVEF improvement reported in 40–70% of MACM patients who achieve sustained sobriety
- Meth half-life ~10–12 hours; cardiovascular effects (tachycardia, hypertension) persist well beyond acute intoxication phase
