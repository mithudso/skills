# NC State Health Plan (SHPNC)

## Overview

The **NC State Health Plan (SHPNC)** provides health insurance coverage to North Carolina's state workforce and their families. It is **not a Medicaid program** — it is an employer-sponsored group health plan for state government employees, distinct from Medicaid and Medicare.

The Plan is:
- A **self-insured** employer plan (the state bears the insurance risk, not a commercial insurer)
- Exempt from the **Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA)** as a government-sponsored plan
- Governed by a **Board of Trustees**
- Administered by the **NC State Treasurer's Office**
- One of the largest group health plans in North Carolina

## Who Is Covered

The Plan covers approximately **750,000 individuals** across four groups:

| Group | Approximate Count |
|---|---|
| Active employees and their dependents | 482,000+ |
| Medicare-eligible retirees | 206,000+ |
| Non-Medicare retirees | 54,000+ |
| COBRA participants | ~980 |

**Eligible employer categories** include:
- State agency employees (all cabinet-level agencies, NCDHHS, etc.)
- Public school teachers and education support personnel (county school districts)
- UNC System university employees
- NC Community College System employees
- State legislators (current and former)
- Judicial branch employees

## Plan Options

### Active Employee and Non-Medicare Retiree Plans

1. **Standard PPO Plan** — lower premiums, higher cost-sharing; freedom of choice among in-network providers
2. **Plus PPO Plan** — higher premiums, lower cost-sharing; same in-network freedom as Standard
3. **High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)** — pairs with a Health Savings Account (HSA); lower premiums, higher deductible; available to eligible employees at employing agency's discretion

All three plans emphasize preventive care with strong emphasis on wellness programs.

### Medicare-Eligible Retiree Plans

- **Medicare Advantage (Part C) plan** — enhanced Medicare benefits through the Plan's Medicare Advantage arrangement
- **70/30 Plan** — traditional-style plan for Medicare retirees; includes SilverSneakers fitness access and disease management programs

## 2026 Changes: Salary-Based Premiums

Beginning **2026**, the NC State Health Plan is introducing **salary-based premiums** — a significant structural change:
- Lower-paid state employees will pay lower premiums
- Higher-paid employees will pay proportionally more
- Goal: Reduce financial burden on lowest-paid state workers (teachers' aides, maintenance, clerical staff)
- The Board of Trustees voted on this in May 2025

### Financial Crisis: $507 Million Deficit

The Plan faces a severe financial challenge:
- **$507 million deficit** as of 2024-2025 reporting
- Cash reserves nearly depleted
- Driven by rising health care costs, aging workforce, and premium structure issues
- The salary-based premium restructuring is partly a response to this crisis

Premiums for **2026** were finalized August 15, 2025, with increases for active members and some cost reductions for employee-plus-children coverage.

## Comparison: NC State Health Plan vs. NC Medicaid

| Feature | NC State Health Plan | NC Medicaid |
|---|---|---|
| Population served | State employees, teachers, retirees | Low-income/disabled individuals |
| Eligibility | Employment or retirement from state/schools | Income/disability criteria |
| Funding | Employee premiums + state employer contributions | State/federal Medicaid match |
| Administration | NC State Treasurer / Board of Trustees | NCDHHS / NC Medicaid |
| Federal program | No (ERISA exemption; state law) | Yes (Title XIX, SSA) |
| Low-income focus | No | Yes |

## Medicare Coordination

Medicare-eligible retirees (typically age 65+) must coordinate their State Health Plan benefits with Medicare. NC DHHS publishes guidance on "Medicare and the State Health Plan for Teachers and State Employees" (NC DOI/SHIIP, 2024 edition) explaining the interaction.

SHIP (State Health Insurance Assistance Program) counselors through NC SHIIP can assist retirees with understanding their options.

## Open Enrollment

Annual open enrollment for State Health Plan members typically occurs in mid-October:
- **2025 plan year**: Open enrollment took place October 13–31, 2025
- Members receive benefits information in advance of open enrollment
