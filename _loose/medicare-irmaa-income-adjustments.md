# IRMAA — Income-Related Medicare Premium Adjustments

> **Educational only, NOT financial, tax, or insurance advice.** IRMAA
> thresholds are indexed and change annually. Verify current brackets at
> **[ssa.gov](https://www.ssa.gov)** and **[medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov/basics/costs/medicare-costs)**.
> **verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

IRMAA is the **income-related monthly adjustment amount** — a surcharge added to
Medicare Part B and Part D premiums for beneficiaries whose income exceeds set
thresholds. It was introduced by the Medicare Modernization Act of 2003 and
expanded over time. About 7–8% of Medicare beneficiaries pay IRMAA.

---

## Part 1 — How IRMAA is calculated

### The two-year MAGI lookback

IRMAA is based on your **Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) from two years
prior**. For 2026 surcharges, SSA looks at your **2024 tax return**.

- If you filed a 2024 return: SSA uses that MAGI
- If 2024 return isn't yet filed (or wasn't required): SSA looks at 2023, then 2022

### What counts as MAGI for IRMAA

MAGI = Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) **+ tax-exempt interest income** (e.g.,
municipal bond interest)

Included in MAGI (examples):
- Wages and self-employment income
- Taxable Social Security benefits
- Pension and annuity income
- Traditional IRA/401(k) distributions
- **Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)** — these count and can push income
- Capital gains (including home sale gains above the exclusion)
- Roth conversions (converted amounts are taxable in the conversion year)
- Rental income

**Not counted:**
- Roth IRA distributions (already after-tax, excluded from MAGI)
- Municipal bond principal returns (but the interest IS counted)
- HSA distributions for qualified medical expenses

## Part 2 — 2026 IRMAA brackets and surcharges [^1][^2]

### Part B IRMAA (added to standard $202.90/month)

| 2024 MAGI — Individual | 2024 MAGI — Joint | Total Part B premium/month |
|------------------------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| ≤$109,000 | ≤$218,000 | $202.90 (no IRMAA) |
| $109,001–$137,000 | $218,001–$274,000 | ~$284.10 (+$81.20) |
| $137,001–$164,000 | $274,001–$328,000 | ~$365.30 (+$162.40) |
| $164,001–$191,000 | $328,001–$382,000 | ~$446.50 (+$243.60) |
| $191,001–$500,000 | $382,001–$750,000 | ~$527.70 (+$324.80) |
| Above $500,000 | Above $750,000 | ~$689.90 (+$487.00) |

### Part D IRMAA (added to plan's own premium)

Same income brackets as Part B. 2026 surcharge ranges:
- Lowest bracket: ~$14.50/month added
- Highest bracket: ~$91.00/month added

> These are approximations from CMS announcements; verify exact figures at
> SSA.gov. Brackets are adjusted annually for inflation.

**Married filing separately:** higher brackets apply — IRMAA brackets for
MFS filers are much less favorable; income above ~$109,000 jumps to the
highest tier immediately. MFS status is a known IRMAA trigger.

## Part 3 — IRMAA appeals: SSA Form SSA-44

You can appeal an IRMAA surcharge when a **life-changing event** has caused your
income to drop significantly from the two-year lookback year:

### Qualifying life-changing events (SSA-44 eligible)

- Marriage or divorce
- Death of a spouse
- Work stoppage (retirement, layoff)
- Work reduction (reduced hours)
- Loss of income-producing property (involuntary — e.g., disaster, not a sale)
- Employer settlement payment ceasing
- Failure of employer pension (bankruptcy/plan termination)

### How to file

1. Complete **Form SSA-44** ("Medicare Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount –
   Life-Changing Event")
2. Document the qualifying event and provide evidence of current/expected income
3. Submit to your local SSA office or online

If approved, SSA uses the more recent year's income instead of the lookback year,
potentially eliminating or reducing IRMAA.

### What does NOT qualify for SSA-44

**Voluntary income events do not qualify:**
- Roth conversions (you chose to convert, generating higher income)
- Home sale gain (voluntary decision to sell)
- Large capital gains harvest (voluntary)
- RMD taken from IRA (mandatory but not a "work stoppage")
- Inheritance or lump-sum distributions (unless specifically qualifying)

> **Key point:** a $500,000 Roth conversion that pushes you into the top IRMAA
> bracket cannot be appealed via SSA-44 — it was your choice. The appeal
> mechanism only addresses involuntary income drops.

## Part 4 — Planning strategies (educational)

> *These are educational concepts, not personalized advice. Work with a tax
> advisor or financial planner for your situation.*

### Roth conversion management

Roth conversions create taxable income in the conversion year. Large conversions
can trigger IRMAA two years later. Strategies: spread conversions over multiple
years to stay below IRMAA thresholds; time conversions to years with lower income.

### Capital gains timing

Long-term capital gain realizations count toward MAGI. If you're near an IRMAA
bracket, consider spreading large gains across years or harvesting losses to offset.

### RMD considerations

RMDs from traditional IRAs and 401(k)s are mandatory starting at age 73 and
count toward MAGI. Qualified Charitable Distributions (QCDs) — direct IRA
transfers to charities up to $105,000/year in 2026 — satisfy RMD requirements
without counting toward MAGI.

### Social Security benefit timing

SS benefits count toward MAGI (up to 85%). Delaying SS can affect which years
trigger IRMAA; coordinating with IRA distributions matters.

### Bracket cliff awareness

IRMAA brackets are "cliff" brackets — one dollar over a threshold moves the
entire premium into the higher bracket. The jump from standard to the first
IRMAA tier in 2026 is ~$81.20/month ($974/year) for one spouse.
