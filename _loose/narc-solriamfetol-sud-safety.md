# Solriamfetol (Sunosi) — Safety in Substance Use Disorder

## Mechanism

Solriamfetol is a selective dopamine and norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (DNRI): it inhibits DAT (Ki ~14 µM) and NET (Ki ~3.7 µM), increasing synaptic DA and NE to promote wakefulness. Structurally derived from phenylalanine — not an amphetamine skeleton — so it lacks the releasing activity that drives the hard stimulant abuse ceiling. Approved for excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) in narcolepsy and OSA.

## Controlled Status: DEA Schedule IV

Solriamfetol is Schedule IV (same tier as modafinil, armodafinil, zolpidem). This matters clinically:

- Requires controlled-substance prescription tracking (PDMP queries)
- In contrast, **pitolisant (Wakix) is non-scheduled** — the default preference in active SUD
- Schedule IV ≠ low risk in SUD populations; it means lower abuse potential than Schedule II/III stimulants, not absence of risk

## Abuse Potential: What the Data Show

The human abuse liability study (Setnik et al., *J Psychopharmacol* 2018; PMC6247449) enrolled recreational polydrug users with stimulant experience — the standard regulatory design:

- **Therapeutic dose (75–150 mg):** Drug-liking scores measurably above placebo but modest; maximum drug-liking VAS ~50/100
- **Supratherapeutic dose (300–450 mg):** Significant drug-liking signal; euphoria higher than modafinil at comparable supratherapeutic doses
- **Comparator context:** Amphetamine produced peak drug-liking ~75/100; solriamfetol's ceiling is meaningfully lower but not negligible
- **Unlike pitolisant:** Pitolisant showed no drug-liking above placebo even at 6× the therapeutic dose — a qualitatively different profile

**Bottom line:** Solriamfetol sits between modafinil and amphetamine on the abuse liability spectrum.

## Cardiovascular Risks

Dose-dependent hypertension and tachycardia are the primary safety signals in trials. Clinically significant in:

- **Meth recovery patients** with residual cardiomyopathy, coronary artery disease, or autonomic instability — common in this population
- Baseline and follow-up BP/HR monitoring required
- **Contraindicated with MAOIs** (hypertensive crisis risk via adrenergic excess)

## Clinical Guidance for SUD Patients

| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Active stimulant SUD | Avoid; use pitolisant first |
| Opioid SUD only, stimulant-naïve | Use with caution; PDMP + close monitoring |
| Stable long-term stimulant recovery (≥2 yr) | May use; lowest effective dose, scheduled dosing |
| Active alcohol use disorder | Caution; combined CNS/CV risk |

Scheduled (fixed-time) dosing rather than PRN reduces behavioral drug-seeking opportunity. Document the SUD history and informed-consent discussion in the chart.

## Efficacy Context

When the risk calculus tips toward prescribing: solriamfetol outperforms modafinil on MWT sleep latency in narcolepsy (indirect comparison: +8.9 min vs +2.0–2.3 min at censored-20-min MWT). In TONES 3, 150 mg and 300 mg doses produced robust, sustained EDS reduction. For secondary narcolepsy or severe EDS that modafinil has failed, solriamfetol may be the most effective available option — the SUD risk is real but should be weighed against the morbidity of undertreated narcolepsy (accidents, cognitive impairment, occupational loss).

## SUD Prescribing Hierarchy (Narcolepsy)

1. **Pitolisant** — non-scheduled, no DA reuptake inhibition, preferred in all SUD contexts
2. **Modafinil/armodafinil** — Schedule IV, similar abuse profile to solriamfetol, slightly lower supratherapeutic euphoria signal
3. **Solriamfetol** — Schedule IV, superior EDS efficacy, higher supratherapeutic drug-liking; reserve for treatment-resistant EDS in stable recovery
4. **Amphetamine salts/oxybate** — Schedule II/I; rarely appropriate in active SUD
