# Recovery Capital & Assessment Instruments

## Core Concepts
- **Recovery capital** (White & Cloud, 2008): total volume of internal and external resources an individual can mobilize to initiate and sustain addiction recovery — shifts clinical focus from pathology to strengths
- **Three domains**: personal capital (physical/psychological health, human capital, values, knowledge); social/family capital (relationships, social networks, recovery-supportive peers); community capital (treatment access, housing, neighborhood safety, policy environment)
- **CHIME framework** (Leamy et al., 2011): five elements of personal recovery — Connectedness, Hope, Identity, Meaning, Empowerment — used to align recovery capital measurement with lived-experience priorities
- **Internal vs. external capital**: internal = psychological resilience, coping skills, recovery motivation, self-efficacy; external = stable housing, employment, sober social network, community recovery resources
- Recovery capital is dynamic and bidirectional — accumulates with sustained recovery; depletion (loss of housing, social isolation) predicts relapse

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Higher recovery capital correlates with greater prefrontal cortical engagement in executive function and inhibitory control — behavioral asset accumulation mirrors neurobiological repair timelines
- Social capital (peer recovery networks, mutual aid) activates reward circuitry via social bonding, offsetting drug-cue salience; community belonging reduces HPA-axis stress reactivity
- Employment and structured daily routine reduce unstructured time that activates craving and habitual drug-seeking circuits (habit loop disruption)
- Housing stability decreases chronic stress load; chronic stress sustains CRF/cortisol dysregulation that sustains relapse risk
- Recovery identity formation (CHIME Identity domain) predicts self-efficacy maintenance over time, a known mediator of abstinence self-efficacy

## Clinical Picture
- 2024 systematic review (PMC11262562): 69 studies, 10 validated recovery capital questionnaires identified; together they measure 41 distinct recovery capital constructs across adult clinical and community settings
- ARC score ≥ 47/50 predicts achieving or sustaining ≥ 12 months of recovery; higher ARC/BARC scores predict higher percent days abstinent (PDA) from alcohol and drugs
- Relapse rates 40–60% in early recovery; drop to < 15% after 5 years of continuous sobriety — trajectory consistent with accumulating capital over time
- Home-level (environmental) recovery social capital predicts relapse in sober living settings independent of individual-level scores — community capital is not reducible to personal capital
- Populations with co-occurring psychiatric disorders, low income, or justice involvement show lower ARC scores; instrument validity in diverse/low-income samples confirmed (PMID 31519121)

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Assessment of Recovery Capital (ARC)**: 50-item, 10-domain self-report (Groshkova et al., 2013); validated predictor of treatment completion and 12-month abstinence; Italian, Bangla, and other cross-cultural validations published 2024–2025
- **Brief Assessment of Recovery Capital (BARC-10)**: 10-item validated short form; domains: sobriety, psychological health, physical health, citizenship, social support, meaningful activities, housing/safety, risk-taking, coping, recovery experience; used in UK/Netherlands/Belgium longitudinal study (n = 367, 12-month follow-up)
- **Recovery Capital Scale (RCS)**: broader 45-item instrument used in justice-involved and peer-support settings; measures situational confidence and community integration
- Treatment applications: ARC/BARC-10 administered at intake, 30/90 days, and discharge to track capital accumulation as a treatment outcome metric alongside traditional abstinence measures
- SAMHSA and CHIME-aligned recovery-oriented systems of care (ROSC) frameworks now incorporate capital assessment to guide individualized care planning and peer-support referrals

## Key Facts
- ARC: 50 items, 10 domains, score range 0–50; cutoff ≥ 47 associated with ≥ 12-month sustained recovery
- BARC-10: 10 items, 6-point Likert, score range 10–60; validated across multiple languages and cultural adaptations
- 69 studies in 2024 systematic review; 46 used one of 10 identified questionnaires; 41 distinct constructs measured across instruments
- Recovery capital is significantly lower in individuals with SUD + psychiatric comorbidity + mild intellectual disability (MDPI 2023 case series)
- Buprenorphine-treated OUD patients show measurable recovery capital gains at 6 months even without full abstinence (PMC8513247), supporting capital as a harm-reduction outcome metric
