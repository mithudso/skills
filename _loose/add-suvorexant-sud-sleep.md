# Suvorexant and DORAs for Sleep in Substance Use Disorder

## Mechanism

Suvorexant (Belsomra) is a competitive dual OX1R/OX2R antagonist (Kd ~0.55 nM OX1R, ~0.35 nM OX2R). Orexin neurons in the lateral hypothalamus fire tonically to sustain wakefulness; suvorexant silences this signal rather than inducing sedation through GABA-A potentiation (the benzodiazepine mechanism). Half-life ~12 h. Lemborexant (Dayvigo) shares the same dual-receptor target with slightly shorter t½ and faster onset; daridorexant (Quviviq) is the third FDA-approved DORA with a shorter 8 h t½.

Both OX1R and OX2R matter in SUD contexts: OX2R blockade drives the sleep benefit; OX1R blockade attenuates the hyperarousal and craving components of withdrawal.

## Opioid Withdrawal Insomnia — Why DORAs Are Mechanistically Superior

Under normal conditions, mu-opioid receptors inhibit orexin neuron firing. During opioid withdrawal, that inhibitory tone is lost → orexin neurons disinhibit → excessive wake-promoting drive → hyperarousal insomnia. Suvorexant directly antagonizes this excess orexin output. Benzodiazepines and z-drugs (zolpidem, eszopiclone) suppress GABA-A without targeting the causative mechanism, carry respiratory-depression risk on top of residual opioids, and carry dependence/abuse liability. Suvorexant avoids all three.

## RCT Evidence in OUD

**Science Translational Medicine 2022 (Huhn et al.):** 38 adults with OUD stabilized on buprenorphine/naloxone, inpatient buprenorphine taper paradigm; suvorexant 20 mg or 40 mg vs. placebo for 4 nights. Results:
- Sleep duration increased ~90 min/night vs. placebo (exceeds AASM threshold for clinically meaningful improvement)
- Sleep benefit persisted ~1 hour/night for the 4-day post-taper monitoring period
- Opioid withdrawal severity (COWS/SOWS) reduced in suvorexant arm
- Opioid "wanting" and craving reduced; self-control over opioid use increased
- Drug-liking, subjective "high," and estimated street value: no significant signal; no serious adverse events
- This is the strongest mechanistically-targeted pharmacotherapy evidence for opioid withdrawal insomnia to date

**Oxford SLEEP 2024 (Huhn et al.):** Suvorexant altered sleep EEG power spectrum dynamics and attenuated depressive-symptom trajectories during inpatient opioid withdrawal — supporting both sleep architecture normalization and mood-adjacent benefit.

Lemborexant has an ongoing Phase 1b/2a DDI + efficacy trial in OUD (NCT04818086) examining adjunctive use with buprenorphine; no completed efficacy readout as of 2026.

## Meth Withdrawal — Scope Limit

Acute methamphetamine withdrawal produces **hypersomnia**, not insomnia (catecholamine depletion → rebound sleep pressure). Suvorexant is not indicated here. In **protracted/late recovery** (weeks to months out), circadian disruption with sleep-onset difficulty can emerge — suvorexant may assist sleep initiation in that phase. Also relevant in meth + opioid polysubstance withdrawal where the opioid-withdrawal insomnia mechanism predominates.

## Dosing and Safety

- Start: 10 mg at bedtime; may increase to **20 mg** (FDA-approved ceiling; trials used up to 40 mg off-label without serious events)
- Reduce dose in moderate hepatic impairment; avoid in severe hepatic impairment
- CYP3A4 substrate: moderate inhibitors (e.g., fluconazole) increase exposure; strong inhibitors (e.g., clarithromycin) — use 5 mg if at all
- Schedule IV DEA; abuse-potential studies show no euphoria and no dose-escalation behavior
- Monitor: next-day sedation (Epworth ≥ 10 is stopping signal), complex sleep behaviors, worsening depression

## Comparator Agents in SUD Sleep

| Agent | Mechanism | SUD Concern | Evidence in OUD Insomnia |
|---|---|---|---|
| Benzodiazepines | GABA-A potentiation | High dependence, respiratory depression | Avoid |
| Z-drugs (zolpidem) | GABA-A (alpha-1 selective) | Abuse liability, similar to BZD | Avoid or use briefly |
| Quetiapine low-dose | H1/alpha antagonism | Metabolic SE, limited insomnia evidence | Common off-label; weak data |
| Trazodone | H1/5-HT2A antagonism | Low abuse risk | Modest effectiveness; no OUD-specific RCTs |
| Suvorexant | OX1R/OX2R antagonism | Schedule IV; low abuse signal | RCT evidence; mechanism-targeted |
| Lemborexant | OX1R/OX2R antagonism | Schedule IV; low abuse signal | DDI trial ongoing; no OUD efficacy RCT yet |

Suvorexant is the preferred pharmacotherapy for **opioid-withdrawal hyperarousal insomnia** when a mechanism-targeted, low-abuse-risk agent is prioritized. Trazodone remains a reasonable first-line choice in settings where Schedule IV prescribing is restricted.
