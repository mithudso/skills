# Meth Pharmacokinetics and Metabolism

## Core Concepts
- Methamphetamine is a highly lipophilic weak base (pKa ~10), enabling rapid CNS penetration and pH-sensitive renal handling
- Primary metabolic pathways: N-demethylation to amphetamine (CYP2D6), aromatic hydroxylation to 4-hydroxymethamphetamine (CYP2D6/FMO3), and minor oxidative routes
- Bioavailability varies markedly by route: ~67% oral, ~79% intranasal, 67–90% smoked, 100% IV
- Elimination half-life ranges 5–30 h (mean ~10–12 h), substantially prolonged by alkaline urine
- Both a CYP2D6 substrate and competitive inhibitor — relevant to polypharmacy toxicity

## Mechanisms / Biology
- High lipophilicity (methyl group on nitrogen) enables blood-brain barrier crossing faster than amphetamine and confers resistance to MAO degradation
- CYP2D6 mediates N-demethylation → amphetamine, which retains pharmacological activity; amphetamine plasma peak occurs 10–24 h post-dose
- FMO3 contributes to 4-hydroxymethamphetamine formation; minor metabolites include norephedrine, phenylacetone, hippuric acid, and benzoic acid
- Renal excretion is pH-dependent: unchanged drug in urine ranges from 2% (alkaline urine, pH ≥8.0) to 76% (acidic urine, pH ≤5.0); alkalinization markedly extends half-life
- CYP2D6 inhibitors (fluoxetine, paroxetine, quinidine) raise systemic meth levels, increasing cardiovascular and neuropsychiatric toxicity risk
- CYP2D6 extensive metabolizers convert more drug to amphetamine and show greater neurocognitive vulnerability in chronic use

## Clinical Picture
- Peak plasma concentration: ~3–6 h oral, ~2.5–2.7 h intranasal/smoked — faster routes correlate with greater abuse liability
- Urinary detection window: 3–5 days typical; extended to 7+ days with alkaline urine or heavy chronic use
- CYP2D6 poor metabolizers accumulate higher parent drug levels; extensive metabolizers accumulate more amphetamine metabolite
- Overdose: hyperthermia, hypertensive crisis, arrhythmia, rhabdomyolysis, serotonin syndrome (especially with CYP2D6 inhibitors)
- Approximately 1.5 million current methamphetamine users in the US (2022 NSDUH); ~32,500 overdose deaths involving stimulants in 2022

## Evidence & Treatment
- No FDA-approved pharmacotherapy for methamphetamine use disorder; contingency management is the highest-evidence behavioral intervention (see meth-contingency-management.md)
- Naltrexone + bupropion combination showed modest efficacy in the ADAPT-2 trial (2021): 13.6% vs 2.5% abstinence rate, NNT ~9
- Urinary acidification (ammonium chloride) historically used to accelerate elimination; rarely used clinically due to metabolic risks; alkalinization contraindicated in overdose management
- CYP2D6 genotyping not routinely recommended in clinical practice but relevant to drug-interaction counseling (SSRIs, antipsychotics commonly co-prescribed)
- Forced diuresis without acidification provides marginal benefit; supportive care (cooling, benzodiazepines, antihypertensives) is mainstay of overdose management

## Key Facts
- Mean elimination half-life: 10–12 h (range 5–30 h); compare amphetamine ~10 h
- Urine unchanged drug: 2% at pH 8.0 vs 76% at pH 5.0 — a 38-fold difference
- Amphetamine metabolite peak: 10–24 h after methamphetamine dose
- Oral bioavailability ~67%; smoked bioavailability 67–90% of delivered dose
- CYP2D6 inhibition by common SSRIs can double systemic meth exposure
