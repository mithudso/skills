# Social Rhythm Therapy / IPSRT for Addiction Recovery

## Core Concepts
- **Social zeitgebers:** external time cues (light, meal times, social contact, physical activity, wake time) that entrain the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) circadian clock; disruption of zeitgebers → circadian instability → sleep-wake dysregulation, mood dysregulation, and elevated relapse risk
- **IPSRT mechanism:** stabilizing social rhythms reduces allostatic load on the circadian system; regular daily timing of key behaviors entrains the SCN via non-photic pathways, normalizing cortisol rhythm, melatonin onset, and orexin-system tone
- **Dual target:** IPSRT addresses *both* rhythm irregularity (circadian dimension) and interpersonal problems (grief, conflict, role transitions linked to addiction) — this dual target distinguishes it from generic sleep hygiene or scheduling advice
- **Social Rhythm Metric (SRM):** 17-item daily diary tracking time-of-occurrence of key activities (wake, first contact with another person, breakfast, exercise, dinner, bed); higher SRM regularity scores predict longer time to relapse in both bipolar disorder and SUD populations

## Mechanism / Biology
- **Meth-specific disruption:** methamphetamine dysregulates SCN clock genes (Per1/Per2) via TAAR1 signaling; binge-crash cycles produce extreme sleep-wake inversion; melatonin production is suppressed during binge phases; post-cessation circadian fragmentation can persist weeks
- **Circadian instability → relapse pathway:** circadian misalignment elevates CRF signaling, reduces prefrontal cortical control, and sensitizes the mesolimbic dopamine system — all known relapse-promoting states; re-establishing stable rhythms partly reverses these vulnerabilities
- **Key zeitgebers ranked by entrainment power in recovery:** (1) fixed wake time (single most powerful behavioral anchor), (2) morning light exposure, (3) meal timing (breakfast anchors morning cortisol peak), (4) planned social contact, (5) consistent exercise timing
- **SRT + pharmacotherapy interaction:** behavioral rhythm stabilization and melatonin/chronobiotic pharmacotherapy are complementary; SRT provides the behavioral scaffolding that pharmacotherapy requires to be effective

## Clinical Picture — IPSRT for SUD
- **Who benefits most:** stimulant and meth users in early-to-mid recovery (weeks 2–8), when circadian system is maximally plastic; also benefits alcohol-use-disorder patients with fragmented sleep-wake patterns and opioid patients transitioning off sedating MOUD
- **Adapted structure (12–16 sessions):** standard IPSRT was 17-week manualized (Frank et al., 2005, JAMA Psychiatry); SUD adaptations typically use 12 sessions × 45–55 min, two/week, starting at week 2 post-cessation
- **Session sequence in SUD adaptation:**
  - Sessions 1–3: SRM baseline mapping + interpersonal inventory (relationship losses/changes due to addiction)
  - Sessions 4–8: Identifying rhythm disruptors (conflict, boredom, social isolation as relapse triggers); building anchored daily schedule
  - Sessions 9–12: Interpersonal grief work (relationships lost to addiction); relapse prevention through rhythm protection
- **Interpersonal problem areas addressed:** grief (loss of relationships/identity to addiction), interpersonal disputes (conflict with family, partner), role transitions (sobriety as a new social identity), interpersonal deficits (isolation, lack of sober social network)

## Evidence
- **IPSRT bipolar foundation:** Frank et al. 2005 (JAMA Psychiatry) — 2-year RCT; acute IPSRT → longer time to new affective episode (p = .01) vs. intensive clinical management; higher SRM regularity at treatment end was the mediating mechanism; this mechanistic pathway (rhythm regularity → outcome) is the theoretical basis for SUD adaptation
- **IPSRT for methamphetamine SUD:** Khezri et al. (2021, Int J High Risk Behav Addict) — 12-session IPSRT vs. control in meth users; significant reduction in emotion dysregulation, addiction severity index scores, and craving at 3-month follow-up
- **IPSRT + cognitive rehabilitation for meth SUD:** trial combining IPSRT with computer-based CRT showed superior response inhibition and emotion regulation outcomes versus IPSRT alone — suggesting rhythm stabilization creates the neurobiological window in which cognitive recovery proceeds
- **Alcohol SUD:** DAILY trial (Frontiers Psychiatry 2020) — personalized circadian zeitgeber therapy (IPSRT-derived protocol) as adjunctive treatment for AUD; RCT protocol showed feasibility; rhythm stabilization reduced craving on delayed measures
- **SRM as relapse predictor:** SRM scores at end of treatment predict 6-month abstinence independent of baseline severity; SRM app-based delivery is being piloted in stimulant-recovery populations (2024 data pending)
- **Comorbid bipolar + SUD:** IPSRT patients with lifetime SUD showed attenuated social adjustment improvement vs. non-SUD counterparts, but equivalent relapse-interval benefit — suggesting rhythm stabilization benefits the circadian dimension even when interpersonal gains are harder to achieve

## Treatment Protocol — Practical Elements
- **Non-negotiable behavioral anchors (introduce weeks 1–2):**
  - Fixed wake time (±30 min, 7 days/week) — single highest-leverage intervention
  - Breakfast within 90 min of wake — anchors morning cortisol
  - Morning light exposure 20–30 min (bright light > 2500 lux or outdoor daylight)
  - No stimulants (caffeine included) after 14:00 in early recovery
- **SRM tracking:** daily self-monitoring using paper diary or app; weekly review with therapist; target SRM regularity score ≥ 4.5/5 by session 8
- **Relapse trigger mapping:** identify *circadian disruptors* that are also craving triggers — late nights, social isolation on weekends, irregular meals during conflict periods
- **Combined protocol for meth recovery:** IPSRT behavioral anchors + morning bright light + melatonin 0.5–1 mg at consistent bedtime (if indicated per meth-withdrawal-melatonin-circadian skill) = strongest circadian reset protocol; SRT provides the scaffold, light entrains the SCN, melatonin shifts and stabilizes the phase
- **Timing of initiation:** weeks 2–8 post-cessation is the optimal window; earlier (week 1) may be premature given acute withdrawal; later (after week 8) circadian plasticity begins to habituate to persistent disrupted pattern

## Key Facts
- SRM regularity is a stronger 6-month abstinence predictor than self-reported sleep quality alone
- Wake time consistency is the single most potent behavioral zeitgeber — irregular wake time overrides every other SRT component
- IPSRT's interpersonal component is often underweighted in SUD adaptations; relationship losses to addiction function as unresolved grief that perpetuates circadian destabilization via rumination and social withdrawal
- Meth users with severe pre-cessation binge cycles (>72-hour awake episodes) may require 4–6 weeks of basic sleep consolidation before SRM tracking is meaningful
- App-based SRM delivery achieves equivalent adherence to paper diary in stimulant-recovery populations and allows real-time therapist monitoring of rhythm drift as an early relapse signal
