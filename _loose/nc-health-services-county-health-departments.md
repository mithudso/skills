# NC Local Health Departments (LHDs)

## Overview

North Carolina's public health system operates through **85 local health departments (LHDs)** serving all 100 counties. (Some LHDs serve multiple counties through district or consolidated health departments.) These agencies form the frontline public health infrastructure of the state, providing essential population health services under state mandate.

LHDs are county-level (or district-level) governmental agencies governed by local boards of health and operating under state statutory authority (NC General Statute Chapter 130A). They are distinct from both:
- **NC Division of Public Health (DPH)** — the state agency that sets standards, provides funding, and oversees LHDs
- **FQHCs/Community Health Centers** — federally funded primary care providers

## Core Services

State law requires LHDs to provide certain mandated services. Services typically include:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Communicable disease control** | Disease reporting, outbreak investigation, TB control, STI testing and treatment, HIV testing |
| **Immunizations** | Childhood and adult vaccines per ACIP schedule, school entry compliance |
| **Women, Infants, and Children (WIC)** | Federal nutrition program; LHDs are major WIC access points |
| **Maternal and child health** | Prenatal care access, newborn home visiting (Nurse-Family Partnership), CDSA (early intervention referrals) |
| **Environmental health** | Septic system permits, well inspections, food service inspections, vector control |
| **Vital records** | Birth and death certificate assistance |
| **Chronic disease prevention** | Diabetes prevention, tobacco cessation, obesity prevention programs |
| **Health education** | Community education, health fairs |

Many LHDs also operate clinical services (primary care, family planning, dental clinics) and may be Medicaid providers.

## Accreditation: NC Leads the Nation

North Carolina is the **first state in the nation to mandate accreditation** for local health departments. Key facts:

- **2002**: Local health directors initiated a voluntary accreditation program with state support
- **2005**: Senate Bill 804 established the program in statute
- **2006**: Accreditation became legislatively mandated
- **July 1, 2014**: NC GS 130A-34.4 requires all LHDs to be accredited to continue receiving state and federal funding

### NCLHDA Standards

The NC Local Health Department Accreditation (NCLHDA) program, administered by the **NC Institute for Public Health at UNC-Chapel Hill** in partnership with the **NC Association of Local Health Directors (NALHD)**, evaluates LHDs against:
- **11 standards** covering core public health functions
- **45 activities** (revised from 147 in 2024-2025 cycle)

### Accreditation Process

1. **Self-Assessment**: The LHD compiles evidence against accreditation standards
2. **Site Visit**: Volunteer peer review teams (other LHD staff) review documentation and interview staff
3. **Adjudication**: The NCLHDA Board makes final accreditation determinations

**2024-2025 program activity**: 21 site visits conducted; training for 185+ staff; new standards adopted; transitioning to PowerDMS dashboard in 2026.

NC's model influenced the **national Public Health Accreditation Board (PHAB)** — the federal accreditation program for local, state, and tribal health departments was modeled on NC's framework.

## Governance and Oversight

| Level | Entity | Role |
|---|---|---|
| State | NC Division of Public Health | Sets standards, provides funding, monitors compliance |
| State | NC DHHS | Cabinet-level department over DPH |
| State | NCLHDA/UNC-IPPH | Accreditation program administration |
| State | NC Association of Local Health Directors (NALHD) | Advocacy and peer support |
| Local | County commissioners | Fund and appoint local board of health |
| Local | Local board of health | Governance; adopt local health ordinances |
| Local | Local health director | Operations; appointed by board |

## Funding Sources

LHDs receive funding from multiple streams:
- **State appropriations** — distributed through DPH via pass-through grants and contracts
- **County appropriations** — local government contribution varies widely by county
- **Federal grants** — CDC grants (immunization, STI, TB), HRSA grants, Title V funds
- **Medicaid billing** — for clinical services provided to Medicaid-enrolled patients
- **Program-specific federal funds** — WIC (USDA), Ryan White HIV, etc.

Funding equity across the state is uneven — wealthy suburban counties (Wake, Mecklenburg) have larger LHDs and broader services than rural, lower-income counties.

## Finding Your NC Local Health Department

The NC DHHS maintains a searchable directory at [ncdhhs.gov/LHD](https://www.ncdhhs.gov/LHD) — searchable by county, city, or zip code.

## Relationship to NC Medicaid

LHDs are major access points for Medicaid-enrolled populations:
- WIC enrollment overlaps significantly with Medicaid eligibility
- LHD STI clinics serve predominantly Medicaid and uninsured populations
- NC Navigators and assisters often partner with LHDs for Medicaid enrollment
- LHDs may be contracted as providers under NC Medicaid managed care for specific services
