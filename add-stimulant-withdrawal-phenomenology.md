# Stimulant Withdrawal Phenomenology

## Core Concepts
- Stimulant withdrawal is a **dopamine/norepinephrine depletion syndrome** — not classical physical withdrawal (no seizures, no autonomic crisis); primary danger is psychiatric
- Three-phase structure: **crash** (hours–days), **withdrawal** (1–3 weeks), **protracted dysphoria** (weeks–months)
- Crash reflects acute pharmacological rebound as drug wears off; protracted phase reflects receptor/transporter adaptation from chronic use
- **Anhedonia** is the cardinal feature of the withdrawal phase and the dominant driver of relapse — not craving for euphoria but flight from dopaminergic blankness
- DSM-5 stimulant withdrawal requires dysphoric mood plus ≥2 of: fatigue, vivid unpleasant dreams, insomnia or hypersomnia, increased appetite, psychomotor retardation or agitation

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Chronic stimulants → counter-regulatory downregulation of D2/D3 receptors, reduced dopamine synthesis capacity, and upregulation of dopamine transporters (DAT); when drug clears, net state is **below-baseline dopaminergic tone**
- Methylphenidate long-term use increases striatal DAT availability ~24% (Kim et al. 2013, PubMed PMC3655054); DAT upregulation accelerates dopamine clearance → worsened inter-dose crashes
- Meth-specific: vesicular monoamine transporter 2 (VMAT2) depletion impairs dopamine storage; recovery of VMAT2 and D2 density takes 6–12+ months
- Hypersomnia mechanism: stimulants suppress slow-wave and REM sleep during use; rebound slow-wave pressure accumulates; orexin/hypocretin system (already suppressed by stimulants) rebounds with enhanced sleep drive
- Anhedonia neural substrate: hypofunctional mesolimbic and mesocortical dopamine circuits; large effect size for anhedonia (Cohen's d ≈ −0.528) demonstrated during cocaine withdrawal
- Craving peaks during crash/early withdrawal; neurobiological trigger is nucleus accumbens hypodopaminergia combined with preserved cue-reactivity in prefrontal-striatal circuits

## Clinical Picture
- **Crash phase** (4–24 hrs post-last-dose): profound fatigue, hypersomnia 12–18 hrs/day, hyperphagia, irritability; dysphoria may reach suicidal ideation intensity
- **Withdrawal phase** (days 2–14): sustained depression, anergia, anhedonia, psychomotor slowing, intense carbohydrate craving; resembles MDD but lacks neurovegetative features (appetite usually excess, not deficit)
- **Protracted dysphoria** (weeks–months): subclinical but functionally impairing low mood, blunted reward, poor motivation; most pronounced after heavy meth or cocaine use
- **EDS-rebound hypersomnia** is phenomenologically indistinguishable from narcolepsy type 2 or idiopathic hypersomnia: irresistible daytime sleep attacks, non-refreshing naps, sleep inertia, cognitive fog — clinicians who don't ask about recent stimulant cessation will miss this iatrogenic or post-use cause
- Majority of people who use psychostimulants regularly experience withdrawal upon abstinence; ~24.7% of therapeutic ADHD patients develop clinically significant tolerance within days to weeks (manifests as afternoon crash even on consistent dosing)

## Evidence & Treatment
- **No FDA-approved pharmacotherapy** for stimulant withdrawal; management is supportive
- Bupropion reduces craving and may modestly attenuate withdrawal depression (dopamine/NE reuptake inhibition provides partial dopaminergic support); weak evidence, not guideline-standard
- Contingency management (CM) is the only intervention with strong RCT evidence for stimulant use disorder — addresses the anhedonia/motivation gap behaviorally by providing immediate alternative rewards
- Sleep hygiene and structured rest are first-line for crash-phase hypersomnia; sedating agents (benzodiazepines) contraindicated — worsen protracted dysphoria and rebound sleepiness
- In narcolepsy/ADHD co-occurring cases: stimulant cessation-induced EDS should be evaluated with MSLT only after ≥2 weeks of stimulant washout to avoid false-positive multiple sleep latency results (mean sleep latency artificially short during rebound phase)

## Key Facts
- Crash-phase hypersomnia: 12–18 hrs/day sleep typical; can persist 3–5 days
- Protracted dopamine receptor recovery after heavy meth use: 6–12 months for partial D2 normalization (PET studies)
- Anhedonia effect size during cocaine withdrawal: d ≈ −0.528 (moderate-large)
- Stimulant withdrawal suicide risk: clinically significant — acute crash dysphoria can reach suicidal ideation in 10–20% of heavy users
- MSLT confound: stimulant rebound hypersomnia can produce mean sleep latency <8 min and ≥2 SOREMPs, meeting narcolepsy criteria — washout ≥14 days required before diagnostic sleep study
