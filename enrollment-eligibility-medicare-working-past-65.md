# Medicare Enrollment When Working Past 65

## The Core Rule

If you have active employer-sponsored health insurance from an employer with **20 or more employees** (based on current employment — yours or a spouse's), you can **delay Part B** without incurring a late enrollment penalty.

- **Employer is primary payer**; Medicare is secondary (if enrolled in both)
- Many people choose not to enroll in Part B while working because of the monthly premium (~$185.00 in 2026)
- Part A is typically premium-free and most people enroll at 65 regardless

## Small Employer Exception (<20 Employees)

If your employer has **fewer than 20 employees**, Medicare is **primary** — meaning Medicare pays first, employer plan pays second. In this case, delaying Part B can create serious coverage gaps because your employer plan may pay little or nothing without Medicare paying first. **Enroll in Part B at 65** if working for a small employer.

## The 8-Month Special Enrollment Period (SEP)

When you retire or lose your employer coverage (whichever comes first), you have **8 months** to enroll in:
- Medicare Part A (if not already enrolled)
- Medicare Part B

For Parts C and D, you have only the **first 2 months** of the 8-month window to enroll without gap.

**The SEP begins on:** The earlier of the date your employment ends OR the date your employer coverage ends.

## The COBRA Trap

**COBRA coverage is NOT considered active employment coverage for Medicare purposes.**

If you retire and take COBRA instead of enrolling in Medicare:
- Your 8-month SEP has already started (it began when employment ended)
- If COBRA runs its course (18 months), you may be past the SEP window
- Result: **late enrollment penalty + possible coverage gap**

Correct sequence: Enroll in Medicare during the 8-month SEP when retiring, even if taking COBRA simultaneously. COBRA will pay after Medicare.

## Documentation Required: CMS-L564

To use the SEP and prove you had qualifying employer coverage, you need:
- **CMS-L564** (Request for Employment Information) — completed and signed by your employer
- Used with **CMS-40B** (Application for Enrollment in Medicare Part B)
- Submit both to your local Social Security office
- If your employer is no longer in business, alternative documentation may be accepted

## Part A at 65 vs. Part B Delay

| Part | Action When Working Past 65 |
|---|---|
| **Part A** | Usually enroll at 65 — no premium if 40+ work quarters; does not affect employer coverage |
| **Part B** | Can delay if employer coverage from 20+ employee firm; must enroll within 8-month SEP when employment ends |
| **Part C (MA)** | Cannot enroll without Part B; delay until Part B active |
| **Part D** | Can delay if employer drug coverage is creditable; must enroll within 8-month window or face penalty |

## HSA Interaction

**Cannot contribute to an HSA once enrolled in Medicare Part A or B** (Medicare is not an HDHP). If you want to continue HSA contributions, delay all Medicare enrollment (including Part A) until you stop working. Stop contributions 6 months before enrolling in Medicare — CMS may retroactively enroll Part A up to 6 months back when you apply for Social Security benefits at retirement.

## Coordination of Benefits When Both Are Active

If enrolled in both employer coverage and Medicare:
- **Large employer (20+)**: Employer plan pays first; Medicare secondary
- **Small employer (<20)**: Medicare pays first; employer secondary
- Medicare as secondary payer can pay some or all cost-sharing the employer plan doesn't cover
- Notify your employer plan of Medicare enrollment; they coordinate payments

## Common Scenarios

| Situation | Recommended Action |
|---|---|
| Turning 65, still employed, 20+ employee firm | Enroll Part A; delay Part B; watch the 8-month SEP window at retirement |
| Turning 65, still employed, <20 employee firm | Enroll Part A and Part B immediately at 65 |
| 65+, taking COBRA after leaving job | Use the 8-month SEP immediately — don't wait for COBRA to end |
| 65+, planning to retire in 2 years | Delay Part B; set a calendar reminder; confirm employer coverage is from active employment |
| Spouse's employer plan covers you at 65 | Same rules apply based on spouse's employer size |
