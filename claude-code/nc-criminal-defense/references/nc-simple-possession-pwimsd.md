# NC Simple Possession and PWIMSD

> **Educational only, NOT legal advice.** Verified-as-of: 2026-06-22.

North Carolina G.S. § 90-95 governs all drug possession and distribution offenses short of trafficking. Two distinct offenses dominate drug cases:

1. **Simple possession** — GS 90-95(a)(3): unlawful possession of a controlled substance
2. **PWIMSD** — GS 90-95(a)(1): possession *with intent* to manufacture, sell, or deliver

---

## Simple Possession (GS 90-95(a)(3))

### Elements
1. Defendant knowingly
2. possessed
3. a controlled substance

"Possession" includes both **actual possession** (on person) and **constructive possession** (under defendant's dominion and control even if not physically present). Constructive possession requires knowledge of the substance's presence and the ability to control it. Proximity alone is insufficient.

### Classification by Schedule

| Schedule | Default Penalty | Exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| I (heroin, LSD, MDMA, etc.) | Class I felony | |
| II (cocaine, fentanyl, oxycodone, meth, etc.) | Class I felony | |
| III (ketamine, buprenorphine, anabolic steroids) | Class 1 misdemeanor | |
| IV (benzodiazepines, tramadol) | Class 1 misdemeanor | |
| V (low-dose codeine combos, pregabalin) | Class 1 misdemeanor | |
| VI — Marijuana | See special tiers below | |

### Marijuana / Schedule VI — Tiered Possession Penalties (GS 90-95(d)(4))

| Quantity | Classification | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 0.5 oz (avoirdupois) marijuana | Class 3 misdemeanor | Fine only; no active jail |
| > 0.5 oz, ≤ 1.5 oz marijuana | Class 1 misdemeanor | Up to 120 days jail |
| > 1.5 oz marijuana OR any synthetic THC/THC resin | Class I felony | |
| > 0.05 oz (1/20 oz) hashish | Class 1 misdemeanor | |
| > 0.15 oz (3/20 oz) hashish | Class I felony | |

**Note (2026):** Even though possession of ≤ 0.5 oz is a Class 3 misdemeanor, marijuana remains fully illegal in NC — there is no legal possession, purchase, or home cultivation. See [nc-marijuana-cannabis-status](nc-marijuana-cannabis-status.md) for full details.

### Conditional Discharge (GS 90-96)

First-time offenders charged with simple possession of Schedule I–VI or paraphernalia (GS 90-113.22 / 90-113.22A) may be eligible for conditional discharge — deferred prosecution without entering a guilty plea. See [nc-conditional-discharge-90-96](nc-conditional-discharge-90-96.md).

## PWIMSD — Possession With Intent to Manufacture, Sell, or Deliver (GS 90-95(a)(1))

### Elements

1. Defendant **knowingly**
2. **possessed** a controlled substance (actual or constructive)
3. **with intent** to manufacture, sell, or deliver it

Intent is the distinguishing element. "Manufacture" includes packaging, repackaging, labeling, and relabeling (not just production). "Sell" includes any exchange for value. "Deliver" includes transfer even without sale.

### Classification by Schedule

| Schedule of Substance | PWIMSD Class | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Schedule I or II | **Class H felony** | 4–25 months under Structured Sentencing |
| Schedule III, IV, V, or VI | **Class I felony** | 3–12 months under Structured Sentencing |

**Exception — Marijuana:** PWIMSD of > 10 lbs marijuana triggers trafficking (GS 90-95(h)(1)) rather than PWIMSD — the trafficking statute displaces PWIMSD when quantities reach trafficking thresholds. See [nc-drug-trafficking-thresholds](nc-drug-trafficking-thresholds.md).

### Proving Intent — Circumstantial Evidence

State typically relies on circumstantial evidence of intent since direct evidence is rare:
- **Quantity**: large amounts inconsistent with personal use
- **Packaging**: multiple small bags/units, scales, baggies
- **Cash**: large amounts of small bills
- **Paraphernalia for distribution**: cutting agents, scales, multiple phones
- **Statements**: by defendant or co-defendants
- **Prior transactions**: observed sales or controlled buys

Courts have held that quantity alone, without other indicia, may not be sufficient — combination of factors needed.

## PWIMSD vs. Trafficking: How the Line Works

| | PWIMSD | Trafficking |
|---|---|---|
| Statute | GS 90-95(a)(1) | GS 90-95(h) |
| Intent required? | **Yes** — element of offense | **No** — weight alone sufficient |
| Weight threshold? | None specified | Yes — varies by substance |
| Mandatory minimum? | **No** — Structured Sentencing applies | **Yes** — fixed, probation not available |
| Prior record relevant? | Yes (for sentencing range) | No (fixed minimums) |

A defendant below the trafficking threshold can still be charged with PWIMSD based on intent evidence. At or above the threshold, the State may charge trafficking (no intent required), PWIMSD, or both — but double jeopardy principles and prosecutorial discretion govern.

---

## Sale and Delivery (GS 90-95(a)(1)) — Separate Offense

Actual sale or delivery is a separate offense, not just PWIMSD. Classification follows the same schedule-based framework:
- Sch. I/II substance: Class H felony
- Sch. III/IV/V/VI substance: Class I felony

School/daycare proximity (GS 90-95(e)(8)): sale within 1,000 feet of school grounds or within 300 feet of school bus stop — enhanced punishment: Class E felony (Sch. I/II) or Class H felony (Sch. III/IV/V/VI), replacing base classification.
