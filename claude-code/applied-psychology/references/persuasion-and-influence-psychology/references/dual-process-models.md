# Dual-Process Models of Persuasion: ELM and HSM

The depth-of-thinking axis. Both models say attitude change can happen via a
thoughtful route or a shortcut route, and that **which route runs determines how
strong and durable the resulting attitude is.**

## Elaboration Likelihood Model (Petty & Cacioppo, 1986)

### The core variable: elaboration
*Elaboration* = the extent to which a person thinks about the issue-relevant
arguments in a message. It runs on a continuum from low to high. Where someone
lands depends on:
- **Motivation** — personal relevance/involvement, need for cognition, personal
  responsibility, number of sources.
- **Ability** — freedom from distraction, relevant knowledge, message
  comprehensibility, repetition, time.

High motivation **and** high ability → high elaboration → **central route**.
A deficit in either → low elaboration → **peripheral route**.

### The two routes
| | Central route | Peripheral route |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | High elaboration | Low elaboration |
| Basis of change | Quality of arguments | Peripheral cues (source attractiveness, credibility, # of arguments, mood, consensus) |
| Cognitive effort | High | Low |
| Resulting attitude | **Strong**: persistent, resistant, predicts behavior | **Weak**: decays, flips under attack, weak behavior prediction |

### The multiple-roles principle (the part people miss)
A single variable can play **different roles** depending on elaboration:
- Under **low** elaboration it acts as a **peripheral cue** (e.g., "she's an
  expert, so she's right").
- Under **high** elaboration it can serve as an **argument** itself (expertise is
  evidence relevant to the merits) or **bias** the direction of elaboration.
- Under **moderate** elaboration it can affect **how much** thinking happens.

So there is no fixed list of "peripheral tactics." Diagnose the elaboration level
first, *then* decide what a given cue is doing.

### Objective vs biased elaboration
High elaboration is not automatically fair-minded. **Objective elaboration**
processes arguments even-handedly; **biased elaboration** (e.g., when the issue
threatens a cherished belief) marshals thinking to defend the prior attitude.
Strong arguments persuade under objective elaboration; under biased elaboration
they can entrench the opposite view.

### Need for cognition (NFC)
A stable individual difference: how much a person *enjoys and seeks out* effortful
thinking. High-NFC people elaborate more, are moved by argument quality, and form
attitudes via the central route by default. Low-NFC people rely more on peripheral
cues. Most technical buyers, architects, and senior engineers skew high-NFC —
which is why a thin, cue-heavy pitch underperforms with them and a rigorous one
overperforms.

### Why central-route attitudes are durable
Thinking hard about arguments builds a richer, more internally consistent,
more accessible attitude structure — which makes it persistent over time,
resistant to counterargument, and predictive of behavior. This is the single most
useful ELM takeaway for anyone who needs a decision to *stick*.

## Heuristic-Systematic Model (Chaiken, 1980)

### Two co-occurring modes
- **Systematic processing** — effortful, detailed scrutiny of message content
  (≈ central route).
- **Heuristic processing** — applying simple decision rules / learned heuristics:
  "experts can be trusted," "length implies strength," "consensus implies
  correctness," "likeable communicators are right" (≈ peripheral route).

### The sufficiency principle and threshold
HSM's signature contribution. People are economy-minded (**principle of least
effort**) but also want enough confidence to act. They process only until
**actual confidence** meets **desired confidence** — the **sufficiency
threshold**. Two practical consequences:
- Raise the **desired** confidence (accountability, accuracy motivation, high
  personal stakes, expecting to justify the decision) → the gap widens → people
  shift into systematic processing.
- Raise the **actual** confidence cheaply (a strong heuristic) → the gap closes →
  people stay heuristic.

### Additivity and bias hypotheses (HSM's edge over ELM)
- **Additivity:** heuristic and systematic processing can occur *at the same time*
  and their effects **add up** (ELM more often treats the routes as a trade-off).
- **Bias:** when message content is ambiguous, an active heuristic can **bias** how
  systematic processing interprets it (e.g., expert source → ambiguous arguments
  read as stronger).

### Motivations beyond accuracy
HSM also models **defense motivation** (protect a valued self-belief) and
**impression motivation** (hold attitudes that please others) — not just accuracy.
These bias which mode runs and what conclusion feels "sufficient."

## ELM vs HSM — how to choose
They're convergent. Use **ELM** when you want the route/elaboration framing and the
attitude-strength consequences. Use **HSM** when the live question is *when and why
someone switches into effortful processing* (the sufficiency threshold) or when
heuristic and systematic cues are operating **simultaneously**.

## Operator cheat-sheet
1. Estimate the audience's **elaboration / motivation+ability** and **NFC**.
2. High elaboration → win on **argument quality** (weak arguments hurt you).
3. Low elaboration → credible source, clean proof points, confident delivery
   carry the load — but expect a **weak** attitude.
4. Need durability? Drive **central/systematic** processing (raise relevance and
   the sufficiency threshold) so the attitude survives the first counterargument.
