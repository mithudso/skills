# Stimulant Withdrawal Hypersomnia: Cocaine and Non-Meth Comparison

## Core Framework

All stimulant withdrawal hypersomnia shares the same root: catecholamine depletion → reduced arousal drive + adenosinergic rebound sleep pressure. What differs across agents is **degree of neurotoxicity**, **half-life** (sets onset/duration), **transmitter selectivity** (DA vs 5-HT vs NE), and whether the **orexin system sustains structural damage**. Meth is the worst-case reference; other stimulants cluster below it on most dimensions.

## Cocaine Withdrawal Hypersomnia

**Mechanism**: cocaine blocks DAT, NET, and SERT simultaneously but does not cause vesicular depletion (no VMAT2 reversal). During chronic use, DAT up-regulates to compensate; abrupt cessation leaves an **overshoot of DAT reuptake capacity** relative to DA release rate → acute DA deficit in mesolimbic/mesocortical circuits → hypersomnia. Same pharmacological logic as Mechanism 1 in meth withdrawal but with critical differences:

- **No significant neurotoxicity to DA terminals** at typical human doses: cocaine does not produce the hyperthermia-driven oxidative stress that destroys meth-exposed DA axons; DAT and TH levels recover fully within days-weeks
- **CSF orexin: not deficient** — orexin neurons are structurally intact; hypersomnia is pharmacological rebound, not structural arousal loss
- **Half-life ~1 h (vs meth ~10–12 h)**: crash onset within 15–30 min of last dose; peak hypersomnia at 24–48 h; typically resolves by 72 h

**Timeline**:
- *Crash* (0–72 h): profound hypersomnia (often 14–18 h/day), fatigue, hyperphagia; begins within hours
- *Protracted EDS* (days 3–14): residual daytime sleepiness, mood dysphoria, cravings; total sleep time normalizes by end of first week in most cases
- Duration rarely exceeds **2–3 weeks** in uncomplicated cases; much shorter than meth's 3–5 week trajectory

## Amphetamine Salts (Adderall, Dextroamphetamine)

Pharmacologically between meth and cocaine. Amphetamine salts cause VMAT2 displacement and DA/NE efflux (like meth) but produce **substantially less neurotoxicity** than meth at therapeutic and moderate recreational doses — lower lipophilicity, less body temperature elevation, and less oxidative metabolite burden.

- Withdrawal hypersomnia duration: **1–3 weeks**, longer than cocaine but shorter than meth's full recovery arc
- DAT down-regulation during use → rebound overshooting on cessation, same mechanism as cocaine/meth
- Orexin neurotoxicity risk: **lower than meth**, unlikely at therapeutic doses; possible at heavy recreational doses but not established
- REM rebound and SWS compensation both occur; PSG picture resembles meth withdrawal but compresses into a shorter window

## MDMA (Ecstasy)

Predominantly **serotonergic** withdrawal profile — MDMA preferentially depletes 5-HT via SERT reversal and VMAT2-mediated serotonin release; DA/NE effects are secondary.

- Sleep architecture: *during use* — REM suppression (5-HT overactivation inhibits REM-on neurons); *during withdrawal* — **REM rebound with vivid, often disturbing dreams** predominates
- DA-driven hypersomnia is much less prominent than with cocaine or amphetamine; fatigue is more mood-mediated (5-HT depletion → low mood, anhedonia, lethargy)
- Timeline: acute crash 1–3 days ("suicide Tuesday" phenomenon); sleep normalization typically within 1–2 weeks
- MDMA is neurotoxic to serotonergic axons (5-HT terminal damage in raphe projections) at heavy/repeated doses — but this does not produce orexin deficiency or DA-depletion hypersomnia

## Modafinil / Armodafinil

Mechanism of wakefulness promotion incompletely understood (DAT partial blockade + orexin activation + NE effects). **Withdrawal hypersomnia is mild**: no VMAT2 reversal, no vesicular depletion, no neurotoxicity at any reported dose. Rebound hypersomnia lasting 24–72 h is typical after extended use; rarely clinically significant. No orexin system damage.

## Comparison Table

| Agent | Crash onset | Hypersomnia duration | Primary mechanism | Orexin deficiency | DA neurotoxicity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Meth** | 12–24 h | 2–5 weeks | DA/NE depletion + VMAT2 damage + neurotoxicity | Functional (not structural) | High |
| **Cocaine** | 15–30 min | 3–14 days | DAT rebound overshoot | No | None |
| **Amphetamine salts** | 12–24 h | 1–3 weeks | DA/NE efflux + VMAT2 partial | No (rare heavy use) | Low–moderate |
| **MDMA** | 1–3 days | 1–2 weeks | 5-HT depletion → REM rebound | No | None (DA) |
| **Modafinil** | 12–24 h | 1–3 days | DAT partial block rebound | No | None |

## Clinical Implications

- **MSLT timing**: ≥14-day washout required regardless of which stimulant; cocaine's shorter half-life means a slightly shorter biologic washout, but DSM/AASM convention still requires 2 weeks
- **Treatment**: no FDA-approved pharmacotherapy for any stimulant withdrawal hypersomnia; supportive care, sleep hygiene, bupropion may accelerate DA recovery (used off-label for cocaine/meth); modafinil used off-label for residual EDS in abstinent stimulant users (evidence limited but signal present in amphetamine cohorts)
- **Differential**: cocaine/amphetamine withdrawal hypersomnia mimics narcolepsy acutely; lack of cataplexy, normal CSF orexin (if measured), and resolution with abstinence distinguish them
- **PAWS**: cocaine and amphetamine withdrawal can produce protracted hypersomnia/fatigue for weeks beyond the acute phase via persistent DA receptor down-regulation — same substrate as meth PAWS but shorter course

## Key Facts

- Cocaine crash hypersomnia: peaks at **24–48 h**, typically resolves by **72 h**; rarely exceeds 2 weeks
- Amphetamine salts: withdrawal hypersomnia **1–3 weeks** vs meth's 2–5 weeks
- MDMA withdrawal: **REM rebound** (not DA hypersomnia) is the dominant sleep finding
- CSF orexin is NOT deficient in cocaine or amphetamine withdrawal (contrast: narcolepsy type 1)
- All stimulant withdrawal hypersomnia is pharmacological rebound; only meth adds significant structural neurotoxicity to that equation
