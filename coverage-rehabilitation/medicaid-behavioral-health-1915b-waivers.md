# Medicaid Section 1915(b) Waivers for Behavioral Health

## Overview

Section 1915(b) of the Social Security Act (enacted 1981, P.L. 97-35) allows states to waive Medicaid's "freedom of choice" requirement — which normally lets beneficiaries choose any qualified provider — to mandate enrollment in managed care or restrict providers. States use 1915(b) waivers to create behavioral health carve-outs and BHOs, authorize use of savings for alternative services, and selectively contract with specific providers. [MACPAC 1915(b) waivers overview; medicaid.gov managed care authorities]

## The Four 1915(b) Sub-Authorities

The statute provides four distinct sub-authorities, each requiring separate CMS approval and cost-effectiveness demonstration (42 CFR 431.55): [verified via MACPAC and medicaid.gov search results, 2026-06-22]

### 1915(b)(1) — Freedom of Choice / Managed Care Enrollment
- State can mandate beneficiaries enroll in a managed care plan (MCO, PIHP, PAHP) or PCCM program
- This is the primary authority for BH carve-out Behavioral Health Organizations (BHOs) — state mandates enrollment in the BHO for covered BH services
- Population: defined Medicaid enrollment groups; typically adults with SMI, SUD populations
- **Historical use**: many states (MA, NJ, PA) built entire BH systems on 1915(b)(1) BH carve-outs in the 1990s–2000s; many transitioned to ACA integrated managed care

### 1915(b)(2) — Central Enrollment Broker
- Authorizes use of a central broker (locality or agent) to assist eligible individuals in choosing among PCCMs or competing MCOs/PIHPs/PAHPs
- Provides enrollees with comparative information about health care options
- Less commonly used for BH carve-outs; supports enrollment assistance functions

### 1915(b)(3) — Alternative Services
- State may use savings generated from managed care efficiencies to fund **alternative services** not otherwise coverable under the state plan
- Source of many BH innovation services: peer warmlines, family support, respite, transportation, housing navigation, crisis stabilization services
- Must demonstrate that savings fund these extras (cost-effectiveness requirement)
- NOTE: NC transitioned 1915(b)(3) services to 1915(i) HCBS State Plan Option when Tailored Plans launched under 1115 authority, because 1915(b)(3) services are not available under 1115 demonstration waivers [NC DHHS, 2024]

### 1915(b)(4) — Selective Contracting
- State limits Medicaid services to specific facilities, practitioners, or provider types
- Examples: disease management programs, transportation, specialty BH providers
- States paying providers on fee-for-service basis may selectively contract under this authority without establishing a full managed care program [medicaid.gov 1915(b)(4) technical guide]

## IMD Exception Under Managed Care Capitation

Medicaid generally cannot pay for services in an IMD (institution for mental diseases with >16 beds) for individuals aged 21–64. Under managed care, however, **capitated managed care organizations can include IMD stays as an in-lieu-of service** under 42 CFR 438.6(e):
- Allows Medicaid funding of up to **15 days per month** in an IMD via capitation payments to MCOs/PIHPs — because FFP flows to the MCO capitation rate, not directly to the IMD
- This exception is available under 1915(b), 1932(a), and 1115(a) managed care programs — it is a managed care capitation rule, not exclusive to 1915(b)
- Unlike other in-lieu-of services, IMD costs must not be used in capitation rate development [42 CFR 438.6(e); CMS SMD 19-003]
- Key mechanism states use to cover residential SUD treatment in larger facilities

## 1915(b)/(c) Combination Waivers

States can stack a 1915(b) waiver (for managed care delivery system) with a 1915(c) HCBS waiver:
- 1915(c) provides home and community-based LTSS services (skills training, supported employment, crisis stabilization)
- 1915(b) manages the delivery system (carve-out BHO or MCO)
- Combined: BHO or MCO manages both acute BH services and LTSS HCBS for people with SMI or I/DD
- NC operated a 1915(b)/(c) combination waiver through its LME-MCO system prior to transitioning to managed care under 1115 and 1932(a) authority [NC DHHS factsheet]

## Parity Under 1915(b) Managed Care

MHPAEA applies to Medicaid managed care organizations under 42 CFR Part 438 Subpart K (sections 438.900 et seq.):
- BHOs operating under 1915(b) waivers must comply with parity
- States must include parity requirements in 1915(b) managed care contracts
- The 2016 CMS rule (81 FR 18390, Mar. 30, 2016) codified MHPAEA requirements for MCOs, PIHPs, and PAHPs in Subpart K
- The September 2024 MHPAEA Final Rule (89 FR 76391, Sep. 23, 2024) strengthened NQTL comparative analysis requirements and applies to Medicaid managed care plans; implementation templates and instructional guides issued to states [Federal Register 2024-20612]

## Approval, Cost-Effectiveness, and Renewal

1915(b) waivers are subject to cost-effectiveness requirements under 42 CFR 431.55 — a different standard than the budget neutrality required for 1115 demonstrations: [MACPAC features of federal managed care authorities; verified 2026-06-22]
- **Standard**: actual expenditures under the waiver cannot exceed what they would have been without the waiver
- **Initial approval**: two years
- **Renewal**: up to two years per renewal cycle
- **Extended period**: five years if waiver covers dually eligible Medicare-Medicaid beneficiaries (per ACA §1915(h)(2), P.L. 111-148)
- Budget neutrality per se is NOT required for 1915(b) (unlike 1115 demonstrations and 1915(c) waivers)

## EQRO External Quality Review

States operating managed care programs under 1915(b) authority must conduct annual external quality reviews by an EQRO (External Quality Review Organization):
- Regulatory authority: 42 CFR 438.350–438.364 (Subpart E)
- EQRO must be independent from both the state Medicaid agency and the managed care plans reviewed (42 CFR 438.354)
- Reviews assess quality, timeliness, and access to services
- Applies to MCOs, PIHPs (including BHOs), and PAHPs operating under 1915(b)

## Transition Away from 1915(b) BH Carve-Outs

Post-ACA trend toward integrated managed care:
- CMS has encouraged states to move from 1915(b) BH carve-outs to integrated MCOs that cover physical and BH together
- Rationale: BH-physical integration better for comorbidities; eliminate coordination gaps
- **NC model**: LME-MCOs operated under 1915(b)/(c) waivers as PIHPs; NC transitioned to:
  - **Standard Plans** (2021–2023): integrated managed care under 1932(a) state plan authority for most Medicaid enrollees
  - **BH I/DD Tailored Plans** (launched July 1, 2024): most complex BH/I/DD populations in specialized plans operating under NC's **Section 1115 Demonstration Waiver** (not 1932(a)); 1915(b)(3) services not available under 1115 authority so NC transitioned those to 1915(i) HCBS State Plan Option [NC DHHS; CMS approval Aug. 2023]

## Comparison: 1915(b) vs. State Plan Managed Care

| Feature | 1915(b) Waiver | State Plan Managed Care (1932(a)) |
|---|---|---|
| CMS approval | Waiver; renewable every 2 years | State plan amendment; no separate waiver |
| Freedom of choice | Can mandate enrollment | Can mandate enrollment |
| Cost-effectiveness | Required (42 CFR 431.55) | Not required |
| Budget neutrality | NOT required (unlike 1115/1915(c)) | Not required |
| Alternative services (b)(3) | Yes | Not available |
| IMD 15-day exception | Available (42 CFR 438.6(e)) | Available (42 CFR 438.6(e)) |
| Stacking with 1915(c) | Yes | Yes (concurrent) |
| EQRO requirement | Yes (42 CFR 438.350) | Yes (42 CFR 438.350) |

## State Examples

- **Massachusetts**: MassHealth BH carve-out under 1915(b)(1) (Beacon Health Options as MBHP); currently integrating into ACO model under 1115 demonstration
- **New Jersey**: 1915(b) BH carve-out historically; now integrated MCOs
- **Georgia**: CMO-contracted BH services; 1915(b) waiver for BH population
- **North Carolina**: Historical LME-MCO system under 1915(b)/(c) waivers (PIHPs); transitioned to 1932(a) Standard Plans (2021–2023) and 1115-authorized BH I/DD Tailored Plans (July 2024)
