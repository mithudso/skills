# Ketamine for Substance Use Disorders

## Core Concepts
Ketamine is being investigated as a treatment for AUD, cocaine use disorder (CUD), and OUD — distinct from its FDA-approved esketamine use for treatment-resistant depression. The key hypothesis: a single or short course of subanesthetic ketamine can durably reduce craving, enhance motivation to quit, and potentiate extinction learning, buying a behavioral-intervention window that standard pharmacotherapy cannot.

## Mechanisms
- **NMDA antagonism / glutamate reset** — disrupts reconsolidation of drug-cue memories; may "erase" or weaken craving triggers
- **BDNF / mTOR / synaptic plasticity** — rapid synaptogenesis paralleling its antidepressant mechanism; restores prefrontal cortical control over limbic craving circuits
- **Extinction enhancement** — subanesthetic doses improve fear/cue extinction retention; theorized to apply to drug-cue conditioning
- **Mystical/insight experiences** — at higher (psychedelic-range) doses (0.5–0.71 mg/kg IV), subjective mystical states (not dissociation per se) mediate motivation-to-quit gains in Dakwar's CUD trials
- **Neuroinflammation modulation** — emerging data on anti-inflammatory effects relevant to alcohol-related neurodegeneration

## Clinical Evidence
**AUD:**
- Yuen et al. (2022, Columbia/NYSPI) — single subanesthetic infusion (0.5 mg/kg) + naltrexone vs. midazolam + naltrexone; primary outcome reduction in heavy drinking days. Naltrexone co-administration does not blunt ketamine's effect and may synergize (naltrexone occupies μ-opioid receptors implicated in alcohol reward).
- Yoon et al. (2019) — 4-week ketamine + naltrexone open-label pilot; 80% of AUD+MDD patients reported reduced cravings and consumption; 100% antidepressant response.
- KARE trial (UK, Grabski 2022) — ketamine + alcohol therapy vs. placebo + therapy; 87% abstinence at 6 months in ketamine arm vs. 2% control.

**Cocaine Use Disorder:**
- Dakwar et al. — 0.4 mg/kg and 0.71 mg/kg IV; 67% reduction in cocaine self-administration vs. baseline; craving and motivation-to-quit gains vs. lorazepam control; mystical experience as mediator.

**Opioid/Heroin:**
- Krupitsky et al. (Russia, multiple trials) — ketamine-assisted psychotherapy achieved 65.8% one-year abstinence in heroin-dependent patients vs. 24% in active psychotherapy control.
- Adjunctive ketamine during detox reduces opioid withdrawal severity and craving acutely.

## Safety & Addiction Liability
- **Ketamine's own abuse liability** is real (dissociative high, "K-hole," recreational use, bladder/uropathy with chronic heavy use) — patient selection and controlled clinic administration are essential.
- In SUD populations, short-course clinic-only protocols (1–3 infusions) show low diversion risk; no IV self-administration outside clinic sessions in published trials.
- Contraindications: active psychosis, uncontrolled hypertension, recent cardiovascular events, pregnancy.
- Respiratory/hemodynamic monitoring required; subanesthetic doses rarely cause apnea but BP/HR increases are common.
- Dissociative effects (at ≥0.5 mg/kg) warrant monitoring; they are not the therapeutic mediator in most models and higher-dose protocols require set/setting controls akin to psychedelic-assisted therapy.

## Trial Landscape & Pipeline
- Racemic IV ketamine dominates SUD trials; intranasal esketamine has not been well-studied in SUD (its approval is MDD/suicidality only).
- Active ClinicalTrials.gov studies: ketamine + MET for tobacco, ketamine for OUD + depression (NCT04177706), and extensions of Dakwar's CUD work.
- Key open questions: optimal dose (subanesthetic 0.5 mg/kg vs. higher "psychedelic-range"), number of infusions, necessity of integrated therapy, long-term relapse outcomes beyond 6 months, biomarker predictors of response.
- FDA breakthrough designation not yet granted for any SUD indication; all use remains investigational.
