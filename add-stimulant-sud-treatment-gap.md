# Stimulant SUD Treatment Gap

## Core Concepts
- No FDA-approved pharmacotherapy exists for cocaine use disorder (CUD) or methamphetamine use disorder (MUD) as of 2026 — the only major SUD class without approved medication
- Agonist replacement strategy (analogous to methadone/buprenorphine for OUD) faces strong regulatory resistance despite biological rationale
- FDA issued Oct 2023 draft guidance to incentivize industry development, acknowledging the critical treatment gap
- Primary validated treatments remain behavioral: contingency management (CM) and cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)
- Treatment gap is worsening as stimulant overdose deaths rose from ~2,266 (2011) to ~34,855 (2023) before a 19.8% decline in 2024

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Cocaine/meth act on DAT (dopamine transporter), NET, and SERT — blocking reuptake or reversing transporter direction, flooding mesolimbic dopamine
- Agonist replacement logic: sustained low-level dopaminergic tone blunts cue-induced craving and reduces reinforcing contrast of illicit use (same principle as NRT for nicotine)
- Regulatory resistance stems from Schedule II classification concerns, diversion risk, and narrow therapeutic window — unlike buprenorphine, which has partial agonism ceiling
- Meth neurotoxicity creates persistent DAT downregulation (20–30% loss in striatum), reducing endogenous dopamine capacity and prolonging anhedonia during abstinence
- CRF (corticotropin-releasing factor) hyperactivity during withdrawal drives stress-induced relapse, a target for non-dopaminergic candidates

## Clinical Picture
- ~1.6 million people met MUD criteria in 2022 (NSDUH); past-year meth use highest in ages 26–49 (1.4%)
- Cocaine overdose deaths: 4,681 (2011) → 29,449 (2023), driven largely by fentanyl co-use contamination
- Treatment retention is poor: 12-month retention in SUD programs for stimulant users <30%, vs ~50–60% for OUD on MOUD
- MUD and CUD have high psychiatric comorbidity: 40–50% lifetime major depression, elevated ADHD prevalence
- Stimulant-associated psychosis (SAP) occurs in 30–40% of heavy meth users and complicates treatment engagement

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Contingency management**: strongest evidence base; SAMHSA 2024 advisory supports CM; effect sizes (SMD ~0.3–0.6) for abstinence and retention
- **Bupropion + extended-release naltrexone (ADAPT-2 RCT)**: participants 2.4× more likely to achieve 3/4 meth-negative urines vs placebo; first Phase 3 signal for MUD
- **Mirtazapine**: two independent RCTs (Gay Men's Health Study, 2010; Colfax 2011) demonstrated reduced meth use; mechanism may involve α2-blockade reducing noradrenergic withdrawal drive
- **N-acetylcysteine (NAC)**: restores glutamate homeostasis in nucleus accumbens; mixed trial results for CUD; better signal in adolescent cannabis SUD suggests developmental specificity
- **AAAP/ASAM 2023 guidelines**: recommend CM as first-line; off-label pharmacotherapy (bupropion, naltrexone, mirtazapine) endorsed for MUD with shared decision-making; no equivalent standard for CUD

## Key Facts
- 0 FDA-approved medications for CUD or MUD (compared to 3 for OUD, 2 for AUD nicotine has 3)
- ADAPT-2 NNT approximately 8 for 3-week abstinence with bupropion/naltrexone combo
- Mirtazapine 30 mg/day reduced meth use days by ~60% vs 32% placebo in Colfax RCT (n=60)
- Stimulant-involved overdose deaths 34,855 in 2023; declined 19.8% to 8.5/100k in 2024 (CDC)
- FDA 2023 draft guidance accepts urine drug screen endpoints (≥3/4 weeks negative) as primary efficacy outcome — a key trial design clarification for industry
