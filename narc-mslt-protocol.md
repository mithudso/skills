# MSLT Diagnostic Protocol

## Core Concepts
- **Test design**: 5 nap opportunities scheduled 2 hours apart beginning ~1.5–3 hours after waking; each nap is 20 min if no sleep onset, or 35 min if sleep occurs (15 min recording continues after sleep onset)
- **Mean Sleep Latency (MSL)**: averaged across all 5 naps; ≤8 min = pathological sleepiness; >10 min generally normal; 8–10 min is borderline
- **SOREMP**: REM onset within 15 min of EEG-confirmed sleep onset; ≥2 SOREMPs (across MSLT + preceding PSG) required for narcolepsy diagnosis
- **PSG prerequisite**: full overnight polysomnography the night before MSLT is mandatory; PSG-SOREMP (REM within 15 min of sleep onset on PSG) counts as one of the two required SOREMPs
- **Indications**: narcolepsy (NT1/NT2), idiopathic hypersomnia (IH); NOT validated for routine OSA sleepiness quantification

## Mechanisms / Biology
- SOREMPs reflect REM dysregulation from orexin/hypocretin deficiency: without orexin stabilization, the brainstem flip-flop switch between wake and REM collapses, permitting REM intrusion within seconds to minutes of sleep onset
- REM-suppressing drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs) raise the apparent SOREMP threshold by inhibiting cholinergic REM-on neurons in the sublaterodorsal nucleus — producing false negatives
- Acute withdrawal of REM suppressants causes REM rebound: sudden release of inhibition floods nap opportunities with SOREMPs — producing **false positives**
- Sleep deprivation elevates homeostatic sleep pressure (Process S adenosine accumulation), compresses sleep latency, and increases SOREMP probability in non-narcoleptic subjects
- Circadian misalignment (shift workers, delayed sleep phase) shifts the REM propensity window, meaning nap timing may coincide with the endogenous circadian REM peak — artificially shortening latency and generating spurious SOREMPs

## Clinical Picture
- **NT1 sensitivity/specificity**: MSL ≤8 min + ≥2 SOREMPs — sensitivity 85–93%, specificity ~96% in cataplexy-confirmed cases; without cataplexy, ~70–80% sensitivity
- **NT2/IH overlap problem**: NT2 (no cataplexy, normal CSF orexin) and IH (MSL ≤8 min, <2 SOREMPs) can yield borderline MSLT profiles; NT2 MSLT test-retest concordance is only ~50%, vs ~90% for NT1
- 2 SOREMPs occur in ~2–4% of healthy subjects and ~14% of subjects with untreated OSA — specificity erodes in comorbid populations
- MSLT is falsely negative in up to 30% of confirmed NT1 cases at initial testing — often due to prior sleep deprivation or undisclosed REM-altering medications
- Pediatric MSLT: MSL ≤8 min + ≥2 SOREMPs applies ≥5 years; younger children have normally shorter sleep latencies; modified thresholds under study

## Evidence & Treatment
- **AASM 2021 protocol requirements**: ≥2 weeks of actigraphy + sleep diary confirming ≥7 h/night sleep; urine drug screen night before; PSG ≥6 h total sleep time (TST) — any deviation invalidates the MSLT
- **Medication washout**: stimulants/wake-promoting agents ≥5 half-lives (typically 2 weeks); REM-suppressants (SSRIs/SNRIs/TCAs) 2-week washout minimum; fluoxetine 4–6 weeks; failure to wash out is the most common cause of false-negative MSLT in clinical practice
- **Shift work**: AASM recommends shifting the patient to a conventional sleep schedule for at least 7 days pre-MSLT; testing on a shifted circadian phase produces unreliable MSL due to circadian REM propensity mismatch
- **Repeating the MSLT**: indicated when initial test is borderline or confounders were present; NT1 repeat is reliable; NT2 repeat frequently switches result — a second positive supports NT2, a negative does not exclude it
- CSF hypocretin-1 ≤110 pg/mL bypasses MSLT entirely for NT1 — preferred when medication washout is unsafe or clinically impractical

## Key Facts
- SOREMP threshold: REM within **15 minutes** of EEG sleep onset (nap or PSG)
- MSL pathological cutoff: **≤8 min**; normal population mean ~**12–15 min**
- Minimum PSG sleep before MSLT: **≥6 hours** TST — below this, MSLT is invalid
- REM-suppressant washout minimum: **2 weeks** (fluoxetine: **4–6 weeks**)
- SOREMPs in healthy controls: ~**2–4%** prevalence — confirms specificity is not 100% even with optimal protocol
