## Capital-markets / trading infrastructure: determinism over throughput

**The defining property is bounded worst-case latency, not raw throughput.** Engineers optimize the **tail (p99, p99.9, max), not the mean** — a system with a 5 µs median but occasional 500 µs spikes "loses exactly when it matters most" during volatile bursts.[^1] The economic driver is latency-arbitrage "races": the peer-reviewed Aquilina-Budish-O'Neill study found races are frequent (~one per minute per symbol on the LSE), won by a **5–10 microsecond** modal margin, concentrated (top-6 firms win >80%), and large (~$5B/year).[^1] *(Confidence: fact.)*

**Critical credibility nuance: "low latency" is a spectrum keyed to strategy, not a single bar.** A common (directional) tiering: HFT/latency-arbitrage needs single-digit-µs tick-to-trade and nanosecond-class hardware; systematic/quant tolerates ~1–10 ms; institutional execution tolerates tens-to-hundreds of ms. **All HFT needs low latency, but not all low-latency strategies are HFT.**[^1] A TAM should **never assume a bank's whole trading estate needs microseconds** — most desks operate well above the FPGA floor. *(Confidence: fact.)*

**Jitter is fought structurally, not tuned away:** pin the hot-path thread to an isolated core, busy-poll instead of sleep, pre-allocate/pre-fault memory, disable CPU frequency scaling, keep data NUMA-local. This is why **C++ (increasingly Rust) dominates the hot path** — garbage-collected runtimes give competitive *median* latency but inject non-deterministic pauses that destroy the tail (the "zero-allocation hot path" discipline).[^1] *(Confidence: fact; specific GC-pause figures DIRECTIONAL.)*

**Co-location & the physics of distance.** Firms place servers in the same data center as the exchange matching engine. Canonical hubs: **Equinix NY4** (Secaucus), **NYSE/ICE Mahwah**, **CME Aurora**, **London LD4/Slough**, **Nasdaq Carteret**. Exchanges engineer **latency-equalized cabling** (identical fiber lengths to every cage) so proximity *within* the building confers no edge — after which hardware differentiates. Light in fiber travels ~⅔ c, so firms moved the Chicago↔NJ route from fiber (~13 ms RT) to line-of-sight microwave (~8 ms RT).[^1] *(Confidence: fact; ms figures are well-bounded ranges.)*

**Hardware acceleration — real but narrow, and routinely overstated.** **FPGAs** implement market-data parsing, order-book maintenance, and pre-trade risk in silicon, collapsing tick-to-trade to tens-to-hundreds of nanoseconds.[^1] *…But* most strategies don't need it (C++ at 1–10 µs covers most institutional desks); the benchmark-to-production gap is where money is lost; in-house FPGA builds run 3+ years / $5M+ with scarce talent.[^1] **Kernel-bypass networking** (Solarflare/AMD OpenOnload, ef_vi, DPDK, RDMA, AF_XDP) moves the network path into userspace with zero-copy I/O; isolated-stack vendor numbers show ~10× gains, but one production auditor measured only a **~20–30% end-to-end** tick-to-trade reduction, eroding in 12–24 months as the bottleneck migrates downstream. *(Confidence: qualified — both true at different scopes; preserve the contradiction.)*

**Market-data protocols.** **FIX** (Financial Information eXchange) is the open, tag=value, text-based **cross-venue standard for order entry and post-trade/STP** (one engine speaks to dozens of venues).[^1] **Native binary feeds** are exchange-proprietary and faster — **Nasdaq ITCH/OUCH** (over MoldUDP64 multicast), **CME MDP 3.0 / iLink 3** (SBE — Simple Binary Encoding), Eurex T7, LSE Millennium — read by **pointer arithmetic at fixed offsets** (no delimiter-scanning), trimming bytes to ~⅓ of FIX. Market data is one-venue → many-listeners, so it uses **UDP multicast** (switch replicates once — efficient *and* fair); order entry is private/reliable → **TCP**. Feeds carry sequence numbers and recover via redundant A/B feeds + snapshot channels.[^1] *(Confidence: fact.)*

**Hot/warm/cold time-series tiering (the tick-data lifecycle; figures DIRECTIONAL):** **Hot** (in-memory, intraday, µs queries, live trading); **Warm** (compressed columnar Parquet/ZSTD on SSD, ms queries, rollups, most backtesting); **Cold** (Parquet/Iceberg on object storage, kept **3–7+ years** for compliance — MiFID II RTS 24 ~5yr, CAT 7+yr).[^1] Specialized columnar/time-series engines win (read only referenced columns, SIMD, 10–100× compression). **kdb+/KX** (with its q language) is the 30-year capital-markets standard (feedhandler → tickerplant → RDB real-time → HDB historical) and leads the vendor-neutral **STAC-M3** benchmark; **ClickHouse** and **QuestDB** increasingly take the warm/cold + research tier (often replacing kdb+ in new builds) but are typically ms-class vs kdb+ µs-class — so the dominant pattern is "run both."[^1] *(Confidence: fact for the tiering and engine set; qualified on displacement.)*

**Cloud caveat (important for FSI conversations):** public cloud still cannot match co-located determinism for ultra-low-latency *matching* — the unsolved problem is *proving deterministic fairness* (no wire-length control, variable VM-to-VM jitter). AWS's own exchange whitepaper and academic work agree; the gap for *optimized* (kernel-bypass) cloud has narrowed to ~2–4× of on-prem (DIRECTIONAL), and CME (with Google Cloud) and Nasdaq are migrating with special designs.[^1] *(Confidence: qualified.)*

## Payments rails and their infrastructure characteristics

**A bank runs several structurally different settlement systems in parallel** — high-value RTGS (real-time, gross, business-hours), batch retail (ACH, deferred net), and instant retail (24x7, near-instant, irrevocable, capped) — each with its own engine, calendar, settlement model, and risk profile.[^2] *(Confidence: fact.)*

**RTGS — Real-Time Gross Settlement (high-value wholesale).** Central-bank operated (settlement in central bank money), high-value, **real-time gross** (each payment settled individually, *not* netted), **settlement finality**, **NOT 24x7** (defined daily windows). Gross settlement eliminates interbank credit risk but maximizes intraday liquidity demand.[^2]
- **Fedwire Funds (US):** Fed-operated; per-transaction max **one penny under $10B**. **Correction of a common myth:** on **Oct 9 2025** the Fed decided to expand to **22x6 (Sunday–Friday), NOT 24x7** (explicitly chosen over 22x7x365), implementation **no earlier than 2028**.[^2]
- **CHAPS (UK):** BoE-operated sterling RTGS; gross/irrevocable; currently ~06:00–18:00 Mon–Fri. Extension underway (earlier start from Sept 2027; phased path toward 22x6 ≥2031).[^2]
- **TARGET2 / T2 (Eurozone):** **T2 replaced TARGET2 on 20 March 2023** (CLM + RTGS, migrated to ISO 20022); Mon–Fri, closed weekends.[^2] *(Confidence: fact.)*

**Instant / real-time rails (24x7x365, near-instant, irrevocable, capped).**
- **FedNow (US):** Fed-operated, **launched July 20 2023**; settles in Fed master accounts (centralized ledger, *not* DLT); value cap raised to **$10M (June 2025)**.[^2]
- **TCH RTP (US):** private-sector, **launched 2017** (first new core US rail in 40+ years); limit raised to **$10M (Feb 2025)**.[^2]
- **UK Faster Payments (FPS):** Pay.UK, launched 2008; near-instant, irrevocable; **£1M cap** (above → CHAPS).[^2]
- **SEPA Instant (SCT Inst, EU):** EPC scheme; funds available in **<10 seconds**; the scheme-level €100k cap has been **removed** (the "€100k cap" is outdated). Settles via **TIPS** (central bank money) or EBA Clearing's **RT1** (commercial bank money). **EU Instant Payments Regulation (Reg (EU) 2024/886):** euro-area PSPs had to **receive by 9 Jan 2025, send by 9 Oct 2025**, with mandatory Verification of Payee and equal pricing — so as of 2026 "instant becomes default."[^2] *(Confidence: fact on regulation dates; coverage figures move.)*

**ACH (batch) vs instant — the core bifurcation.** ACH (governed by Nacha; FedACH + TCH's EPN) processes **large batches** submitted 1–2 business days ahead using **deferred net settlement**; **Same Day ACH is still batch** (extra windows, business days only) — *not* real-time. Instant rails process **per-transaction in real time** with **gross** settlement. They're **complementary, not replacement** — ACH (~3B+/month) still dwarfs instant volumes.[^2] *(Confidence: fact.)*

**The payment-hub / orchestration pattern.** To manage rail heterogeneity, banks deploy a **payment hub** — a centralized orchestration layer between the core and the scheme connectors that routes each transaction to the appropriate **execution engine** (ACH/wire/instant), normalizes across ISO 20022 and legacy formats, and enables attribute-driven **intelligent routing** (e.g. FedNow vs RTP; Fedwire vs CHIPS) — replacing point-to-point sprawl.[^2] *(Confidence: fact; "intelligent routing" qualified to a Cleveland Fed brief.)*

**Settlement models — the conceptual spine.** **Deferred Net Settlement (DNS):** net obligations settled (usually end-of-day) — saves liquidity but creates interbank credit risk (ACH is the canonical retail DNS). **RTGS:** each payment settled individually with immediate finality — eliminates that credit risk but imposes higher intraday liquidity demand. **Important nuance — gross-vs-net is a spectrum: CHIPS** (TCH's private USD high-value system, ~$2T/day) is a **hybrid — continuous net settlement with finality** via multilateral netting + queuing (~$26 settled per $1 funded) — the cleanest counterexample to "high-value = pure RTGS." And "instant" ≠ one settlement model: FedNow/TIPS settle in central bank money, RTP against a prefunded Fed account, RT1 in commercial bank money — don't flatten this.[^2] *(Confidence: fact / qualified.)*

## ISO 20022: the rich-data messaging standard and its migration

**What it is.** An ISO/TC68 standard that is *not itself a format* but a **standardization methodology + central data dictionary + business model**, rendered into **XML** (most common). It replaces legacy **SWIFT MT** messages (fixed-format, length-constrained, largely free-text); SWIFT brands ISO 20022 messages **MX**. Canonical mappings (equivalences, not strict 1:1): **MT103 → pacs.008** (customer credit transfer), **MT202 → pacs.009** (FI-to-FI), plus the camt.* family.[^3] The richness is concrete — where MT103 had one ~140-char free-text remittance field, ISO 20022 allows up to ~9,000 chars of *structured* remittance plus distinct purpose-code, regulatory-ref, and party-address elements.[^3] *(Confidence: fact.)*

**Why richer data matters** (SWIFT, BIS/CPMI, consultancies converge): better STP; **better sanctions/AML screening with fewer false positives** (distinct name+address fields disambiguate "CA"=Canada vs California) — an oft-cited EY/Citi estimate of **25–30% false-positive reduction** (qualified — a consultancy estimate); reduced truncation; analytics/fraud-ML; cross-rail interoperability.[^3] *(Confidence: fact for direction; the 25–30% figure qualified.)*

**Migration timeline (verified against primary/operator sources — the highest-value facts):**
- **TARGET2 → T2 (ECB):** live **20 March 2023**, deliberate big-bang, no MT/MX coexistence.[^3]
- **CHAPS / BoE:** ISO 20022 messaging from **19 June 2023**; enhanced-data mandates (Purpose Codes, LEIs) from **1 May 2025**.[^3]
- **CHIPS (TCH):** migrated **8 April 2024**.[^3]
- **Fedwire Funds (the date most often misremembered):** original big-bang **10 March 2025** → rescheduled (Feb 13 2025) to **14 July 2025**, executed as a single-day, no-fallback cutover. *State it as: original March 10 2025 → actual July 14 2025.*[^3]
- **SWIFT cross-border (CBPR+) MT→ISO coexistence END: 22 November 2025** — after which FIN MT payment instructions are no longer delivered normally; SWIFT applies surcharges from 1 Jan 2026 for contingency/in-flow translation.[^3]
- **FedNow launched natively ISO 20022** (greenfield, not a migration).[^3] *(Confidence: fact.)*

**The risk / critique — "compliance-box-checking with translation layers"** (corroborated by *neutral* bodies): institutions relying long-term on SWIFT's contingency/translation "never truly migrated" — they receive rich MX, convert it back to MT for legacy cores, and **strip out the structured data that made migration worthwhile**. The benefit lives *downstream of the gateway* — "Phase 1 was translation; Phase 2 is transformation." **BIS/CPMI Brief No 11 (Apr 2026)** independently confirms some PMIs still rely on translation and that rich-data use "remains constrained."[^3] *(Confidence: fact.)*

## 24x7 / always-on expectations and the tension with batch

**Instant rails run 24x7x365 and remove the scheme-level overnight window** (FedNow "uninterrupted 24x7x365"; RTP 24/7/52; UK FPS "24*7 without exception"; SEPA Instant "all calendar days"); TCH data indicates ~50% of RTP volume is outside traditional banking hours.[^4] *(Confidence: fact.)* **The tension with batch** (deep-dived in `batch-and-mainframe-modernization.md`): traditional cores assume a nightly offline window; instant rails are "incompatible with a system that waits until end-of-day to clear." Banks historically bridged this with **stand-in / shadow-ledger** layers; by 2026 instant payments are framed as **an operating-model issue**, not just a tech integration.[^4] *(Confidence: fact.)*

**High availability vs continuous availability — a precise distinction:** **HA** masks *unplanned* outages (nines; 99.999% ≈ 5 min/yr) and HA systems can still have a maintenance window; **continuous operation** masks *planned* outages; **continuous availability (CA) = HA + CO** masks both. Instant-rail participation pushes toward CA because **downtime in a 24x7 rail = missed payments in real time**, immediately customer-visible (unlike a batch outage caught up overnight).[^4] *(Confidence: fact.)*

**Key disconfirming nuance — "24x7" is scheme-level, not literally zero-maintenance per bank, and RTGS is still windowed:** instant-rail rulebooks permit limited *participant* planned downtime (FedNow ≤2h/Sun 2–6am; SEPA "short and foreseeable"; FPS stand-in); "100% uptime" is a TCH self-claim contradicted by real 2026 SEPA Instant outages; banks still run 6h+ windows while branding "always-on"; and RTGS chose 22x6 with deliberate change windows retained.[^4] *(Confidence: fact / qualified.)*

## Disconfirming evidence

1. **Most firms don't need ultra-low latency** — only pure latency-arbitrage HFT needs co-lo + FPGA + kernel-bypass + microwave; C++ at 1–10 µs covers most institutional desks.[^1]
2. **FPGA, kernel-bypass, and kdb+ are real but routinely overstated** — kernel-bypass gives ~20–30% *real* end-to-end gain (not the headline ~10× isolated-stack); kdb+ is challenged by ClickHouse/QuestDB on cost/SQL-talent and replaced in new builds, though still winning the µs hot path. **Treat all single-vendor latency numbers as DIRECTIONAL.**[^1]
3. **Cloud cannot yet prove deterministic fairness for ULL matching** (AWS's own whitepaper + academia agree); the optimized-cloud gap is ~2–4× on-prem, not 10–100×.[^1]
4. **Instant-payment adoption is slower than expected ("no hockey stick")** — RTP/FedNow volumes are a fraction of ACH; a pronounced "Send Gap" (more banks receive than send) reflects the 24x7 operating-model burden, liquidity, and fraud — not just connectivity. ACH and instant coexist.[^2][^4]
5. **Irrevocability is double-edged — APP fraud.** Instant-and-irrevocable attracts Authorized Push Payment scams; US consumer-protection law generally does *not* require reimbursement for *authorized* fraud (the UK has closed this by mandate).[^2]
6. **ISO 20022 dates were repeatedly *delayed*, and "97% compliant" overstates adoption** — Fedwire slipped March→July 2025; "97% native" counts messages that *look* ISO 20022 but are often translated back to MT internally; ROI is openly admitted unproven by senior bank practitioners; BIS/CPMI confirms translation-reliance.[^3]

## Seller takeaways

- **Segment the trading estate before pitching** — only a slice needs microseconds; most needs ms and tolerates general-purpose infrastructure.
- **Speak the rail-and-settlement vocabulary** — RTGS vs DNS vs instant, finality, the payment hub, ISO 20022 pacs/camt. The credible angle on ISO 20022 is "real value is downstream transformation, not gateway translation."
- **Always-on is sharpest at the payment-rail edge** — general digital channels still tolerate windows; don't over-claim a universal 24x7 requirement.
