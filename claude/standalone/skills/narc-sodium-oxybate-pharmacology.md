# Sodium Oxybate Pharmacology

## Core Concepts
- Sodium oxybate is the sodium salt of gamma-hydroxybutyrate (GHB); a CNS depressant approved for narcolepsy cataplexy and EDS
- Brands: Xyrem (twice-nightly IR oral solution), Xywav (low-sodium, twice-nightly), Lumryz (once-nightly ER suspension, FDA-approved 2023)
- Approved for adults and children ≥7 years; Schedule III controlled substance (high abuse potential as illicit GHB)
- Bioavailability ~88% orally; rapid absorption; half-life 30–60 min; nonlinear kinetics — saturable first-pass metabolism via GHB dehydrogenase

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Dual receptor agonism: high-affinity GHB-specific receptor (Gi/Go-coupled) at lower concentrations; GABA-B receptor agonism at therapeutic doses
- GABA-B action at noradrenergic (locus coeruleus) and dopaminergic (VTA) neurons suppresses monoamine release, reducing the instability of wake/sleep switching in orexin-deficient patients
- Thalamocortical GABA-B agonism hyperpolarizes relay neurons, generating slow oscillations (delta waves) → consolidates N3 slow-wave sleep (SWS)
- Increased serotonin turnover + dopamine inhibition secondary to GHB receptor activation reinforces SWS architecture
- Nocturnal SWS consolidation restores restorative sleep pressure, reducing fragmented nighttime sleep and secondary EDS the following day; anterior cingulate GABA/glutamate balance improves with chronic use (fMRI/MRS evidence)

## Clinical Picture
- Narcolepsy type 1 (NT1, orexin-deficient): primary indication — addresses both EDS and cataplexy via single agent
- Efficacy in NT2 (no cataplexy) for EDS: supported; Xywav FDA-approved for NT2 in 2021
- High sodium load in Xyrem (~1640 mg Na/9 g dose) clinically relevant: narcolepsy patients have elevated cardiovascular and cardiometabolic risk vs general population
- Patients with heart failure, hypertension, or renal disease particularly disadvantaged by traditional high-sodium formulations

## Evidence & Treatment
- Phase 3 RCT (Xyrem): dose-dependent reduction in weekly cataplexy attacks and Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) scores vs placebo; effect seen at 6 g/night, optimized at 9 g/night
- Xywav (JZP-258) pivotal trial: randomized withdrawal design — sustained efficacy on cataplexy and EDS with 92% less sodium (131 mg Na/9 g dose) vs Xyrem (1640 mg Na); non-inferiority established (SLEEP 2021)
- Lumryz (sodium oxybate ER) phase 3 (REST-ON): once-nightly dosing dose-dependently improved MWT sleep latency, reduced cataplexy, and improved DNS vs placebo in adults ≥16
- Dosing: start 4.5 g/night split (IR) or single dose (ER); titrate by 1.5 g increments over weeks; max 9 g/night
- Contraindicated with succinic semialdehyde dehydrogenase deficiency; strong caution with opioids/alcohol/sedatives (synergistic respiratory depression)

## Key Facts
- 92% sodium reduction: Xywav contains 131 mg Na/9 g vs 1640 mg Na/9 g for Xyrem
- Number needed to treat: ~2–3 for ≥50% cataplexy reduction at optimal dose
- SWS increase: 50 mg/kg dose increases N3 duration at expense of N1 sleep; REM preserved
- Lumryz approval: July 2023 — first once-nightly GHB formulation; eliminates middle-of-night wake for second dose
- Abuse potential: Schedule III; distributed only via REMS program (Xyrem/Xywav REMS); illicit GHB (date-rape drug) is Schedule I
