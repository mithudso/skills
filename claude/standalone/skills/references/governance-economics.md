## Voting mechanisms (token-weighted, quadratic, conviction)

**Token-weighted ("coin") voting** — one-token-one-vote, the dominant DAO model (Compound/Uniswap-style Governor contracts). Vitalik's critique: coin voting is **plutocratic** and only "feels credibly neutral" — "in practice it may only appear secure today precisely because of the imperfections in its neutrality (large portions of supply in the hands of a tightly-coordinated clique of insiders)." It also suffers structural problems: low participation, whale dominance, and misaligned incentives (a token holder's stake ≠ the protocol's long-run interest).[^1] **[FACT]**

**Quadratic voting (QV)** — voting power ∝ **square root** of resources committed (1,000 votes costs 1,000,000 credits), so intensity of preference is expressed while a wealthy minority's disproportionate power is curbed (Posner & Weyl; Buterin–Hitzig–Weyl 2018 formalization of Quadratic *Funding*).[^2][^4] Vitalik frames it as a hybrid: the financial component lets voters credibly signal *how strongly* they care.[^3] **Gitcoin Quadratic Funding** is the canonical large-scale deployment.[^4][^5] **[FACT]**

- **The fatal dependency — identity & sybil.** QV "relies heavily on verifiable, separate human identities," because splitting resources across many wallets multiplies effective influence. Permissionless blockchains "cannot reliably tie wallets to identity," so the single-identity assumption fails — which is why large-scale QV ships mostly in **non-binding/funding** contexts (Gitcoin) with anti-sybil add-ons (Gitcoin Passport), not in binding governance.[^2][^6] An academic result ("Concave is the New Linear: The Impossibility of Anti-Plutocratic DAO Governance," 2026) shows an attacker can split tokens across wallets to obtain disproportionate power the chain can't trace — formalizing the impossibility.[^6] **[FACT]**

**Conviction voting (CV)** — Commons Stack / 1Hive / BlockScience (2019): voters **continuously** stake tokens behind proposals (all live simultaneously, no A-vs-B), and **conviction accumulates over time along an exponential-decay/half-life curve** up to a cap; a proposal passes when accumulated conviction crosses a **threshold** (set by the proportion of the common pool requested). Switching support drains conviction from the old proposal. This dampens abrupt token movements (eliminating arbitrary lock periods), removes the per-proposal quorum bottleneck, and prevents "double-counting" influence across proposals. Live on 1Hive (xDAI) allocating its Honey community currency.[^7][^7a][^7b] **[FACT]**

> **Net read:** coin voting is the deployed default *because* it's permissionless and sybil-resistant (you must *buy* the token); QV and CV try to escape plutocracy but pay for it in identity assumptions (QV) or implementation complexity / continuous-attention demands (CV). The genuine frontier — and Vitalik's recommendation — is "moving away from the idea that coin voting is the only legitimate form of governance decentralization," toward proof-of-personhood / proof-of-participation + **anti-collusion infrastructure** (MACI).[^1][^8] **[QUALIFIED.]**

## Vote-buying & bribe markets (Curve wars)

**The mechanism.** When a vote has cash value (e.g., directing token emissions), a market for buying it emerges. The archetype is the **Curve Wars**: DeFi protocols compete to control **veCRV** (vote-escrowed CRV) gauge-weight votes, which direct CRV emissions — and thus liquidity — toward their pools.[^v1][^v2][^v5] Rather than accumulate veCRV directly, a protocol **bribes** existing voters: it deposits its native token into a bribe contract for a target gauge; veCRV (or vote-locked Convex `vlCVX`) holders who vote for that gauge claim the bribe pro-rata to their voting weight after the epoch snapshot.[^v1][^v3][^v4] **[FACT]**

- **It's a real, "legal," capital-efficient market.** Bribing is rational when `bribe_cost < emissions_attracted × token_price`; it's *cheaper* to bribe than to buy and lock the votes outright, and cheaper still to bribe **meta-governance** (CVX/vlCVX) than veCRV directly, due to the leverage effect.[^v6][^v3] **[FACT]**
- **Platforms:** **Votium** (Curve/Convex; weekly epochs), **Hidden Hand** (Redacted Cartel; multi-protocol — Balancer veBAL, Frax veFXS), **Warden** (Paladin). Aggregators (Convex, Aura, Stake DAO) pool ve-tokens.[^v1][^v2][^v6] **[FACT]**
- **Scale (volatile):** peak bribe volumes ~$10–20M/round through Votium/Hidden Hand (Votium's April 2022 round ~$21M); stablecoin issuers (FRAX, MIM, pre-collapse UST) spent millions weekly; top protocols claimed 3–10× ROI on bribe spend. As of early 2026 the market has matured (optimized spend; aggregators control a large ve-share).[^v3][^v6] **[QUALIFIED — volatile figures.]**
- **veTokenomics as a commitment device** — locking for max voting power filters out short-term holders and aligns governor/protocol horizons, doubling as a velocity-reduction sink (see `references/tokenomics.md`).[^v6] **[FACT]**

> **Negation / the economic critique.** Bribe markets can produce an **oligopoly in liquidity management** (large bribe budgets capture the bulk of emissions, starving smaller pools), and create a **reflexive fragility**: when bribe ROI turns negative, bribing stops → ve-token demand falls → unlocks → supply rises → price drops.[^v6] More fundamentally, bribe markets are vote-buying made frictionless — the very thing coercion-resistance and anti-collusion infrastructure (MACI) exist to *prevent* in governance — here re-introduced as a *feature* of incentive-direction governance.[^8][^v6] **[QUALIFIED.]**

## DAO treasuries

**Composition & sources.** Treasury funds come from (1) **initial token allocation** (Uniswap ~43% to treasury vested over 4 yrs; ENS 50%; Optimism 25% foundation + 5% citizens' house), (2) **protocol revenue** (Aave's Collector aggregated ~$190M through Q1 2026; Lido captures 5% of staking rewards), and (3) **investment returns** from deployed capital. Typical composition: native governance token (usually the largest USD position), stablecoins (USDC/DAI/USDT), and ETH.[^t6][^t1] **[FACT]**

**The native-token concentration doom-loop (the central, multiply-attested finding).** A study of the 20 largest DAO treasuries (~$9.6B) found **~81.67% of assets held in native DAO tokens**; many DAOs hold 60–95% in their own governance token.[^t2][^t1][^t6] This creates a **reflexive catastrophe risk**: treasury value is correlated with token price, so it collapses *precisely* in the scenarios where the DAO most needs capital — a security breach, governance failure, or regulatory action simultaneously destroys the token's value and creates peak demand for a response. "Treasuries built entirely in native governance tokens have approximately zero resilience to exactly the scenarios in which treasury resources are most needed."[^t1][^t3] During the 2022 bear market, DAOs that "appeared richly funded" found their treasuries insufficient for even two years of stablecoin-denominated runway.[^t3][^t4] **[FACT]**

**Diversification & its governance friction.** The fix is converting native tokens to stablecoins/ETH (ETH being a "middle ground" — lower correlation to a single protocol's token while retaining ecosystem upside). But execution is the hard part:[^t1][^t3][^t4]
- Selling large quantities of the native token **creates market impact** and may **signal declining internal conviction**; mitigations include dollar-cost-averaging, OTC sales, structured products, and **token swaps** with peer protocols (MakerDAO, Lido have done significant swaps).
- The governance dimension is the key friction: **token holders who hold for price-appreciation upside resist treasury sales that add sell pressure** — pitting the DAO's collective financial prudence (diversify for resilience) against individual holders' economic interest (avoid sell pressure). Diversification proposals "regularly generate contentious governance battles." **[FACT]**
- The maturity frontier: governance frameworks that **mandate diversification targets**, risk-management committees with genuine authority, and compliance infrastructure for institutional counterparties.[^t3] **[QUALIFIED.]**

## Governance attacks (the negation layer)

**Permissionless voting is vulnerable to in-protocol governance attacks** — an attacker acquires voting power (buying tokens, or borrowing them) and uses it for their own benefit; because the attack uses the mechanism *as designed*, it "can't be prevented through cryptography" (a16z).[^a1] **[FACT]**

- **Flash-loan governance attack — Beanstalk (Apr 2022).** An attacker flash-borrowed >$1B (Aave/Uniswap/SushiSwap), converted it to enough governance tokens to instantly hold a **supermajority (~67–79%)**, voted through a malicious proposal, and used the protocol's `emergencyCommit()` (which bypassed timelocks) to **drain ~$182M** (~$76M net profit) — **all in a single transaction**, loans repaid in the same block. No contract bug was exploited; it was a *governance-logic* flaw (votable LP tokens could be created via flash loan, with no anti-flash-loan/snapshot protection).[^a1][^a2][^a3][^a4] **[FACT]**
- **Quorum-apathy attack.** Chronic low turnout (average DAO voter turnout <10%; top 10% of holders control ~76% of voting power; quorum thresholds often ~4%) means an attacker needs only a small fraction of supply to become a "dictator" (e.g., Moonwell's attacker hit quorum in 11 minutes with ~$1,808).[^a5][^a6] **[QUALIFIED.]**
- **Surreptitious accumulation.** An attacker can create many anonymous accounts and slowly accumulate tokens (dormant, even *adding to the appearance of healthy decentralization*) until they can unilaterally control governance.[^a1] **[FACT]**
- **Defenses (and their cost).** **Timelocks** (delay between a passed vote and execution — Beanstalk's `emergencyCommit` bypassed this), **quorum thresholds**, **proposal thresholds**, and **time-weighted / snapshot voting** (measure voting power *before* the proposal existed → flash-loaned tokens don't count). Wang et al. (2025): the standard toolkit (lock-ups, delays, quorum) "addresses the symptom by penalizing the entire voter base," whereas a time-weighted-snapshot framework "addresses the root cause" — a single-block measurement — making flash loans irrelevant. But lock-ups are **regressive**: they exclude smaller holders with higher opportunity cost, *centralizing* governance as a side-effect of the security mechanism.[^a6][^a7] **[FACT / QUALIFIED.]**

> **Through-line:** governance attacks confirm the voting-mechanism critique — coin voting's permissionlessness is exactly what lets *transient capital* (flash loans) or *apathy* (low quorum) capture the protocol; and the standard defenses re-introduce plutocratic centralization. The attack surface has shifted "to social and economic vectors: token accumulation, bribery markets, Trojan proposals, and chronic voter apathy."[^a6] **[QUALIFIED.]**
