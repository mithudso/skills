reading notes, various

From Make It Stick (Brown et al):
- testing yourself beats rereading. "retrieval practice" - the act of recalling strengthens memory more than re-exposure
- spacing out practice feels worse but works better. massed practice = fast gains that evaporate
- interleaving different problem types beats blocking, even though blocked feels easier
- "desirable difficulties" (Bjork) - harder encoding = stronger retention

Ahrens, How to Take Smart Notes:
- writing IS thinking, not the record of it
- elaboration = connecting new info to what you know. thats what makes it stick
- notes should be in your own words. copying quotes bypasses understanding

karpicke & blunt 2011 science paper - retrieval practice beat elaborative studying (concept mapping) for learning science texts. even when students predicted the opposite

cepeda et al meta-analysis: optimal spacing gap scales with retention interval. cram for tomorrow, space for forever

random: anki uses SM-2. intervals expand on success. leitner boxes = analog version

matuschak - "evergreen notes" concept oriented not source oriented. second source on same concept lands on the existing note and they compound. note titles like APIs

Bjork again (diff paper?) - "storage strength" vs "retrieval strength". forgetting is retrieval strength decay, storage persists. thats why relearning is faster

dunlosky 2013 review: practice testing + distributed practice rated "high utility". highlighting and rereading rated LOW. summarization middling
