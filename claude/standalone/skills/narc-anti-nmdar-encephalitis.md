# Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis — Sleep & Psychiatric Differential

## Epidemiology
Most common autoimmune encephalitis (~1.5/million incidence; likely underestimated). Predominantly young females (60–70%); 20–50% of female cases have ovarian teratoma. Males and older adults affected but less commonly. Increasing recognition drives apparent incidence rise.

## Clinical Staging
**Stage 1 — Prodrome** (days): fever, headache, viral-like illness. Often attributed to flu; retrospectively identified.

**Stage 2 — Psychiatric** (days–weeks): anxiety, psychosis, bizarre behavior, mania, catatonia. 75% of patients first see a psychiatrist. Mimics meth psychosis or first-episode schizophrenia closely. Abnormal orofacial/limb/trunk movements begin to emerge and are a critical differentiating feature.

**Stage 3 — Neurological**: decreased consciousness, seizures, autonomic instability (fever, BP lability, tachycardia, bradycardia), central hypoventilation. Dyskinesias become prominent and persistent.

## Sleep-Specific Manifestations
- **Agripnia excitata**: paradoxical insomnia — patient appears exhausted but cannot sleep despite severe fatigue; a hallmark of the encephalitic phase
- **Severe PSG disruption**: loss of normal NREM/REM architecture; abnormal wakefulness patterns; disorganized sleep staging
- **Delta brush on EEG**: high-amplitude delta with superimposed beta-frequency "brush" — pathognomonic when present (~30% of severe cases); also consistent with anti-NMDAR even absent delta brush
- **Recovery-phase hypersomnia**: as encephalitis resolves, patients shift to prolonged sleep; can mimic secondary narcolepsy phenotype — distinguish by antibody testing and trajectory

## Mechanism
IgG autoantibodies against GluN1 subunit → receptor internalization → NMDA receptor hypofunction. This mirrors the ketamine/PCP mechanism used to model schizophrenia: NMDAR hypofunction disinhibits dopaminergic projections → psychosis; disrupts hippocampal/limbic circuits → memory loss and behavioral dysregulation.

## Meth Psychosis Differential (Critical)

| Feature | Meth Psychosis | Anti-NMDAR Encephalitis |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Hours–days | Days–weeks (subacute) |
| Course | Clears within days of abstinence | Progressive deterioration |
| Abnormal movements | Absent | Orofacial/limb dyskinesias present |
| Autonomic instability | Absent (or tachycardia only from stimulant) | Present (temp, BP, HR swings) |
| CSF | Normal | Pleocytosis (~80%) |
| Anti-GluN1 antibody | Negative | Positive (CSF > serum sensitivity) |
| EEG | Normal or diffuse slowing | Generalized slowing; delta brush |
| Response to abstinence | Resolves | Does not resolve; worsens |

**Red flags in apparent meth psychosis that demand encephalitis workup:** psychosis lasting >1 week after confirmed abstinence; orofacial stereotypies or limb dyskinesias not attributable to stimulant; autonomic instability beyond expected stimulant toxidrome; CSF pleocytosis; treatment-resistant psychosis on antipsychotics.

## Narcolepsy Differential
Recovery-phase hypersomnia can mimic secondary narcolepsy; distinguish by: clinical trajectory (improving vs. stable/chronic), preceding encephalitic episode, positive anti-GluN1 antibodies, abnormal PSG architecture (not classic REM-onset sleep). Note: a 2012 study (Landolfi et al.) found anti-NMDAR antibodies in some narcolepsy cases with psychotic features — intersection warrants antibody testing when narcolepsy presents atypically with psychosis.

## Diagnosis Workup
1. **CSF anti-GluN1 IgG** — gold standard; serum is less sensitive and can be negative when CSF is positive
2. **MRI brain** — often normal (unlike LGI1 encephalitis, which shows medial temporal signal); normal MRI does not exclude diagnosis
3. **EEG** — generalized slowing; delta brush when present is pathognomonic
4. **CSF analysis** — pleocytosis in ~80%, mild protein elevation
5. **Tumor workup** — CT chest/abdomen/pelvis; MRI/transvaginal ultrasound pelvis for ovarian teratoma; testicular ultrasound in males; repeat if initially negative

## Treatment
**First line:** IV methylprednisolone 1 g/day × 5 days + IVIG 2 g/kg OR plasmapheresis (choose one or combine)

**Teratoma:** Surgical resection, when found, often dramatically accelerates recovery — search aggressively and remove promptly

**Second line (refractory):** Rituximab + cyclophosphamide

**Prognosis:** ~80% good outcome with appropriate immunotherapy; recovery over months to 2 years; 5–10% relapse rate; delayed treatment worsens outcome

## When to Invoke This Differential
- Any psychosis in young female without prior psychiatric history
- Meth psychosis that fails to clear ≥7 days post-abstinence
- Psychosis + orofacial/limb movements (not tardive dyskinesia pattern)
- Psychosis + autonomic instability + seizures in same patient
- Apparent first-episode schizophrenia with rapid progression to coma/hypoventilation
- Post-encephalitis hypersomnia before labeling secondary narcolepsy
