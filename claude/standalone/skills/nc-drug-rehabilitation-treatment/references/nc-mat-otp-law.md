## 1. Overview — MOUD Types and Regulatory Framework

Three FDA-approved medications for opioid use disorder (MOUD / MAT) are the core of treatment:

| Medication | Schedule | Who Can Prescribe | Dispensing Setting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Methadone (for OUD) | Schedule II | OTPs only (not office-based) | Licensed OTP clinic or mobile unit |
| Buprenorphine (mono or combo) | Schedule III | Any DEA-registered practitioner (post-2023) | Office-based (OBOT), OTP, telehealth |
| Naltrexone (oral or injectable Vivitrol) | Not scheduled | Any licensed prescriber | Office-based, urgent care, corrections |

**Key regulatory layers:**
- **Federal:** 42 CFR Part 8 (SAMHSA certification for OTPs); 21 CFR (DEA Schedule registration); Ryan Haight Act (telehealth prescribing of controlled substances)
- **State:** NC DHHS / DHSR licensure (10A NCAC 27G .3600 for OTPs); NC Board of Medicine / Pharmacy rules; Medicaid clinical coverage policies

## 2. Opioid Treatment Programs (OTPs) — Federal Requirements (42 CFR Part 8)

An **Opioid Treatment Program (OTP)** is the only setting where methadone may be dispensed for OUD treatment.[^1] OTPs also dispense buprenorphine and naltrexone, though office-based prescribers may do the latter two independently.

### Federal Certification Steps for an OTP

1. **DEA Registration** — OTP must register with DEA under 21 CFR Chapter II before administering MOUD.[^1]
2. **SAMHSA Certification** — Must be certified under 42 CFR § 8.11; certification is valid for up to 3 years and must be renewed in the final year.[^1]
3. **Accreditation Body Approval** — Must hold current accreditation from a SAMHSA-approved accreditation body (e.g., CARF, Joint Commission, COA).[^1]
4. **Treatment Standards Compliance** — Must comply with 42 CFR § 8.12 Federal Opioid Use Disorder treatment standards, including individualized treatment plans, counseling availability, and medical oversight.[^1]
5. **Medical Director** — OTP must notify SAMHSA in writing within 3 weeks of any change in program sponsor or medical director.[^1]

## 3. 2024 Final Rule — Major Changes to 42 CFR Part 8

SAMHSA's final rule (effective April 2, 2024; compliance deadline October 2, 2024) introduced the most significant reforms to OTP regulations in decades.[^2]

### Key Changes

**Take-Home Methadone Flexibility**
- **Old rule:** Rigid criteria governed take-home doses based solely on months in treatment.
- **New rule:** Practitioners have clinical discretion to determine take-home dose quantities based on individualized patient stability assessments.[^2]
  - Patients may receive up to **7 take-home doses in the first 14 days** of treatment.
  - Patients demonstrating stability after **1 month** may receive up to a **28-day supply**.
  - No longer a prescriptive grid of waiting periods.[^2]

**Mobile Medication Units**
- Mobile units may now provide the **full range of OTP services**, including MOUD inductions (not just observed dosing).[^2]
- OTPs can bring treatment to homeless encampments, rural communities, and other underserved areas.
- Mobile units require separate state licensure in NC (see Section 4).

**Patient-Centered Care**
- Methadone counseling requirements are more flexible — counseling availability required but not mandatory attendance as condition of dosing (individualized based on treatment need).[^2]
- Reduced documentation burden for routine clinical decisions.

**Buprenorphine Inductions**
- OTPs can now conduct buprenorphine inductions via telehealth within the OTP setting.[^2]

## 4. NC-Specific OTP Licensing and SOTA Oversight

### Dual-Track Requirement

Every OTP operating in North Carolina must hold **both**:[^3]
1. **Federal SAMHSA certification** (42 CFR Part 8) — from SAMHSA
2. **State DHSR licensure** — from NC Division of Health Service Regulation under 10A NCAC 27G .3600

Operating without a state license is a **Class H felony** with fines of $1,000 per day.[^3]

### North Carolina State Opioid Treatment Authority (SOTA)

- **Contact:** NC Division of MHDDSUS, 3004 Mail Service Center, Raleigh, NC 27699; (984) 289-1511[^4]
- **Role:** The NC SOTA provides oversight and support for OTPs; **preliminary SOTA approval is required before applying for DHSR licensure**.[^3]
- **2024 SOTA Guidance:** Following the 2024 42 CFR Part 8 final rule, NC SOTA issued specific implementation guidance for NC OTPs on how take-home flexibility and mobile unit rules apply within the state regulatory framework.[^4]

### NC OTP Application Pathway

| Step | Action | Agency |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contact NC SOTA for preliminary approval | NC DHHS/MHDDSUS |
| 2 | Apply for DHSR licensure (10A NCAC 27G .3600) | NC Division of Health Service Regulation |
| 3 | Concurrently: DEA registration (21 CFR) | U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration |
| 4 | SAMHSA certification application | SAMHSA/CSAT |
| 5 | Accreditation body approval | CARF/Joint Commission/COA |

### NC OTP Access Gap

OTP services are available in only **55 of NC's 100 counties**.[^4] NC has been expanding mobile OTP units to reach underserved areas, with mobile units authorized under Session Law 2023-65 (House Bill 190).[^3]

## 5. Buprenorphine Prescribing — X-Waiver Elimination (2023)

### What Changed

The **Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2023** (signed December 2022, effective January 2023) eliminated the DATA-Waiver (X-Waiver) requirement for prescribing buprenorphine to treat OUD.[^5]

### New Rules Under the MAT Act

- **Who can prescribe:** Any DEA-registered practitioner with **Schedule III authority** can prescribe buprenorphine for OUD — no waiver application required.[^5]
- **Patient limits removed:** No cap on the number of patients a single practitioner can treat for OUD with buprenorphine.[^5]
- **Training requirement:** All DEA-registered practitioners must complete an **8-hour training requirement** on substance use disorder treatment (applicable to DEA registrations submitted after June 27, 2023).[^5]
- **State law still applies:** Practitioners must comply with NC Board of Medicine rules and other applicable state laws.[^5]
- **Estimated impact:** Expanded the potential buprenorphine prescriber pool from ~130,000 to ~1.8 million.[^5]

### NC Impact

Buprenorphine remains a Schedule III controlled substance. NC practitioners must:
- Hold active DEA registration with Schedule III authority
- Comply with the NC Controlled Substances Reporting System (CSRS) — report buprenorphine prescriptions to NC DHHS
- Ensure prescribing falls within their scope of practice under NC licensure

## 6. DEA Telehealth Buprenorphine Rules (2025)

### Background — COVID-Era Flexibilities

During the COVID-19 public health emergency, DEA temporarily allowed prescribing of controlled substances (including buprenorphine) via telehealth without a prior in-person visit. These flexibilities were extended multiple times.

### 2025 Final Rule

- On January 15, 2025, DEA released a **final rule permanently expanding telehealth buprenorphine prescribing**.[^6]
- Implementation was delayed to **December 31, 2025**.[^6]
- Clinicians can prescribe up to **6 months** of OUD treatment medications to patients seen only via telehealth (audio-visual encounter).[^6]
- **PDMP check required:** Before prescribing via telehealth, clinicians must check NC's prescription drug monitoring program (CSRS) data.[^6]
- **Pharmacist identity verification:** Pharmacists must verify patient identity before dispensing.[^6]

### Proposed Special Registrations

A parallel proposed rule would create three new special telemedicine DEA registrations allowing online platforms to dispense certain controlled substances via telehealth — pending finalization as of 2026.[^6]

## 7. Office-Based Opioid Treatment (OBOT) and NC Medicaid

**OBOT** refers to prescribing buprenorphine (and naltrexone) in outpatient medical office settings — primary care, addiction medicine, OB/GYN, etc. — outside of a licensed OTP clinic.

### NC Medicaid Clinical Coverage Policy 1A-41 (OBOT)

**verified-as-of: 2026-06-22** — Revised December 20, 2024, effective January 1, 2025.[^3]

Key coverage rules:
- NC Medicaid covers **Suboxone, buprenorphine/naloxone tablets, and buprenorphine mono tablets** under FFS and managed care plans.[^3]
- **Prior authorization required** for office visits for OBOT (managed care plans; FFS has own PA requirements).[^3]
- **Counseling NOT required** as a prerequisite for coverage — patients do not need proof of SUD counseling enrollment to receive buprenorphine coverage.[^3]
- Counseling beyond induction/stabilization is at clinician/patient discretion based on ongoing treatment needs.[^3]
- Providers must **inquire** about counseling enrollment and refer to licensed behavioral health professionals if not enrolled.[^3]

### NC Medicaid OTP Service Policy (CCP 8A-9)

- Covers OTP services including methadone, buprenorphine, and naltrexone.
- Revised December 20, 2024 (effective January 1, 2025) under the NC 1115 SUD Demonstration Waiver.
- Mobile units now approved as a place of service.[^3]

## 8. Naltrexone/Vivitrol — NC Medicaid Coverage

Naltrexone (oral or injectable Vivitrol) is not a controlled substance and does not require DEA registration to prescribe.

- NC Medicaid covers injectable naltrexone (Vivitrol) under FFS and managed care plans.[^7]
- Covered as both a **medical benefit** (for injection administration) and **pharmacy benefit**.[^7]
- No counseling enrollment requirement for coverage.[^7]
- For Medicaid patients, out-of-pocket cost is typically **$4 or less per month** (as of 2024).[^7]
- As of 2025, mobile OTP units traveling to underserved areas also administer naltrexone.[^3]

## 9. Anti-Patterns / Common Misunderstandings

**"Methadone can be prescribed at a doctor's office for OUD"**
False. Methadone for OUD treatment may only be dispensed through a licensed OTP clinic (or mobile unit). Office-based prescribers cannot prescribe methadone take-home for OUD. Methadone CAN be prescribed by any DEA-registered practitioner for pain management.[^1]

**"You still need an X-Waiver to prescribe Suboxone"**
False as of January 2023. The X-Waiver was eliminated. Any DEA-registered practitioner with Schedule III authority can prescribe buprenorphine for OUD — subject to the 8-hour training requirement for new/renewing registrants.[^5]

**"OTP patients must attend counseling to receive their methadone dose"**
No longer required as an absolute condition under the 2024 42 CFR Part 8 final rule. Counseling access must be available; mandatory attendance as a dosing condition must be individualized and clinically justified.[^2]

**"A single state OTP license covers mobile units"**
No — mobile medication units require their own separate NC DHSR licensure alongside the parent OTP's license.[^3]

**"Telehealth buprenorphine prescribing is now fully permanent and unrestricted"**
Close but not complete. The 2025 DEA final rule made certain flexibilities permanent but imposed PDMP check requirements and pharmacy identity verification — and implementation was delayed until December 31, 2025.[^6]

**"Naltrexone (Vivitrol) for OUD requires any special DEA registration"**
False. Naltrexone is not a scheduled controlled substance. Any licensed prescriber can prescribe it.[^7]

## 10. Practical Notes for Patients and Providers

- **Finding NC OTPs:** The SAMHSA Treatment Locator (findtreatment.gov) and NC DHHS provider search list licensed OTPs. As of 2025, services are in 55/100 counties; mobile units are expanding access.
- **Insurance parity:** Under MHPAEA, NC Medicaid and most private insurers must cover MOUD on parity with other medical treatments. Tailored Plans (launched July 1, 2024) now cover MAT for members with severe SUD.
- **Pregnancy and MAT:** Buprenorphine and methadone (via OTP) are the standard of care for OUD in pregnancy. NC Medicaid covers MOUD for pregnant patients; involuntary detox is contraindicated. See `nc-sud-family-law.md` for custody/CPS intersections.
- **Corrections setting:** NC jails and prisons are under evolving federal requirements to provide MOUD. The NC Medicaid OBOT policy covers bridge prescriptions for individuals recently released.
