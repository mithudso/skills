# CSF Orexin-1 Assay

## Core Concepts
- Orexin-A (hypocretin-1) is a neuropeptide produced exclusively by ~70,000 neurons in the lateral hypothalamus; CSF levels reflect hypothalamic output
- Diagnostic threshold: CSF orexin-A ≤110 pg/mL = narcolepsy type 1 (NT1); intermediate 111–200 pg/mL; normal >200 pg/mL
- Requires lumbar puncture (L3–L4 interspace); sample processed by radioimmunoassay (RIA), ELISA, or LC-MS/MS
- ICSD-3 criteria: NT1 can be diagnosed by low CSF orexin alone (≤110 pg/mL), independent of MSLT findings or cataplexy presence
- Specificity is exceptionally high: in healthy controls and non-NT1 hypersomnias, CSF levels virtually always exceed 200 pg/mL

## Mechanisms / Biology
- NT1 involves autoimmune destruction of up to 95% of orexin-producing neurons, reducing CSF orexin-A from ~300–500 pg/mL (normal) to undetectable or trace levels
- HLA-DQB1*06:02 allele present in >90% of NT1 patients with low CSF orexin, supporting immune-mediated neuron loss
- Orexin-A and orexin-B are cleaved from prepro-orexin; CSF reflects free peptide diffusion from hypothalamus via interstitial fluid
- Intermediate CSF levels (111–200 pg/mL) may reflect partial neuron loss; ~10% of clinically typical NT1 patients fall in this range
- LC-MS/MS method quantifies both orexin-A and orexin-B simultaneously using stable isotope-labeled internal standards, improving accuracy over immunoassays

## Clinical Picture
- NT1 prevalence: 0.02–0.04% globally; ~95% of confirmed cases show CSF orexin-A ≤110 pg/mL
- Sensitivity of CSF assay for NT1: ~94% (106/113 clinically confirmed cases in landmark validation studies); specificity ~99% vs other sleep disorders
- False positives reported with ELISA methods lacking validated antibody standards; RIA remains gold-standard reference method
- Intermediate levels with typical cataplexy: ~6% of NT1 patients — these cases can still meet PSG/MSLT criteria; strict threshold creates diagnostic gaps
- Low CSF orexin also documented in secondary causes: hypothalamic tumors, Niemann-Pick type C, Whipple's disease, Prader-Willi syndrome

## Evidence & Treatment
- AASM/ICSD-3: CSF orexin ≤110 pg/mL is a standalone diagnostic criterion for NT1 (no PSG/MSLT required) when LP is feasible
- Mayo Clinic Laboratories (test code ORXNA) offers RIA-based assay; only a handful of US reference labs provide validated testing — primary availability constraint in clinical practice
- LP invasiveness limits uptake: historically underused in the US vs Europe/Japan; emerging orexin receptor agonists (e.g., danavorexton, OX2R-selective) are increasing clinical motivation for biomarker confirmation
- Current immunoassays lack certified reference materials; LC-MS/MS overcomes this but is not yet widely deployed clinically
- Danavorexton phase 2 trial (2024): mean +11.1-point improvement on MWT, outperforming modafinil — CSF orexin confirmation used as enrollment criterion

## Key Facts
- <110 pg/mL: diagnostic cutoff for NT1 per ICSD-3 and AASM
- >200 pg/mL: virtually universal in healthy controls and non-NT1 hypersomnias
- 95%: proportion of lateral hypothalamic orexin neurons lost in NT1 postmortem studies
- ~94% sensitivity, ~99% specificity for NT1 vs REM-confirmed MSLT (~70–90% sensitivity)
- LP required: no validated blood or urine orexin-1 assay exists for clinical diagnosis as of 2026
