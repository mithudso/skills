# Medicaid Fraud, Waste, and Abuse (FWA)

> **Educational only — NOT legal advice.** FWA determinations require legal analysis; consult a healthcare attorney for individual situations.

**verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

---

## 1. Definitions

**Fraud**: Intentional deception or misrepresentation that results in unauthorized Medicaid payment. Requires criminal intent (mens rea for criminal charges) or knowing submission of false claims (civil liability).

**Waste**: Overutilization or inappropriate resource use without fraudulent intent — poor care coordination, medically unnecessary services, inefficient processes.

**Abuse**: Practices inconsistent with sound medical, business, or fiscal practices that result in unnecessary costs — may shade into fraud depending on intent.

**Key distinction**: Fraud requires intent; waste and abuse typically do not. Criminal prosecution requires fraud. Civil False Claims Act liability requires "knowingly" submitting false claims (but "knowingly" includes reckless disregard — not just explicit intent).

---

## 2. Scale of FWA

Medicaid FWA is estimated to account for **3–10%** of total Medicaid spending (~$30–90 billion annually). The wide range reflects difficulty in measuring; not all improper payments are fraudulent. CMS's annual **Payment Error Rate Measurement (PERM)** program estimates improper payment rates for Medicaid and CHIP.

Common fraud categories by dollar volume:
1. Home health and personal care services
2. Durable medical equipment (DME)
3. Pharmacy fraud (drug diversion, pill mills)
4. Behavioral health billing fraud
5. Transportation billing fraud
6. Provider identity theft (billing under stolen NPI)

## 3. Federal Legal Framework

### False Claims Act (FCA) — 31 U.S.C. § 3729 et seq.
The primary civil enforcement tool. Imposes liability on entities that:
- Submit false or fraudulent claims to the federal government
- Make or use false records material to a false claim
- Conspire to commit such violations

**Penalties**: $13,000–$27,000 per false claim + treble damages (3x the government's loss). CMS adjusts annually for inflation.

**Knowing standard**: Actual knowledge, deliberate ignorance, or reckless disregard. Does NOT require proof of specific intent to defraud.

### Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS) — 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b(b)
Criminalizes offering, paying, soliciting, or receiving "remuneration" (anything of value) to induce referrals of Medicaid/Medicare-covered services. Violations generate FCA liability.

Common AKS issues: Physician self-referral arrangements, consultant payments, billing arrangements, marketing incentives.

### Stark Law (Physician Self-Referral) — 42 U.S.C. § 1395nn
Prohibits physicians from referring Medicare/Medicaid patients to entities with which they have a financial relationship, unless exceptions apply.

### Criminal Health Care Fraud — 18 U.S.C. § 1347
Federal criminal statute; up to 10 years imprisonment per violation (20 years if serious bodily harm, life if death results).

## 4. Whistleblower (Qui Tam) Provisions

The False Claims Act includes **qui tam** provisions allowing private citizens ("relators") to file FCA suits on behalf of the government:
- Relator files under seal; DOJ investigates and decides whether to intervene
- If government intervenes and case succeeds: relator receives 15–25% of recovery
- If government declines and relator proceeds alone: relator receives 25–30%

Qui tam cases have recovered billions in Medicaid fraud — pharmaceutical pricing fraud, hospital billing fraud, home health fraud.

**NC parallel**: NC has a **State False Claims Act** (NC GS § 1-605 to 1-618) mirroring the federal FCA, applicable to state Medicaid funds.

---

## 5. Medicaid Fraud Control Units (MFCUs)

**MFCUs** are state agencies (required by federal law in all states) that investigate and prosecute Medicaid provider fraud. MFCUs:
- Are typically housed within the state Attorney General's office
- Receive federal funding (75% federal, 25% state match — CMS grants)
- Investigate criminal fraud (not just civil)
- Can investigate patient abuse and neglect in Medicaid-funded facilities

**NC MFCU**: Located within the NC Department of Justice (Attorney General). Investigates billing fraud, drug diversion, and patient abuse/neglect in nursing homes and other Medicaid-funded facilities.

---

## 6. CMS Program Integrity

**CMS Center for Program Integrity (CPI)** oversees federal Medicaid program integrity:

- **Medicaid Integrity Program (MIP)**: Contractors audit Medicaid claims data, identify overpayments, and refer to state Medicaid agencies
- **Exclusion database (OIG List of Excluded Individuals/Entities — LEIE)**: Excluded providers cannot bill Medicaid/Medicare. States must screen providers against LEIE; Medicaid plans must screen too.
- **Provider enrollment**: Enhanced screening (background checks, site visits) for high-risk provider types (home health, DME, behavioral health)
- **Pre-payment review**: Certain provider types subject to pre-payment claims review
- **Automated claims analysis**: Predictive analytics to flag aberrant billing patterns

**NC Medicaid Program Integrity**: NCDHHS Division of Health Benefits has a dedicated Program Integrity unit that audits providers, recovers overpayments, and refers fraud cases to MFCU.

## 7. Common Fraud Patterns

| Pattern | Description |
|---|---|
| **Upcoding** | Billing for a higher-severity service than was provided |
| **Phantom billing** | Billing for services not provided |
| **Unbundling** | Billing separately for services that should be billed as a single code |
| **Duplicate billing** | Submitting same claim twice |
| **Drug diversion** | Prescribing or dispensing controlled substances without legitimate medical purpose; billing for drugs not dispensed |
| **Transportation fraud** | Billing for non-emergency medical transport not provided or for ineligible trips |
| **Identity theft** | Using a Medicaid enrollee's ID to bill for services they didn't receive |
| **Credential fraud** | Billing under a licensed provider's NPI when services were provided by unlicensed staff |

---

## 8. Managed Care and FWA

NC Medicaid managed care (Standard Plans, Tailored Plans) creates additional FWA dimensions:
- MCOs must have FWA compliance programs
- MCOs are required to report suspected FWA to NCDHHS
- MCOs may conduct their own pre- and post-payment audits
- MCOs are responsible for ensuring network providers are not excluded from Medicaid

---

## 9. Anti-Patterns

**"Overpayments must be intentional to create FCA liability"** — False. FCA's "knowing" standard includes reckless disregard. A provider who submits claims without checking whether documentation supports them may face FCA liability even without specific fraudulent intent.

**"Exclusion from Medicare doesn't affect Medicaid"** — OIG exclusion applies to BOTH Medicare and Medicaid (and all federal healthcare programs). An excluded provider cannot receive Medicaid payments either directly or indirectly.

**"Small overpayments don't need to be returned"** — False. The ACA created a 60-day rule: providers who identify an overpayment must report and return it within 60 days, or keeping it becomes an FCA violation.
