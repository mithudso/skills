# Pharmacology Fundamentals

## Core Concepts
- **Pharmacodynamics (PD):** what a drug does to the body — receptor binding, signal transduction, dose-response relationships, and therapeutic/toxic effects
- **Pharmacokinetics (PK):** what the body does to a drug — absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (ADME)
- Therapeutic index (TI = LD50/ED50) defines the safety margin; narrow-TI drugs (lithium, digoxin, warfarin, aminoglycosides) require monitoring
- Drug effect is determined by free (unbound) drug concentration at the receptor; protein binding limits active fraction
- Most PK parameters are linear (first-order) at therapeutic doses; saturable (zero-order) kinetics occur at enzyme capacity (phenytoin, ethanol)

## Pharmacodynamics — Mechanisms

**Receptor theory**
- Drugs bind receptors to produce effects; affinity (Ka) describes binding strength; efficacy describes magnitude of response produced per unit receptor occupancy
- **Full agonist:** binds receptor, produces maximal response (Emax); requires less than 100% receptor occupancy for Emax if receptor reserve exists
- **Partial agonist:** binds receptor, produces submaximal Emax regardless of dose; can act as functional antagonist in presence of full agonist (e.g., buprenorphine at μ-opioid receptor)
- **Inverse agonist:** binds receptor, produces effect opposite to agonist (reduces constitutive activity); GABA-A modulators are key example
- **Competitive antagonist:** competes with agonist for same binding site; rightward shift of dose-response curve without changing Emax; reversible by increasing agonist concentration (e.g., naloxone at μ-opioid)
- **Non-competitive/allosteric antagonist:** binds separate site; reduces Emax regardless of agonist concentration

**Dose-response relationships**
- Graded dose-response curve: sigmoidal; EC50 = concentration producing 50% maximal effect
- **Emax:** maximum drug effect achievable; **EC50:** potency measure (lower = more potent); **Hill coefficient (n):** slope steepness
- IC50 (inhibitory): concentration producing 50% inhibition of a process (enzyme, receptor, transporter)
- Quantal dose-response: proportion of population responding vs dose; ED50 = dose effective in 50%; LD50 = lethal dose in 50%
- **Therapeutic index:** TI = LD50/ED50; clinically, ratio of minimum toxic concentration to minimum effective concentration

**Receptor types**
- Ligand-gated ion channels: fastest (milliseconds) — GABA-A, nicotinic ACh, NMDA, AMPA
- G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs): seconds-to-minutes — dopamine D1/D2, β-adrenergic, opioid, cannabinoid, serotonin 5-HT1A/1B
- Receptor tyrosine kinases: minutes-to-hours — insulin, EGF
- Nuclear receptors: hours-to-days — corticosteroids, thyroid hormone, sex hormones

## Pharmacokinetics — ADME

**Absorption**
- Oral bioavailability (F) = fraction reaching systemic circulation; reduced by first-pass hepatic metabolism and poor GI absorption
- Routes by bioavailability: IV = 100% → inhalation ≈ 70-90% → sublingual ≈ 70-90% → intranasal ≈ 50-80% → transdermal (variable) → oral (highly variable) → rectal (variable)
- GI absorption depends on: lipophilicity, pKa (ionization state at GI pH), molecular weight (<500 Da favors passive diffusion), transporters (P-gp efflux, OATP uptake)
- Henderson-Hasselbalch equation: ionized fraction = f(drug pKa, pH) — weak acids absorbed in stomach (acidic pH); weak bases absorbed in small intestine

**Distribution**
- Volume of distribution (Vd = dose / plasma concentration): low Vd (<1 L/kg) = largely intravascular/plasma protein-bound; high Vd (>10 L/kg) = extensive tissue distribution
- Plasma protein binding: albumin binds acidic drugs (warfarin, NSAIDs, phenytoin); α1-acid glycoprotein binds basic drugs (lidocaine, propranolol, many CNS drugs)
- Blood-brain barrier (BBB): tight junctions + efflux pumps (P-gp/MDR1) — lipophilic, uncharged, low-MW drugs cross best; P-gp substrates (loperamide) excluded from CNS
- Lipophilic drugs accumulate in adipose tissue (reservoir effect); polar drugs confined to water compartments

**Metabolism**
- **Phase I:** oxidation (CYP450 most important), reduction, hydrolysis — often produces reactive/active metabolites
- **Phase II:** conjugation (glucuronidation UGT, sulfation SULT, acetylation NAT, methylation) — typically inactivates and increases water solubility for excretion
- CYP450 isoforms: CYP3A4 (largest fraction ~50% of hepatic CYP; metabolizes majority of drugs), CYP2D6 (genetic polymorphism; meth, codeine, antidepressants, antipsychotics), CYP2C9 (warfarin, NSAIDs, phenytoin), CYP2C19 (PPIs, clopidogrel, some SSRIs), CYP1A2 (clozapine, caffeine, theophylline)
- **Inducers** (increase CYP activity → reduce drug levels): rifampin (potent CYP3A4/2C9), carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, St. John's Wort
- **Inhibitors** (decrease CYP activity → raise drug levels): ketoconazole (CYP3A4), fluoxetine/paroxetine (CYP2D6), fluvoxamine (CYP1A2), cimetidine (multiple)
- First-pass metabolism: extensive hepatic extraction before drug reaches systemic circulation; drugs with high extraction ratio (nitroglycerin, lidocaine, morphine) unsuitable for oral route
- **CYP2D6 genetic polymorphism:** poor metabolizers (~7% Caucasians) — accumulate parent drug; ultra-rapid metabolizers (~2-5%) — may need higher doses; clinical relevance: codeine→morphine conversion, opioid efficacy/toxicity, psychiatric drug levels

**Excretion**
- Renal: glomerular filtration of free drug + tubular secretion (OAT/OCT transporters) − tubular reabsorption (pH-dependent for ionizable drugs)
- Hepatic/biliary: large MW conjugates excreted in bile → enterohepatic recirculation possible (morphine glucuronide)
- Renal dosing adjustments needed when CrCl < 50-60 mL/min for renally cleared drugs; hepatic dosing adjustments for hepatically cleared, narrow-TI drugs

## Key PK Parameters

| Parameter | Definition | Clinical relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Bioavailability (F)** | Fraction dose reaching systemic circulation | Oral vs IV dosing equivalence |
| **Vd** | Apparent distribution volume | Low = plasma-bound; high = tissue-distributed |
| **Clearance (CL)** | Volume of plasma cleared per time (L/h) | Determines maintenance dose rate |
| **Half-life (t½)** | Time to reduce concentration by 50% | Dosing interval; time to steady state (~5 t½) |
| **Css** | Steady-state concentration | Reached after ~5 half-lives on regular dosing |
| **Protein binding** | % drug bound to plasma proteins | Bound = inactive; displacement = DDI risk |

- t½ = 0.693 × Vd / CL
- Time to steady state independent of dose; only clearance and Vd determine t½
- Loading dose = Vd × target Css / F — used when immediate effect needed (digoxin, warfarin, antiepileptics)

## Drug-Drug Interactions
- **PK interactions:** induction/inhibition of CYP450, competition for protein binding, altered absorption (chelation, pH changes), altered renal elimination (probenecid blocks OAT tubular secretion)
- **PD interactions:** additive (two drugs with same mechanism), synergistic (greater than additive), antagonistic (reversal — naloxone + morphine), competitive (same receptor), functional (different pathways, opposing effects)
- High-risk DDI categories: narrow-TI drugs (warfarin, lithium, digoxin, phenytoin, carbamazepine), QT-prolonging drugs, serotonergic drugs (serotonin syndrome risk), CNS depressants (respiratory depression)

## Key Facts
- CYP3A4 metabolizes ~50% of all marketed drugs; found in liver AND small intestinal wall (first-pass)
- Protein binding: >99% bound → 1% free; small change in binding = large change in free concentration (warfarin, phenytoin)
- Steady state in ~5 half-lives; predict half-life from t½ = 0.693 × Vd / CL
- First-pass effect can reduce oral bioavailability from 100% to <10% (nitroglycerin <1%; lidocaine ~35%; morphine ~30%)
- CYP2D6 poor metabolizers: ~7% Caucasian, ~1-3% Asian, ~5-8% African American populations
