# Illicit vs Prescription Amphetamine Pharmacology

## Core Concepts

- **Structural relationship**: Methamphetamine is the N-methyl derivative of amphetamine; both are phenethylamines sharing identical primary mechanism (reverse DAT/NET transport + VMAT2 displacement) but differing in CNS penetration, potency, and metabolism
- **Rx amphetamine spectrum**: Mixed amphetamine salts (Adderall: 75% d-AMP, 25% l-AMP), dextroamphetamine (Dexedrine), and lisdexamfetamine (Vyvanse, prodrug requiring enzymatic cleavage to d-AMP) — all engineered for slow oral-route Tmax
- **Illicit amphetamine spectrum**: d-Methamphetamine ("crystal meth," "ice"), d/l-amphetamine sulfate (powder, UK predominant), and diverted Rx formulations re-routed intranasally/IV — differing primarily by Tmax, not mechanism
- **Rate hypothesis (Volkow/Koob)**: Dopamine-mediated euphoria correlates not with peak striatal DA concentration but with *rate of rise* (dDA/dt) — fast-rising DA produces reinforcement/euphoria; slow-rising DA (oral Rx formulations) produces therapeutic effects with minimal hedonic impact
- **Prodrug design rationale**: Lisdexamfetamine's enzymatic activation (RBC hydrolysis, rate-limited) flattens the PK curve regardless of route — intranasal or IV LDX produces the same slow Tmax as oral, defeating rate-hypothesis exploitation

## Mechanisms / Biology

- **DAT/NET reverse transport**: Both amphetamine and methamphetamine enter monoamine terminals via DAT/NET, trigger carrier-mediated efflux of DA/NE (not vesicular exocytosis), and block reuptake — net effect: massive non-quantal neurotransmitter release into synapse
- **VMAT2 disruption**: Both drugs redistribute vesicular DA into the cytoplasm (via VMAT2 competition), increasing substrate available for reverse transport; methamphetamine is more potent at VMAT2 disruption, contributing to greater neurotoxic potential at high doses
- **CNS penetration advantage of meth**: Methamphetamine's N-methyl group increases lipophilicity and CNS penetrance vs. amphetamine; meth achieves higher brain:plasma ratios and greater striatal DA release per equivalent dose — estimated 5× greater DA release at comparable in vitro concentrations (Fleckenstein et al.)
- **Serotonin co-release**: Methamphetamine has ~3× greater potency than amphetamine at serotonin transporters (SERT); contributes to hyperthermia, appetite suppression, and some of the mood dysregulation distinguishing high-dose meth toxicity from amphetamine toxicity
- **Oral Rx kinetics suppress rate hypothesis**: Oral d-AMP (Adderall IR): Tmax ~3 h, controlled-release ~7 h; plasma rise is gradual enough that striatal DA increase is sub-threshold for euphoria at therapeutic doses — therapeutic window exploits functional improvement without triggering reinforcement circuitry
- **Intranasal/IV route: rate hypothesis activation**: Intranasal d-AMP reaches peak effect within ~60 min (vs. ~3 h oral), dramatically steepening dDA/dt; subjective euphoria and abuse liability increase significantly — same molecule, different pharmacokinetic profile

## Clinical Picture

- **Subjective distinguishability**: At controlled equivalent doses via identical routes, meth and amphetamine are "almost indistinguishable" subjectively (Angrist et al.); perceived superior potency of street meth often reflects route (smoked/IV) not molecular difference
- **Euphorogenic threshold by route**: Smoked/IV meth → near-instant Tmax, highest dDA/dt, intense rush; intranasal: intermediate; oral: lowest — same hierarchy applies to amphetamine, but meth's superior CNS penetration compresses the Tmax further at equivalent doses
- **Half-life parity**: Oral meth t½ = 10–12 h; d-amphetamine t½ = 10–13 h — elimination kinetics are similar; meth's longer perceived duration of action is largely a function of CNS residence, not plasma clearance
- **Self-medication route-shift rationale**: ADHD/narcolepsy patients inadequately treated with oral Rx stimulants may shift to insufflation/illicit product because: (1) faster onset restores function more urgently, (2) higher peak DA better compensates for structural hypodopaminergia, (3) access barriers force non-Rx sourcing; NOT necessarily seeking euphoria — often seeking symptom control
- **Epidemiology**: ~16 million US adults used prescription stimulants in 2016; 5 million misused them; of misusers, >20% report non-oral routes; psychostimulant-involved deaths reached 37,096 in 2024 (CDC)

## Evidence & Treatment

- **Pharmaceutical methamphetamine (Desoxyn)**: FDA-approved 5 mg tablet for ADHD and short-term obesity — demonstrates that meth's therapeutic-to-abuse liability distinction is route and dose, not molecule; rarely prescribed due to stigma and abuse potential
- **Rate hypothesis clinical support**: PET imaging (Volkow et al.) shows IV methylphenidate produces faster striatal DA rise and more euphoria than oral; extrapolates to amphetamines — oral formulation design is an evidence-based abuse-deterrent strategy
- **Abuse-deterrent formulations**: Extended-release beads (Adderall XR), osmotic delivery (not applicable to amps), and prodrug activation (Vyvanse) specifically target Tmax prolongation; FDA requires abuse potential studies for all new stimulant NDAs
- **Treatment of stimulant use disorder**: No FDA-approved pharmacotherapy; contingency management (CM) is the most evidence-supported behavioral intervention (see `meth-contingency-management`); off-label mirtazapine (Mexico RCT, 2021), naltrexone, and bupropion have limited trial evidence
- **Adequate Rx treatment as protective**: Treating ADHD/narcolepsy to adequate symptom control with appropriate oral formulations reduces illicit stimulant seeking — undertreated dopamine deficit sustains craving for fast-onset alternatives

## Key Facts

- Methamphetamine releases ~5× more dopamine than amphetamine at equivalent in vitro concentrations (DAT-transfected cells, Fleckenstein 2007)
- Oral d-amphetamine Tmax: ~3 h; intranasal d-amphetamine peak effect: <60 min — same drug, 3× faster onset shifts abuse liability substantially
- Meth t½ ≈ 10–12 h; d-AMP t½ ≈ 10–13 h — elimination half-lives are nearly identical despite perceived potency difference
- Lisdexamfetamine PK is route-independent — oral, intranasal, and IV produce equivalent Tmax (~3.5 h), a direct pharmacological countermeasure to the rate hypothesis
- Pharmaceutical methamphetamine (Desoxyn 5 mg) is FDA-approved, confirming molecule ≠ abuse liability; route + dose determine addictive potential
