# Catecholamine Depletion Hypersomnia — Class Context

## Core Principle

Hypersomnia from catecholamine depletion is a mechanistic class, not a single diagnosis. Dopamine (DA) and norepinephrine (NE) in the mesolimbic/mesocortical and locus coeruleus circuits are the primary central arousal promoters. When DA/NE tone falls — by any route — the result is hypoarousal with prolonged sleep. Meth-withdrawal hypersomnia (Mechanism 1) is one instance of this class.

Key class features shared across all members:
- **Hypoarousal** (opposite of opioid/alcohol withdrawal hyperarousal)
- **CSF orexin typically normal** — not a structural/neurological lesion
- **Responds to stimulant treatment** targeting DA/NE
- **Resolves with catecholamine system recovery** or dose adjustment
- **MSLT false positives possible** during active depletion; timing validity matters

## Class Members

**1. Meth-withdrawal hypersomnia (Mechanism 1)**
Massive DA/NE efflux during use → VMAT2 reversal and receptor down-regulation → vesicular stores depleted at cessation. Hypersomnia 14–20 h/day, peaks 72–96 h, resolves in 2–3 weeks. Reference skill: `meth-withdrawal-hypersomnia`.

**2. Reserpine-induced sedation/hypersomnia**
Historical antihypertensive; binds VMAT2 *irreversibly* → empties presynaptic DA, NE, and serotonin vesicles → profound sedation, motor slowing, and hypersomnia. Used as the canonical animal model of catecholamine depletion EDS. Mechanistically links to meth withdrawal because meth toxicity also targets VMAT2 (oxidative damage, vesicular dysfunction). Largely obsolete clinically due to this side-effect burden.

**3. Tetrabenazine / deutetrabenazine (Huntington's, Tourette's)**
*Reversible* VMAT2 inhibition → dose-dependent DA/NE depletion → sedation is the primary dose-limiting side effect in >25% of patients. Deutetrabenazine (longer half-life, smoother depletion curve) has improved but not eliminated EDS. Management: dose reduction, dose-splitting, or switching agent. Mechanistically identical to reserpine, reversibility is the key difference.

**4. Post-viral / long-COVID catecholamine depletion**
Post-COVID fatigue and EDS is partially attributed to central DA/NE depletion — proposed mechanisms include neuroinflammation targeting monoaminergic nuclei, mitochondrial dysfunction in DA neurons, and post-viral autonomic dysregulation. CSF orexin normal in documented cases. Treatment targets overlap with meth-withdrawal: methylphenidate produced favorable response in 3 of 4 cases in a published case series (PMC10899667). "Brain fog" and EDS share neurobiological substrate with post-stimulant hypersomnia.

**5. Antipsychotic-induced EDS**
DA receptor blockade (D2 antagonism) creates *functional* catecholamine depletion at the postsynaptic level even when presynaptic stores are intact. Profound EDS in susceptible patients, particularly with high-potency typical antipsychotics or clozapine (additional H1/alpha-1 antagonism adds sedation). Management: dose reduction, dosing at night, or switching to lower-sedation agent. Distinct from presynaptic depletion but same downstream hypoarousal pathway.

**6. ADHD stimulant discontinuation**
Methylphenidate/amphetamine cessation → rebound hypersomnia, typically mild (1–3 days), self-limiting. Same Mechanism 1 as meth withdrawal but orders of magnitude smaller DA depletion; therapeutic doses do not produce neurotoxic vesicular damage. Clinically benign but recognizable as same class.

## Clinical Utility of Class Recognition

**Workup shortcut:** Patient with hypersomnia + known DA-depleting medication (tetrabenazine, reserpine, high-dose antipsychotic) → MSLT and CSF orexin are not indicated. The diagnosis is the exposure; workup should focus on confirming absence of structural cause.

**Differential from structural hypersomnia:** Catecholamine depletion hypersomnia is reversible and exposure-linked; idiopathic hypersomnia, narcolepsy type 2, and central CNS lesions are not. Normal orexin and absence of SOREMPs on a valid MSLT (≥2 weeks from exposure) confirms functional/depletion etiology.

**Treatment target:** DA/NE-enhancing agents (bupropion, modafinil, low-dose stimulants) address the shared mechanism. Serotonergic-only antidepressants (SSRIs) are ineffective for depletion-class hypersomnia — consistent across all class members.

## Evidence Anchors

- AMPT (alpha-methyl-para-tyrosine) human paradigm: tyrosine hydroxylase inhibition → catecholamine depletion → measurable sleepiness on PSG/MSLT in healthy volunteers (ScienceDirect, neural correlates of AMPT-induced sleepiness)
- Tetrabenazine FDA label: somnolence/sedation reported in 31% of patients; dose-dependent (Xenazine label, 2008)
- Post-COVID methylphenidate response: 3/4 cases with post-COVID central hypersomnia responded to methylphenidate; CSF orexin normal in tested cases (PMC10899667)
- DA dysfunction in stimulant-use disorders mechanistic review confirms shared VMAT2/DAT substrate across stimulant-class depletion (PMC8664889)
