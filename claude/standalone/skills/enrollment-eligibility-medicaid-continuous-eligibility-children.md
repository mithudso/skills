# Medicaid and CHIP Continuous Eligibility for Children

## What Is Continuous Eligibility?

Continuous eligibility (CE) means that once a child is enrolled in Medicaid or CHIP, their coverage cannot be terminated mid-year simply because their family's income changes or increases — they remain enrolled for a full period regardless of fluctuating circumstances.

## Federal Mandate: CAA 2023

**Effective January 1, 2024**, the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023 (CAA 2023) requires:
- All states to provide **12 months of continuous eligibility** for children under age 19 in both Medicaid and CHIP
- No exceptions for income increases during the 12-month period
- No disenrollment for **non-payment of CHIP premiums** during the 12-month period

**Previous framework**: Before CAA 2023, states could elect (but were not required) to provide continuous eligibility. Many states used narrower periods or applied exceptions. CAA 2023 turned continuous eligibility from a state option into a federal floor.

## What Continuous Eligibility Protects Against

During the 12-month enrollment period, a child enrolled in Medicaid/CHIP **cannot be disenrolled** because:
- Family income increases above the eligibility threshold mid-year
- Parent changes jobs (income fluctuation)
- Family composition changes
- CHIP premiums are not paid on time

At the **end of the 12-month period**, the state must conduct a standard redetermination. If the child no longer qualifies (income too high), coverage ends at the renewal point — not mid-year.

## State Options Beyond the Federal Minimum

Some states have received CMS approval for **multi-year continuous eligibility** for young children:

| State | Continuous Eligibility Until |
|---|---|
| Colorado | Child's 3rd birthday |
| Hawaii | 6th birthday; 24 months for ages 6–19 |
| Minnesota | 6th birthday; 12 months for ages 19–21 |
| New York | 6th birthday |
| Pennsylvania | 6th birthday |

These states received formal CMS approval through state plan amendments or waivers.

## CHIP Premium Non-Payment — Key Protection

A significant change in CAA 2023: states **cannot disenroll a child from CHIP for non-payment of premiums** during the 12-month continuous eligibility period. Previously, states could terminate CHIP coverage after a grace period for missed premiums. This protection prevents coverage gaps when families face temporary financial hardship.

**After the 12-month period**: Standard CHIP premium rules resume; non-payment may again trigger disenrollment after the grace period.

## Newborn Automatic Enrollment

Newborns born to Medicaid-enrolled mothers are **automatically enrolled in Medicaid** from birth — they do not need a separate application. This automatic enrollment is separate from continuous eligibility but provides seamless coverage from day one.

## H.R.1 (One Big Beautiful Bill, 2025) Impact on Children

The 2025 reconciliation law does **not** eliminate the 12-month continuous eligibility mandate for children. Children, pregnant women, seniors, and people with disabilities remain on annual (not semi-annual) redetermination cycles. The semi-annual redetermination requirement applies only to ACA expansion adults (ages 19–64, not disabled).

However, other H.R.1 provisions may indirectly affect child coverage:
- More frequent family redeterminations for parent/guardian could create churn
- Stricter documentation requirements could create barriers at renewal for children too
- CHIP enhanced FMAP changes may affect state program funding

## Redetermination at 12 Months

When the 12-month continuous eligibility period ends:
1. State conducts standard redetermination
2. State first attempts ex parte (automatic) renewal using available data
3. If ex parte fails, a prepopulated form is mailed — parent/guardian must respond
4. If income now exceeds the applicable limit: child may lose Medicaid but may qualify for CHIP (if income is in CHIP range) or ACA marketplace family coverage

## Interaction with Retroactive Medicaid and Newborn Provisions

- **Retroactive Medicaid for children**: Like adults, children can receive up to 3 months of retroactive Medicaid for eligible medical expenses incurred before the application date (in states that haven't waived retroactivity)
- **Newborn automatic enrollment + continuous eligibility**: Newborns automatically enrolled through their mother's Medicaid receive 12 months of continuous coverage as well

## NC-Specific Notes

NC provides 12-month continuous eligibility for children consistent with the CAA 2023 mandate. NC Health Choice (CHIP for children ages 6–18 at 133–210% FPL) also must comply with the continuous eligibility requirement. Families should complete renewals at the end of the 12-month period through NC FAST (epass.nc.gov) or their local DSS.
