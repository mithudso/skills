# NC School-Based Health Centers (SBHCs)

## Overview

North Carolina operates **90+ school-based health centers (SBHCs)** serving more than **30,000 students** statewide. SBHCs are health care delivery sites located on school campuses or in close proximity to schools, providing accessible, comprehensive health services to students — including children who might otherwise have no regular health care provider.

NC DHHS oversees SBHCs primarily through the **Division for Child and Family Well-being (DCFW)**, which manages child and family health programs including SBHCs, Title V programs, and school health more broadly.

## Services Provided

NC SBHCs provide a broad scope of services based on assessed community need:

| Service Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Acute care | Illness evaluation, injury care, urgent needs |
| Chronic disease management | Asthma, diabetes, ADHD medication management |
| Mental health counseling | Individual therapy, group sessions, crisis intervention |
| Preventive care | Physical exams, well-child visits, sports physicals |
| Dental services | Dental exams, sealants, fluoride varnish |
| Vision and hearing | Screenings, referrals |
| Nutrition services | Counseling, obesity prevention |
| Immunizations | Vaccines per ACIP schedule |
| Health education | STI/HIV prevention, substance use prevention |

Many centers are credentialed by DHHS to ensure quality standards.

### Behavioral Health Services — Priority Area

Behavioral health is a major and growing component of NC SBHCs:

- **$7 million investment** (from a historic $835 million NC behavioral health investment) was directed specifically toward strengthening behavioral health in schools, including:
  - Adding or expanding behavioral health services at existing SBHCs
  - Expanding **tele-behavioral health** services in rural schools (matching support from The Duke Endowment)
- SBHCs provide individual mental health counseling, substance use screening/brief intervention, and referrals to higher levels of care
- School-based services reduce stigma and logistical barriers (transportation, missed work for parents) that prevent children from accessing community mental health services

## Service Models

NC SBHCs operate under several service models:

- **Full-service SBHCs**: Staffed by nurse practitioners, PAs, or physicians; provide comprehensive primary and behavioral health care on-site
- **Wellness centers**: Focus on health education, chronic disease management, and behavioral health with limited acute care
- **Mobile medical clinics**: Serve multiple school sites from a mobile unit
- **Mental health clinics**: Stand-alone behavioral health services attached to school setting
- **Telemedicine services**: Connect students to remote providers for both primary and behavioral health care

## Geographic Distribution

SBHCs are distributed across NC but concentrated in:
- Urban/suburban districts (Charlotte-Mecklenburg, Wake County)
- Rural counties with limited alternative health care access
- School districts serving high proportions of Medicaid-enrolled students

NC DHHS has published maps of SBHC locations; an updated data set as of March 2025 shows county-by-county distribution. Rural expansion is a stated policy priority.

## Funding and Medicaid Billing

NC SBHCs are funded through multiple streams:

- **Medicaid billing**: SBHCs can bill NC Medicaid for services provided to Medicaid-enrolled students; this is a major revenue source for centers serving high-Medicaid-enrollment schools
- **State and federal grants**: DHHS administers grant funding; Title V funds support maternal and child health components
- **School district support**: Some districts fund SBHC staff or facilities
- **Private philanthropy**: Duke Endowment and other funders support expansion and behavioral health integration

NC DHHS publishes an **annual report on School Health Centers** (FY 2024-25 Annual Report published) documenting service statistics and outcomes.

## NC School Based Health Alliance (NCSBHA)

The **NC School Based Health Alliance** is the statewide advocacy and support organization for SBHCs:
- Provides technical assistance to SBHCs
- Advocates for funding and policy support at the state level
- Publishes data on SBHC coverage and services
- Website: [ncsbha.org](https://ncsbha.org)

## Policy Context

SBHCs are positioned as a key strategy for:
- **Reducing health disparities**: Serving students from low-income families who lack access to primary care
- **Improving educational outcomes**: Students who receive care at school miss fewer instructional days and perform better academically
- **Behavioral health access**: School is where mental health problems often first manifest; on-site services enable early intervention
- **Rural health access**: In counties with few providers, an SBHC may be students' primary access point

NC DHHS's **Rural Health Transformation Program** (the $213M CMS award) includes SBHC expansion as part of rural health infrastructure improvement.
