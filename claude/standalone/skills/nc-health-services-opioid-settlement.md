# NC Opioid Settlement Funds

## Overview

North Carolina is receiving **$1.4 billion in total** from national opioid settlements — agreements with pharmaceutical manufacturers, distributors, and other entities that contributed to the opioid epidemic. These funds are earmarked for **opioid remediation** (treatment, recovery, prevention, harm reduction).

**Central resource**: [ncopioidsettlement.org](https://ncopioidsettlement.org/) — NC's official settlement tracking site
**Transparency tool**: **CORE-NC** (Community Opioid Resources Engine for North Carolina) — tracks how local governments plan to spend funds

## Settlements Generating NC Funds

Major settlements contributing to NC's allocation:
- **Distributors** (McKesson, AmerisourceBergen/Cencora, Cardinal Health) — largest national settlement
- **Johnson & Johnson** (manufacturer)
- **Purdue Pharma / Sackler family** — Purdue bankruptcy settlement
- **McKinsey & Company** — consulting firm that advised Purdue
- **Mallinckrodt, Endo, Teva, Allergan** — additional manufacturer settlements
- **Pharmacy chains** (CVS, Walgreens, Walmart, Rite Aid)

## Fund Flow and Allocation

**FY 2023–2024 receipts**: NC received **$220,244,633** in a single fiscal year.

### Allocation Structure
| Recipient | Share | FY 23-24 Amount |
|---|---|---|
| **Local governments** (counties/cities) | **85%** | $176,787,053 |
| **NC General Assembly** (state) | **15%** | $43,457,579 |

### State-Level Funds (15%)
The NC General Assembly controls state share; recent appropriations include:
- **$5.2 million (FY 23-24)** and **$5.5 million (FY 24-25)** to UNC System for **opioid abatement research and development** grants
- NCDHHS grant allocations from McKinsey settlement: 20 agencies funded statewide for treatment, recovery, and prevention services

### Local Government Funds (85%)
Counties and municipalities receive their share directly based on population and harm metrics. They must:
- Use funds for opioid remediation (approved uses listed in settlement agreements)
- Report spending publicly through CORE-NC

## Approved Uses of Settlement Funds

Settlement agreements specify **allowable uses** (Exhibit E/F in national settlements), including:
- MAT (buprenorphine, methadone, naltrexone) access expansion
- Recovery housing
- Naloxone distribution and harm reduction
- Prevention programs (youth, school-based)
- Recovery support services (peer specialists)
- Reentry programs for justice-involved individuals
- Data and surveillance (overdose tracking)
- First responder training
- Mental health services for OUD comorbidity

## Political Tensions: MAT vs. Abstinence

A significant policy debate emerged in 2024 over fund use:
- **NC House proposal (2024)**: Spend opioid settlement money on **abstinence-based recovery centers** (no medication)
- **Public health experts and addiction specialists**: Strongly argued for **medication-first** approaches (buprenorphine, methadone), citing evidence base
- NC Health News (July 2024): Covered tension between legislative preferences and evidence-based medicine
- Ultimate direction of state funds involved compromise language

This tension reflects national debates about whether opioid settlement funds will be spent on evidence-based treatments or on programs with weaker evidence.

## NCDHHS Overdose Epidemic Response

NCDHHS coordinates the statewide overdose epidemic response, of which settlement funds are one piece:
- **NC overdose data**: Suspected overdose deaths down **27%** and ED visits down **29%** from 2023 to 2024 (coinciding with Medicaid expansion, naloxone access, and settlement-funded programs)
- NCDHHS Overdose Epidemic initiative: coordinates across Medicaid, Public Health, MH/SUD divisions

## CORE-NC Transparency Tool

North Carolinians can track local opioid settlement spending at the **Community Opioid Resources Engine for North Carolina (CORE-NC)**:
- Shows each county/municipality's planned and actual expenditures
- Allows public accountability for fund use
- Distinguishes categories (treatment, prevention, recovery, harm reduction, administration)
