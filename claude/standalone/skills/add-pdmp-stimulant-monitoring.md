## PDMP Overview

Prescription Drug Monitoring Programs (PDMPs) are state-administered databases tracking all Schedule II–V controlled substance prescriptions dispensed within a state. As of 2023, all 50 states plus DC operate PDMPs. Interstate data sharing occurs via PMIX/PMP InterConnect — multiple states can query a shared hub covering dozens of participating states. Most states now mandate prescriber and/or dispenser PDMP checks for scheduled substances before writing or filling a prescription; verify your state's specific requirement.

PDMPs surface: prescriber shopping (multiple providers writing the same drug class within 30 days), early refill patterns, overlapping concurrent prescriptions, and geographic outliers (filling at distant pharmacies inconsistent with the patient's location).

## PDMP Use in Meth Recovery Patients Prescribed Stimulants

**Before initiating:** PDMP check is mandatory prior to writing any Schedule II or IV stimulant. Document the check date and findings.

**Ongoing:** Recheck at every refill or at minimum quarterly. Chart note template: *"No concerning patterns on [date] PDMP review"* or *"Patient receives [medication] from [provider]; coordinated care confirmed."*

**Red flags requiring action:**
- Multiple prescribers for the same drug class within 30 days
- Consistent early refill requests (consumption exceeds prescribed rate)
- Concurrent Schedule II and Schedule IV stimulants from different prescribers without documented clinical justification
- Prescriptions filled at geographically distant or inconsistent pharmacies

Document the specific pattern observed; do not simply note "PDMP reviewed."

## Urine Drug Monitoring (UDM)

Risk-stratified UDM schedule:
- **High-risk** (recent use, early recovery <12 months): monthly
- **Moderate-risk**: every 3 months
- **Low-risk** (stable recovery >12 months, no prior diversion): every 6 months

Random testing is preferred over announced scheduled testing — it limits manipulation. Positive screens for unexpected substances or negative screens when the prescribed stimulant should be present both require follow-up. Negative UDS in a patient who should test positive indicates possible diversion. For confirmatory testing and meth/amphetamine interpretation specifics, see *meth-urine-drug-testing-interpretation*.

## Pill Counts

Not routine for all patients, but indicated in high-risk circumstances: prior diversion history, inconsistent UDS, unexplained early refill requests, or active illicit use suspicion. Unannounced pill counts (phone-in or in-person) are preferred. Document the count result and whether it matches the expected dose math.

## Controlled Substance Agreement (CSA)

The CSA documents informed consent and expectations — it is not a contract with independent legal force. Required elements for stimulant prescribing in SUD patients:
- Single prescriber and single pharmacy designation
- Consent to PDMP access
- Agreement to UDM monitoring as scheduled
- No early refill policy
- Lost or stolen medications will not be replaced
- Conditions under which prescribing will be discontinued

## Prescription Logistics

Schedule II stimulants (methylphenidate, amphetamine salts, lisdexamfetamine): **no refills permitted**; a new prescription is required each month. E-prescribing for Schedule II is now mandatory in many states — verify state-specific requirements for DEA Schedule II e-prescribing compliance. Maximum 30-day supply per fill is standard; some states allow up to a 90-day supply for Schedule III–V but not Schedule II.

## Interstate Considerations

Patients crossing state lines during treatment require PDMP checks in both states. Confirm which states participate in your interstate compact and whether your state's PDMP queries neighboring states automatically or requires manual lookup.
