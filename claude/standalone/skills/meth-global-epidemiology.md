# Global Meth Epidemiology and Seizure Trends

## Core Concepts
- Methamphetamine dominated by two primary production zones: Myanmar's Shan State (East/Southeast Asia supply) and Mexican TCOs (North American supply)
- Two physical forms: powder (yaba/shabu tablets in Asia; "crank" in US) vs. crystal methice — ice now dominates global seizures by weight due to higher purity and value density
- UNODC tracks via World Drug Report (annual) and regional Synthetic Drugs Reports; seizures are the primary proxy for production volume given the absence of direct manufacture data
- Golden Triangle (Myanmar, Laos, Thailand triangle) accounts for the majority of global methamphetamine production; Shan State's ongoing civil conflict has accelerated, not disrupted, production
- Mexican TCOs (Sinaloa, CJNG) supply virtually all US methamphetamine using P2P synthesis; precursor chemicals largely sourced from China

## Mechanisms / Biology
- P2P (phenyl-2-propanone) synthesis now dominates globally (98.4% of US samples), producing racemic d/l-methamphetamine; earlier ephedrine/pseudoephedrine routes produced predominantly d-isomer (more potent)
- Reductive amination of P2P is the preferred clandestine route; accessible precursors have made it difficult to interdict at the chemical level
- Wholesale price in Myanmar production zones dropped to ~$400/kg (2024), reflecting massive supply surplus
- Purity of seized methamphetamine in the US averaged 95.5% in 2023 (down slightly from 96.6%); potency tracks purity — nearly all l-isomer has been removed
- ~19% of US samples cut with dimethylsulfone (DMS); 12% of Los Angeles consumer samples (2023–2024) contained adulterants beyond methamphetamine

## Clinical Picture
- Global amphetamine-type stimulant (ATS) use estimated at ~34 million people (UNODC WDR 2024); methamphetamine is the most-seized ATS, comprising ~72% of global ATS seizures 2015–2019
- Heaviest use burden: East/Southeast Asia, North America (US/Mexico), Australia; rapidly expanding in Near/Middle East, South-West Asia, Southern/Eastern Europe, and sub-Saharan Africa
- South Africa (particularly Cape Town) reports up to 2% adult population prevalence — among highest recorded globally
- Record 236 metric tons seized in East/Southeast Asia alone in 2024 (+24% YoY); Thailand seized ~1 billion meth tablets in that period
- Treatment gap widening globally: LMIC nations and women disproportionately affected; number of people in treatment is decreasing even as use rises

## Evidence & Treatment
- No FDA-approved pharmacotherapy for methamphetamine use disorder (MUD); contingency management (CM) remains the only intervention with consistent RCT support
- Bupropion + naltrexone combination (ibudilast, low-dose) showed modest efficacy in ADAPT-2 trial (2021); mirtazapine reduced use in MSM populations
- Methamphetamine's dual-mechanism (dopamine release + reuptake blockade) and neurotoxicity profile make medication development uniquely difficult vs. opioid use disorder
- UNODC/WHO push for scaled CM implementation in high-burden low-resource settings; prize-based incentive models showing feasibility in Southeast Asia pilot programs
- Expansion into East Africa and Southern Africa (as trafficking corridors become consumer markets) creates acute need for treatment capacity that does not yet exist

## Key Facts
- **236 metric tons** seized in East/Southeast Asia in 2024 — single-year record (+24% vs. 2023)
- **~$400/kg** wholesale price in Myanmar production areas (2024) — collapse of retail pricing reflects production surplus
- **85%** of East/Southeast Asian seizures originate from Southeast Asia; Myanmar's Shan State is primary source
- **95.5%** average purity of US-seized methamphetamine (2023); P2P route accounts for 98.4% of profiled US samples
- Fastest-growing meth trafficking routes in 2024: Myanmar→Cambodia corridor and into Near/Middle East and South-Eastern Europe
