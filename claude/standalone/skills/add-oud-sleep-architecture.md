# OUD Sleep Architecture & Withdrawal Sleep Disturbances

## Sleep During Active Opioid Use

Mu-opioid receptor (MOR) activation suppresses both REM and slow-wave sleep (SWS/NREM stage 3):

- **REM suppression**: Acute heroin reduces REM by ~50%; MOR activation inhibits cholinergic REM-generating nuclei (LDT/PPT) in the brainstem. Partial tolerance develops with chronic use.
- **SWS suppression**: NREM stage 3/4 reduced; NREM stage 2 (light sleep) increases proportionally.
- **Net architecture**: Fragmented, light sleep despite subjective somnolence — high arousals, increased wake transitions.
- **Central sleep apnea (CSA)**: Opioids suppress medullary respiratory drive → CSA common at higher doses, especially with methadone. Distinct from obstructive apnea; worsens with dose escalation.

## OUD Withdrawal Sleep: Hyperarousal Pattern

Opioid withdrawal produces **insomnia** — the opposite of stimulant-withdrawal hypersomnia:

- **REM rebound**: Vivid dreams, nightmares; rebound persists days to weeks after last use.
- **Sleep-onset and maintenance insomnia**: Difficulty initiating and staying asleep; hyperarousal state dominates.
- **Orexin disinhibition**: MORs tonically inhibit orexin (hypocretin) neurons in the lateral hypothalamus. Withdrawal removes this brake → orexin tone surges → arousal, craving, dysphoria. Chronic opioid users show ~54% more orexinergic neurons than controls (neuroadaptive upregulation).
- **HPA/noradrenergic activation**: Locus coeruleus rebound adds to hyperarousal; sweating, tachycardia, and sleeplessness co-occur.

## Comparison: Opioid vs Stimulant Withdrawal Sleep

| Feature | **Opioid withdrawal** | **Stimulant withdrawal** |
|---|---|---|
| Primary complaint | Insomnia, hyperarousal | Hypersomnia, EDS |
| REM | Rebound (vivid dreams) | Rebound (also vivid) |
| Orexin | Disinhibited ↑ | Depleted ↓ |
| PAWS pattern | Persistent insomnia 6–18 mo | Persistent fatigue/EDS weeks–months |
| Craving link | Insomnia predicts relapse | EDS/dysphoria predicts relapse |

## Post-Acute Withdrawal Syndrome (PAWS) — Opioid

Opioid PAWS insomnia outlasts acute withdrawal by months:

- Persistent sleep fragmentation and reduced SWS can persist 6–18 months in abstinence.
- Sleep disturbance is among the strongest relapse predictors; unresolved insomnia intensifies craving.
- Full sleep normalization does **not** reliably occur even with MAT.

## MAT and Sleep

- **Buprenorphine**: Partial MOR agonist; normalizes sleep architecture more than full agonists. Reduces CSA risk vs methadone.
- **Methadone**: High-dose methadone worsens CSA; also suppresses REM at therapeutic doses. Despite this, stabilizes the hyperarousal of acute withdrawal.
- **Naltrexone (XR)**: Removes MOR suppression entirely — improves SWS and REM; may transiently worsen withdrawal-like hyperarousal in early induction.
- **Suvorexant** (dual orexin antagonist): RCT-level evidence for improving sleep and reducing withdrawal severity during buprenorphine taper — targets the orexin disinhibition mechanism directly.

## Polysubstance Context

Concurrent meth + opioid withdrawal produces competing signals:
- Meth withdrawal → hypersomnia drive (orexin depletion, dopamine deficit)
- Opioid withdrawal → insomnia drive (orexin disinhibition, noradrenergic rebound)
- Net result depends on relative dependence severity and timing offset; clinically unpredictable; requires symptom-guided management rather than protocol-driven sleep treatment.

## Clinical Takeaways

1. **Opioid withdrawal = insomnia**, not hypersomnia — distinguish from stimulant withdrawal immediately.
2. **Orexin antagonists** (suvorexant) are mechanistically rational and have early efficacy evidence for OUD sleep.
3. **CSA screening** is warranted for any patient on high-dose methadone or chronic opioids.
4. **PAWS insomnia** is a relapse risk factor requiring active management, not watchful waiting.
5. In **polysubstance withdrawal**, sleep direction is not predictable — assess empirically.
