# Buprenorphine Partial Agonism & Ceiling Effect

## Core Concepts
- **Partial agonist**: Buprenorphine activates mu-opioid receptors (MOR) to only ~40–50% of maximum capacity, even at complete receptor occupancy — contrasted with full agonists (morphine, fentanyl, heroin) which reach 100%
- **Intrinsic efficacy**: Quantified as ~0.67 for respiratory depression (1.0 = full agonist), creating a pharmacological ceiling that does not rise with increasing dose
- **Ceiling effect**: Dose-response curve flattens; doubling the dose above threshold produces no additional respiratory depression or euphoria
- **Buprenorphine/naloxone (Suboxone)**: Naloxone (opioid antagonist) added to deter injection misuse — naloxone has poor sublingual bioavailability but blocks MOR if injected
- **Formulations**: Sublingual film/tablet (Suboxone), sublingual tablet (Subutex), extended-release subdermal implant (Probuphine), monthly injectable (Sublocade)

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **MOR binding affinity**: Dissociation constant (Kd) ≈ 0.3 nM — extremely high affinity, orders of magnitude greater than full agonists and naloxone
- **Slow receptor dissociation**: Half-life at the receptor is prolonged (t½ ~37 hrs for the drug-receptor complex), contributing to once-daily dosing and blunting breakthrough cravings
- **Gi/o protein pathway**: Activates Gi/o → inhibits adenylate cyclase → ↓ cAMP → ↓ calcium influx + ↑ potassium efflux → neuronal hyperpolarization → reduced neurotransmitter release
- **Differential receptor biology**: Ceiling applies to respiratory depression pathways but NOT to analgesia pathways — buprenorphine behaves as a near-full agonist for pain control, explaining its use in chronic pain at low doses (Belbuca, Butrans)
- **Receptor blockade**: High affinity means buprenorphine displaces and blocks full agonists; precipitated withdrawal occurs if buprenorphine is given while full agonists still occupy receptors (COWS score ≥ 8–12 required before induction)
- **Kappa & ORL1 activity**: Also a kappa antagonist (may contribute to antidepressant/anti-craving effects) and partial ORL1 (nociceptin) agonist

## Clinical Picture
- **OUD prevalence**: ~2.7 million Americans met DSM-5 criteria for OUD in 2023 (SAMHSA); only ~25% received any medication-assisted treatment (MAT)
- **Buprenorphine treatment reach**: ~70,000 individuals receiving buprenorphine for OUD reported by CDC 2024 estimates; DEA X-waiver eliminated in 2023 (DATA Waiver Elimination Act), broadening prescriber access
- **Overdose safety profile**: Buprenorphine-only fatal overdose is rare; nearly all buprenorphine-associated deaths involve co-ingestion of benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other CNS depressants — the ceiling effect does not protect against sedative polypharmacy
- **Effective dose range**: Opioid agonist effects plateau between 8 mg/2 mg and 16 mg/4 mg buprenorphine/naloxone (sublingual) in opioid-experienced non-dependent subjects (Johns Hopkins dose-escalation studies)
- **Treatment retention**: Buprenorphine/naloxone achieves ~50% 12-month retention vs ~20–30% with placebo/detox alone (NIDA CTN-0030 trial)

## Evidence & Treatment
- **NIDA CTN-0030 (2010)**: RCT demonstrating extended buprenorphine/naloxone superior to short-term detox for opioid abstinence at 12 weeks (49% vs 8.6% negative urine screens)
- **X-STOPMed / 2023 federal policy**: Elimination of the X-waiver requirement under the MATE Act means any DEA-licensed practitioner can now prescribe buprenorphine for OUD, dramatically expanding access
- **SAMHSA guidelines**: Target maintenance dose 16 mg/day (range 4–24 mg); lower doses (2–4 mg) used for low-level dependence or pain; no established maximum for analgesia ceiling
- **Low-dose induction (Bernese method)**: Micro-dose titration starting at 0.5–1 mg/day avoids precipitated withdrawal — allows induction without full agonist cessation, increasingly used for fentanyl-dependent patients
- **Sublocade (injectable)**: Monthly 300 mg SC injection achieves steady-state plasma ≥ 2 ng/mL (therapeutic threshold); reduces diversion risk, improves adherence

## Key Facts
- Buprenorphine MOR intrinsic efficacy for respiratory depression: **~0.67** (vs 1.0 for full agonists)
- Plasma half-life: **24–42 hours** (supports once-daily dosing; some patients dose every 48–72 hrs)
- Receptor occupancy at 16 mg sublingual: **~95% of MOR** — near-complete blockade, explaining reduced "high" from concurrent full agonist use
- Ceiling dose for respiratory depression (human studies): approximately **8–16 mg sublingual** — further dose escalation adds negligible respiratory risk in opioid-naive patients
- Buprenorphine reduces OUD mortality by **~50%** relative to no treatment (meta-analysis, Sordo et al., BMJ 2017, n = 122,885)
