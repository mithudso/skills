# Medicare Supplement (Medigap) Enrollment

Medigap policies are sold by private insurers to fill gaps in Original Medicare (Parts A and B) — covering copays, coinsurance, and deductibles. Timing of enrollment is critical because outside the Open Enrollment Period, insurers can deny or medically underwrite.

## The 6-Month Medigap Open Enrollment Period (OEP)

**When:** Begins the first day of the month you have Medicare Part B AND are age 65 or older. Runs for **6 consecutive months** — one-time, non-renewable under federal law.

**During OEP:**
- Insurer cannot refuse to sell you any Medigap plan it offers
- Insurer cannot use medical underwriting (no health questions)
- Cannot charge higher premiums due to pre-existing conditions
- May apply a 6-month waiting period for pre-existing conditions (unless you had creditable coverage — then waived)

**After OEP:** Federal law no longer guarantees access; most states allow insurers to medically underwrite, charge higher premiums, or deny coverage outright.

## Guaranteed Issue Rights (Outside OEP)

Certain triggering events confer guaranteed issue rights regardless of health status:

- Leaving a Medicare Advantage plan during its first year (Trial Right)
- Medicare Advantage plan leaves your area or stops covering your county
- Moving out of a Medicare Advantage plan's service area
- Employer group coverage ends
- Plan is found to have misled you or violated rules
- Losing eligibility for Medicaid that was supplementing Medicare

**Important:** Returning to Original Medicare after Medicare Advantage disenrollment does **not** by itself guarantee Medigap access — a triggering event or active GI right must exist.

## State Birthday Rules and Additional Protections

As of 2026, 21+ states provide annual guaranteed-issue windows beyond federal minimums, often allowing plan switching once per year (birthday rule or equivalent). States with birthday rules include California, Idaho, Illinois, Louisiana, Maryland, Missouri, Nevada, Oregon, and others. Check your state's insurance department for current rules; Kentucky, Utah, Virginia, Indiana, Wyoming, and Delaware added protections in 2024–2026.

## Available Plan Types (Standardized by Federal Law)

| Plan | Popular? | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Plan G** | Most popular (new enrollees) | Covers everything except Part B deductible ($257 in 2026) |
| **Plan N** | Growing popularity | Covers most gaps; copays up to $20 office/$50 ER; no Part B excess charges |
| **Plan A** | Basic | Covers Part A coinsurance; limited other coverage |
| **Plan F** | Closed to new enrollees (post-Jan 2020) | First-dollar coverage; still available if eligible before Jan 1, 2020 |
| **Plan K/L** | Lower premium, higher cost-sharing | 50%/75% cost-sharing limits |
| **Plan M** | Uncommon | Covers 50% Part A deductible |
| **High Deductible G** | Lower premium option | Same as G but must meet $2,870 deductible first (2026) |

## Under-65 Access

Federal law does NOT require insurers to sell Medigap to people under 65 who qualify for Medicare due to disability or ESRD. Many states mandate access; check state law. Coverage is typically more expensive for under-65 enrollees. When you turn 65, a fresh 6-month OEP begins.

## Enrollment Process

1. First, ensure you are enrolled in Medicare Part A and Part B
2. Compare plans at medicare.gov or through your state's SHIP (State Health Insurance Assistance Program)
3. Contact insurers directly — plans are standardized so compare premiums, not just plan letters
4. Apply during your OEP or a qualifying guaranteed issue event
5. Pay first premium; coverage begins per policy effective date

## Critical Timing Interactions

- **Medicare Advantage → Medigap:** Returning to Original Medicare does not automatically restore Medigap OEP; you must have a guaranteed issue right or be within an applicable state window
- **Employer coverage → Medigap:** Losing employer coverage is a guaranteed issue event; act within the window (varies by insurer, typically 63 days)
- **New to Medicare at 65:** Your Medigap OEP starts with Part B; if you delay Part B for employer coverage, OEP starts when you eventually take Part B
