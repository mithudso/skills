# Medicare Eligibility Criteria

## The Four Pathways to Medicare Eligibility

### 1. Age 65 (Primary Pathway)

**Requirements:**
- Age 65 or older
- US citizen OR lawfully present permanent resident who has lived in the US for 5 continuous years
- Must also meet work credit requirement for premium-free Part A

**Work credits for premium-free Part A:**
- 40 quarters (10 years) of Medicare-taxed employment — by you OR your current/former spouse
- If < 40 quarters: Part A available but with monthly premium
  - 30–39 quarters: ~$278/month (2025)
  - < 30 quarters: ~$505/month (2025)

**Part B:** Always has a premium regardless of work history (standard ~$185/month in 2025; IRMAA surcharge for income > $106,000/yr single).

### 2. Disability via SSDI (Under 65)

**Requirements:**
- Receiving Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) benefits
- 24-month waiting period from first month of SSDI payment eligibility
- After 24 months, auto-enrolled in Parts A and B

**SSDI Medicare recipients under 65:** ~9 million Americans (as of 2024)

**Disability qualifying conditions:** SSDI qualification itself (inability to perform substantial gainful activity due to medically determinable impairment expected to last 12+ months or result in death) drives Medicare eligibility — SSA determines disability, not CMS.

**SSI (Supplemental Security Income):** SSI alone does NOT confer Medicare eligibility (it may confer Medicaid eligibility — different program).

### 3. End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)

**Requirements:**
- Permanent kidney failure requiring regular dialysis or a kidney transplant
- Works credits: you, your spouse, or a dependent must have sufficient work history
- No age requirement — can be any age

**Coverage timing:**
- Dialysis at home or in facility: Medicare starts month 4 of dialysis treatment (3-month waiting period)
- Self-dialysis training: coverage can start month 1
- Kidney transplant: coverage starts month of transplant if hospitalized that month
- Exception: no waiting period if transplant expected to succeed without dialysis

**Duration:** ESRD Medicare continues for 36 months after a successful kidney transplant, then ends unless another qualifying condition exists.

### 4. ALS (Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis / Lou Gehrig's Disease)

**Requirements:**
- Diagnosed with ALS
- Receiving SSDI benefits for ALS

**Critical difference from other SSDI:** ALS is the ONLY SSDI condition that waives the 24-month waiting period. Medicare begins the same month SSDI payments start (no wait).

**ALS Medicare:** Part A and B, eligible for Part D and Medicare Advantage.

## Citizenship and Residency

**US Citizens:** Eligible if meeting work/disability/disease criteria above.

**Lawful Permanent Residents (Green Card holders):**
- Must reside in US for 5 continuous years immediately before applying
- Then eligible same as citizens if meeting age/work or disability criteria

**Undocumented immigrants:** Not eligible for standard Medicare. Emergency Medicaid may be available.

**Temporary visa holders:** Not eligible.

## Railroad Retirement Board (RRB)

Railroad workers and their spouses may qualify through the Railroad Retirement Board rather than Social Security. Eligibility criteria are equivalent; enrollment processed through RRB instead of SSA.

## Medicare Part C and D Eligibility

- Must have both Part A and Part B to join Medicare Advantage (Part C)
- Part D: must have Part A and/or Part B; open to all Medicare-eligible individuals regardless of health status
- ESRD historically blocked Part C enrollment (Medicare Advantage); ESRD patients can now enroll in MA plans as of January 1, 2021 (21st Century Cures Act change)

## Summary Table

| Pathway | Age | Work Credits | Wait |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age 65 | 65+ | 40 qtrs for premium-free A | None |
| SSDI disability | Any | Through SSDI qualification | 24 months |
| ESRD | Any | Sufficient | 3 months (dialysis) / 0 (transplant) |
| ALS | Any | Through SSDI | 0 months |
