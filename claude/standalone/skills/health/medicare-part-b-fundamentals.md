# Medicare Part B — Medical Insurance

> **Educational only, NOT insurance, medical, or financial advice.**
> Costs change annually. Verify at **[medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov)**
> and **[cms.gov](https://www.cms.gov)**.
> **verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

Medicare Part B is the "medical insurance" component of Original Medicare. Where
Part A covers inpatient hospitalizations, Part B covers **outpatient services**:
doctor visits, outpatient procedures, preventive care, durable medical equipment,
and certain drugs administered in a clinical setting. Unlike Part A, Part B
**always has a monthly premium**.

---

## Part 1 — What Part B covers

### Physician and outpatient services

- Visits to doctors, specialists, and other healthcare providers
- Outpatient surgery (hospital outpatient department or ambulatory surgical center)
- Emergency room visits (even if not admitted as inpatient)
- Second surgical opinions
- Mental health care (outpatient psychotherapy, psychiatric visits)
- Outpatient physical, occupational, and speech therapy
- Cardiac and pulmonary rehabilitation programs
- Diabetes self-management training and supplies (glucose monitors, test strips)

### Preventive care

Part B covers many preventive services **at no cost** (no deductible or coinsurance
when provided by a participating provider):
- Annual Wellness Visits; Welcome to Medicare preventive visit (one-time)
- Screenings: mammography, colorectal cancer, cervical/vaginal, prostate, lung
  cancer (CT scan for eligible smokers), abdominal aortic aneurysm
- Cardiovascular disease risk reduction visits; depression screening
- Vaccinations: flu, pneumococcal, hepatitis B (for those at risk), and COVID-19
- Bone mass measurement; diabetes screening

### Durable Medical Equipment (DME)

Part B covers medically necessary equipment prescribed by a doctor for home use:
wheelchairs, walkers, hospital beds, oxygen, CPAP/BiPAP machines, blood glucose
monitors, prosthetics, and orthotics. You pay 20% coinsurance after the deductible.
Equipment must be obtained from a Medicare-enrolled DME supplier.

### Home health care (primarily Part B)

For beneficiaries who did not have a qualifying 3-day hospital stay, home health
is generally covered under Part B when:
- Ordered by a physician
- You are homebound
- You need intermittent skilled nursing, physical therapy, or speech therapy

No deductible or coinsurance for home health visits (20% coinsurance applies
to DME supplied during the episode).

### Drugs covered under Part B (not Part D)

Part B covers drugs that are typically **administered in a clinical setting**
(by infusion or injection) rather than self-administered at home. Key categories:
- Chemotherapy (infused or oral, if requiring close clinical management)
- Immunosuppressive drugs for kidney transplant patients
- Osteoporosis drugs (certain injectable forms)
- Certain biologics (e.g., Eylea/Lucentis injections for eye conditions)
- Hemophilia clotting factor
- Flu and pneumococcal vaccines

> **Distinction:** self-administered drugs taken at home (tablets, capsules,
> most inhalers) are covered under **Part D**, not Part B.

### Ambulance services

Emergency and some non-emergency (medically required) ambulance transport.
Part B covers 80%; you pay 20%.

### Mental health

Outpatient mental health care — psychotherapy, group therapy, psychiatric
medication management — is covered under Part B, subject to the annual
deductible and 20% coinsurance. Mental health parity rules apply.

## Part 2 — 2026 costs [^1][^2]

| Item | 2026 amount |
|------|------------|
| **Standard monthly premium** | **$202.90** |
| **Annual deductible** | **$283** |
| **Coinsurance (most services)** | **20%** of Medicare-approved amount |
| IRMAA surcharge range (higher earners) | $81.20 – $487.00/month added |

> **The deductible is annual** (calendar year), unlike Part A's per-benefit-period
> structure. Once you've spent $283 in covered services for the year, the deductible
> is satisfied.

> **Coinsurance is 20% of the Medicare-approved amount**, not the provider's
> actual charge. If a provider accepts assignment (agrees to Medicare's approved
> amount), your 20% is based on that lower figure. If they do not accept assignment,
> they can charge up to 15% above Medicare's approved amount — this "excess charge"
> is your responsibility unless you have a Medigap Plan F or G that covers it.

### IRMAA (Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount)

Higher-income beneficiaries pay more than the standard $202.90 premium. IRMAA
is based on your **Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) from two years prior**
(so 2026 IRMAA is based on 2024 tax return). For 2026, IRMAA applies to
individuals with MAGI above **$109,000** (above $218,000 for married filing
jointly).

| 2024 MAGI (individual) | 2026 Part B total monthly premium |
|------------------------|----------------------------------|
| ≤$109,000 | $202.90 (standard) |
| $109,001–$137,000 | ~$284.10 |
| $137,001–$164,000 | ~$365.30 |
| $164,001–$191,000 | ~$446.50 |
| $191,001–$500,000 | ~$527.70 |
| Above $500,000 | ~$689.90 |

> Figures approximate from CMS announcement; verify at SSA.gov for exact
> thresholds. See `medicare-irmaa-income-adjustments` for full bracket tables,
> Part D IRMAA, the SSA-44 appeal form for life-change events, and planning
> strategies.

## Part 3 — What Part B does NOT cover

| Not covered | Where to look |
|-------------|---------------|
| Most outpatient prescription drugs (self-administered) | Part D |
| Dental, routine eye exams, hearing aids | Not covered by Original Medicare (some MA plans add these) |
| Cosmetic surgery | Not covered |
| Acupuncture (except for chronic low back pain, which IS now covered) | Varies |
| Long-term custodial care | Medicaid or private pay |
| Routine foot care (except for certain conditions) | Generally not covered |
| Services outside the U.S. (with narrow exceptions) | Not covered |
| Inpatient room/meals (while admitted) | Part A |

## Part 4 — How Part B interacts with Part A during a hospital stay

During an inpatient admission covered by Part A, the hospital's room, nursing,
and facility services are Part A. **Physician fees billed separately** (your
surgeon, anesthesiologist, the radiologist reading your X-ray) are **Part B**.

This means during a hospitalization you can face both the Part A deductible AND
Part B coinsurance — a Medigap policy or Medicare Advantage plan can help
consolidate these exposures.

---

## Part 5 — Declining Part B (and the risk)

Part B is **voluntary**. Some people delay or decline it:
- **Active employer coverage:** if you have employer group health coverage
  through your own (or your spouse's) employer (at a company with ≥20 employees),
  you may delay Part B enrollment without penalty while that coverage is active.
- **Penalty for late enrollment without a valid excuse:** 10% of the standard
  Part B premium for each full 12 months you were eligible but not enrolled.
  This penalty is **permanent** (added for as long as you have Part B).
  See `medicare-enrollment-periods-penalties`.
