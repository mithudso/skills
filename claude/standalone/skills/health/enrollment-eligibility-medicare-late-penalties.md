# Medicare Late Enrollment Penalties

## Part B Late Enrollment Penalty

**When it applies:** Failure to enroll in Part B during your IEP or an applicable SEP, without having other qualifying coverage (employer group health plan for an active worker).

**Calculation:**
- 10% added to standard Part B premium for every **full 12-month period** you were eligible but not enrolled
- Penalty is **lifetime** — added to your premium every month as long as you have Part B

**Example:**
- Standard Part B premium 2025: ~$185/month
- Delayed enrollment by 2 full 12-month periods (24 months):
- Penalty: 10% × 2 = 20% surcharge
- Monthly premium: $185 × 1.20 = $222/month — for life

**Creditable coverage exemption:** If you were covered by an employer group health plan as an active worker (or as a dependent of an active worker), that period does NOT count toward the penalty calculation. You get a SEP and no penalty if you enroll within 8 months of losing that coverage.

**Note:** COBRA and retiree health coverage are NOT creditable for the Part B penalty exception (they are creditable for Part D).

## Part D Late Enrollment Penalty

**When it applies:** Had a period of 63+ consecutive days without Part D or other creditable prescription drug coverage after the end of your IEP.

**Calculation:**
- 1% of the national base beneficiary premium × number of uncovered months
- National base beneficiary premium 2025: ~$36.78/month
- Penalty is **lifetime** and recalculated annually as the base premium changes

**Example:**
- 24 months without creditable drug coverage
- Penalty: 24 × 1% × $36.78 = ~$8.83/month added for life

**Creditable drug coverage** (prevents penalty):
- Employer/union drug plan certified as "creditable" (actuarially equivalent to Part D)
- VA drug benefits
- TRICARE
- Medicaid
- Indian Health Service
- PACE
- Employer COBRA (if originally creditable)

**Required notice:** Employers/plan sponsors with drug coverage MUST notify Medicare-eligible members annually (before October 15) whether their coverage is creditable or non-creditable.

## Part A Premium Penalty

**Only applies to those who do NOT qualify for premium-free Part A** (< 40 work quarters).

**Calculation:**
- 10% added to Part A premium
- Duration: **twice the number of years** you delayed enrollment (not lifetime)

**Example:**
- Delayed premium Part A enrollment by 2 years
- Pay the 10% surcharge for 4 years (2 × 2)

## Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount (IRMAA)

Not technically a late-penalty, but affects premium:
- Part B and Part D premiums increase based on Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) from 2 years prior
- 2025 IRMAA thresholds: >$106,000 individual / >$212,000 married filing jointly
- Surcharges range from 40% to 240% above standard premium
- Appeals available via SSA Form SSA-44 for "life-changing events"

## Avoiding Penalties

1. **Enroll during IEP** at 65 if no employer coverage
2. **Working past 65 with employer coverage:** Enroll within 8-month SEP window when you retire or coverage ends
3. **Track creditable coverage:** Get annual creditable coverage notice from employer drug plan
4. **Never go 63+ days without creditable drug coverage** — even a gap between employer plan ending and Part D starting triggers Part D penalty

## Appealing a Penalty

**Part B penalty appeal:**
- Submit CMS-40B + CMS-L564 (Request for Employment Information) if SEP claim
- Request reconsideration from SSA within 60 days of premium notice
- Equitable relief possible if misinformation from federal employee caused late enrollment

**Part D penalty appeal:**
- Contact your Part D plan; plans process penalty appeals
- SHIP (State Health Insurance Assistance Program) counselors can help document creditable coverage
