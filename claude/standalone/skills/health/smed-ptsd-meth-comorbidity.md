# PTSD and Methamphetamine Use Disorder: Comorbidity, Mechanisms, and Integrated Treatment

## Prevalence

PTSD occurs in 30–40% of people with meth SUD — 5× the general-population rate (~6–8%). Trauma histories in meth-using populations: childhood abuse 50–70%, sexual assault 40–60%, domestic violence, combat exposure. Adolescents with PTSD are ~9× more likely to develop stimulant SUD than peers. High ACE scores are among the strongest predictors of adult meth SUD onset; each additional ACE point compounds dopamine-system vulnerability.

## Bidirectional Relationship

**Trauma → meth (self-medication pathway)**
- PTSD hyperarousal + nightmare-driven sleep disruption → stimulant use for daytime alertness and vigilance management (overlaps with `meth-sleep-architecture-effects.md`)
- Emotional numbing and anhedonia → dopaminergic stimulation sought as affective substitute
- Hypervigilance → stimulant-enhanced arousal becomes negatively reinforcing (removes dysphoria)
- Childhood maltreatment → epigenetic downregulation of D2 receptors and DAT → increased addiction vulnerability from first use

**Meth → trauma (victimization pathway)**
- Meth intoxication impairs threat assessment → assault exposure
- Meth psychosis places users in dangerous environments
- Meth-associated sex work and coercive exchange → assault, trafficking
- Intimate partner violence rates elevated in meth-using couples; perpetration and victimization both elevated

## Neurobiological Overlap

Both conditions dysregulate the same three systems:

| System | PTSD effect | Meth effect | Combined |
|--------|-------------|-------------|----------|
| HPA axis | Glucocorticoid dysregulation, CRF hypersecretion | Acute HPA activation, PAWS HPA blunting | Amplified allostatic load |
| Amygdala | Hyperreactivity, extinction failure | DA-sensitized fear responding | Compounded fear generalization |
| PFC | Hypofunction — impaired extinction recall | Structural/functional damage (gray matter loss) | Severe executive impairment; relapse-promoting |

CRF/norepinephrine dysregulation is a shared hub — see `nat-crf-antagonists-addiction-sleep.md`.

## Sleep Intersection

PTSD nightmares + hyperarousal + sleep avoidance compound meth-withdrawal sleep disruption (protracted insomnia, REM rebound). Clinical differentiation matters for treatment:

- PTSD nightmare: trauma-specific content, night-terror phenomenology, daytime hyperstartle
- Meth-withdrawal dreaming: vivid but non-trauma content, resolves over weeks, accompanied by hypoarousal (not hyperarousal)
- Overlap window: 2–6 weeks post-cessation; prazosin for nightmares should be considered early if trauma history is confirmed

## Evidence-Based Integrated Treatments

**Seeking Safety** (Najavits): present-focused coping; does not require trauma processing; 20+ RCTs, including meth-using samples; safe at any recovery stage; addresses PTSD and SUD simultaneously in 25 topics. First-line for early or unstable recovery.

**COPE / Prolonged Exposure + SUD** (Ruglass/Najavits): concurrent PE delivery with SUD treatment; not contraindicated by active use or early recovery; fear hierarchy must exclude substance-related cues as exposures to avoid iatrogenic craving.

**CPT (Cognitive Processing Therapy) adapted for SUD**: addresses stuck points about blame and self-worth common in trauma+addiction presentations; fewer sessions than PE.

**EMDR in SUD**: emerging evidence; Phase 2 stabilization (safety/grounding) must precede trauma processing; contraindicated during acute meth withdrawal (first 2–4 weeks); dissociation screening required.

**Integrated > sequential**: Multiple RCTs show simultaneous PTSD+SUD treatment outperforms treating SUD first and PTSD later. PTSD symptom exacerbations are more frequent during SUD-only treatment.

## Pharmacotherapy in Meth Recovery

| Agent | Indication | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| Prazosin | PTSD nightmares, hyperarousal sleep | Alpha-1 antagonist; preferred over benzodiazepines; titrate to 1–15 mg HS |
| Sertraline / paroxetine | PTSD (FDA-approved) | Modest effect size; useful adjunct; monitor activating side effects in meth recovery |
| Bupropion | Depression + craving (off-label) | See `smed-bupropion-adhd-stimulant-sud.md`; also attenuates meth craving |
| **Avoid: benzodiazepines** | PTSD anxiety | High SUD cross-addiction risk; worsen meth recovery outcomes; high craving in this population |

## Clinical Pearls

- Screen every meth-using patient for PTSD (PCL-5); screen every PTSD patient for stimulant use — standard unidirectional screening misses half the comorbidity
- "Can't I just treat the drug problem first?" — no; untreated PTSD is a leading relapse driver; waiting is not neutral
- Meth-induced paranoia and psychosis can mimic PTSD hypervigilance; distinguish by trauma history, timeline, and resolution trajectory post-abstinence
- Motivational interviewing adapted for trauma ("trauma-informed MI") improves engagement before formal evidence-based treatment begins
