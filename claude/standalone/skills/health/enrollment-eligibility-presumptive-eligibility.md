# Medicaid Presumptive Eligibility

## What Is Presumptive Eligibility?

Presumptive eligibility (PE) allows specially qualified entities to make a same-day determination that an individual is likely eligible for Medicaid, providing immediate temporary Medicaid coverage before the full eligibility determination is completed.

**Key features:**
- Coverage begins immediately upon PE determination — no waiting
- Based on a simplified screening, not the full Medicaid eligibility process
- Temporary — valid until the full application is determined (or a set period)
- Does NOT replace the full Medicaid application; full application must be submitted

## Legal Basis

- **Children and pregnant women PE:** Authorized since OBRA 1986 / OBRA 1990
- **ACA §2201:** Expanded PE to all Medicaid eligibility groups; states may opt in
- **42 CFR §435.1100–§435.1110** — Presumptive eligibility regulations

## Qualified Entities (QEs) — Who Can Make PE Determinations

Not everyone can make PE determinations — only organizations that apply to and are approved by the state Medicaid agency as Qualified Entities:

- Hospitals and hospital systems
- Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs)
- Community health centers
- Rural health clinics
- Schools (for children)
- Outpatient mental health / SUD treatment facilities (in many states)
- Certain Navigators and enrollment assisters
- Pharmacies (in some states)

**Process:** Organizations apply to state Medicaid agency; train staff; make PE determinations using standardized simplified screening tool.

## Qualifying Groups

**All states must offer PE for:**
- Children (Medicaid and CHIP)
- Pregnant women

**States may also offer PE for (ACA expansion option):**
- Adults 19–64 in Medicaid expansion states
- Former foster youth
- Other eligible groups

**NC:** Offers PE for pregnant women, children, and expansion adults.

## How PE Works — Step by Step

### Step 1: PE Determination

Qualified entity worker (e.g., hospital social worker) completes simplified screening:
- Household size
- Estimated monthly income (MAGI-based)
- Basic categorical eligibility (pregnant, age, parent, etc.)
- State residency
- Citizenship/immigration (self-attestation)

If applicant appears likely eligible → PE granted immediately.

### Step 2: Coverage Begins

- PE coverage effective the date of PE determination
- Coverage is real Medicaid coverage — for services from enrolled Medicaid providers
- Does NOT cover services before PE determination date (unlike retroactive Medicaid)
- Covers services needed NOW (the hospital visit, prenatal care, etc.)

### Step 3: Full Application Submitted

The applicant (or the QE on their behalf) must submit a full Medicaid application:
- **Deadline:** 10 days from PE determination (applicant or QE submits full application)
- If full application not submitted within 10 days: PE coverage terminates at end of month following PE grant month

### Step 4: Full Eligibility Determination

State Medicaid agency processes the full application:
- If approved: Coverage continues without gap; no re-enrollment needed
- If denied: PE coverage terminates when denial becomes effective

## Duration of PE Coverage

- PE coverage runs through the end of the month following the month of PE determination
- Full application must be submitted within 10 days

**Example:**
- PE granted June 15
- Coverage through July 31
- Full application must be filed by June 25 (10 days)
- If application filed timely and approved → continuous coverage

## Scope of PE Coverage

PE provides coverage for Medicaid-covered services from enrolled Medicaid providers during the PE period. Limitations:
- Provider must be Medicaid-enrolled — PE doesn't cover out-of-network non-enrolled providers
- Long-term services and supports not covered under PE
- Drug coverage may vary (state-dependent)

## PE in the SUD Treatment Context

PE is particularly important in substance use disorder treatment:
- Person arrives at ED or detox in crisis
- Hospital social worker makes PE determination → immediate Medicaid coverage
- Medicaid covers the acute detox / treatment episode
- Full application submitted while patient is in treatment
- Ideal pathway: PE → full application → Medicaid approved → continuous SUD treatment coverage

Many NC hospitals and FQHCs have trained PE workers specifically to address gaps in SUD treatment access.

## Limits of PE

1. **Not retroactive:** PE covers from determination date forward, not prior services
2. **No asset determination:** PE doesn't assess assets — full determination may reveal non-MAGI issues
3. **Not a guarantee:** Full application may be denied; PE coverage then ends
4. **10-day application deadline:** Missing this deadline ends coverage

## NC Presumptive Eligibility

NC offers PE for:
- Pregnant women: Hospital-based PE widely available
- Children: Schools, FQHCs, hospitals
- Adults (expansion group): Hospitals, FQHCs, and other QEs
- SUD focus: NC has encouraged SUD treatment facilities to become QEs

NC DHHS maintains list of approved QEs; county DSS offices not typically QEs but can expedite processing when PE is pending.
