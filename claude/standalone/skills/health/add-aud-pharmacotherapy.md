## AUD Pharmacotherapy

Only ~8% of patients with AUD receive any pharmacotherapy — the single most actionable gap in addiction medicine.

### FDA-Approved (First-Line)

**Naltrexone** — mu-opioid receptor antagonist; blocks endorphin release from alcohol, blunting reward. Best outcome: reduction in heavy drinking days (not necessarily abstinence).
- Oral (ReVia): 50 mg/day. Hepatotoxic at high doses; avoid with acute hepatitis, liver failure, or current opioid use.
- XR-injectable (Vivitrol): 380 mg IM monthly. Eliminates adherence barrier; preferred when daily pill burden is a concern.
- COMBINE trial (N=1383, 2006): oral naltrexone + behavioral therapy reduced heavy drinking; XR formulation validated in subsequent RCTs.

**Acamprosate** — modulates GABA/glutamate hyperactivity during protracted withdrawal. Best outcome: abstinence maintenance after detox, not harm reduction.
- 666 mg TID (2 g/day). Dose-reduce for CrCl 30–50; contraindicated in severe renal impairment.
- European trials (COMBINE precursor) strongly positive; US COMBINE trial negative — difference attributed to higher baseline abstinence in European cohorts and concurrent psychosocial support intensity.
- Safe in liver disease; use when naltrexone is contraindicated.

**Disulfiram (Antabuse)** — inhibits aldehyde dehydrogenase → acetaldehyde accumulates → aversive flushing, nausea, hypotension. Deterrent, not craving-targeted.
- 250–500 mg/day. Evidence vs. placebo limited; efficacy is adherence-dependent.
- Supervised use models (observed dosing, partner-witnessed ingestion) dramatically improve outcomes.
- Avoid with metronidazole, alcohol-containing products, cardiac disease, psychosis.

### Off-Label (Evidence-Supported)

**Gabapentin** — GABAergic; addresses craving, protracted withdrawal anxiety, and sleep disruption (the triad driving relapse at 1–3 months).
- 900–1800 mg/day in divided doses; evidence strongest at higher end.
- FDA rejected AUD indication (2023) citing abuse potential; ASAM Clinical Practice Guidelines (2020) and VA/DoD guidelines support use, particularly post-withdrawal.
- Monitor for dependence, especially at doses >1800 mg/day or in patients with opioid history.

**Topiramate** — enhances GABA, inhibits glutamate (AMPA/kainate); reduces heavy drinking days and cravings.
- 25 mg/day titrated slowly to 150–300 mg/day over 8 weeks (tolerability-limiting if rushed).
- Moderate evidence (Johnson et al. RCTs); tolerability concerns include cognitive slowing ("dopamax"), paresthesias, kidney stones, teratogenicity.
- Less-used than gabapentin in practice but evidence base is comparable.

**Baclofen** — GABA-B agonist; approved for AUD in France (2014) at high doses (up to 300 mg/day).
- US evidence mixed; lower-dose trials (30–60 mg/day) underwhelming; French approval based on Addolorato et al. and ALPADIR trial.
- May have niche in patients with anxiety comorbidity or hepatic disease (renally cleared).

**Nalmefene** — mu/kappa opioid receptor modulator; approved EU (Selincro) for as-needed dosing before anticipated drinking — harm-reduction model without requiring abstinence intent.
- Not FDA-approved. EU ESENSE trials positive for heavy drinking day reduction.

**Ondansetron** — 5-HT3 antagonist; evidence specific to early-onset AUD subtype (onset <25 years), where serotonin dysregulation is more prominent. 4 mcg/kg BID in trials.

### Emerging

**GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide)** — preclinical and early human data show reduced alcohol intake across multiple drinking models. Mechanistic hypothesis: mesolimbic dopamine pathway modulation via GLP-1 receptors in VTA/nucleus accumbens. Active RCTs underway (NCT05895643). No current clinical indication for AUD; watch space.

### Treatment-Goal Alignment

| Goal | Preferred agents |
|------|-----------------|
| Reduce heavy drinking days | Naltrexone (oral or XR), nalmefene (EU), topiramate |
| Abstinence maintenance | Acamprosate, naltrexone, disulfiram (supervised) |
| Post-withdrawal symptoms (anxiety, sleep, craving) | Gabapentin |
| Liver disease present | Acamprosate, gabapentin (naltrexone relatively contraindicated) |
| Opioid use present | Acamprosate, gabapentin (naltrexone/nalmefene contraindicated) |

### Combination Therapy

COMBINE showed naltrexone + behavioral therapy additive; naltrexone + acamprosate combination not superior to monotherapy in most trials. Disulfiram + naltrexone associated with lower alcohol-related hospitalization in register studies. No strong RCT evidence mandates combination as first-line.

### Underutilization

Only ~8% of AUD patients receive pharmacotherapy. Barriers: clinician discomfort, insurance coverage gaps, stigma, lack of integration with primary care. Buprenorphine/naloxone for OUD has ~20× higher prescribing rate despite similar evidence base — an equity and training gap.
