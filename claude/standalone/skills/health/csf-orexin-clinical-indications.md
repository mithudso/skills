# CSF Hypocretin-1: Clinical Indications and Interpretation

## Core Concepts

- CSF hypocretin-1 (orexin-A) measurement via lumbar puncture is a ICSD-3 diagnostic criterion for Narcolepsy Type 1 (NT1) alongside MSLT
- Low CSF orexin reflects irreversible loss of hypothalamic orexin neurons, making it a stable biomarker unlike MSLT (state-dependent)
- No special timing requirements for collection — unlike MSLT, circadian phase and sleep schedule do not affect CSF orexin levels
- Three-zone reference range: <110 pg/mL (diagnostic for NT1), 110–200 pg/mL (indeterminate), >200 pg/mL (normal)
- Test is ordered by Mayo Clinic Labs (ORXNA assay) and select academic centers; not widely available in routine clinical labs

## Mechanisms / Evidence

- Sensitivity 87–88.5% and specificity 99% for NT1 with typical cataplexy (Mignot et al.; multiple validation cohorts)
- For NT2 (narcolepsy without cataplexy): specificity remains 99% but sensitivity drops to ~33% — most NT2 patients have normal orexin levels
- Undetectable orexin (<110 pg/mL) is essentially pathognomonic: when positive, PPV approaches 94–100% in high-prevalence sleep clinic populations
- Intermediate values (110–200 pg/mL) occur in ~10% of NT1 cases; typical cataplexy history upgrades diagnostic confidence even in this zone (Oxford SLEEP, 2022)
- Secondary orexin deficiency documented in: Guillain-Barré syndrome (acute phase, reversible), paraneoplastic encephalitis (anti-Ma2, anti-NMDAR), Niemann-Pick type C, hypothalamic injury (tumor, trauma, surgery), and Whipple disease
- CSF orexin does NOT fluctuate with stimulant or sedating medication use, distinguishing it from MSLT

## Clinical Application

- **Preferred over MSLT when:** (1) patient cannot withdraw REM-suppressing medications (SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, stimulants); (2) shift workers or circadian-misaligned patients where actigraphy-verified schedule normalization is impractical; (3) atypical cataplexy requiring diagnostic certainty before starting sodium oxybate; (4) pediatric patients with unclear sleep architecture
- **MSLT preferred when:** orexin level is in indeterminate range (110–200 pg/mL) and cataplexy is absent or atypical; also preferred for NT2 workup since CSF orexin sensitivity is low
- **Collection procedure:** standard lumbar puncture (L3–L4 or L4–L5 interspace); collect 1–2 mL CSF into polypropylene tube; freeze at −80°C immediately; avoid glass tubes (orexin adsorbs to glass); no dietary restrictions or sleep schedule adjustment required
- **Decision algorithm:** typical cataplexy + sleepiness → CSF orexin (skip MSLT if orexin <110 pg/mL confirms NT1); sleepiness without cataplexy → MSLT first; borderline MSLT in confounded patient → add CSF orexin; secondary narcolepsy suspicion → CSF orexin + targeted workup for underlying cause

## Key Facts

- <110 pg/mL cutoff: derived from mean of normal controls minus 2 SD; ~30% of healthy controls' mean is ~400–500 pg/mL
- False-positive rate with current RIA: <1%; one published case of false-positive via enzyme immunoassay (JCSM, 2022) — RIA remains gold standard
- CSF orexin is low (<110 pg/mL) in 10–15% of Guillain-Barré cases during acute phase and typically normalizes with recovery
- ICSD-3 (2014) formally incorporated CSF orexin <110 pg/mL as a standalone diagnostic criterion for NT1 — the only sleep disorder with a validated biomarker at this level
