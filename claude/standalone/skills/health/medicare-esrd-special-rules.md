# Medicare for End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)

ESRD (permanent kidney failure requiring dialysis or transplant) is one of three pathways to Medicare before age 65, alongside SSDI disability and ALS. ESRD has unique coverage rules that differ significantly from standard Medicare.

## Who Qualifies for Medicare via ESRD

Any person — **regardless of age** — with end-stage renal disease requiring:
- Regular dialysis (hemodialysis or peritoneal), OR
- A kidney transplant

No minimum work history requirement beyond being entitled to Social Security benefits (or a spouse/parent entitled). Most ESRD patients qualify via their own or a family member's work record.

## When Medicare Coverage Begins

### Outpatient dialysis at a facility
Medicare coverage begins the **first day of the 4th month** of dialysis. Example: dialysis starts January — Medicare begins April 1.

**Exception:** Coverage begins sooner (first month) if:
- You participate in a home dialysis training program during the waiting period
- You plan to perform home dialysis and began training in month 1

### Home dialysis
Coverage begins the **first month** home dialysis begins (no waiting period if training starts in month 1 of dialysis).

### Kidney transplant
If you receive a transplant before starting dialysis (or within the waiting period):
- Coverage begins the month of transplant
- If already on dialysis with Medicare, coverage continues

### Application
Apply through Social Security (SSA) — same as other Medicare pathways. You cannot auto-enroll with ESRD; you must apply.

## What Parts Are Available for ESRD Patients

- **Part A** (hospital): Covers inpatient hospitalizations for dialysis-related and unrelated conditions, kidney transplants
- **Part B** (medical): Covers outpatient dialysis sessions, physician services, dialysis supplies and equipment at home
- **Part D** (drugs): Covers certain ESRD medications (oral equivalents of dialysis-related drugs were added to the ESRD bundle)
- **Medicare Advantage**: Now available to new ESRD enrollees (see below)

## Dialysis Cost-Sharing Under Original Medicare

| Service | Cost |
|---------|------|
| Inpatient dialysis (hospital) | Standard Part A deductible + coinsurance |
| Outpatient dialysis sessions | 20% coinsurance after Part B deductible ($283 in 2026) |
| Home dialysis supplies/equipment | 20% coinsurance after deductible |
| Dialysis-related medications | Usually bundled into dialysis payment; some oral drugs under Part D |

Medigap plans (Plan G, Plan N, etc.) can cover the 20% coinsurance.

## The 30-Month Coordination Period (ESRD + Employer Coverage)

If you have ESRD and are also covered by an employer group health plan (GHP) through work or a working spouse:

- **First 30 months:** Employer GHP is PRIMARY; Medicare is secondary
- **After 30 months:** Medicare becomes PRIMARY; employer GHP is secondary

**Why this matters:** If you don't enroll in Medicare Part B during the 30-month coordination period, you'll lack secondary coverage. After 30 months when Medicare becomes primary, gaps in Part B enrollment may result in penalties and coverage lapses.

**ESRD-specific Part B note:** There is **no Special Enrollment Period** specifically for ESRD — even if you're enrolled via ESRD and later become eligible at 65 (dual entitlement), you cannot use an employer SEP to enroll in Part B for the ESRD-based coverage period.

## Medicare Advantage for ESRD Patients

**Before 2021 (old rule):** ESRD patients could NOT enroll in Medicare Advantage plans.

**2021+ (current rule):** ESRD patients CAN enroll in Medicare Advantage:
- May enroll in any MA plan during appropriate enrollment periods
- **ESRD-specific D-SNPs** (Dual Eligible Special Needs Plans) designed for ESRD patients who are also dual eligible (Medicare + Medicaid) — provide coordinated care

**Considerations:** Some MA plans have limited dialysis provider networks. Verify that your dialysis center is in-network before enrolling in an MA plan.

## Kidney Transplant Coverage

### Recipient (patient receiving transplant)
- Medicare Part A covers the inpatient hospital stay for the transplant
- Part B covers physician services, follow-up care, immunosuppressive drugs (orally administered) after transplant
- Medicare covers transplant preparation, surgery, and post-transplant care

### Donor (living or deceased organ donor)
- Medicare covers the **donor's hospital costs** for donation surgery — even if the donor is not a Medicare beneficiary
- Covers donor's evaluation, surgery, follow-up care directly related to the donation
- The patient/recipient does NOT incur cost-sharing for the donor's covered services

## After a Successful Transplant: When Medicare Ends

Medicare coverage for ESRD continues for **36 months after a successful kidney transplant** for those who gained Medicare solely due to ESRD (not based on age or disability):
- Coverage ends 36 months after the month of successful transplant
- Exception: If the transplant fails and dialysis restarts, ESRD Medicare resumes

### The Immunosuppressive Drug Benefit (Standalone)
For people who lose Medicare after the 36-month post-transplant period, CMS created a standalone **Immunosuppressive Drug Benefit** (effective January 2023):
- Available to kidney transplant recipients who no longer have Medicare but need ongoing immunosuppressive drugs
- **2026 premium:** $121.60/month (or higher with IRMAA)
- **2026 deductible:** $283
- **Cost-sharing:** 20% coinsurance after deductible
- Only covers immunosuppressive drugs — not other Medicare benefits

## ESRD Network Programs

CMS funds 18 ESRD Networks nationwide to improve dialysis quality and patient outcomes:
- Monitor dialysis facility quality metrics
- Patient advocacy and grievance resolution
- Coordinate care transitions
- Support patient education

If you have concerns about your dialysis facility's care, contact your regional ESRD Network (find at cms.gov/medicare/quality/esrd-network-programs).
