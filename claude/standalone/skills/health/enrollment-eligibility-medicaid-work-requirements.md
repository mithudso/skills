# Medicaid Work Requirements / Community Engagement

## What Was Passed: One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R.1)

The **One Big Beautiful Bill Act**, signed July 4, 2025, is the 2025 federal budget reconciliation law. It includes mandatory Medicaid community engagement requirements (work requirements) for the first time as a federal mandate.

**Effective date: January 1, 2027** (states required to implement; may implement earlier via 1115 waiver)

## Who Is Affected

Work requirements apply to **ACA Medicaid expansion adults**:
- Ages 19 through 64
- Covered through the ACA Medicaid expansion group (138% FPL income limit)
- OR covered under certain Section 1115 demonstration waivers providing minimum essential coverage

**44 states + DC** (all states that expanded Medicaid) must implement; non-expansion states are not affected by this provision.

## The Requirement: 80 Hours Per Month

To maintain Medicaid eligibility, covered individuals must complete **80 hours per month** of qualifying activities:

- **Employment** (paid work)
- Job training programs
- Educational enrollment (at least half-time)
- Community service
- Vocational rehabilitation
- Combinations of the above

## Exemptions

The following individuals are **exempt** from work requirements:
- Pregnant women
- Individuals with a physical or mental disability that prevents compliance
- Primary caregivers of a dependent child under 14 or a disabled adult family member
- Students enrolled at least half-time
- Tribal members (on tribal lands or tribal enrollees per tribal determination)
- Individuals in substance use disorder treatment
- Age 65+ (not in expansion group anyway) and under 19
- Active-duty military service members

States may recognize additional exemptions; good cause/hardship processes are required.

## Verification and Compliance Process

**State responsibilities:**
- Must conduct member outreach between **June 30 and August 31, 2026** (by mail + one additional method: phone, text, website, or electronic means)
- Must implement a **"look-back" verification** at application: check whether the applicant met the requirement in 1–3 months before applying
- For current enrollees: verify compliance for at least 1 month within each **6-month eligibility review period** (tied to the new semi-annual redetermination cycle)

**HHS/CMS must issue implementation guidance** to states by June 1, 2026, including definitions and standards.

## Early State Implementation (Before Jan 1, 2027)

States may implement work requirements early through **Section 1115 waivers**. As of June 2026:
- **Georgia Pathways**: Already operating since July 2023; extended through December 31, 2026; 80 hours/month
- **Nebraska**: Announced implementation May 1, 2026 via state plan amendment
- **Montana**: Planned implementation July 1, 2026
- **Iowa**: Planned implementation December 1, 2026

## Projected Impact

The **Congressional Budget Office (CBO)** projects work requirement provisions will:
- Reduce federal Medicaid spending by **$326 billion** over 10 years
- Cause more than **5 million people** to lose coverage by 2034
- Have minimal impact on employment rates (most who lose coverage will already be working, exempt, or face documentation barriers)

Health policy researchers find the majority of people subject to work requirements are already working, exempt, or face systemic barriers to documentation — meaning coverage losses are primarily administrative, not behavioral.

## Coverage Loss Risks

High-risk scenarios for losing coverage even while complying:
- Documentation and reporting errors
- Address change / notice not received
- Administrative barriers for rural or homeless individuals
- Difficulty obtaining hardship exemption documentation
- Technology access barriers to online reporting portals

## Historical Context: 1115 Waivers Pre-H.R.1

Before H.R.1, work requirements required CMS approval through Section 1115 waivers and were subject to legal challenge. Gresham v. Becerra struck down Arkansas's work requirement in 2020; federal courts blocked multiple state programs. H.R.1 changes the legal landscape by making work requirements a statutory mandate.

## What Affected Individuals Should Know Now (2026)

1. Watch for state outreach notices June–August 2026 — your state is required to contact you
2. Understand your exemption status — if you are pregnant, disabled, a caregiver, or a student, document this
3. If employed, maintain records of work hours (pay stubs, employer statements)
4. Contact SHIP counselors or Navigators for assistance understanding your state's specific requirements
5. If you lose coverage, you can reapply immediately or appeal the termination

## NC-Specific

NC expanded Medicaid October 2023 and will be required to implement by January 1, 2027. NC DHHS will be developing the compliance verification system and outreach process per H.R.1 requirements. Monitor ncdhhs.gov for updates.
