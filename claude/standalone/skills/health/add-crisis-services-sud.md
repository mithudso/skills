# Crisis Services for SUD Populations — Meth Recovery Focus

## Core Principle

Crisis services in the US were historically siloed from SUD treatment. The 988 Lifeline (2022) and the SAMHSA three-element framework ("Someone to Contact / Someone to Respond / A Safe Place") have partially bridged this gap, but SUD-competent crisis care remains unevenly distributed. Meth recovery patients face crises at the intersection of psychiatric decompensation, suicidal ideation, relapse, and stimulant-induced psychosis — all of which require different pathways than a standard psychiatric presentation.

## 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline

Launched July 16, 2022; replaced 1-800-273-8255. Covers **any** behavioral health crisis — suicidal ideation, acute psychiatric decompensation, substance use crisis, and overwhelming relapse urges. Contacts: call, text, or chat at 988lifeline.org.

Specialized sub-lines:
- **Spanish language** — automated option at connect
- **Veterans** — press 1
- **LGBTQ+** — press 3
- **ASL videophone** — available via chat

In 2025, 988 received >8 million contacts. October 2024 FCC rules require all wireless carriers to implement georouting, directing calls to local crisis centers rather than national overflow — improving local resource linkage.

**SUD relevance for meth recovery:** Suicidal ideation during meth withdrawal or early recovery (anhedonia, dysphoria, PAWS) is a primary indication. Callers do not have to be actively suicidal — overwhelming craving, psychiatric crisis, and acute relapse also qualify. 988 counselors are trained for SUD presentations.

## Mobile Crisis Units

Co-responder teams dispatched for behavioral health crises — typically a crisis counselor plus an EMT/paramedic or peer specialist, without police as primary responder. As of 2024, SAMHSA's National Survey identified 2,111 mobile crisis teams nationwide serving ~900,000 people/year, backed by >$900M in public investment.

**Access:** In many jurisdictions, calling **988** (not 911) triggers mobile crisis dispatch. This is the preferred pathway for meth-related crises — reduces law enforcement contact, connects to SUD-aware services, and decreases use of force. The CAHOOTS model (Eugene, OR, est. 1989) is the original template; now widespread.

**Limitation:** Only 24% of mobile crisis teams use integrated EHRs as of 2024 (NRI survey), creating dispatch coordination gaps. Coverage is urban-concentrated; rural areas often lack mobile response entirely.

## Crisis Stabilization Units (CSU)

Short-term, less-restrictive alternative to psychiatric inpatient. Two tiers:
- **23-hour CSU:** Outpatient-licensed; evaluate and stabilize for <24 hours; appropriate for acute relapse or mild-moderate psychiatric decompensation not requiring hospitalization
- **Short-term residential CSU (3–7 days):** Can manage concurrent acute detox + psychiatric stabilization; some facilities operate dedicated SUD detox beds alongside mental health beds (e.g., 6 SUD detox + 6 MH stabilization)

CSUs are the appropriate **step-up** from outpatient SUD treatment when acuity exceeds outpatient capacity, and the **step-down** from ED when full hospitalization is not warranted. SUD-capable CSUs are increasing but remain geographically uneven. Find local CSUs via 988 referral or SAMHSA's treatment locator (findtreatment.gov).

## Emergency Department

Default crisis setting when other options are unavailable or acuity is high. Key considerations for SUD/meth presentations:
- **SBIRT approach** applies at triage (see meth-sbirt-ed skill)
- **Peer recovery specialists** in the ED provide same-session SUD engagement
- **Bridge Clinic model** links patients to SUD treatment same-day or next-day post-ED
- **Psychiatric consultation** for stimulant-induced psychosis or co-occurring psychiatric emergency

Limitation: EDs are suboptimal for SUD crisis without co-occurring psychiatric emergency — high cost, low SUD competence in many settings, no treatment continuity.

## Peer Warmlines

Non-crisis, low-intensity emotional support; active listening, not therapy. Staffed by volunteer peer specialists with lived experience. Appropriate for **pre-crisis states** — overwhelmed, strong urges, post-relapse shame — before a situation escalates.

- **National Alliance on Mental Illness (NAMI):** nami.org/help → state warmline directory
- **Depression and Bipolar Support Alliance (DBSA):** 1-800-826-3632
- **SUD-specific peer warmlines** exist in some states (CA, NY, OR have established programs); availability varies

## SAMHSA National Helpline

1-800-662-4357 (1-800-662-HELP); 24/7; free, confidential; available in Spanish. Not crisis counseling — a **treatment referral and information service**. Appropriate for: post-crisis linkage to local outpatient/residential SUD treatment, navigation when patient is stable but needs treatment connection.

## MAT Continuity Across Crisis Settings — Critical Principle

Patients in meth recovery who are also receiving MAT for opioid use disorder (buprenorphine or methadone) face a high-risk discontinuity gap when they present to crisis services. **Do not stop MAT during psychiatric or SUD crisis.**

Evidence: Buprenorphine discontinuation within 6 months substantially increases relapse, ED visit, hospitalization, and overdose mortality risk. Patients retained 15–18+ months on buprenorphine have markedly lower post-discontinuation hospitalization rates.

Clinical action points:
1. Confirm MAT status at every crisis presentation
2. Advocate explicitly for MAT continuation if patient is transferred to CSU, inpatient psychiatric unit, or ED
3. At discharge from any crisis setting, ensure MAT prescription or OTP linkage is reestablished before patient leaves
4. Do not interpret MAT as contraindicated during meth-related psychiatric crisis — the medications are protective and neurobiologically independent of stimulant use
