## Pitolisant in Substance Use Disorder: Safety Profile

### Why this question arises
Most narcolepsy pharmacotherapy is DEA-scheduled: amphetamines (Schedule II), sodium oxybate (Schedule I), modafinil/armodafinil/solriamfetol (Schedule IV). Pitolisant (Wakix, FDA-approved 2019) is the sole unscheduled narcolepsy agent, making it clinically distinct for patients with SUD.

---

### Mechanism: Why the reward pathway is largely bypassed

Pitolisant is an H3 receptor inverse agonist/antagonist. H3 receptors are presynaptic autoreceptors on histaminergic neurons in the tuberomammillary nucleus (TMN); blocking them removes tonic inhibition → increased histamine release → wake promotion.

**Key dopaminergic contrast:**
- Pitolisant does **not** bind DAT or NET directly
- Does **not** cause presynaptic dopamine release at nucleus accumbens terminals
- Does **not** bind dopamine receptors (D1–D5)
- Secondary monoaminergic effects (modest NE/DA release via histamine projections to LC and VTA) are **indirect and minor** — insufficient to generate conditioned reinforcement

Classic stimulants (amphetamines, methylphenidate) flood the mesolimbic DA system directly. Pitolisant's histaminergic mechanism produces wakefulness without triggering the dopamine reward cascade that drives addiction and misuse.

---

### DEA scheduling: non-controlled substance

FDA approved pitolisant August 2019 (NDA 211150). Despite initial internal consideration of Schedule IV placement, the DEA's Controlled Substances Staff (CSS) concluded there was insufficient evidence of abuse potential and **did not recommend scheduling**. Pitolisant remains unscheduled — the only FDA-approved narcolepsy drug without a DEA schedule.

---

### Human laboratory evidence: drug-liking studies

The pivotal abuse-potential study (Setnik et al., *SLEEP* 2020; PMC7157189) randomized **non-dependent recreational stimulant users** in a double-blind crossover design to:

| Arm | Drug Liking Emax |
|-----|-----------------|
| Pitolisant 35.6 mg (therapeutic) | ≈ placebo |
| Pitolisant 213.6 mg (supratherapeutic, 6×) | ≈ placebo |
| Phentermine HCl 60 mg (active control) | Significantly > placebo |
| Placebo | Reference |

Maximum Drug Liking, Overall Drug Liking, and Take Drug Again scores for pitolisant were **statistically indistinguishable from placebo** at both doses. No euphoria, no "high," no physiological dependence signals. This held even in subjects with stimulant use history — the population most sensitive to reinforcing drug effects.

---

### Efficacy benchmarks (for clinical expectations)

| Outcome | Pitolisant | Notes |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| Cataplexy reduction | ~35–65% | Adequate for mild-moderate; insufficient for severe/refractory |
| MWT improvement vs placebo | ~6–8 min | HARMONY I/II; comparable to modafinil |
| vs sodium oxybate cataplexy | Less effective | Oxybate ~85% reduction |
| vs solriamfetol EDS | Somewhat less | Solriamfetol has greater MWT effect |

---

### Drug interactions relevant to SUD/MAT patients

| Co-medication | Interaction | Action |
|--------------|------------|--------|
| Buprenorphine (OUD MAT) | Both CYP2D6 substrates; potential competitive inhibition | Monitor; titrate slowly |
| Naltrexone (OUD/AUD) | No known interaction | Safe to combine |
| Methadone (OUD MAT) | Limited data; methadone is CYP3A4/CYP2D6; monitor QTc | Caution; ECG monitoring |
| Alcohol | No pharmacokinetic interaction; additive sedation unlikely (histamine is wake-promoting) | Low concern |

---

### Preferred clinical scenarios in SUD

1. **Active MAT (buprenorphine/methadone + narcolepsy)** — non-scheduled, no diversion concern, manageable CYP interaction
2. **Stimulant SUD history** — no DAT activity, no dopamine surge, placebo-level drug liking even in stimulant users
3. **Alcohol SUD with sodium oxybate risk** — oxybate is CNS depressant with fatal alcohol interaction; pitolisant has no such risk
4. **GHB/oxybate history or diversion risk** — sodium oxybate is GHB; pitolisant carries no cross-addiction signal
5. **Mild-to-moderate cataplexy** — adequate efficacy; for severe cataplexy, risk-benefit of oxybate or MAOIs must be weighed individually

---

### European observational data

Pitolisant received EU approval (Bioprojet) in 2016, providing ~8 years of post-marketing surveillance. Interim analysis of the EU post-authorization safety study (Lecendreux et al., *Sleep Medicine* 2025; doi:10.1016/j.sleep.2025) reported no signals of misuse, dependence, or diversion in the registry cohort — consistent with the mechanistic and laboratory predictions.

---

### Summary

Pitolisant's histaminergic wake-promotion mechanism bypasses mesolimbic dopamine reward circuitry. Human laboratory studies in recreational stimulant users confirm drug-liking equivalent to placebo, the DEA declined to schedule it, and European post-marketing data show no misuse signals. It is the default first consideration for narcolepsy in patients with active SUD, MAT, stimulant SUD history, or any situation where oxybate's CNS-depressant/diversion risks are unacceptable. Its limitation is reduced efficacy for severe cataplexy.
