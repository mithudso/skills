# Sodium Oxybate (GHB) Mechanism

## Core Concepts
- Sodium oxybate is the pharmaceutical sodium salt of gamma-hydroxybutyrate (GHB), an endogenous CNS metabolite produced from GABA via succinic semialdehyde
- Dual receptor target: high-affinity GHB-specific receptor (GHBR, Gi/Go-coupled) activated at low/endogenous concentrations; GABA-B receptor agonism at therapeutic (higher) concentrations
- The identical molecule is Schedule I as illicit GHB (no accepted medical use) but Schedule III as Xyrem/Xywav — the scheduling paradox arises from FDA REMS approval, not pharmacological difference
- Approved for narcolepsy type 1 (cataplexy + EDS) and type 2 (EDS only, Xywav 2021); distributed exclusively via REMS (Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy) program
- ADHD-narcolepsy comorbidity relevance: ~20–30% of narcolepsy patients have comorbid ADHD symptoms; GHB-mediated SWS consolidation may reduce secondary inattention driven by chronic sleep deprivation

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **GHBR (low dose):** Gi/Go activation reduces cAMP; presynaptic inhibition of dopamine and norepinephrine release in VTA and locus coeruleus — net reduction in arousal-system over-firing that drives cataplexy triggers
- **GABA-B (therapeutic dose):** Thalamocortical relay neuron hyperpolarization via Kir3 (GIRK) channels and inhibition of HCN channels; generates synchronized slow oscillations (0.5–4 Hz delta waves) → consolidated N3 slow-wave sleep
- **SWS consolidation mechanism:** Increased delta power and N3 duration reduces sleep fragmentation; restored sleep pressure reduces compensatory REM intrusions (the pathological substrate of cataplexy, sleep paralysis, and hypnagogic hallucinations)
- **Cataplexy suppression:** Tonic GABA-B–mediated inhibition of amygdala-to-brainstem REM atonia pathway during waking hours; effect latency ~days vs EDS improvement at 6–8 weeks — suggests distinct downstream mechanisms
- **Dopamine rebound hypothesis:** Nocturnal dopamine suppression via GHBR produces daytime dopaminergic rebound, partially explaining reduced EDS; relevant to ADHD comorbidity (dopaminergic hypofunction)
- Anterior cingulate GABA/glutamate balance normalizes with chronic use (MRS evidence); may contribute to reduced cognitive fog and improved executive function in treated patients

## Clinical Picture
- Narcolepsy prevalence: 1 in 2,000 (US ~200,000 cases); ~75% have cataplexy (NT1); diagnosis-to-treatment lag often 10+ years
- Self-medication risk: illicit GHB circulates in harm-reduction communities as narcolepsy/ADHD treatment; same molecule, no quality control, no REMS safeguards, high overdose/respiratory-depression risk
- ADHD-narcolepsy overlap: stimulants (first-line for ADHD) can worsen cataplexy or fragment sleep further; sodium oxybate uniquely addresses both EDS and cataplexy without stimulant-class risks
- Key contraindication: succinic semialdehyde dehydrogenase (SSADH) deficiency — impaired GHB catabolism leads to toxic accumulation
- Half-life 30–60 min with nonlinear (saturable) kinetics; requires split dosing (twice-nightly IR) because drug is fully metabolized before morning

## Evidence & Treatment
- Phase 3 RCT (Xyrem pivotal trials): dose-dependent reduction in weekly cataplexy attacks vs placebo; 9 g/night reduced attacks by ~80%; ESS improvement of 3–4 points
- REST-ON trial (Lumryz, once-nightly ER): significantly improved MWT latency (+6.6 min vs placebo), reduced cataplexy, improved DNS score; FDA-approved July 2023
- SWS quantification: sodium oxybate increases N3 duration and delta power dose-dependently; N1 sleep decreases proportionally; REM architecture preserved or modestly reduced
- Combination with stimulants: common clinical practice — oxybate addresses nocturnal fragmentation and cataplexy; stimulant (modafinil, amphetamine) augments daytime alertness; risk of CNS interaction low but monitor
- REMS requirement: prescribers and pharmacies must enroll; single certified pharmacy (Express Scripts); 30-day supply max; rationale is abuse potential and respiratory depression risk when diverted

## Key Facts
- Cataplexy onset relief: within days; EDS improvement requires 6–8 weeks of consistent nightly dosing
- Scheduling paradox: GHB is Schedule I (Controlled Substances Act, 21 USC §812) when illicit; sodium oxybate (same molecule) is Schedule III under FDA-approved REMS — scheduling determined by regulatory context, not chemistry
- REMS program: ~800 certified prescribers nationally (historically); creates major access barrier for rural/uninsured patients — known driver of self-medication with illicit GHB
- Delta power increase: doses of ~50 mg/kg raise SWS duration by ~40–60 min vs placebo in narcolepsy patients
- Respiratory depression risk: synergistic with alcohol, opioids, benzodiazepines; 9 g + alcohol documented as lethal combination; major illicit-use fatality mechanism
