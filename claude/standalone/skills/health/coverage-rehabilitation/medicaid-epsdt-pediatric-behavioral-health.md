# EPSDT and Pediatric Behavioral Health in Medicaid

## Overview

EPSDT (Early and Periodic Screening, Diagnostic, and Treatment) is Medicaid's comprehensive children's health benefit for all Medicaid enrollees under age 21. It is the strongest coverage guarantee in the Medicaid program: states must provide any service that is medically necessary for a child, regardless of whether that service is included in the state's adult Medicaid plan. [SSA §1905(r), 42 U.S.C. 1396d(r); MACPAC EPSDT subtopic] This makes EPSDT the primary vehicle for accessing intensive pediatric behavioral health services.

## EPSDT — The BH Coverage Guarantee

### Statutory Basis
EPSDT rests on two interlocking SSA provisions [MACPAC EPSDT subtopic; Medicaid.gov EPSDT page]:
- **SSA §1902(a)(43)** (42 U.S.C. 1396a(a)(43)): requires states to inform eligible children of EPSDT and provide or arrange for required screenings
- **SSA §1905(r)** (42 U.S.C. 1396d(r)): defines the scope of EPSDT services — screening, diagnosis, and treatment — and obligates states to provide any additional services available under §1905(a) when found medically necessary
- Implementing regulations: **42 CFR Part 441 Subpart B** (§§441.50–441.62) [eCFR, LII/Cornell]
- "Medically necessary" under EPSDT: federal law does not define medical necessity — state definitions vary, but services need not cure a condition; coverage extends to services that maintain or improve a health condition or relieve pain [MACPAC EPSDT subtopic]
- States cannot deny EPSDT services solely because they are not in the adult state plan

### What This Means for BH
- A state that does not cover psychiatric day treatment for adults in its Medicaid state plan **must** cover it for children under EPSDT if medically necessary [Medicaid.gov EPSDT Coverage Guide; SHO #24005]
- Residential psychiatric treatment for children under 21 is covered under EPSDT through the §1905(a)(16) inpatient psychiatric benefit, which operates as a statutory exception to the IMD exclusion for this population (see PRTFs below)
- EPSDT enables coverage of: intensive in-home BH services, multisystemic therapy (MST), family preservation services, therapeutic foster care, intensive case management, applied behavior analysis (ABA) for autism [Medicaid.gov State Medicaid & CHIP BH-EPSDT Toolkit]
- Since February 2026, CMS guidance clarifies that states may allow BH services to begin **before formal diagnosis**, addressing delays from waitlists [Georgetown CCF, June 2026]
- Maintenance services that prevent regression — not only curative services — qualify for EPSDT coverage [Georgetown CCF, June 2026; May 2026 updated EPSDT Coverage Guide]

## Psychiatric Residential Treatment Facilities (PRTFs)

PRTFs are inpatient psychiatric residential programs for children under 21 that are not hospitals [CMS PRTF provider page; Medicaid.gov inpatient psych under-21 page]:
- Covered under EPSDT even though facilities may have >16 beds (contrast with IMD exclusion for adults)
- **Governing regulations — two separate parts apply**:
  - Service requirements: **42 CFR Part 441 Subpart D** (§§441.150–441.184) — "Inpatient Psychiatric Services for Individuals Under Age 21" — governs certification of need, plan of care, and active-treatment standards [eCFR 42 CFR §441.150; §441.151; §441.152]
  - Conditions of Participation (restraint/seclusion): **42 CFR Part 483 Subpart G** — applies to PRTFs specifically [CMS PRTF CoP; eCFR search results confirming dual regulatory path]
- **Certification of need** (§441.152): an independent team with competence in child psychiatry must certify that (1) inpatient treatment is required and (2) services can reasonably be expected to improve the child's condition or prevent further regression [eCFR §441.152]
- For emergency admissions, certification must be completed within 14 days [eCFR §441.152]
- **Length of stay**: no federal maximum; must be medically necessary with active treatment reviews [Medicaid.gov inpatient psych under-21]
- PRTFs serve children and youth with acute psychiatric crises, trauma, disruptive behavior disorders who require more structure than community-based settings

### PRTF vs Inpatient Psychiatric Hospital
| Feature | PRTF | Inpatient Psychiatric (hospital) |
|---|---|---|
| Setting | Residential (non-hospital) | Hospital or distinct part |
| Age | Under 21 (EPSDT) | Any age |
| IMD exclusion | Statutory exception under §1905(a)(16) | Applies for adults; not for children |
| Typical LOS | Illustrative — weeks to months (varies; no federal cap) | Illustrative — days to a few weeks |
| Medicaid authority | 42 CFR 441 Subpart D + EPSDT | Inpatient hospital benefit |

## EPSDT and Autism/ABA

Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) for children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) [Medicaid.gov Autism Services; CMS ABA FAQ; Medicaid.gov EPSDT Coverage Guide]:
- ABA is a covered EPSDT service when medically necessary; widely covered in Medicaid for ASD
- **CMS does not mandate ABA specifically**: CMS is explicit that it is not endorsing or requiring any particular treatment modality for ASD. However, states must meet EPSDT obligations to cover medically necessary services available for the treatment of ASD, which may include ABA [CMS ABA FAQ, medicaid.gov]
- ABA falls within §1905(a) service categories including: §1905(a)(6) Other Licensed Practitioner, §1905(a)(11) Therapies, and §1905(a)(13) Preventive Services [CMS ABA FAQ]
- Some states have an explicit ABA benefit in their state plan; others rely solely on EPSDT
- ABA intensity is determined by individual medical necessity assessment, not a federal hour standard; clinical practice commonly ranges from lower-intensity maintenance to more intensive early intervention programs, but no federal floor or ceiling is set [CMS ABA FAQ]
- **Provider requirements**: Board Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA) supervision; registered behavior technicians (state requirements vary)

## School-Based Mental Health Under EPSDT

EPSDT + Medicaid enables billing for school-based mental health [Medicaid.gov School-Based Services; MACPAC March 2024; KFF 2023; Bipartisan Safer Communities Act §11004 (2022)]:
- Schools (through LEA Medicaid billing) can bill for mental health services provided to Medicaid-enrolled students
- Two distinct billing pathways exist: (1) services in a student's IEP/IFSP under IDEA (34 CFR 300.154), and (2) services meeting medical necessity for any Medicaid-enrolled student regardless of IEP status — EPSDT does not require an IEP
- School-based therapy (counseling, social work, occupational therapy for sensory/BH issues) billable under EPSDT
- **Administrative match**: schools receive federal Medicaid administrative match for outreach and enrollment activities [CMS School-Based Administrative Claiming Guide, 2003; MACPAC 2024]
- The **Bipartisan Safer Communities Act (BSCA) of 2022**, §11004, required CMS to strengthen EPSDT guidance and allocated $50 million in state planning grants to expand school-based services [KFF, BSCA analysis; CMS press release Aug 2022]
- CMS and ED launched a school-based services technical assistance center in June 2023 [KFF examining new Medicaid resources, 2023]

## CHIP Behavioral Health Coverage — Comparison to EPSDT

CHIP does NOT automatically provide the EPSDT guarantee [MACPAC EPSDT subtopic; Medicaid.gov CHIP parity SPA guide, Jan 2025]:
- CHIP plans set their own benefit packages (with minimum requirements)
- CHIP plans must cover BH services under MHPAEA parity rules
- **Exception — EPSDT deeming**: CHIP state plans that provide full EPSDT coverage consistent with Medicaid requirements are **deemed** in compliance with MHPAEA and need not submit a separate parity analysis [Medicaid.gov CHIP parity SPA guide, Jan 2025]
- CHIP plans that do not include full EPSDT must submit a CHIP parity state plan amendment [Medicaid.gov CHIP parity SPA guide]
- CHIP children without full EPSDT do NOT have the same guaranteed access to PRTFs as Medicaid EPSDT children
- Key difference: a child transitioning from Medicaid to CHIP may lose access to intensive EPSDT BH services unless the state's CHIP plan mirrors EPSDT

## EPSDT Enforcement Challenges

EPSDT has strong legal backing but enforcement has historically been uneven [MACPAC EPSDT subtopic; Georgetown CCF; healthlaw.org]:
- **Lack of access in practice**: even with guaranteed EPSDT coverage, workforce shortages mean children cannot always access providers
- **Managed care EPSDT**: when children are in Medicaid MCOs, MCOs must cover EPSDT services but may apply utilization management that creates access barriers; the 2024 SHO guidance addresses algorithmic UM guardrails [Georgetown CCF, June 2026]
- **Litigation — Rosie D. v. Romney** (D. Mass. 2001, decided Jan. 26, 2006): class action on behalf of Massachusetts Medicaid children with emotional, behavioral, or psychiatric disabilities; court found the Commonwealth violated EPSDT by failing to provide medically necessary home-based assessment and intervention services [Civil Rights Litigation Clearinghouse; Center for Public Rep]
- **Litigation — Katie A. v. Bonta** (C.D. Cal., filed July 18, 2002): class action on behalf of foster children denied medically necessary mental health services under EPSDT; settled with California agreeing to improve EPSDT BH access [TASC fact sheet; FindLaw 9th Cir. 2007]
- **CMS oversight**: CMS requires states to report EPSDT screening rates annually (CMS Form 416); the BSCA §11004 (2022) added a requirement for CMS to conduct comprehensive reviews of state EPSDT implementation every five years [KFF BSCA analysis]
- **September 2024 SHO #24005**: CMS issued the most comprehensive EPSDT guidance in a decade, required by BSCA §11004; covers BH care continuum, UM guardrails, and strategies for states [CMS press release; Medicaid.gov EPSDT guidance page]
- **February 2026 BH Toolkit** and **May 13, 2026 updated EPSDT Coverage Guide**: most recent CMS guidance updates, first comprehensive Coverage Guide revision since 2014 [Georgetown CCF, June 2026]

## Transition-Age Youth — Loss of EPSDT

At age 21, individuals age out of EPSDT [MACPAC EPSDT subtopic; Medicaid.gov EPSDT page]:
- Transition to adult Medicaid state plan coverage — which may be significantly less comprehensive
- Services available under EPSDT (intensive BH case management, PRTF, therapeutic foster care) may no longer be covered under the adult state plan
- **Transition planning requirement**: states must plan for transition; MCOs responsible for care continuity
- **Young adult behavioral health crisis**: age 21 loss of EPSDT coincides with peak onset of SMI (schizophrenia, bipolar disorder) — a particularly vulnerable time for coverage reduction
