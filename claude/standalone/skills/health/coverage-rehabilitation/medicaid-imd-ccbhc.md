# Medicaid IMD Exclusion and Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinics (CCBHC)

The IMD exclusion is a foundational constraint on Medicaid's ability to fund intensive inpatient behavioral health care. CCBHCs represent a major policy response to the resulting access gap, using a prospective payment model to support comprehensive community-based behavioral health services.

---

## The IMD Exclusion

### Statutory Basis [^1][^2]

Since 1965, SSA §1905(a)(30)(B) (42 U.S.C. §1396d(a)(30)(B)) has prohibited federal Medicaid matching funds for services to individuals aged **21–64** who are patients in an **Institution for Mental Diseases (IMD)**. [^1][^2]

### Definition: What Is an IMD? [^1][^2]

**Statutory definition (SSA §1905(i))**: A hospital, nursing facility, or other institution of **more than 16 beds** that is **primarily engaged** in providing diagnosis, treatment, or care of persons with mental diseases, including medical attention, nursing care, and related services. [^1]

**Determining IMD status** — two tests:
1. **Licensure/accreditation test**: If the facility is licensed or accredited as a psychiatric facility, it is an IMD.
2. **50% rule**: If mental disease (including SUD, per ICD classification) is the current reason for institutionalization for **>50% of patients**, the facility is an IMD.

**Key threshold**: The 16-bed rule is absolute — facilities with ≤16 beds are NOT IMDs regardless of patient mix. [^1]

**SUD facilities and IMD status** [^1][^3]:
- Because ICD classifies SUD as mental disorders, SUD residential facilities with >16 beds may be classified as IMDs.
- Chemically dependent patients admitted for SUD treatment count as "mentally ill" under the 50% threshold.
- This means large SUD residential programs (ASAM 3.x levels, residential >16 beds) can be subject to the exclusion.

### What the Exclusion Prohibits [^1]

- Federal Medicaid matching funds (FMAP) cannot be paid to states for services to adults aged 21–64 who are patients in an IMD.
- States may use 100% state funds to pay for IMD services, but receive no federal matching.
- The exclusion applies regardless of whether the IMD provides high-quality, evidence-based care.

### Age-Group Exceptions [^1][^2]

| Population | IMD exclusion applies? |
|------------|----------------------|
| Children (under age 21) | **No** — states can cover IMD services for minors (e.g., PRTFs — Psychiatric Residential Treatment Facilities) |
| Adults age 21–64 | **Yes** — exclusion applies |
| Older adults (age 65+) | **No** — states can cover IMD services for elderly |

### Managed Care Exception — SUPPORT Act §1013 [^1][^3]

**Section 1013 of the SUPPORT Act (P.L. 115-271, 2018)** codified a CMS managed care regulation (originally in effect July 2016):

States may make payments to Medicaid managed care organizations (MCOs) for enrollees aged 21–64 who are patients in an IMD, **provided**:
- Length of stay is **no more than 15 days** during the calendar month of the payment.
- This enables short-term acute IMD stays (detox, crisis stabilization) to be covered through MCO capitation.

### Section 1115 SUD Waiver IMD Option [^3][^4]

CMS has approved Section 1115 demonstration waivers allowing states to claim federal Medicaid matching funds for SUD residential treatment in IMDs beyond the 15-day managed care exception.

**Key requirements for 1115 SUD/IMD waiver approval** (per CMS November 2017 guidance): [^4]
- Provide full ASAM continuum of care (not just residential)
- Require residential providers to meet ASAM criteria or equivalent within 12–24 months of approval
- Require residential providers to offer MAT access
- Implement evidence-based patient placement criteria by month 24
- States must demonstrate the waiver expands access (not just substitute existing state spending)
- Submit evaluation design for CMS approval

### SUPPORT Act IMD State Plan Option — SSA §1915(l) [^1][^3]

**Section 5052 of the SUPPORT Act** (P.L. 115-271, 2018) added a new permanent state plan option under **SSA §1915(l)**, effective October 1, 2019:

States may cover Medicaid beneficiaries aged 21–64 with **at least one SUD** who are patients in an eligible IMD for **no more than 30 days (whether or not consecutive) during a 12-month period**. [^3]

**Eligible IMD requirements**:
- Must follow reliable, evidence-based practices.
- Must offer at least two forms of MAT for SUD on-site.

**State requirements**:
- Maintain annual state and local funding levels for IMD and specified outpatient services (maintenance of effort; compliance deadline: March 9, 2025).
- Cover four levels of outpatient SUD treatment and at least two of five inpatient SUD levels of care.
- Implement evidence-based, SUD-specific individual placement criteria by October 1, 2025.

**CAA 2024, Section 204** (P.L. 118-42) removed the September 30, 2023, expiration date, making this option permanent. As of 2025, two states (South Dakota and Tennessee) have implemented it. [^3]

### Reform Status [^2]

- The IMD exclusion remains in law as of 2026.
- Bipartisan **Michelle Alyssa Go Act (H.R. 5462)**, reintroduced September 2025: would modernize the IMD exclusion and expand access.
- Full repeal has been estimated to carry significant Medicaid fiscal impact; Congressional Budget Office has analyzed the exclusion. [^2]
- NAMI, NAMD, and other advocacy organizations consistently call for full or partial repeal.

## Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinics (CCBHC)

### Origin: Section 223 Demonstration [^5][^6]

Established by the **Protecting Access to Medicare Act of 2014 (PAMA, Section 223)**, CCBHCs launched a Medicaid demonstration program beginning in 2017 with **8 pilot states**: Minnesota, Missouri, Nevada, New Jersey, New York, Oklahoma, Oregon, and Pennsylvania. [^5]

**Purpose**: Fund comprehensive community-based behavioral health services through a prospective payment model — addressing the access and provider sustainability gaps left by the IMD exclusion and fee-for-service underpayment.

### Permanent State Plan Option — CAA 2024, Section 209 [^6][^7]

**Section 209 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act (CAA) of 2024** (P.L. 118-42, signed March 9, 2024) made CCBHC a permanent optional Medicaid state plan benefit, adding CCBHC services at **SSA §1905(a)(31) and (jj)**: [^6][^7]
- States may implement CCBHC program without time-limited demonstration authority.
- Adds CCBHC services to Medicaid "medical assistance" scope as an optional service.
- Same 9 required service categories as demonstration; same certification process.
- States certify individual clinics using HHS-developed program criteria (updated 2023 SAMHSA Certification Criteria).
- The permanent state plan benefit does **not** provide an enhanced federal match (unlike the demonstration program).

### Status (2025–2026) [^5][^6]

- **500+ CCBHCs** operating nationally (demonstration participants, CCBHC-E grantees, state CCBHC programs).
- January 2025: SAMHSA awarded 1-year CCBHC demonstration planning grants ($1M each) to 14 states + DC.
- New demonstration states (starting July 2024–July 2025): Alabama, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Maine, New Hampshire, New Mexico, Rhode Island, Vermont.

### 9 Required CCBHC Service Categories [^5][^7]

CCBHCs must provide all 9 categories (per 2023 SAMHSA Certification Criteria):

1. **Crisis mental health services** — 24-hour/7-day crisis care (mobile crisis, crisis stabilization)
2. **Screening, assessment, and diagnosis** — including risk assessment for MH and SUD
3. **Outpatient mental health and SUD treatment** — individual, group, family therapy
4. **Case management** — care coordination and service linkage
5. **Psychiatric rehabilitation services** — functional skills, community integration
6. **Peer and family supports** — certified peer specialists, family support
7. **Primary care screening and monitoring** — physical health integration
8. **Targeted case management** — for high-need populations
9. **Community-based mental health care for veterans** — coordination with VA/DoD

**Population served**: CCBHCs must serve all individuals seeking care regardless of ability to pay, residence, or diagnosis — no exclusion of uninsured or underinsured.

### CCBHC Prospective Payment System (PPS) [^7][^8]

**Payment structure**: States use a Medicaid PPS methodology covering expected cost of providing CCBHC services to Medicaid beneficiaries.

**CMS revised guidance (February 2024)** added: [^8]
- New rate options for **special crisis services** (crisis stabilization, mobile crisis)
- New flexibilities for **quality bonus payment (QBP)** development
- Multiple PPS methodology options for states (per-visit, per-diem, enhanced PPS)

**Quality Bonus Payments (QBP)**: States may award additional payments to CCBHCs meeting quality performance thresholds — incentive for care quality improvement.

### ARP Section 9813 — Mobile Crisis Services [^9]

**Section 9813 of the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021** (ARP, P.L. 117-2) added **SSA §1947**: a separate state plan option for **community-based mobile crisis intervention services**, available April 1, 2022–March 27, 2027, with an **85% FMAP** for the first 12 fiscal quarters of a state's participation. This is distinct from the CCBHC state plan option but CCBHCs may provide qualifying mobile crisis services under it. [^9]

### CCBHC vs. Standard Community Mental Health Center

| Feature | Standard CMHC | CCBHC |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| Required services | Varies by state | 9 mandated categories |
| Payment model | Fee-for-service (often) | Prospective payment (PPS) |
| 24/7 crisis | Not required | Required |
| Primary care integration | Optional | Required |
| Peer support | Optional | Required |
| Serve regardless of insurance | Not required | Required |
| SUD treatment | Optional | Required alongside MH |

## Policy Interaction: IMD + CCBHC

CCBHCs operate in community (outpatient/community-based) settings — not as IMDs. The PPS model is designed to make CCBHC financial sustainability possible without relying on high-volume inpatient care that the IMD exclusion blocks. Together, the IMD waiver/exception pathways (for residential/inpatient) and CCBHCs (for community-based intensive care) form the primary Medicaid framework for comprehensive behavioral health access.

## Anti-Patterns / Common Mistakes

- **16-bed threshold is exact**: 16 beds = NOT an IMD; 17 beds = IMD (if primarily psychiatric/SUD). The threshold is in SSA §1905(i).
- **Managed care 15-day exception is monthly, not per stay**: 15 days per calendar month — a single stay spanning two calendar months can qualify for 15 days in each month. (SUPPORT Act §1013, SSA §1915(l) managed care provision.)
- **SUPPORT Act §1013 vs. §5052**: Section 1013 = managed care 15-day exception; Section 5052 = new §1915(l) state plan option allowing 30 days per year for SUD IMD stays. These are two separate provisions.
- **§1915(l) limit is 30 days/year, not 15/month**: The state plan option allows 30 non-consecutive days per 12-month period — distinct from the 15-day monthly cap in managed care.
- **CCBHC is not just for mental health**: SUD treatment is a required service category — CCBHCs serve both populations.
- **CAA 2024 made CCBHC permanent, but states must still opt in**: CCBHC is an optional state plan benefit; states must elect to implement it. No enhanced FMAP in the permanent option (unlike demonstration).
- **ARP §9813 is mobile crisis, not CCBHC state plan**: §9813 is a separate mobile crisis state plan option with 85% FMAP — not the CCBHC permanence provision (CAA 2024 §209).
- **IMD exclusion is not a quality determination**: facilities excluded are not excluded for poor quality — high-quality SRFs over 16 beds are excluded by definition.
