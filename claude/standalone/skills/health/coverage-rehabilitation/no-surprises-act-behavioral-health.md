# No Surprises Act and Behavioral Health

## Overview

The No Surprises Act (NSA) was enacted as Division BB of the Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2021 (CAA 2021), signed December 27, 2020, and effective January 1, 2022. [CMS CAA overview](https://www.cms.gov/marketplace/about/oversight/other-insurance-protections/consolidated-appropriations-act-2021-caa) Behavioral health services — emergency psychiatric care, out-of-network BH providers at in-network facilities, and scheduled BH services — are fully within NSA's scope. The implementing regulations are codified at 45 CFR Part 149 (HHS), 29 CFR Part 2590 (DOL), and 26 CFR Part 54 (IRS). [Federal Register Part I IFR, 86 FR 36872 (Jul. 13, 2021)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2021/07/13/2021-14379/requirements-related-to-surprise-billing-part-i)

## Core NSA Protections

### 1. Emergency Services (Psychiatric and SUD Emergencies) — 45 CFR 149.110

Plans must cover emergency services at in-network cost-sharing levels **regardless of whether the facility or providers are in-network**. [45 CFR 149.110](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/149.110)

"Emergency medical condition" is **explicitly defined to include a mental health condition or substance use disorder** manifesting with acute symptoms of sufficient severity that a prudent layperson could reasonably expect the absence of immediate medical attention to result in serious jeopardy, serious impairment to bodily functions, or serious dysfunction of any bodily organ or part. [45 CFR 149.110; law.cornell.edu](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/149.110)

- Providers and facilities cannot bill the patient above the in-network cost-sharing amount (copay, coinsurance, deductible)
- **Covered facilities**: emergency departments of hospitals and independent freestanding emergency departments (IFEDs). Note: psychiatric crisis services provided only in a physician's office (no ED capacity) do not qualify as emergency services under NSA
- **Psychiatric emergency examples**: acute suicidality requiring stabilization, psychosis with immediate safety risk, acute overdose stabilization in an ED, involuntary psychiatric hold evaluation conducted in an ED setting

### 2. Out-of-Network Providers at In-Network Facilities — 45 CFR 149.120

If a patient receives non-emergency care at an in-network hospital or facility, out-of-network providers **cannot surprise-bill** for:
- Ancillary services (anesthesia, radiology, pathology, assistant surgeons)
- Services the patient did not know were out-of-network (absent a valid consent/waiver)

**BH application**: a psychiatrist consulting on an in-network medical-surgical hospital patient who is out-of-network cannot surprise-bill; a BH provider delivering services at an in-network behavioral health facility who is out-of-network cannot surprise-bill. [45 CFR 149.120](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/149.120)

### 3. Out-of-Network Air Ambulance — 45 CFR 149.130

Specific protections for air ambulance; less directly applicable to BH but relevant to psychiatric transport.

## Good Faith Estimate (GFE) for Scheduled BH Services — 45 CFR 149.610

Providers and facilities must give **uninsured (or self-pay)** patients a Good Faith Estimate of expected charges before scheduled services. [45 CFR 149.610; eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-B/part-149/subpart-G/section-149.610)

**Timing requirements** (verified against 45 CFR 149.610):
- Service scheduled **10+ business days** in advance: GFE must be provided **no later than 3 business days** after scheduling
- Service scheduled **3–9 business days** in advance: GFE must be provided **no later than 1 business day** after scheduling
- Patient requests GFE without scheduling: GFE due **within 3 business days** of the request

**GFE must include**: expected services, expected charges, facility and provider information, and notice of PPDR rights.

**Dispute threshold**: If actual charges exceed GFE by **$400 or more**, patient can dispute via Patient-Provider Dispute Resolution (PPDR) process. [CMS GFE FAQ](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/faqs-good-faith-estimate-uninsured-self-pay-part-4.pdf)

**BH significance**: Many BH providers (therapists, psychologists) do not accept insurance. NSA GFE requirements apply to ALL licensed/certified providers for self-pay patients — including private-practice therapists — effective January 1, 2022. This affected solo BH practices significantly. [National Council for Mental Wellbeing NSA Overview](https://www.thenationalcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Overview-of-No-Surprises-Act-Requirements.pdf)

## Provider Directory Accuracy — PHS Act § 2799B-6

Health plans must verify and update provider directory information **at least once every 90 days**. Plans must remove providers that cannot be verified during any 6-month period. Plans must respond within **1 business day** to patient requests verifying network status, and must respond to directory inaccuracy corrections within **2 business days**. [CMS Provider Directory Training](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/a274577-1b-training-2nsa-disclosure-continuity-care-directoriesfinal-508.pdf)

**BH implication**: if a patient relies on an inaccurate directory listing (provider listed as in-network who is not), the patient receives in-network cost-sharing protection. Patient must document reliance on directory; plan must provide in-network level benefit.

## Independent Dispute Resolution (IDR) for BH Claims — 45 CFR 149.510

When NSA applies and payer/provider disagree on payment: [eCFR 45 CFR 149.510](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-B/part-149/subpart-F/section-149.510)

1. Provider and plan have a **30-business-day open negotiation period** (approximately 42 calendar days) — not 30 calendar days
2. If unresolved, either party may initiate IDR within the following **4-business-day window**
3. Arbitrator (certified IDR entity) selects one of two "final offers" using baseball-style arbitration; must consider the qualifying payment amount (QPA = plan's 2019 median in-network contracted rate for the item/service, indexed for inflation) and other statutory factors
4. IDR entities may **not** consider typical provider charges (billed charges) or Medicare/Medicaid payment rates

**Court challenges**: The Texas Medical Association filed multiple lawsuits challenging QPA-weighting rules. A February 2023 ruling by the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Texas vacated requirements that IDR entities presume QPA is the appropriate rate and select the offer closest to QPA. CMS has issued revised guidance following these rulings. [KFF/Peterson-KFF IDR Explainer](https://www.healthsystemtracker.org/brief/independent-dispute-resolution-explainer/)

**BH IDR outcomes**: Providers initiated ~90% of disputes; providers won approximately 80% of disputes through mid-2024. [Commonwealth Fund 2024](https://www.commonwealthfund.org/blog/2024/report-shows-dispute-resolution-process-no-surprises-act-favors-providers) The IDR process was first fully operational (without suspension) in 2024, following multiple court-ordered pauses in 2022–2023. [CRS Report R48738](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48738)

## NSA + MHPAEA Interaction

The NSA and MHPAEA address related but distinct problems:
- **MHPAEA**: plan cannot impose more restrictive *limitations* on BH coverage than medical/surgical (parity in benefits)
- **NSA**: plan cannot charge more to patients for out-of-network BH in covered scenarios
- **Combined impact**: a plan with inadequate BH in-network network (a MHPAEA concern) and high out-of-network BH use faces both MHPAEA scrutiny (why is out-of-network use so high?) and NSA obligations (if emergency, patient protected from surprise bill)
- 2024 MHPAEA rule notes that high out-of-network BH utilization is evidence of network inadequacy — a NQTL problem

## What NSA Does NOT Cover

- Routine out-of-network BH services the patient *chose* (e.g., choosing an out-of-network therapist who does not participate in any plan)
- NSA does not require plans to cover out-of-network services beyond the emergency/ancillary scenarios
- Ongoing out-of-network therapy with a provider who opted out of networks — cost-sharing is determined by the plan's out-of-network benefits (NSA doesn't apply)
- **Access problem NSA cannot fix**: if there are no in-network BH providers available (network inadequacy), NSA applies to emergencies but not to routine care accessed out-of-network due to network gaps — this is a MHPAEA/network adequacy issue, not an NSA issue

## State Balance Billing Laws

- **33 states** had enacted some form of balance billing law before the NSA (17 with comprehensive protections; 16 with partial protections) [CMS State Laws Q&A](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/nsa-state-laws-q-and-a.pdf)
- NSA operates as a **floor**: state laws providing *greater* patient protection remain in effect; weaker state laws are preempted
- Strong state laws (CA, NY, TX) may provide broader BH balance billing protections
- **Enforcement split**: states enforce against fully-insured licensed plans; CMS directly enforces against ERISA self-funded plans (which are exempt from state insurance regulation under ERISA preemption). NSA specifically permits state laws to apply to emergency services despite ERISA preemption where the NSA defers to states [CMS State Laws overview](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/nsa-state-laws.pdf)

## Practical Implications for BH Patients

1. **ED psychiatric visit**: if involuntary hold or acute crisis requires hospital ED, in-network cost-sharing applies regardless of whether the psychiatric provider is in-network
2. **Inpatient BH**: if admitted to in-network psychiatric unit, consulting physicians who are out-of-network cannot surprise-bill
3. **Self-pay therapy**: private-pay therapist must provide Good Faith Estimate — timing depends on how far in advance service is scheduled (3 business days if 10+ days out; 1 business day if 3–9 days out)
4. **Wrong directory**: used directory, saw a psychiatrist listed as in-network who wasn't — report to plan; entitled to in-network cost-sharing if reliance on directory is documented
