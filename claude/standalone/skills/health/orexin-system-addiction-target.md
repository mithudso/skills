# Orexin System as Addiction Therapeutic Target

## Core Concepts
- Orexin neurons in the lateral hypothalamus (LH) project to the VTA, nucleus accumbens, prefrontal cortex, and amygdala — directly embedding the system in reward circuitry
- Two receptor subtypes: **OX1R** (orexin-A selective, Gq-coupled) mediates high-motivation drug-seeking; **OX2R** (dual-ligand, Gq/Gi) drives arousal and psychostimulant reinstatement via the paraventricular thalamus (PVT)
- **DORAs** (dual orexin receptor antagonists) — suvorexant (FDA-approved 2014), lemborexant (2019) — block both subtypes; approved for insomnia but under investigation for SUD
- Orexin-addiction link is bidirectional: chronic substance use dysregulates orexin tone, which in turn amplifies cue-reactivity and stress-induced craving
- OX1R blockade selectively reduces cue- and stress-induced reinstatement without impairing baseline locomotion or satiety (dissociable from sedation)

## Mechanisms
- OX1R antagonist **SB-334867** dose-dependently suppresses cue-induced cocaine reinstatement; OX2R antagonist 4PT does not — establishing OX1R as the primary cue-reinstatement gating receptor
- Intra-LH or systemic OX1R blockade reduces break-point on progressive-ratio cocaine and heroin schedules, indicating reduced motivational salience rather than motor suppression
- Orexin-A infusion into the PVT reinstates cocaine-seeking; blocked by OX2R antagonist TCS-OX2-29 (not SB-334867) — the PVT/OX2R axis is substrate-specific for psychostimulants
- Alcohol activates LH orexin neurons via CRF signaling; orexin then amplifies VTA dopamine release during ethanol consumption, reinforcing binge-pattern intake
- Sleep disruption during drug withdrawal elevates orexin tone, sustaining hyperarousal and stress reactivity — DORA treatment addresses both withdrawal insomnia and craving concurrently
- Suvorexant (10–20 mg) reduced next-day alcohol craving and cue-reactivity in preliminary human work; mechanism includes attenuation of amygdala threat-processing

## Clinical Picture
- Insomnia affects ~80% of patients in early alcohol and stimulant withdrawal; disrupted sleep is among the strongest predictors of relapse within 30 days
- In AUD, orexin-A CSF levels are elevated during active drinking and early withdrawal, normalizing with sustained abstinence — suggesting a state marker of craving load
- Suvorexant case report (2024): 20 mg/day for 13 weeks in AUD + comorbid insomnia — Obsessive Compulsive Drinking Scale dropped from 44/56 at baseline to 3/56 at week 13 (93% reduction); complete abstinence confirmed at all visits
- Phase II RCT of suvorexant in AUD (NCT03897062) was disrupted by COVID-19 and did not reach primary completion; Phase II stimulant study (NCT, enrolled July 2024) targets 40 participants, completion January 2027

## Evidence
- Preclinical consensus across cocaine, heroin, alcohol, nicotine, and opioid models: OX1R blockade reduces reinstatement under high-motivation conditions with minimal sedation or withdrawal suppression — high translational consistency
- First-in-human suvorexant study in non-treatment-seeking cocaine users: improved sleep quality, reduced stress reactivity, and attenuated craving — but a separate study found suvorexant enhanced reinforcing (not subjective) effects of IV cocaine, signaling paradoxical risk at high doses
- SB-334867 studies show OX1R antagonism reduces opioid (remifentanil) PR break-point persistently even after washout, suggesting durable motivational recalibration
- Orexin-1 receptor blockade in the bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (BNST) specifically attenuates stress-induced (not cue-induced) reinstatement — anatomical specificity enables targeted intervention strategies
- No DORA is yet FDA-approved for any SUD indication; suvorexant remains the most clinically advanced candidate given existing safety data at 10–20 mg

## Key Facts
- OX1R antagonist SB-334867 reduces cue-induced cocaine reinstatement **dose-dependently** without affecting extinction responding — selectivity for high-motivation states
- Suvorexant case report: OCDS craving score reduced **93%** (44→3 out of 56) over 13 weeks in AUD
- Insomnia in stimulant/alcohol withdrawal predicts **relapse within 30 days** — orexin system is the mechanistic bridge between sleep disruption and craving
- OX1R vs OX2R dissociation: **OX1R** gates cue/stress reinstatement broadly; **OX2R** in PVT gates psychostimulant-specific reinstatement
- Orexin neurons number ~70,000 in the human LH; despite small population, their projections span the entire addiction circuitry (VTA, NAc, PFC, amygdala, BNST)
