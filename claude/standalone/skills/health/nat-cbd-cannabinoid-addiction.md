# Cannabidiol and Cannabinoid Pharmacotherapy for SUD

## Core Concepts
- **CBD (cannabidiol)**: Non-intoxicating phytocannabinoid; FDA-approved (Epidiolex) for refractory epilepsy; non-CB1 agonist with pleiotropic CNS mechanisms
- **Endocannabinoid system (ECS)**: CB1/CB2 receptors + endogenous ligands (anandamide/AEA, 2-AG); dysregulated in addiction across opioid, stimulant, and alcohol use disorders
- **THC:CBD ratio paradox**: High-CBD products marketed as "safer cannabis" but controlled trials show CBD at ≥450 mg inhibits CYP450-mediated THC metabolism, *increasing* THC plasma levels and psychoactive effects — not attenuating them
- **CUD paradox**: CBD is investigated to *treat* cannabis use disorder despite being a cannabis constituent; 20–30% of regular cannabis users develop CUD; modern high-THC products (3–5× potency increase over 20 years) drive this risk
- **Translational gap**: Robust preclinical signal + two Phase 2 RCTs in opioid craving; cocaine/stimulant human data remains negative or inconclusive

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **CB1 negative allosteric modulation**: CBD does not agonize CB1 directly; acts as negative allosteric modulator reducing THC efficacy and attenuating reward salience via mesolimbic dopamine circuits
- **FAAH inhibition → anandamide elevation**: CBD inhibits fatty acid amide hydrolase (FAAH), raising AEA tone; AEA modulates stress-response circuitry (amygdala, BNST) relevant to cue-induced craving and withdrawal anxiety
- **5-HT1A agonism**: Partial agonist at serotonin 5-HT1A receptors; mediates anxiolytic and anti-stress effects independent of ECS — critical for opioid withdrawal anxiety reduction
- **TRPV1 desensitization**: CBD activates then desensitizes transient receptor potential vanilloid 1 channels, blunting stress-sensitized neural circuits
- **Opioidergic cross-talk**: CBD modulates μ-opioid receptor signaling indirectly; reduces corticotropin-releasing factor (CRF) expression, dampening HPA-axis hyperreactivity during opioid abstinence
- **Dopamine/glutamate normalization**: In stimulant models, CBD reverses cocaine-induced sensitization and reduces reinstatement of cocaine/methamphetamine seeking via prefrontal glutamate normalization

## Clinical Picture
- **Target population**: Abstinent individuals with opioid use disorder (OUD), crack-cocaine use disorder; also explored in alcohol use disorder and cannabis use disorder
- **Cue-reactivity profile**: Drug-related visual cues trigger craving spikes + cortisol surges in OUD; CBD specifically blunts this cue-induced response without impairing cognition or producing euphoria
- **Withdrawal anxiety**: CBD addresses the anxiety/hyperarousal component of opioid withdrawal — a common relapse driver not fully covered by methadone/buprenorphine
- **CUD treatment context**: CBD (400–800 mg/day) reduced cannabis use and withdrawal symptoms vs. placebo in a King's College London RCT (n=48); mechanistically plausible via AEA elevation
- **Safety signal**: CBD is well-tolerated at 300–800 mg/day in trials; main concerns are CYP2C19/CYP3A4 drug-drug interactions (including opioid agonists) and hepatotoxicity at high doses

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Yale/Hurd heroin RCT (2019, *AJPJ*, n=42)**: Single-site double-blind RCT; CBD 400 or 800 mg/day × 3 days vs. placebo in heroin-abstinent adults; both doses significantly reduced cue-induced craving and anxiety; effects persisted 7 days post-last-dose; cortisol and heart-rate attenuation confirmed physiologic effect
- **Cocaine RCT (PMC8451934, n=78)**: 800 mg/day CBD vs. placebo in cocaine use disorder; primary endpoint (relapse prevention) not met; no significant reduction in cocaine use — negative result for stimulants in humans
- **Crack-cocaine exploratory RCT (Brazil, n=31)**: 300 mg/day CBD × 10 weeks reduced crack-cocaine use and craving vs. placebo; effect size moderate; replication needed
- **CUD RCT (King's College, 2021)**: 400 mg/day CBD reduced cannabis use by ~50% from baseline over 4 weeks vs. placebo; first human evidence CBD can treat what it is derived from
- **Ongoing phase 3 (NCT06940674)**: NIDA-funded "Adjunctive CBD for Recovery from Opioid Study" — CBD as adjunct to OAT (buprenorphine/methadone); primary endpoint illicit opioid use; results pending
- **2025 Lancet Psychiatry meta-analysis**: Systematic review found CBD evidence for SUD "promising but limited"; strongest signal in opioid craving; insufficient evidence to support broad clinical adoption pending larger RCTs

## Key Facts
- CBD 800 mg/day × 3 days reduced heroin cue-craving scores by ~50% in Hurd 2019 trial; effect lasted 1 week after last dose
- CBD 450 mg co-administered with THC *increased* THC plasma AUC — CBD is not a reliable "THC buffer" at therapeutic doses
- 20–30% of regular cannabis users meet DSM-5 CUD criteria; modern cannabis averages 15–25% THC vs. <5% in 1990s
- FAAH inhibition elevates anandamide; AEA has 10–100× lower affinity for CB1 than THC, explaining why CBD-driven AEA elevation does not produce intoxication
- No CBD-based SUD pharmacotherapy is FDA-approved as of 2026; Epidiolex approval covers epilepsy only
