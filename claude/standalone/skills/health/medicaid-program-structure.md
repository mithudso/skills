# Medicaid Program Structure

> **Educational only — NOT health or legal advice.** Medicaid rules vary by state and change frequently; verify current rules with your state Medicaid agency.

**verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

---

## 1. What Medicaid Is

**Medicaid** is the United States' joint federal-state public health insurance program for low-income individuals. It is:
- **The largest single health insurer in the US** by enrollment (~94 million enrollees as of 2026)
- **Title XIX** of the Social Security Act (enacted 1965, same year as Medicare/Title XVIII)
- **Jointly funded** by federal and state governments (FMAP formula determines federal share)
- **Administered by states** within federal minimum standards
- **Optional for states** to participate in — but all 50 states + DC participate

The federal agency responsible is **CMS** (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services) within HHS.

## 2. Medicaid's Scale (2026)

| Metric | Approximate Value |
|---|---|
| Total enrollees | ~94 million |
| Annual federal + state spending | ~$900 billion |
| Share of US population | ~28% |
| Share of total US health spending | ~17% |
| Children covered | ~40 million |
| People with disabilities | ~10 million |
| Elderly (65+) | ~7 million |
| Non-elderly adults | ~40 million |

Medicaid covers approximately **1 in 3 children** and **2 in 3** nursing home residents in the US.

## 3. Medicaid vs Medicare vs ACA Marketplace

| Feature | Medicaid | Medicare | ACA Marketplace |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who** | Low-income; categorical + income eligibility | Age 65+ or disabled (2-yr SSDI) | All; income-based subsidies |
| **Income test** | Yes (MAGI or non-MAGI) | No | Subsidies based on income |
| **Federal law** | Title XIX SSA | Title XVIII SSA | ACA 2010 |
| **Administration** | States | Federal (CMS) | Federal/state (SBMs) |
| **Cost to enrollee** | Very low (some copays) | Premiums + deductibles | Premiums + deductibles (subsidized) |
| **Benefits** | Comprehensive (varies by state) | Standard federal package | ACA Essential Health Benefits |
| **Funding** | Federal-state matching (FMAP) | Payroll taxes + general revenue | Subsidies (federal); premiums |

## 4. Coverage Groups

### Federal Mandatory Groups (all states must cover)
- **Children** up to 133% FPL (ages 1–18; infants up to 185%+ FPL)
- **Pregnant women** up to 133% FPL
- **Parents/caretaker relatives** up to state-specific threshold
- **Adults up to 138% FPL** (in the 41+ states that accepted ACA expansion)
- **SSI recipients** (elderly 65+, blind, disabled) meeting financial criteria
- **People with disabilities** who meet financial criteria
- **Foster care youth** up to age 18 (states may extend to 26)
- **Former foster youth** up to age 26 (mandatory since ACA)
- **Breast and cervical cancer** patients (screened through CDC program, income up to 250% FPL)

### Federal Optional Groups (states may choose to cover)
- Medically needy (spend-down) — people who exceed income limits but have high medical expenses
- Adults 19–64 up to 138% FPL (ACA expansion — "optional" since NFIB v. Sebelius)
- Children up to 300% FPL (some states)
- Pregnant women up to 200%+ FPL
- People with severe disabilities who work (Medicaid Buy-In / Section 1619b)
- Individuals in institutions (ICF/I/DD, nursing facilities) above community income limits
- People with HIV/AIDS (optional, pre-ACA; now generally covered through expansion)

## 5. Benefit Structure

### Mandatory Benefits (all states must cover for all groups)
- Inpatient hospital services
- Outpatient hospital services
- Physician services
- Laboratory and X-ray
- EPSDT (Early and Periodic Screening, Diagnostic, Treatment) — all medically necessary services for children
- Family planning services and supplies
- Federally Qualified Health Center (FQHC) and Rural Health Clinic services
- Nurse midwife services
- Pediatric and family nurse practitioner services
- Nursing facility services for adults 21+

### Optional Benefits (states may choose to cover)
- Prescription drugs (virtually all states cover)
- Dental care (almost all states cover at least emergency; many cover comprehensive)
- Vision care
- Physical/occupational/speech therapy
- Home health aides
- Personal care services
- Hospice
- Mental health services (most states)
- Substance use disorder services (most states; ACA-required parity)
- Home and community-based services (HCBS waivers)

### EPSDT — The Children's Benefit
The EPSDT mandate requires states to cover **any service necessary to prevent, treat, or ameliorate a condition** identified in a screening for children under 21 — even if the service isn't in the state plan for adults. This is the most comprehensive insurance benefit package in the US for children.

## 6. Delivery Systems

**Fee-for-service (FFS) / Medicaid Direct**: State reimburses providers per claim; traditional model.

**Managed care**: State contracts with MCOs (capitated payment); MCOs are responsible for providing all covered services within the per-member-per-month payment. Now the dominant model — ~75% of Medicaid enrollees are in managed care nationally.

**Hybrid**: Some states use managed care for physical health but FFS for LTSS, pharmacy, or behavioral health.

---

## 7. State Flexibility

States have significant flexibility within federal minimums:
- **Benefit design**: Can add optional benefits or restrict optional benefits
- **Provider payment rates**: Can set their own (subject to CMS access standards)
- **Delivery system**: Can use FFS, managed care, or mixed
- **Eligibility expansions**: Can cover optional groups
- **Waivers**: 1115 demonstrations, 1915(b) managed care waivers, 1915(c) HCBS waivers

This flexibility means **Medicaid in NC is different from Medicaid in Texas, California, or New York** — same federal floor, very different programs above that floor.

---

## 8. Anti-Patterns

**"Medicaid is just welfare insurance for people who don't work"** — Medicaid covers children (40M+), people with disabilities, elderly nursing home residents, pregnant women, and since ACA expansion, working adults above poverty. Many Medicaid enrollees work in low-wage jobs without employer coverage.

**"Medicaid and Medicare are the same program"** — Different statutes, different eligible populations, different financing, different administration. Medicare is federal; Medicaid is federal-state.

**"All states have the same Medicaid"** — False. The 50 state programs plus DC vary significantly in eligibility, benefits, provider payment, and delivery systems — all within federal minimum standards.
