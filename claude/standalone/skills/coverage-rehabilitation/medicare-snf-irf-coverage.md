# Medicare SNF and IRF Coverage (Part A)

Medicare Part A covers two post-acute inpatient rehabilitation settings: skilled nursing facilities (SNF) and inpatient rehabilitation facilities (IRF). Each has distinct admission criteria, cost-sharing structures, and clinical appropriateness standards.

---

## Skilled Nursing Facility (SNF) Coverage

### Admission Eligibility Requirements

Four conditions must ALL be met: [^1][^2]

1. **Qualifying inpatient hospital stay** — at least 3 consecutive days as a formal inpatient (not outpatient/observation). Discharge day does not count. Emergency department or observation time does not count, even overnight. (42 CFR 409.30)
2. **Related condition** — SNF admission must be for the same condition (or a condition directly related to) the qualifying hospital stay.
3. **Skilled care need** — physician must certify the patient requires daily skilled nursing care (e.g., IV medications, wound care) or skilled therapy (PT/OT/SLP) at least 5 days/week.
4. **Timely admission** — patient must enter the SNF within 30 calendar days of hospital discharge (or within a time period CMS finds medically appropriate). (42 CFR 409.30(e))

### Benefit Period and Coverage Duration [^1]

| Days | Patient Pays | Medicare Pays |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| Days 1–20 | $0 | 100% |
| Days 21–100 | $217.00/day (2026) | Balance |
| Days 101+ | 100% | $0 |

- **Benefit period** begins the day admitted as a hospital or SNF inpatient; ends after 60 consecutive days as a non-inpatient not receiving skilled SNF care. (42 CFR 409.60)
- Maximum 100 days covered per benefit period.
- New benefit period starts after 60-day qualifying absence — resets the 3-day rule requirement.
- **2025 coinsurance**: $209.50/day for days 21–100. [^7]
- **2026 coinsurance**: $217.00/day for days 21–100. [^7]

### The Observation-Status "SNF Trap" [^3]

**Core problem**: patients labeled "outpatient on observation" cannot access SNF coverage even if clinically needing post-acute rehab.

- Observation status = outpatient monitoring. A patient can spend multiple nights in a hospital and still be outpatient.
- Observation nights do NOT count toward the 3-day inpatient requirement, regardless of length of stay.
- A 4-night hospital stay can yield zero qualifying inpatient days if the status was observation.
- Patients on observation pay Part B cost-sharing (not Part A deductible) for hospital services, and often pay out-of-pocket for any drugs administered.

**MOON Notice Requirements** [^3]
- Hospitals must deliver the Medicare Outpatient Observation Notice (MOON) no later than 36 hours after observation services begin, or upon transfer/discharge (whichever is sooner). Required for outpatient observation stays exceeding 24 hours.
- MOON informs patients their status affects SNF eligibility and cost-sharing.
- CMS updated the MOON form in 2026; providers must use the new form as of April 20, 2026 (OMB-reauthorized, valid through February 28, 2029).

**Patient protections** (Alexander v. Azar final rule, 89 FR 83598, Oct. 2024): [^3a]
- If admitted as inpatient, then reclassified to observation: patient qualifies for a prospective fast appeal before discharge.
- Retrospective appeal filing window for older stays (back to Jan. 1, 2009) closed January 2, 2026; requests after that date denied as untimely unless "good cause" shown (42 CFR 405.932(a)(2)(ii)).
- Medicare Advantage plans may waive the 3-day rule — check plan benefit details.

### Jimmo v. Sebelius: Maintenance Standard [^8]

The 2013 Jimmo settlement (approved January 24, 2013; CMS FAQs ongoing) clarified:
- SNF coverage does **not** require patient improvement potential.
- Coverage turns on need for **skilled care**, not expectation of functional improvement.
- Skilled maintenance services are covered where a qualified therapist or nurse is needed to perform services safely, even where no improvement is expected.
- CMS must apply the maintenance standard; denials solely on "no improvement expected" are improper.

### SNF Appeals Process [^1][^3a]

| Appeal Type | Timeframe | When Used |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|
| Expedited (fast) appeal | Before discharge | Status changed inpatient → observation |
| Standard appeal | 60–120 days after MSN | Denied coverage |
| ALJ hearing | After reconsideration denial | Continuing dispute |
| Beneficiary and Family Centered Care QIO | Immediate coverage end | SNF issuing "Notice of Medicare Non-Coverage" |

## Inpatient Rehabilitation Facility (IRF) Coverage

### What Qualifies as an IRF

An IRF is a hospital or distinct unit providing intensive rehabilitation, subject to Medicare's IRF prospective payment system (PPS). To be paid under IRF PPS, a facility must meet the **60% rule**.

### The 60% Rule (Compliance Threshold) [^4][^5]

**Requirement**: At least 60% of a facility's total inpatient population must require IRF treatment for one or more of the **13 qualifying conditions** listed in **42 CFR 412.29(b)(2)**. (verified-as-of: 2026-06-22)

**The 13 Qualifying Conditions** (42 CFR 412.29(b)(2), verbatim categories):
1. Stroke
2. Spinal cord injury
3. Congenital deformity
4. Amputation
5. Major multiple trauma
6. Fracture of femur (hip fracture)
7. Brain injury
8. Neurological disorders — including multiple sclerosis, motor neuron diseases, polyneuropathy, muscular dystrophy, and Parkinson's disease
9. Burns
10. Active polyarticular rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, and seronegative arthropathies — with functional impairment following unsuccessful outpatient therapy
11. Systemic vasculitides with joint inflammation — requiring prior unsuccessful outpatient therapy
12. Severe or advanced osteoarthritis involving two or more major weight-bearing joints with documented deformity and functional impairment
13. Knee or hip joint replacement when: bilateral surgery, BMI ≥50, or patient age ≥85

**Compliance determination**:
- Presumptive methodology: based on IRF-PAI data vs. diagnosis codes.
- Medical review methodology: applied when presumptive review fails.
- MACs (Medicare Administrative Contractors) determine compliance.
- Non-compliant facilities face retroactive payment adjustments.

### Individual Patient Admission Criteria [^4][^6]

A patient must meet ALL (42 CFR 412.622):

1. **Intensive therapy tolerance** — can participate in ≥3 hours of therapy/day, ≥5 days/week (or ≥15 hours in 7 consecutive calendar days with clinical justification).
2. **Multiple therapy disciplines** — active and ongoing therapeutic intervention of multiple disciplines (PT, OT, SLP, or prosthetics/orthotics); one must be PT or OT.
3. **Expected measurable benefit** — physician reasonably expects intensive rehabilitation will improve functional capacity within a prescribed period.
4. **Medical stability** — sufficiently stable at time of admission to actively participate; not requiring acute hospital-level monitoring.
5. **Physician supervision** — requires face-to-face visits by a rehabilitation physician at least 3 days per week throughout the stay (after week 2, one visit/week may be by a non-physician practitioner).
6. **Therapy within 36 hours** — required therapy treatments must begin within 36 hours from midnight of the day of IRF admission.

### Required Documentation [^6]

**Preadmission Screening (PAS)** (42 CFR 412.622(a)(4)):
- Must be completed within the 48 hours immediately preceding IRF admission (earlier assessments acceptable if updated within 48 hours).
- Completed by a clinician, reviewed and signed by rehabilitation physician.
- Must include: prior functional level, expected improvement, estimated LOS, comorbidity evaluation, discharge planning considerations.

**Post-Admission**:
- **Individualized Plan of Care (IPOC)**: due within 4 days, specifying therapy intensity, frequency, and duration.
- **IRF-PAI** (Patient Assessment Instrument): required admission and discharge assessments.
- **Interdisciplinary Team (IDT) meetings**: minimum weekly, led by rehabilitation physician, documenting progress and plan revisions.

**Common documentation errors leading to denials**:
- Insufficient medical necessity justification
- Missing therapy treatment notes with documented time
- Inadequate IDT meeting documentation lacking physician concurrence
- Missing or illegible signatures on screening documents
- IRF-PAI not aligned with medical record

### IRF Cost Sharing Under Part A [^7]

IRF uses the same Part A benefit structure as acute hospital:

| Days | Patient Pays (2026) |
|------|---------------------|
| Days 1–60 | $1,736 deductible (once per benefit period) |
| Days 61–90 | $434/day coinsurance |
| Days 91–150 (lifetime reserve) | $868/day |
| Beyond lifetime reserve | 100% |

- **2025 deductible**: $1,676 per benefit period.
- **2026 deductible**: $1,736 per benefit period.
- Part A cost-sharing applies to IRF (not the SNF-style day-1-20 / day-21-100 structure).

## IRF vs. SNF: Placement Decision Framework [^4][^6]

| Factor | IRF | SNF |
|--------|-----|-----|
| Therapy intensity | 3 hrs/day, 5 days/week | 1–2 hrs/day typical |
| Physician access | Physiatrist on-site, visits ≥3x/week | Physician available, not required daily |
| Medical complexity | Moderate-high; stable but needs monitoring | Lower acuity acceptable |
| Patient stamina | Must tolerate intensive therapy | Less tolerance acceptable |
| Primary conditions | Typically stroke, TBI, spinal injury, amputation, major trauma | Broader range including orthopedic, cardiac, pulmonary |
| Cost structure | Part A hospital deductible/coinsurance | Day 1-20 free; Day 21-100 $217/day |
| Expected LOS | Typically 10–20 days | Can extend to 100 days |
| Improvement standard | Functional improvement expected | Maintenance standard applies (Jimmo) |

**Clinical decision points**:
- Patient stamina is often the key factor: inability to tolerate 3 hrs/day → SNF.
- Stroke, TBI, spinal cord injury, amputation → IRF typical first choice if tolerated.
- Orthopedic post-op, deconditioning, chronic conditions → often SNF.
- Step-down path: IRF → SNF when patient regresses or can't maintain IRF intensity.
- Medicare Advantage plans may have different criteria — verify prior authorization requirements.

## SNF Value-Based Purchasing (VBP) Program [^9]

Established by the IMPACT Act; CMS adjusts SNF payments by ±2% based on quality performance. FY 2026 program measures four domains:
1. **SNF 30-Day All-Cause Readmission Measure (SNFRM)** — risk-standardized rate of unplanned hospital readmissions.
2. **SNF Healthcare-Associated Infections Requiring Hospitalization (SNF HAI)** — risk-standardized infection rate.
3. **Total Nursing Staff Turnover** — annual turnover rate for RNs, LPNs, and nurse aides.
4. **Total Nursing Hours per Resident Day** — staffing hours metric.

SNF VBP scores are reported on Care Compare (Medicare.gov). High performers receive an incentive payment; low performers receive a reduction.

## IRF Review Choice Demonstration (RCD) [^10]

CMS's Review Choice Demonstration for IRF services gives facilities a compliance monitoring choice (100% pre-claim review or 100% postpayment review). Active in Alabama (since Aug. 2023), Pennsylvania (since June 2024), Texas and California (effective March–May 2026). Facilities demonstrating compliance gain additional review options and reduced audit burden.

## Anti-Patterns / Common Mistakes

- **Assuming observation nights count**: They never do for SNF eligibility. Verify inpatient status before discharge.
- **Accepting SNF denial based on "no improvement"**: Jimmo settlement prohibits denials on this basis alone.
- **Not reading the MOON**: Observation status patients unaware of implications until after discharge.
- **IRF without documentation**: IRF denials most commonly trace to preadmission screening deficiencies and inadequate IDT documentation.
- **Assuming MA plans follow Original Medicare rules**: MA plans may waive the 3-day rule OR impose tighter prior authorization requirements.
- **Missing appeal deadlines**: Expedited appeals for status changes must happen before discharge.
