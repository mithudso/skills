# Network Adequacy for Behavioral Health Providers

## Overview

Network adequacy — whether health plans have sufficient in-network providers for members to access care — is a chronic problem for behavioral health. BH provider shortages, low reimbursement rates driving out-of-network practice, and inaccurate provider directories create access gaps even when coverage exists on paper.

## Why BH Network Adequacy Is Structurally Harder

1. **Workforce shortage**: Approximately 50% of all US counties and 65% of rural counties have no practicing psychiatrist; shortages are worse for child/adolescent and addiction psychiatry. HRSA projects a national shortage of approximately 43,660 adult psychiatrists by 2038. [HRSA Behavioral Health Workforce Brief 2023](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/sites/default/files/bureau-health-workforce/Behavioral-Health-Workforce-Brief-2023.pdf) [verified 2026-06-22]; [HRSA Behavioral Health Workforce Brief 2025](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/sites/default/files/bureau-health-workforce/data-research/Behavioral-Health-Workforce-Brief-2025.pdf) [verified 2026-06-22]
2. **Low reimbursement**: BH providers receive lower Medicaid reimbursement than physical health providers for equivalent services; as of FY 2024 most states had non-physician BH rates set below physician rates, with 34 states implementing FFS rate increases for outpatient BH in FY 2024. [KFF 50-State Medicaid Budget Survey FY 2023-2024](https://www.kff.org/report-section/50-state-medicaid-budget-survey-fy-2023-2024-provider-rates-and-taxes/) [verified 2026-06-22] (Note: the "~20% lower" figure from prior drafts of this skill is not pinned to a specific published KFF statistic; cite the KFF survey directly.)
3. **High telehealth penetration**: telehealth expanded BH access post-COVID, but adequacy frameworks lagged
4. **Ghost networks**: provider directories list providers who are not accepting new patients, have left the network, or have incorrect contact information — a May 2023 Senate Finance Committee secret shopper study of Medicare Advantage plans found more than 80% of listed mental health providers were inaccurate or unavailable; staff could secure an appointment only 18% of the time. [Senate Finance Committee Ghost Network Secret Shopper Study, May 3, 2023](https://www.finance.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/050323%20Ghost%20Network%20Hearing%20-%20Secret%20Shopper%20Study%20Report.pdf) [verified 2026-06-22]

## Federal Standards — Medicare Advantage

**42 CFR 422.116** sets network adequacy criteria for MA plans. [eCFR 42 CFR 422.116](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-C/section-422.116) [verified 2026-06-22]

- **Time/distance standards** by county type (urban/suburban/rural/rural outlying) apply to four BH specialty types as of CY 2024: Psychiatry, Clinical Psychology, Clinical Social Work, and Inpatient Psychiatric Facility Services.
- **CY 2024 final rule (CMS-4201-F)**: Added Clinical Psychology and Licensed Clinical Social Work as evaluated specialty types; tightened overall BH network adequacy requirements; applies to coverage beginning January 1, 2024. [CMS CY2024 MA Final Rule Fact Sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2024-medicare-advantage-and-part-d-final-rule-cms-4201-f) [verified 2026-06-22]; [Federal Register, Apr. 12, 2023](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/04/12/2023-07115/medicare-program-contract-year-2024-policy-and-technical-changes-to-the-medicare-advantage-program)
- **Appointment wait time standards (42 CFR 422.112)**: CMS-4201-F extended access standards to behavioral health services: (A) urgently needed/emergency services — immediately; (B) non-emergency services where enrollee requires attention — within 7 business days; (C) routine/preventive outpatient BH — within 30 business days. [eCFR 42 CFR 422.112](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-422/subpart-C/section-422.112) [verified 2026-06-22] (Note: the "routine 15 days / urgent 48 hours" figures in earlier drafts of this skill are inaccurate for MA; the correct standards are 7 and 30 business days per 42 CFR 422.112.)
- **Telehealth credit**: MA plans receive a **10 percentage point** credit toward the percentage of beneficiaries residing within published time/distance standards for a specialty type when the plan includes telehealth providers offering additional telehealth benefits in that specialty. Clinical Psychology and Clinical Social Work are eligible for this credit. [CMS-4201-F](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2024-medicare-advantage-and-part-d-final-rule-cms-4201-f) [verified 2026-06-22] (Note: a "35% of required providers" telehealth credit figure cited in earlier drafts is inaccurate — the actual mechanism is a 10 percentage point credit toward time/distance compliance.)

### MA Network Adequacy Review
- Plans submit network data annually; CMS conducts review via the Health Plan Management System (HPMS)
- Plans failing standards can be denied new service area or required to file corrective action plans
- **Accreditation exception**: NCQA-accredited plans may receive expedited CMS review

## Federal Standards — Medicaid Managed Care

**42 CFR 438.68** requires states to establish time/distance standards. States must cover at minimum: inpatient BH, outpatient BH, outpatient SUD, inpatient SUD. [eCFR 42 CFR 438.68](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-C/part-438/subpart-B/section-438.68) [verified 2026-06-22]

**CMS-2439-F (Medicaid and CHIP Managed Care Access, Finance, and Quality Final Rule)**, published May 10, 2024, effective July 9, 2024: [Federal Register, May 10, 2024](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/05/10/2024-08085/medicaid-program-medicaid-and-childrens-health-insurance-program-chip-managed-care-access-finance) [verified 2026-06-22]
- Establishes national maximum **appointment wait time standards**: outpatient mental health and SUD services (adult and pediatric) — **10 business days** from request; primary care and OB/GYN — 15 business days (stricter for BH than for primary care).
- Compliance expectation: 90% of appointments must meet the standard.
- Full state compliance required by the first rating period beginning on or after 3 years after July 9, 2024 (approximately July 2027).
- **Secret shopper surveys**: states must conduct independent secret shopper surveys of MCO plans to validate compliance with appointment wait time standards and identify provider directory inaccuracies.
- Standards vary by state above the federal floor; states set actual time/distance values but must include BH providers.

## Commercial Insurance — State Standards

No federal adequacy standard applies to fully insured commercial plans (ACA deferred to states):
- **NAIC Network Adequacy Model Act (2015)**: adopted by approximately 20 states; covers appointment wait times and geographic standards
- States with strong BH-specific timely-access standards include CA (**SB 221**, 2021 — requires appointment availability within specified timeframes) and WA (**RCW 48.43.765** — network adequacy for mental health and SUD treatment). [CA SB 221](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202120220SB221) [verified 2026-06-22]; [WA RCW 48.43.765](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=48.43.765) [verified 2026-06-22] (Note: CA AB 988 is California's 988 crisis-line law, not a network adequacy statute.)
- **NQTL analysis under MHPAEA**: a plan's network adequacy standard is subject to comparative analysis as a non-quantitative treatment limitation (NQTL). If a plan's inadequate BH network causes members to use out-of-network providers and then applies higher OON cost-sharing to BH than to medical/surgical, this may violate MHPAEA. The 2024 MHPAEA Final Rule (effective November 22, 2024) explicitly codifies network composition as an NQTL subject to comparative analysis. [DOL MHPAEA 2024 Final Rule Fact Sheet](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/fact-sheets/final-rules-under-the-mental-health-parity-and-addiction-equity-act-mhpaea) [verified 2026-06-22]; [Federal Register, Sep. 23, 2024](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/09/23/2024-20612/requirements-related-to-the-mental-health-parity-and-addiction-equity-act)

## Provider Directory Integrity

The **No Surprises Act** (effective January 1, 2022) imposed:
- Health plans must verify and update provider directories at least every 90 days
- Plans must update the directory within 2 business days of receiving provider changes
- Members who rely on inaccurate directory information for in-network care are protected from higher cost-sharing

[CMS No Surprises Act Resources](https://www.cms.gov/nosurprises) [verified 2026-06-22]

**Behavioral health directory inaccuracy drivers**:
- High provider turnover / panel closure
- Solo practices updating information inconsistently
- Telehealth-only providers listed under physical addresses
- Providers listed for a condition category but no longer accepting that condition (e.g., listed as "mental health" but no longer treating schizophrenia)

A 2025 OIG report (OEI-02-23-00540) found that many MA and Medicaid managed care plans had limited BH provider networks further undermined by inactive providers who did not provide any services to enrollees but made networks appear larger. [OIG Report OEI-02-23-00540 (Oct. 2025)](https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2025/many-medicare-advantage-and-medicaid-managed-care-plans-have-limited-behavioral-health-provider-networks-and-inactive-providers/) [verified 2026-06-22]

## Secret Shopper Studies

CMS (required under CMS-2439-F for Medicaid), state insurance departments, and researchers use secret shopper studies to test actual network access:
- Call listed BH providers and attempt to schedule appointments
- **Senate Finance Committee 2023**: called listed in-network mental health providers across 12 MA plans in 6 states; more than 80% of listings were inaccurate or unavailable; only 18% of calls resulted in a schedulable appointment. [Senate Finance Secret Shopper Study Report, May 3, 2023](https://www.finance.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/050323%20Ghost%20Network%20Hearing%20-%20Secret%20Shopper%20Study%20Report.pdf) [verified 2026-06-22]; [Senate Finance Hearing page](https://www.finance.senate.gov/hearings/barriers-to-mental-health-care-improving-provider-directory-accuracy-to-reduce-the-prevalence-of-ghost-networks)
- **Regulatory use**: states use secret shopper results to cite MCOs for network adequacy violations; CMS-2439-F mandates state-run secret shopper programs for Medicaid MCOs

## Enforcement Mechanisms

| Level | Action |
|---|---|
| Federal/MA | CMS can impose civil money penalties, suspend enrollment, terminate contract (42 CFR 422.750) |
| Federal/Medicaid | CMS can issue corrective action requirements; withhold of FFP possible (42 CFR 438.700) |
| State (commercial) | State insurance commissioner can issue corrective action, fine, limit enrollment |
| Member remedy | NSA protection (directory inaccuracy); Medicaid members can request out-of-network at in-network cost-sharing if no adequate in-network provider |

## Telehealth's Role Post-COVID

- Telehealth dramatically expanded BH access during COVID; utilization remains elevated
- **CMS-4201-F telehealth credit for MA**: 10 percentage point credit toward time/distance compliance for specialty types where plan includes telehealth providers — reduces pressure for in-person BH network but risks further reduction of in-person BH capacity
- **Digital divide**: rural/elderly patients less able to use telehealth; adequacy standards that rely heavily on telehealth may mask access gaps for vulnerable populations
- SAMHSA has published research on telehealth BH access and equity; concern remains about adequacy for people without broadband. [SAMHSA telehealth resources](https://www.samhsa.gov/telehealth)

## Adequacy and MHPAEA Interaction

Network adequacy functions as an NQTL under MHPAEA:
- If a plan has tighter geographic standards for psychiatrists than for equivalent specialists (e.g., cardiologists), this constitutes a disparity subject to comparative analysis
- If a plan's inadequate BH network causes out-of-network use, and the plan applies higher OON cost-sharing to BH than to medical/surgical, this may be a NQTL violation
- The **2024 MHPAEA Final Rule** (effective November 22, 2024) explicitly includes network composition as an NQTL subject to comparative analysis, requiring plans to document and justify any differential in network adequacy standards between BH and medical/surgical. [Federal Register, Sep. 23, 2024](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/09/23/2024-20612/requirements-related-to-the-mental-health-parity-and-addiction-equity-act) [verified 2026-06-22]
