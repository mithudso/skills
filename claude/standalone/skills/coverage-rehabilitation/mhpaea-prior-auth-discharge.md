# MHPAEA, Prior Authorization Reform, and Discharge Planning

This skill covers three interlocking coverage administration topics: parity requirements constraining how plans limit behavioral health benefits, prior authorization reform creating faster and more transparent decisions, and discharge planning requirements ensuring patients have meaningful choices about post-acute care.

---

## Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act (MHPAEA)

### Scope: Which Plans Are Covered [^1][^2]

**Statute**: 29 U.S.C. § 1185a (ERISA plans); parallel provisions at 42 U.S.C. § 300gg-26 (public health service act) and 26 U.S.C. § 9812 (tax code). [verified-as-of: 2026-06-22]

MHPAEA applies to:
- Employer-sponsored group health plans (ERISA and non-ERISA)
- Health insurance issuers offering group or individual coverage
- Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP)
- Medicaid managed care organizations

**NOT covered by MHPAEA**:
- Original Medicare (explicitly excluded by statute) [^1]
- Medicare Advantage plans (also excluded by statute — not subject to MHPAEA's requirements) [^1]
- Small-employer plans with ≤50 employees (federal exemption under ERISA and IRC definitions; some states extend parity to small group via ACA EHB requirements) [^2]

### Core Parity Requirements [^1][^2]

**1. Quantitative Treatment Limitations (QTLs)**:
No QTL (numerical limit) applied to mental health/SUD benefits may be more restrictive than the predominant financial requirement or quantitative treatment limitation applied to substantially all medical/surgical benefits in the same classification.

Examples: annual visit limits, day limits, dosage limits.

**2. Financial Requirements**:
Copays, coinsurance, deductibles, and out-of-pocket maximums for MH/SUD benefits must comply with parity across 6 benefit classifications:
1. Inpatient in-network
2. Inpatient out-of-network
3. Outpatient in-network
4. Outpatient out-of-network
5. Emergency care
6. Prescription drugs

**3. Non-Quantitative Treatment Limitations (NQTLs)**:
Any process, strategy, evidentiary standard, or factor used to limit the scope or duration of benefits — and applied more stringently to MH/SUD than to medical/surgical — violates MHPAEA.

**NQTL examples**:
- Prior authorization requirements
- Step therapy (fail-first) requirements
- Network adequacy standards
- Reimbursement rate methodologies affecting provider participation
- Geographic limits
- Facility-type limits
- Medical necessity criteria and evidentiary standards
- Formulary placement restrictions

### NQTL Comparative Analysis Requirement [^1][^2]

**Statutory requirement** (CAA 2021, Section 203 of Title II of Division BB — enacted December 27, 2020) [^2]:
Plans must conduct and maintain a written comparative analysis demonstrating that NQTLs applied to MH/SUD benefits are comparable to and no more stringent than those applied to medical/surgical benefits. This is a statutory requirement that took effect 45 days after CAA 2021 enactment (i.e., February 10, 2021).

The comparative analysis must document:
- The specific NQTLs applied to MH/SUD and medical/surgical benefits
- The factors used in the design and application of the NQTLs
- The evidentiary standards used
- The results of the comparative analysis

Plans must make the comparative analysis available to CMS, DOL, or state authorities upon request. Agencies may require submission independent of a formal complaint.

### 2024 MHPAEA Final Rule and Enforcement Status [^2][^3]

**Released**: September 9, 2024. **Published in Federal Register**: September 23, 2024. [^3]

**Effective dates of 2024 final rule**:

| Provision | Effective date |
|-----------|---------------|
| **General NQTL comparative analysis enhancements** (codifying CAA 2021 requirements) | January 1, 2025 |
| **Fiduciary certification** (named fiduciary must certify prudent process to select PA/NQTL analysis service providers) | January 1, 2025 |
| **Meaningful benefits standard** (plans offering MH/SUD benefits in any classification must provide meaningful benefits in every classification where M/S benefits are provided) | January 1, 2026 |
| **Prohibition on discriminatory factors/evidentiary standards** | January 1, 2026 |
| **Outcomes data evaluation requirement** (material differences in access) | January 1, 2026 |

**Enforcement status (as of May 15, 2025)**:
The Departments of Labor, HHS, and Treasury announced non-enforcement of provisions of the 2024 final rule that are new relative to the 2013 rule — effective until the ERIC litigation is finally resolved plus an additional 18 months. [^3]

| Requirement | Status |
|-------------|--------|
| **Fiduciary certification** | NOT enforced (2024 rule; on hold) |
| **Meaningful benefits standard** | NOT enforced (2024 rule; on hold) |
| **Outcomes data evaluation** (material differences in access) | NOT enforced (2024 rule; on hold) |
| **QTL parity** (2013 rule) | **Still enforced** |
| **NQTL parity** (2013 rule) | **Still enforced** |
| **Comparative analysis documentation** (CAA 2021, statutory) | **Still enforced** — statutory obligation; agencies still conduct investigations |

**State parity laws remain unaffected** by the federal non-enforcement notice.

**Compliance risk**: Plans remain exposed to QTL and NQTL violations and comparative analysis failures — DOL/CMS still investigate. Failure can result in fiduciary liability, penalties, and claim re-adjudication.

## Prior Authorization Reform

### CMS Interoperability and Prior Authorization Final Rule (CMS-0057-F) [^4]

**Published**: January 17, 2024. **Key operational implementation**: January 1, 2026. **API implementation**: January 1, 2027.

**Scope**: Medicare Advantage organizations, state Medicaid FFS programs, Medicaid managed care plans, CHIP managed care entities, and QHP issuers on Federally Facilitated Exchanges (FFEs).

**Standardized decision timeframes** (effective January 1, 2026):

| Request type | Decision timeframe |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Standard prior authorization | **7 calendar days** |
| Expedited (urgent) prior authorization | **72 hours** |

**Important**: QHP issuers on FFEs are excluded from the prior authorization decision timeframe requirements (72 hours/7 days), though they remain subject to denial-reason and public metrics reporting requirements. [^4]

**Prior authorization transparency** (effective January 1, 2026):
- Impacted payers must publicly post aggregated prior authorization metrics from the previous year on their public-facing websites annually.
- Plans must implement API-based prior authorization data sharing.
- Specific denial reasons must be provided.

**Interoperability requirements** (effective January 1, 2027):
- Electronic prior authorization via standardized APIs (HL7 FHIR).
- Improves automated status tracking and reduces phone-based PA delays.

### Medicare Advantage Prior Authorization Challenges [^5][^6]

Despite reforms, MA prior authorization remains a significant access barrier for rehab services:
- MA plans routinely require prior authorization for inpatient psychiatric stays, mental health specialty services, and rehab services (PT, OT, SNF, IRF) under plan-specific requirements.
- CMS suspended new MA prior authorization transparency rules in 2025 amid public concerns about care denials (alternative approach under development). [^6]

**2026 MA final rule provisions**:
- Clarifies meaning of "internal coverage criteria" to constrain utilization management.
- Requires MA plans to make internal coverage policies publicly accessible.
- Addresses plan reversals of coverage decisions that affect payment for rural hospitals.

### Medicaid Managed Care Prior Authorization [^4]

Medicaid managed care plans subject to the same CMS-0057-F timeframes (7-day standard / 72-hour expedited) effective January 1, 2026.

MCO PA requirements for behavioral health services vary by:
- State contract requirements
- MCO utilization management policies
- Whether the state has MHPAEA-equivalent standards for Medicaid managed care

## Discharge Planning Requirements

### CMS Discharge Planning Final Rule (2019, CoP) [^7][^8]

**Authority**: Improving Medicare Post-Acute Care Transformation (IMPACT) Act of 2014.
**Published**: September 30, 2019 (CMS-3317-F). **Compliance deadline**: November 29, 2019 (60 days after publication). [^8]
**Applies to**: Acute hospitals, long-term care hospitals (LTCHs), rehabilitation hospitals, psychiatric hospitals, critical access hospitals (CAHs), and home health agencies participating in Medicare and Medicaid.
**CFR citation**: 42 CFR § 482.43 (hospitals); 42 CFR § 485.642 (CAHs).

### Core Requirements [^7][^8]

**1. Patient-centered planning**:
- Discharge process must focus on patient's goals and treatment preferences.
- Patients and caregivers must be active partners in planning.
- Discharge plan must be consistent with patient's goals for care and treatment preferences.

**2. Freedom of choice**:
- Hospitals must inform patients of their freedom to choose among participating Medicare providers and suppliers.
- Hospitals must NOT specify or limit qualified providers available to the patient.
- Must not steer patients toward specific facilities (anti-self-referral protections apply).

**3. Provider lists for post-acute care**:
For patients discharged to SNF, IRF, LTCH, or home health agencies:
- Hospitals must provide a list of Medicare-participating post-acute providers serving the patient's geographic area.
- List must include quality data (CMS star ratings, quality measures).
- Cannot contain only facilities with financial relationships with the hospital.

**4. Transition of care documentation**:
At discharge, hospitals must transfer all necessary medical information including:
- Current course of illness and treatment
- Post-discharge goals of care
- Treatment preferences
- To receiving post-acute care providers, facilities, and outpatient services

**5. Readmission reduction focus**:
Discharge plans must include steps to reduce risk of preventable readmissions.

### July 1, 2025 CoP Updates: Emergency Services and Transfer Protocols [^9]

Effective **July 1, 2025** (per CMS QSO-25-24), hospitals and CAHs must comply with updated CoP requirements that are related to — but distinct from — the 2019 discharge planning rule:
- Hospitals must have written policies and procedures for transferring patients to the appropriate level of care (including inter-hospital transfers), under 42 CFR § 482.43.
- Annual training required for all applicable staff on these transfer policies and procedures.
- Emergency services CoP updates (42 CFR § 482.55): written protocols based on nationally recognized, evidence-based guidelines; annual staff training.

*Note*: These 2025 updates address transfer protocols and emergency readiness under the emergency services and transfer CoPs; they are distinct from the core 2019 discharge planning rule requirements. CMS surveyors are expected to document patient and caregiver meaningful engagement in discharge planning under the 2019 rule.

### Discharge Planning for Behavioral Health [^7][^8]

The 2019 rule explicitly covers psychiatric hospitals and rehabilitation hospitals. For behavioral health discharges:
- Patient choice among outpatient MH/SUD providers must be honored.
- Follow-up care coordination should be included in plan.
- MAT continuity (buprenorphine, naltrexone) must be addressed in discharge from SUD treatment settings.
- Crisis safety planning is expected component for psychiatric discharges.

## Anti-Patterns / Common Mistakes

- **Assuming MHPAEA covers Medicare**: it does not; Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage are both explicitly excluded by statute. [^1]
- **Grouping meaningful benefits standard and outcomes data with January 2025 provisions**: the meaningful benefits standard, discriminatory factors prohibition, and outcomes data evaluation all apply January 1, 2026 — not 2025. Only fiduciary certification and general comparative analysis enhancements apply January 1, 2025. [^3]
- **Treating 2024 MHPAEA final rule as fully in effect**: three major new requirements are on non-enforcement hold (until ERIC litigation resolves + 18 months); the 2013 NQTL requirements and CAA 2021 statutory comparative analysis obligation still apply. [^3]
- **Ignoring comparative analysis documentation obligation**: CAA 2021 statutory requirement — non-enforcement of 2024 rule does not stay this; agencies still investigate.
- **Assuming MA prior authorization rules match Original Medicare**: MA PA requirements are plan-specific and typically far more restrictive.
- **Assuming QHP issuers on FFEs must meet 72-hour/7-day timeframes**: they are excluded from CMS-0057-F decision timeframe requirements, though still subject to denial-reason and reporting requirements. [^4]
- **Providing only a single SNF on discharge list**: patient freedom of choice requirement means providing a list — not steering to one facility.
- **Missing 72-hour expedited PA for urgent care (for covered payers)**: the CMS-0057-F finalized this standard effective January 1, 2026; MA, Medicaid MCO, and CHIP plans that take longer may be out of compliance.
- **Conflating 2025 transfer-protocol CoP updates with discharge planning requirements**: the July 1, 2025 updates are about emergency services and transfer protocols (QSO-25-24), not a revision of the core 2019 discharge planning CoP rule. [^9]
