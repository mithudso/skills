# Medicaid Coverage of Peer Support Specialist Services

## Overview

Peer support specialists (PSS) — people with lived experience of mental illness or substance use disorder who support others in recovery — represent one of Medicaid's fastest-growing behavioral health services. As of 2022, **40 states** covered Medicaid peer support services, with 5 states not covering and 6 not reporting [KFF 2022 data; verified-as-of 2022-07-01]. Coverage has continued to expand but is not yet universal across all 50 states and DC.

## Medicaid Authority for Peer Support

States bill peer support under several state plan authorities [CMS FAQ, June 2024]:

### 1. Rehabilitative Services (Section 1905(a)(13) of the Social Security Act)
- Most common authority used by states
- Peer support is "rehabilitative" — helps restore or maximize functioning
- Reimbursement requires an identified unit of service, a single peer support provider per unit, an approved plan of care, and state mechanisms to prevent over-billing
- **No federal certification standard specified**; states set their own, but must provide assurances that providers have completed training sufficient for basic competencies
- Must be provided by or under supervision of a qualified professional in most state implementations

### 2. Preventive Services Option (Section 1905(a)(13), alternate application)
- CMS guidance also identifies the preventive services option alongside the rehabilitative option as an authority states use for peer support [CMS FAQ, June 2024]
- Note: §1905(a)(14) covers inpatient/NF services for individuals 65+ in institutions for mental disease (IMD) — it is **not** a peer support billing authority [SSA §1905(a)(14)]

### 3. 1915(i) State Plan HCBS
- Peer support frequently included as a 1915(i) service for individuals with SMI
- Enables peer support outside traditional clinical settings

### 4. Section 1115 and 1915(c) Waiver
- Peer support as a waiver service for SUD or LTSS populations

### 5. CCBHC (Section 1905(a)(31) — Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinics)
- Peer support and family support is one of the **9 required service categories** for CCBHC certification under the 2023 SAMHSA criteria [SAMHSA CCBHC Certification Criteria, 2023]
- Includes peer support for mental health AND SUD
- Prospective payment (PPS) includes peer support costs; updated PPS guidance issued February 2024 [CMS CCBHC PPS Guidance, 2024]

## Certification Requirements

Federal Medicaid law does not mandate a specific peer support specialist certification, but [CMS FAQ June 2024]:
- States must require peer support providers to have completed training and certification **as defined by the state**, providing basic competencies to perform peer support services
- SAMHSA published **National Model Standards for Peer Support Certification** in June 2023 — covering mental health, SUD, and family peer certifications; advisory/voluntary guidance for states, not binding [SAMHSA PEP23-10-01-001, 2023]
- States set their own training hour minimums and continuing education requirements; state training requirements vary significantly
- **Supervision**: CMS guidance confirms states typically require PSS to work under supervision of a qualified professional for Medicaid billing [CMS FAQ, June 2024]
- **Recovery Coach Authority**: many states separately certify Recovery Coaches for SUD peer support; some distinguish from PSS

### NC Example (verified)
- CPSS requires at least 18 months of lived experience plus completion of an NCCPSS-approved training course [NCDHHS, 2024]
- Recertification every 2 years: 20 hours of additional training + 3 hours of ethics/peer support boundaries training [NCDHHS, 2024]
- Supervised by Qualified Professional; QP-to-CPSS ratio 1:8; CPSS-to-beneficiary ratio 1:12 [NC Medicaid Clinical Coverage Policy 8G]

## Billable Activities

Medicaid peer support may cover:

| Activity | Description |
|---|---|
| Skill development | Assistance with illness management, daily living skills, self-advocacy |
| Crisis support | Peer assistance during mental health crisis; non-clinical support |
| Recovery planning | Collaboration on WRAP, recovery plans, goal-setting |
| Linkage/navigation | Connecting to community resources, benefits, employment, housing |
| Recovery community support | Peer-run drop-in centers, wellness programs |
| Recovery coaching (SUD) | Motivational support, accountability, MAT navigation |
| Family peer support | Peer specialists supporting family members of individuals with BH conditions |
| Youth/young adult peer | Near-peer support for transition-age youth (16–25) |

## Medicare Coverage

Medicare does **not** currently cover peer support specialist services as a stand-alone Part B benefit. However:
- Medicare OTPs (operating under the SUPPORT Act, effective January 1, 2020) include peer recovery support services as **add-on codes within the bundled payment**; payment amounts are geographically adjusted via the GAF [CMS OTP Billing & Payment, 2024]
- Peers employed by FQHCs may bill under the FQHC encounter rate when services fall within the scope of a qualified provider
- **Policy gap**: individuals dually enrolled in Medicare and Medicaid can access Medicaid-covered peer support, but Medicare does not separately reimburse peer services outside OTP bundled payments
- Advocacy groups have pushed for standalone Medicare peer support coverage; no final rule as of 2026

## Reimbursement and Sustainability

- Medicaid reimburses peer support in 15-minute service units; rates vary considerably by state
- Maryland's 2023 approved SPA: individual peer support at $16.38/15-minute unit; group at $4.55/15-minute unit [CMS SPA MD-23-0003-A, 2023] — illustrative; rates vary by state
- State rates vary significantly; state-specific fee schedules are authoritative for exact reimbursement
- **Rate adequacy problem**: CMS encourages states to ensure rates support a living wage; low rates drive high PSS turnover [CMS FAQ, June 2024]
- CCBHC PPS methodology includes peer support as part of the encounter rate (more sustainable); updated PPS guidance issued February 2024
- Recovery Community Organizations (RCOs) frequently struggle to meet Medicaid billing requirements (supervision mandates, documentation burden)

## Evidence Base

Peer support is recognized as a core component of behavioral health recovery. Published studies associate peer support with:
- Reduced hospitalizations and emergency department use
- Improved treatment engagement and retention in SUD treatment
- Improved housing stability and social support outcomes
- **SUD-specific**: recovery coaches associated with improved 12-month abstinence rates and reduced time to treatment engagement
- Cost-effectiveness: peer support may offset costs through reduced inpatient utilization (see CMS MMRR, 2014)

SAMHSA recognizes peer support as a key principle of recovery and has acknowledged individual peer support models (e.g., WRAP) in its former NREPP evidence registry. SAMHSA's NREPP was discontinued in 2018. A categorical "SAMHSA-designated EBP since 2011" claim for peer support generally is not confirmed by current authoritative sources and should not be stated without citation to a specific program.

## State Variation and Innovation

| State | Innovation |
|---|---|
| Georgia | First state to receive Medicaid reimbursement for peer support as a statewide Rehabilitation Option service in **July 1999** (not 2001); GMHCN began the first Medicaid-billable CPS training program in 2001 [Peers for Progress; ASPE HHS] |
| NC | Peer Support Specialists covered under BH I/DD Tailored Plans and Standard Plans; CPSS certified by DMHDDSUS; recertification every 2 years [NCDHHS, 2024] |
| California | SB 803 (signed September 25, 2020) required DHCS to establish statewide certification requirements; Medi-Cal Peer Support Services benefit effective **July 1, 2022** (not at signing) [CA DHCS, 2022] |
| New York | Peer-run Advocacy Centers; family peer support specialist separately certified |
| Massachusetts | Certified Peer Specialist (CPS); included in community mental health center benefits |

## Population-Specific Applications

- **SMI**: peer mentors in ACT teams; peer support in supportive housing programs
- **SUD/OUD**: recovery coaches in emergency departments (ED-based peer recovery navigation); jail diversion peer support
- **Youth and Transition-Age**: near-peer support; youth advisory councils
- **Maternal BH**: peer support for perinatal mental health and SUD
- **Veterans**: VA operates Peer Support Specialist program (separate from Medicaid); some duals access both
