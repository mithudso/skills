## 1. Overview and Statutory Basis

**North Carolina Drug Treatment Courts** (now officially called **NC Recovery Courts**) were established by the NC General Assembly in 1995 under **GS Chapter 7A, Article 62** (GS 7A-790 et seq.).[^1] The program sits within the Administrative Office of the Courts (AOC).

> Note on numbering: The prior brief description cited "GS 7A-272" — this is incorrect. The enabling statute is **Article 62 of Chapter 7A**, codified at GS 7A-790 through 7A-801. Always cite the Article 62 sections, not 7A-272.

As of 2025, NC operates approximately **69–71 treatment courts** across **31–32 counties**, with 17 more planned.[^2] The program handles **nonviolent felony and multiple misdemeanor offenders** with moderate-to-severe substance use disorders who would otherwise face significant incarceration.

---

## 2. Court Types (NC Recovery Court Program)

The renamed "Recovery Court" umbrella covers six distinct court types:[^2][^3]

| Court Type | Target Population | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Adult Treatment Court** | Nonviolent felony/misdemeanor offenders with SUD | Most common type; general eligibility |
| **DWI Treatment Court** | Repeat DWI offenders with alcohol/drug dependence | Uses the Ten Guiding Principles of DWI Courts; DWI under GS 20-138.1 ineligible for deferred prosecution/dismissal route |
| **Mental Health Court** | Individuals with severe persistent mental illness | Addresses co-occurring MH/SUD |
| **Veterans Treatment Court** | Military veterans with SUD, PTSD, TBI, or mental health disorders | Connects veterans with VA benefits and treatment |
| **Family Treatment Court** | Parents with SUD involved in child welfare/DSS cases | Interfaces with CPS/DSS; see `nc-sud-family-law.md` |
| **Juvenile Treatment Court** | Youth adjudicated in delinquency court with SUD/MH | Separate juvenile justice track |

## 3. Therapeutic Jurisprudence Model

NC Recovery Courts operate on **therapeutic jurisprudence** principles — a problem-solving approach replacing the traditional adversarial model:[^2]

- **Interdisciplinary team:** Judge, prosecutor, defense attorney, probation officer, treatment provider, and recovery coach/peer specialist collaborate in pre-court staffing meetings.
- **Frequent judicial contact:** Participants appear before the judge **every two weeks** for 12–18 months. The judge's ongoing involvement is central to the model.
- **Graduated sanctions and incentives:** Non-punitive responses to relapse; escalating sanctions only for non-engagement.
- **Holistic focus:** Addresses employment, housing, education, transportation, and family as barriers to recovery alongside substance use treatment.
- **Drug testing:** Participants undergo drug testing **at least twice weekly**.[^2]

## 4. Eligibility Criteria

Eligibility is determined by both **legal criteria** and **clinical criteria**:[^1][^4]

### Legal Eligibility

- Charged with a **misdemeanor or felony** that was committed as a result of drug use or addiction.
- Eligible for **intermediate or community punishment** under NC Structured Sentencing (typically H and I felony classes on the sentencing grid).
- Generally limited to **nonviolent offenders** — violent offenses usually disqualify.
- Must be eligible under **Article 62, Chapter 7A** (GS 7A-790 et seq.).
- For the deferred prosecution/dismissal track: **prosecutor consent required**.

### Clinical Eligibility

- Acknowledges a **personal chemical dependency or history of substance abuse** (including alcohol).
- Typically assessed at **moderate-to-severe SUD** level (ASAM criteria).
- Co-occurring mental health conditions may route to Mental Health Court instead.

### Disqualifiers (Common)

- Prior violent felony convictions (varies by court).
- Sex offender registration requirements (most courts).
- Pending federal charges.
- Note: DWI under GS 20-138.1 **cannot use the discharge/dismissal track** (though DWI courts exist as separate programs).

## 5. Program Phases and Structure

Most adult treatment courts use a **multi-phase structure** (specific phase names vary by court):[^2][^4]

| Phase | Approximate Duration | Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 (Orientation/Stabilization) | Months 1–3 | Most intensive — frequent court appearances, daily/near-daily programming, individual therapy weekly |
| Phase 2 (Treatment) | Months 4–8 | Continued therapy, group programming, employment/education focus |
| Phase 3 (Transition) | Months 9–12 | Reduced court contact, relapse prevention planning, community integration |
| Phase 4/Aftercare | Post-graduation | 6–12 months of continued monitoring (varies by court) |

**Total duration:** Most participants graduate in **approximately 12 months** (can extend to 18 months or longer).[^2]

**Sanctions and incentives:** The judge uses a graduated system. A single positive drug test does not automatically result in termination — the team assesses engagement, honesty, and overall trajectory.

## 6. Legal Outcomes — Deferred Prosecution and Discharge/Dismissal

Two statutory mechanisms under **GS 15A-1341** govern what happens to criminal charges:[^5]

### Pre-Plea Deferred Prosecution (GS 15A-1341(a2))
- Defendant enters treatment court **before** pleading guilty.
- Requires **prosecutor's consent**.
- Upon successful completion: **charges dismissed**.
- Eligible for expunction under GS 15A-146 (general dismissal expunction statute).

### Post-Plea Discharge and Dismissal (GS 15A-1341(a5))
- Defendant pleads guilty, then enters treatment court under suspended sentence.
- Upon successful completion: **plea is discharged and charges dismissed**.
- Also eligible for expunction under GS 15A-146.

**Critical exception:** Impaired driving under **GS 20-138.1** is **ineligible** for either dismissal track — though DWI courts still exist as a separate supervised probation program.[^5]

### AOC Forms
AOC forms AOC-CR-633A through 633F govern these proceedings, with variations based on offense date.

## 7. Interaction with NC Structured Sentencing

Drug treatment court is an **intermediate punishment** under NC Structured Sentencing:[^4]

- Available when the defendant's offense class and prior record level place them in the **intermediate punishment range** on the NC sentencing grid (or when the court has discretion to impose intermediate punishment).
- Court suspends the active sentence and places defendant on supervised probation **with drug treatment court as a condition**.
- **Not available** for defendants who must receive an active prison sentence under structured sentencing (unless post-conviction program in corrections system).
- Successful completion avoids prison; failure typically results in activation of the suspended sentence.
- Prior record level is calculated the same way for treatment court participants — participation does not reduce prior record points, though dismissal upon completion prevents the conviction from adding to the record.

## 8. LME-MCO and Treatment Funding

**Local Management Entities / Managed Care Organizations (LME-MCOs)** are the administrative gatekeepers for behavioral health treatment in NC:[^6]

- LME-MCOs (Alliance Health, Partners Health Management, Trillium Health Resources, Vaya Health) administer Medicaid behavioral health benefits and state-funded services.
- Drug treatment court teams coordinate with the local LME-MCO to **connect participants to treatment services**.
- The LME-MCO provides input to ensure adequate and appropriate treatment services are available for court participants.
- Treatment cost for Medicaid-enrolled participants is covered through Medicaid managed care (Tailored Plans as of July 1, 2024, for those with severe SUD).
- Non-Medicaid participants may access state-funded SUD services through LME-MCOs.
- **Funding gap:** Treatment courts are chronically underfunded — courts must often piece together federal Bureau of Justice Assistance (BJA) grants, state appropriations, and LME-MCO dollars.[^6]

## 9. Participant Rights and Termination

### Voluntary Entry
Participation is **voluntary** — defendants choose to enter the program rather than proceeding to trial or accepting a standard plea.

### Rights During Participation
- Participants retain constitutional rights, though drug testing is a mandatory program condition.
- Right to **legal representation** throughout the process.
- Right to appeal termination from the program (see below).

### Sanctions, Incentives, and Termination
- The drug court team (judge + interdisciplinary team) makes decisions on sanctions and incentives.
- **The judge is the final decision-maker** when the team cannot reach consensus.
- Termination is not automatic on a single positive test — the team assesses the full picture.
- Terminated participants return to the regular court docket and face activation of suspended sentence or trial on original charges.

### LME-MCO Service Appeals
- Medicaid participants have the right to **appeal any denial, reduction, or termination of behavioral health services** by the LME-MCO.
- First step: **Reconsideration** (required before formal appeal).
- Disability Rights NC provides assistance with Medicaid LME-MCO appeals.[^6]

## 10. County Coverage

**verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

NC Recovery Courts operate in approximately **31–32 counties** (of NC's 100 counties).[^3] Not all court types exist in all covered counties. Counties without treatment courts must rely on standard probation with treatment conditions.

## 11. Anti-Patterns / Common Misunderstandings

**"Drug treatment court is a diversion program that avoids all criminal process"**
Not entirely accurate. Most NC drug treatment courts involve either a deferred prosecution (pre-plea) or a suspended sentence after a guilty plea. Participants are under court supervision and subject to sanctions; it is not a free pass.[^5]

**"Any drug charge qualifies"**
No. Eligibility depends on the offense class, prior record level (must qualify for intermediate or community punishment), prosecutorial consent for deferred prosecution, and whether the offense was drug-related. Violent offenders are typically excluded.[^1]

**"A DWI conviction can be dismissed through drug treatment court"**
No. GS 20-138.1 DWI offenses are specifically excluded from the deferred prosecution and discharge/dismissal tracks under GS 15A-1341. However, DWI Treatment Courts exist as a separate program to address repeat DWI offenders with a focus on behavioral change and reduced recidivism.[^5]

**"Relapse means automatic termination"**
No. A single positive drug test triggers a team staffing to assess engagement and trajectory. Graduated sanctions (increased reporting, brief jail sanctions) are used before termination, consistent with the therapeutic jurisprudence model.[^2]

**"Completion means the record is automatically expunged"**
No. Successful completion leads to dismissal of charges. Expunction requires a separate petition under GS 15A-146. See `nc-criminal-defense` references for expungement process.[^5]
