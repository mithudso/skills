## 1. Overview — What is a Peer Support Specialist?

A **Peer Support Specialist (PSS)** is an individual with **lived experience** of serious mental illness and/or substance use disorder who uses that experience to support others in their recovery journey. In NC, the role is formalized through the **NC Certified Peer Support Specialist (CPSS)** credential.

**Core distinction:** A CPSS draws on personal recovery experience as a primary tool — unlike licensed clinicians who use formal clinical training. This "experiential authority" makes CPSS unique in the behavioral health workforce and produces outcomes that clinical services alone do not achieve (engagement, hope, stigma reduction).

---

## 2. NC Statutory and Regulatory Framework

NC's CPSS program operates under the NC DHHS Division of Mental Health, Developmental Disabilities, and Substance Use Services (MH/DD/SUS).[^1]

There is **no standalone NC statute** specifically governing CPSS certification (unlike licensed clinical professions such as LCSW or LPC). The CPSS program is established through:
- NCDHHS administrative policy and program guidance
- NC Medicaid service definitions and clinical coverage policies
- LME-MCO provider contracts that specify CPSS requirements for Medicaid billing
- The CPSS service definition issued by NCDHHS MH/DD/SUS

The Division of Mental Health, DD, and SUS requires that **only certified CPSS** may deliver peer support services billed through Medicaid or state funds.[^1]

---

## 3. NC Certified Peer Support Specialist (CPSS) Program — UNC Partnership

The **NC Certified Peer Support Specialist (NCCPSS) Program** is a partnership between:[^2]
- NC DHHS Division of Mental Health, Developmental Disabilities, and Substance Use Services
- UNC-Chapel Hill School of Social Work

The program trains, certifies, and supports CPSS workforce members statewide. The UNC School of Social Work administers the program under NCDHHS policy direction.

**Program website:** pss.unc.edu

## 4. Certification Requirements

**verified-as-of: 2026-06-22**

To become a NC Certified Peer Support Specialist:[^2][^3]

### Eligibility Requirements
- **Lived experience** of serious mental illness and/or substance use disorder (self-identified)
- At least **18 years old**
- In self-identified recovery (general requirement; specific abstinence timeframes may be addressed in policy)
- No disqualifying criminal history per NCDHHS background check policy

### Training Requirements
- Complete an **approved CPSS training course** — minimum **50 hours** total:
  - At least **40 hours of face-to-face instruction** (in-person or synchronous virtual)
  - **10 hours of pre-work or homework**
- The **NCCPSS Program Foundations Course** is the standardized curriculum (available through UNC and approved training organizations)
- **3 hours of Ethics Training** (required as of July 1, 2024 — see Section 5)

### Application Process
- Submit application to the NCCPSS Program through UNC
- Include proof of training completion
- Pass background check
- Submit to and receive approval from NCDHHS

---

## 5. 2024 Ethics Training Requirement

Effective **July 1, 2024**, the NCCPSS Program added an **ethics training requirement**:[^3]

- New applicants must submit **3 hours of ethics training** as part of their application
- The 3 ethics hours count toward the **20 additional training hours** required for full certification (beyond the core 50-hour foundations course)
- Existing certified CPSS must complete ethics training as part of their recertification cycle

---

## 6. Recertification

NC CPSS certification must be renewed **every two years**.[^2]

**Recertification requirements:**
- Complete required continuing education hours (verify current hours at pss.unc.edu/certification/recertification)
- Satisfy any updated ethics training requirements
- Maintain employment or volunteer status in peer support
- Submit recertification application through the NCCPSS Program

**Important:** Lapse in certification means the individual can no longer bill Medicaid or state funds for peer support services until recertified.

## 7. Scope of Practice

**What a CPSS does (within scope):**[^1][^2]
- Provide peer support, mentoring, and encouragement based on shared lived experience
- Assist individuals in identifying personal recovery goals and resources
- Model recovery and self-advocacy
- Support engagement with treatment services and community resources
- Conduct outreach and engagement for hard-to-reach populations
- Provide practical assistance navigating the behavioral health system
- Facilitate peer support groups

**What a CPSS does NOT do (outside scope):**
- Provide clinical assessment, diagnosis, or therapy (requires licensed clinician)
- Make clinical recommendations about medications
- Function as a case manager or provide case management activities (unless separately certified)
- Supervise other clinical staff

A CPSS must be **employed by or contracting with a licensed provider organization** to deliver Medicaid-billable peer support services. CPSS cannot independently bill Medicaid.

## 8. Medicaid Billing and LME-MCO Requirements

**Peer support services are billable under NC Medicaid** when delivered by a certified CPSS through a licensed provider organization.[^1]

**Billing structure:**
- Peer support services are billed through the **LME-MCO** (for Tailored Plan / state-funded members) or Standard Plan
- **Specific billing questions** must be directed to the member's LME-MCO (Alliance, Partners, Trillium, or Vaya)
- Service must be documented in the member's Person-Centered Plan (PCP) or treatment plan
- Supervision by a licensed clinical supervisor is required per NCDHHS policy

**Medicaid clinical coverage policy:** Peer Support services are defined in the NCDHHS service definitions; see the relevant CCP on the NC Medicaid clinical coverage policy page. Services are billable per ASAM principles where applicable (CPSS commonly supports SAIOP 2.1 and outpatient 1.0 level members).

**Note on 1115 Waiver:** The NC 1115 SUD Demonstration Waiver includes peer support services as a component of the expanded SUD service array — see `nc-behavioral-health-medicaid.md` for Medicaid context.

## 9. CPSS in SUD Treatment — Specific Context

CPSS are particularly impactful in SUD treatment settings:[^2]

**SUD-specific CPSS roles:**
- Supporting medication-assisted treatment (MAT) engagement and adherence (working alongside OTP and OBOT providers — see `nc-mat-otp-law.md`)
- Harm reduction outreach — connecting people to clean supplies, naloxone, and treatment referrals (see `nc-harm-reduction-laws.md`)
- Recovery Court participant support — CPSS are embedded in many NC Recovery Courts as recovery coaches (see `nc-drug-treatment-courts.md`)
- Bridging from acute detox/residential to outpatient care — a common drop-off point where peer support significantly improves retention
- Family support — connecting family members to Al-Anon, NAR-Anon, and other resources

**CPSS credential in drug treatment courts:** Recovery Courts often use certified peer support specialists or recovery coaches as part of the interdisciplinary team. Some courts require CPSS certification; others accept equivalent credentials. Verify with the specific local court.

## 10. Recovery Coach vs. CPSS — Distinction

| Feature | CPSS | Recovery Coach |
|---|---|---|
| NC credential | NCDHHS CPSS certification via UNC | Varies; no separate NC certification (recovery coaches typically hold CPSS or similar) |
| Medicaid billing | Yes (with licensed provider org) | Only if also a CPSS |
| Primary setting | Behavioral health provider organizations, LME-MCO networks | Recovery community organizations, peer centers, drug courts |
| Supervision requirement | Licensed clinical supervisor | Varies by employer |
| Focus | Mental health AND SUD recovery (broader) | Often primarily SUD recovery |

In NC, "recovery coach" is commonly used for CPSS working in SUD-specific settings, particularly in drug treatment courts and recovery community organizations. The underlying credential is typically the CPSS certification.

## 11. Anti-Patterns / Common Misunderstandings

**"CPSS can be hired as an independent contractor and bill Medicaid directly"**
No. NC Medicaid requires that CPSS deliver services through a licensed provider organization that is enrolled in Medicaid and contracted with the relevant LME-MCO. CPSS cannot independently bill.[^1]

**"Lived experience in recovery is enough to work as a CPSS; training is optional"**
No. The formal certification — including 50+ hours of training, ethics training, and application through the NCCPSS/UNC program — is required to hold the CPSS credential and deliver Medicaid-billable peer support services.[^3]

**"CPSS is a licensed clinical profession in NC"**
No. CPSS is a certification, not a clinical license. CPSS do not diagnose, treat, or provide clinical therapy. They operate within a defined scope of practice that is distinct from (and complementary to) licensed clinical services.

**"Once certified, CPSS never need to renew"**
No. Recertification is required every two years. Allowing certification to lapse disqualifies the individual from delivering Medicaid-billable peer support services until recertified.[^2]
