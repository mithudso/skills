# NC Bail & Bond — Pretrial Release Framework (GS 15A-531 through 15A-547)

> **Educational only, NOT legal advice.** verified-as-of: 2026-06-22

North Carolina's pretrial release system is governed by Article 26 of Chapter 15A (Criminal Procedure Act). The system underwent significant reform effective December 1, 2025 under **Session Law 2025-93 (Iryna's Law)**, which eliminated written promises to appear and expanded mandatory judicial review for violent offenses.

---

## Overview

Pretrial release governs whether and under what conditions a person arrested in NC may be released from custody before their case concludes. GS 15A-531 through 15A-547 establish definitions, types of release, bail-setting procedure, bail bondsman regulations, bond forfeiture, and modification/revocation procedures.

**Constitutional baseline:** The 8th Amendment prohibits excessive bail (*Stack v. Boyle*, 342 U.S. 1, 1951); preventive detention is permissible when justified by safety interests (*United States v. Salerno*, 481 U.S. 739, 1987). NC Constitution Art. I § 27 also prohibits excessive bail.

---

## 1. Definitions (GS 15A-531)

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Bail bond** | An undertaking by the defendant (or surety) to appear in court as required, upon penalty of forfeiting bail to the State |
| **Unsecured appearance bond** | Defendant signs a promise to pay if they fail to appear; no upfront payment or collateral required |
| **Cash-secured bond** | Appearance bond secured by a cash deposit equal to the full bond amount |
| **Mortgage-secured bond** | Appearance bond secured by a mortgage under GS 58-74-5 |
| **Surety bond** | Appearance bond secured by at least one solvent surety (a bail bondsman or accommodation bondsman) |
| **Accommodation bondsman** | A NC resident 18+ who signs a bond as surety for a defendant without compensation, based on personal relationship |
| **Professional bondsman** | A licensed person who deposits cash or securities with the state insurance commissioner as collateral for bail bonds |
| **Bail agent** | A licensed surety representative appointed by an insurance company to execute bail bonds |
| **House arrest with electronic monitoring** | Pretrial release requiring the defendant to remain at their residence except for court-approved activities (employment, counseling, education); defendant wears an electronic monitoring device |

## 2. Types of Pretrial Release (Post-December 1, 2025)

Under GS 15A-534(a) as amended by SL 2025-93, a judicial official must impose **at least one** of these four release types (written promise to appear was **eliminated** effective December 1, 2025):

### A. Unsecured Appearance Bond
- Defendant signs a promise to pay a specified amount if they fail to appear
- No money or property deposited upfront; no co-signer required
- Least restrictive financial condition after written promise (now abolished)
- Used for lower-risk defendants and minor offenses

### B. Custody Release
- Defendant released into the custody of a designated person or organization
- Custodian must agree to supervise the defendant and assure appearance
- Suitable when financial bond is inappropriate but supervision is available
- Example: release to a pretrial services program, family member, or treatment facility

### C. Secured Appearance Bond
- Defendant (or a surety on their behalf) must post security in one of three forms:
  1. **Cash deposit** of the full bond amount
  2. **Mortgage** under GS 58-74-5
  3. **Solvent surety** (bail bondsman or accommodation bondsman)
- Most common mechanism; bail bondsman charges a non-refundable premium (typically 10–15% of bond amount)
- A surety-signed bond is treated the same as a cash deposit for most purposes

### D. House Arrest with Electronic Monitoring
- A form of secured bond; requires execution of a secured bond plus ankle monitor or GPS device
- Department of Adult Correction or pretrial services program supervises compliance
- For violent offense defendants post-Iryna's Law: mandatory if certain prior record thresholds are met

## 3. Bail-Setting Procedure (GS 15A-534)

### Who Sets Bail

| Situation | Authority |
|---|---|
| Initial appearance (most cases) | Magistrate or clerk |
| Capital offense charges | Judge only (magistrate has NO authority) |
| Domestic violence charges (GS 15A-534.1) | Judge only (within 48 hrs; magistrate acts if no judge available after 48 hrs) |
| Pretrial Integrity Act violent offenses (post-10/1/2023) | Judge only (within 48 hrs; magistrate acts if no judge available after 48 hrs) |
| Defendant already on pretrial release (new arrest) | Judge only |
| Motor vehicle impairment offenses (new Iryna's Law category) | Judge first; magistrate after 48 hrs |

### Factors for Setting Conditions (GS 15A-534(c))

The judicial official must consider:
- Nature and circumstances of the offense
- Weight of the evidence against the defendant
- Defendant's **criminal history** (required post-Iryna's Law; must obtain criminal history report)
- Family ties and community relationships
- Employment status and history
- Length of residency in the community
- History of failure to appear
- Defendant's **housing situation** (new post-Iryna's Law factor)
- Financial resources and ability to post bond
- Any danger the defendant poses to the alleged victim or community

### Presumptions

| Offense Type | Presumption |
|---|---|
| Non-violent offense | Unsecured bond or custody release presumed appropriate unless court finds release will not assure appearance, poses danger, or risks evidence destruction |
| Violent offense (post-Iryna's Law) | **Rebuttable presumption** that no condition will assure appearance and community safety; if released, secured bond or house arrest required |
| Capital offense | Judge's complete discretion whether to allow any release |

### Written Findings Required (Post-Iryna's Law)
For defendants with 3+ prior misdemeanors within 10 years, or charged with violent offenses, the judicial official must document **written findings** explaining conditions imposed.

## 4. Additional Conditions of Release (GS 15A-534(a1))

Beyond the primary release type, a judicial official may impose:
- Travel restrictions
- Restrictions on associations, conduct, or place of abode
- Abstinence from alcohol (with continuous monitoring approved by Department of Adult Correction; violations reported to district attorney)
- No-contact orders with alleged victims
- Fingerprinting or DNA collection if required by statute and not yet completed
- Any other condition "reasonably necessary" to assure appearance and community safety

---

## 5. Domestic Violence Bond Conditions (GS 15A-534.1)

Applies when defendant is charged with:
- Assault, stalking, or communicating a threat against a spouse, former spouse, cohabitant (current or former), or dating partner
- A felony against any such person
- Domestic criminal trespass
- Violation of a Chapter 50B (Domestic Violence) protective order

**Key requirements:**
- Only a **judge** sets bail (not a magistrate); magistrate may act only after 48-hour hold expires
- Judge must obtain and consider the defendant's **criminal history report** before setting conditions
- If immediate release poses danger of injury to victim or risk of intimidation, judge may **retain defendant in custody** while determining conditions

**Specific conditions available under GS 15A-534.1:**
1. Stay away from victim's home, school, business, or workplace
2. Refrain from assaulting, beating, molesting, or wounding the victim
3. Refrain from removing, damaging, or injuring victim's identified property
4. Child visitation only as permitted by existing court order
5. Abstain from alcohol with continuous electronic monitoring

**Safe release protocol:** Before any release, a law enforcement officer must verify the defendant understands the conditions; the victim must be notified of the release when possible.

## 6. Preventive Detention & No-Bail Scenarios (GS 15A-533)

### Capital Offenses
A judge has **complete discretion** whether to allow any pretrial release for capital offenses (first-degree murder where death penalty may be sought). Magistrates and clerks have **no authority** to set bail for capital charges.

### Expanded Preventive Detention (Post-October 1, 2023 — Pretrial Integrity Act; extended by Iryna's Law)

Judge has discretion whether to set any conditions for defendants charged with:
- First- and second-degree murder (and attempts)
- First- and second-degree kidnapping
- Forcible rape and sexual offenses (multiple degrees)
- Statutory rape and sexual offenses
- Human trafficking
- Assault with deadly weapon with intent to kill inflicting serious injury
- Discharging firearm into occupied property
- First-degree burglary
- First-degree arson
- Armed robbery

**48-Hour Rule:** In mandatory judge-review cases, if a judge has not acted within **48 hours of arrest**, the magistrate must then set conditions of pretrial release.

### Rebuttable Presumptions Against Release
For defendants charged with certain drug trafficking, gang-related, or firearm offenses meeting prior conviction criteria, there is a statutory presumption that no condition will assure appearance and safety. Defendants must rebut this presumption to obtain release.

### Mental Health Involuntary Commitment (New — Iryna's Law)
If a defendant charged with a violent offense was previously involuntarily committed to a mental health facility, or if the judicial official believes the defendant poses a danger to themselves or others, the official must initiate involuntary commitment proceedings instead of standard pretrial release.

## 7. Bond Modification & Reduction Hearings (GS 15A-538, 15A-539)

### Defendant's Motion to Modify (GS 15A-538)
- Defendant may move at any time for modification of pretrial release conditions
- Motion should generally be in writing; oral motions may be accepted at certain procedural stages
- District court judge modifies orders before trial; superior court judge modifies for superior court cases
- **Rules of evidence do not apply** at pretrial release hearings (GS 15A-534(g))

### Prosecutor's Motion to Modify (GS 15A-539)
- Prosecutor may apply to district or superior court judge for modification or revocation of a release order
- Prosecutor **may not** seek ex parte revocation or bond increase
- Standards: showing that conditions are inadequate to assure appearance or protect the community

### Source of Funds Hearing
- A judge may, on State motion or sua sponte, conduct a hearing into the source of money or property offered as bond security
- If State proves by **preponderance of the evidence** that the source will not reasonably assure appearance, judge may refuse to accept that security

### Post-Conviction Release (GS 15A-536)
- After conviction in superior court but before sentencing: judge may release defendant on same types of bonds, with or without conditions
- After sentencing: release pending appeal governed by different standards

## 8. Bail Bondsmen in NC — Licensing & Regulation (NC DOI)

NC Department of Insurance regulates three bail bondsman license types:

### Surety Bail Bondsman License
- **Age:** 21+
- **Education:** High school diploma; 12 hours approved pre-licensing education
- **Application:** Through National Insurance Producer Registry (NIPR) electronically; includes fingerprinting
- **Exam:** Pearson Vue testing center; state licensing exam
- **Fee:** ~$311 application fee + $51.50 exam fee (2025 rates)
- **Validity:** 2-year license
- **Processing time:** 30–45 days from complete application + fingerprint results; license issued 10–12 business days after passing exam
- **Endorsement:** Must be endorsed by a professional bail agent

### Professional Bondsman License
- Must deposit cash or securities with NC Commissioner of Insurance as collateral
- Subject to ongoing regulatory oversight and financial requirements

### Bail Runner License
- Works under supervision of a licensed bail bondsman
- Lower threshold requirements than full bondsman license

**Key regulatory rules:**
- Bondsmen may NOT pay or offer anything of value to referral sources (court officials, jail staff)
- Premium charged to defendant is typically 10–15% of bond amount (non-refundable)
- Bondsman may surrender a defendant to the court (GS 15A-540) before forfeiture to cancel liability on the bond

## 9. Bond Forfeiture & Remission (GS 15A-544.1 through 15A-544.8, 15A-547.1)

### When Forfeiture Is Entered (GS 15A-544.3)
Forfeiture is entered when the defendant fails to appear and the court announces the forfeiture in open court. Entry must be made within 150 days of the failure to appear; a written order is issued.

### Notice Requirements (GS 15A-544.4)
Within 30 days of forfeiture entry, the court must send notice to:
- The defendant at their last known address
- Each surety who signed the bond (bail bondsman)
Notice triggers the time period for setting aside forfeiture.

### Setting Aside Forfeiture (GS 15A-544.5)
Surety or defendant may apply to set aside forfeiture within **150 days** of the notice date. Grounds include:
- Defendant surrendered to court or law enforcement
- Defendant's incarceration in NC or another jurisdiction
- Defendant's serious illness or incapacity
- Defendant's death
- Defendant's inability to appear due to a condition outside their control
- "Any other reason the court finds just and proper" (catch-all)

Setting aside is **mandatory** (not discretionary) if a listed statutory ground is shown.

### Final Judgment (GS 15A-544.6)
If forfeiture is not set aside within the 150-day window, the court enters a final judgment of forfeiture — enforceable as a civil judgment against the bondsman.

### Relief from Final Judgment (GS 15A-544.8)
Even after final judgment, a motion for relief may be granted if the defendant is surrendered and the court finds substantial justice requires relief. Courts have discretion; typically a showing of good faith by the bondsman is required.

### Remission After Non-Incarceration Sentence (GS 15A-547.1)
If a defendant is convicted and sentenced to community punishment or intermediate punishment (not active incarceration), the court may remit (return) bail bond funds to the surety. This recognizes that community supervision has replaced pretrial detention.

## 10. Failure to Appear (GS 15A-543)

Criminal offense in NC: A defendant who willfully fails to appear as required while on pretrial release commits:
- **Misdemeanor** FTA: if the underlying charge is a misdemeanor → Class 1 misdemeanor
- **Felony** FTA: if the underlying charge is a felony → Class I felony

FTA offense is separate from bond forfeiture (a civil consequence) and from any underlying charge.

## 11. Iryna's Law (SL 2025-93) — Key Changes Summary

Effective December 1, 2025 (most provisions):

| Change | Pre-Iryna's Law | Post-Iryna's Law |
|---|---|---|
| Written promise to appear | Permitted | **Eliminated** |
| Criminal history review | Not always required | **Mandatory** before setting conditions |
| Housing situation | Not a required factor | **Must be considered** |
| Violent offense release | No specific presumption | **Rebuttable presumption against release**; if released, secured bond or house arrest required |
| Written findings | Limited circumstances | Required for defendants with 3+ misdemeanors in 10 years or violent offense charges |
| Involuntary commitment | Not linked to bail | If violent offense + prior commitment or danger shown, judicial official must initiate commitment |
| Magistrate discipline | Existing procedures | Expanded disciplinary authority added |

*Named for Iryna Zarutska, killed in 2024 by an assailant who had been released on a written promise to appear after a prior misdemeanor arrest.*

## 12. Constitutional Constraints

### 8th Amendment (Federal)
- **Excessive bail prohibited:** *Stack v. Boyle* (342 U.S. 1, 1951): bail set higher than reasonably calculated to assure appearance is excessive; must be individualized
- **Preventive detention permitted:** *United States v. Salerno* (481 U.S. 739, 1987): bail may be denied entirely for compelling government interests (public safety); the Excessive Bail Clause does not guarantee bail in every case
- **Financial ability is a factor** in assessing whether bail is excessive

### NC Constitution Art. I § 27
- "Excessive bail shall not be required"
- Mirrors 8th Amendment; NC courts interpret consistent with federal doctrine
- Habeas corpus remedy preserved under GS 15A-547 for persons detained on allegedly excessive or unlawful bail

## 13. Procedure Flow — NC Pretrial Release

```
ARREST
   │
   ▼
INITIAL APPEARANCE (magistrate or judge)
   │
   ├─ Non-violent / non-capital offense → Magistrate may set conditions
   │   └─ Unsecured bond, custody release, or secured bond
   │
   ├─ Violent offense (post-10/1/2023) / DV charge → 48-hr hold for judge
   │   └─ Judge sets conditions; if no judge within 48 hrs → magistrate
   │
   └─ Capital offense → Judge only; magistrate cannot act
           │
           ▼
    CONDITIONS SET → Defendant released or detained
           │
           ├─ Modification available: Defendant moves (GS 15A-538) or
           │   State moves (GS 15A-539) before same-level judge
           │
           ├─ Failure to appear → Bond forfeiture + FTA criminal charge
           │
           └─ Compliance → Bond discharged upon case resolution
```

## Anti-Patterns / Common Errors

- **Confusing bond forfeiture with FTA criminal charge:** Forfeiture is a civil action against the bondsman's security; the FTA is a separate criminal charge against the defendant — both may occur simultaneously
- **Assuming magistrate can set bail for DV or violent offenses:** Post-2023, magistrates must hold these defendants for up to 48 hours pending judge review; magistrate acts only if no judge acts within 48 hours
- **Missing the written promise abolition:** As of December 1, 2025, written promises to appear are no longer a valid release type in NC
- **Treating rebuttable presumption as absolute bar:** For violent offenses, the presumption against release can be rebutted; defendants retain the constitutional right to challenge excessive bail
- **Ignoring premium economics:** A bail bondsman premium (typically 10–15%) is non-refundable even if charges are dismissed or defendant is acquitted — this is a frequent source of client confusion
- **Conflating "bond reduction" with modification:** GS 15A-538 allows modification of conditions broadly, not just reduction; conditions can be increased or changed as well as reduced
