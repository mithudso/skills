# Poetry Craft

Hub for the craft of writing poetry: the metrical and formal architecture a
poem can choose (or depart from), and the line-level, sound-level, and
revision techniques that operate inside that architecture. This hub treats
poetry as a **writer's discipline** — what to do on the page — not literary
history or criticism for its own sake, though both references draw on
literary-critical sources where a craft claim's authority depends on them.

A cross-cutting note that applies to both references in this hub: poetry
craft has more live, credentialed disagreement in it than most craft
instruction admits. Whether iambic pentameter is "natural" to English,
whether free verse is ever truly free of pattern, whether "show don't tell"
is an absolute rule, whether the volta is a universal feature of poems, and
how much a poem should be revised are all open disagreements among
practicing poets and critics — not settled law. Both references flag these
points inline rather than picking a winner; treat a single, unhedged claim
about "the" correct way to scan, revise, or structure a poem with the same
skepticism the sources themselves apply.

## When to use this skill

See `whenToUse` above. Typical asks: "does this line scan as iambic
pentameter", "what's the rhyme scheme and volta placement for a Petrarchan
sonnet", "why does this stanza feel flat even though the images are vivid",
"should this line break here or run on", "is this metaphor doing too much
work", "how do I cut this draft down without losing what it's doing".

## When NOT to use this skill

See `whenNotToUse` above and the SKIP line in the description. The
highest-risk collision is `fiction-writing-craft`: if the ask names a
manuscript, character, scene, or narrative POV, it belongs there; if it names
a poem, line, stanza, or poetic form, it belongs here. The second-highest
risk is scope creep into songwriting/lyric craft — poetry craft assumes no
melody, meter-to-music mapping, or performance delivery constraint; treat any
music-paired-verse question as out of scope rather than answering it from
this hub's content.
