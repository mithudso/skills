# Psychoplastogens and Structural Neuroplasticity

## Core Concepts
- **Psychoplastogens**: small molecules that rapidly promote structural and functional neural plasticity — defined by David Olson (UC Davis, 2018); distinguished from classical antidepressants by speed and persistence of effect after single/few doses
- **Structural neuroplasticity**: physical remodeling of synaptic architecture — dendritic spine density, arbor complexity, synaptogenesis — as distinct from mere functional modulation
- **Olson's psychoplastogen hypothesis**: therapeutic benefit in depression, PTSD, and addiction derives primarily from structural remodeling, not subjective psychedelic experience; this separability motivates non-hallucinogenic candidates
- **Two compound classes**: hallucinogenic psychoplastogens (psilocin, LSD, DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, ketamine) and non-hallucinogenic psychoplastogens (tabernanthalog/TBG, zalsupindole/AAZ-A-154/DLX-001)
- **Atrophy hypothesis**: stress and depression cause dendritic atrophy in PFC and hippocampus; plasticity-promoting drugs reverse this, explaining rapid antidepressant effects

## Mechanisms / Biology
- **5-HT2A receptor activation** initiates plasticity cascade; LSD and psilocin also act as positive allosteric modulators at TrkB (BDNF receptor) — ~1,000-fold higher affinity than SSRIs, potentially explaining superior efficacy
- **Three BDNF activation routes**: (1) indirect — 5-HT2A → glutamate release → AMPA/kainate receptor activation; (2) direct — 5-HT2A receptor stimulation triggers BDNF release; (3) hyperdirect — psychedelics directly bind allosteric TrkB site
- **mTOR pathway**: TrkB activation drives mTOR signaling, initiating the protein synthesis required for sustained dendritic spine growth and synaptogenesis; rapamycin (mTOR inhibitor) blocks plasticity in preclinical models
- **Stimulation vs. growth phases**: changes require a stimulation phase (TrkB activation) followed by a growth phase (sustained mTOR + AMPA activation); transient exposures of 15 min–6 h are sufficient to initiate full neuronal growth
- **Tabernanthalog challenge to Olson hypothesis** (Nature Neuroscience, 2025): TBG promotes cortical spinogenesis via 5-HT2A/TrkB/mTOR/AMPA without triggering the glutamate burst or immediate early gene (IEG) activation previously thought essential — IEG activation is not required for plasticity
- **Ketamine mechanism**: NMDA receptor blockade → disinhibition → AMPA receptor activation → BDNF release → TrkB/mTOR signaling; effects emerge within hours vs. weeks for SSRIs

## Clinical Picture
- Target conditions: treatment-resistant depression (TRD), PTSD, and substance use disorders (alcohol, opioid, stimulant)
- Ketamine/esketamine (Spravato): FDA-approved for TRD and MDD with acute suicidal ideation; antidepressant effect within 24 hours in ~50–70% of TRD patients; effect duration 1–2 weeks without maintenance
- Psilocybin: response rates of ~67% in major depression (COMP360 Phase IIb, 2023, 25 mg dose); remission ~29% at 3 weeks
- Systemic BDNF levels: meta-analysis (Molecular Psychiatry, 2024) confirms psychoplastogens elevate serum BDNF in humans, supporting translational validity of preclinical structural findings
- Clinical gap: hallucinogenic psychoplastogens require supervised set/setting and carry abuse/cardiovascular/psychosis risks; non-hallucinogenic candidates aim to eliminate these barriers for scalable outpatient use

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Zalsupindole (DLX-001 / AAZ-A-154)**: Delix Therapeutics; Phase 1 clinical trial for MDD initiated 2025–2026; preclinical data show dendritic arbor complexity equivalent to ketamine; reduces alcohol-, heroin-, and sucrose-seeking in rodent models; no head-twitch response (hallucinogenic proxy)
- **Tabernanthalog (TBG)**: ibogaine-derived, non-hallucinogenic; reduces opioid/alcohol self-administration in mice; promotes cortical spinogenesis; mechanism confirmed via Nature Neuroscience 2025 study
- **Ketamine**: IV racemic ketamine (0.5 mg/kg over 40 min) is gold standard rapid antidepressant in TRD; esketamine (Spravato) intranasal 56–84 mg twice weekly for 4 weeks
- **Therapeutic synergy**: 2025 Frontiers Psychiatry review emphasizes that neuroplasticity from psychoplastogens creates a "critical window" of heightened neural flexibility — structured psychotherapy during this window maximizes durable behavioral change
- **Addiction mechanism relevance**: stress-induced PFC hypofunction and maladaptive striatal plasticity underlie compulsive drug seeking; structural restoration of PFC-to-striatum projections may reset reward circuitry

## Key Facts
- Psychoplastogens can induce spinogenesis within 24 hours vs. 2–4 weeks for SSRIs
- LSD/psilocin bind TrkB allosteric site with ~1,000× greater affinity than fluoxetine
- Transient 15-minute psychoplastogen exposure sufficient to initiate sustained neuronal growth (ACS Pharm Transl Sci)
- TBG does not activate IEGs (c-Fos, FosB) yet produces equivalent structural plasticity to psilocybin — published Nature Neuroscience 2025
- Zalsupindole (AAZ-A-154) entered Phase 1 clinical trials for MDD as of early 2026, the first dedicated non-hallucinogenic psychoplastogen in human trials
