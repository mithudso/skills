# Methamphetamine Recovery: Integrated Clinical Timeline

Synthesizes dopaminergic, noradrenergic, orexin/sleep, cognitive, cardiovascular, and social recovery trajectories across abstinence phases. Each phase has distinct clinical features and decision gates for the meth-narcolepsy differential.

---

## Phase 1 — Acute Withdrawal (0–72 h)

| Domain | What Happens |
|--------|-------------|
| DA/NE | Depletion state: profound fatigue, dysphoria, hypersomnia onset |
| Craving | Peaks 24–48 h; environmental/stress triggers dominant |
| Vitals | Hypertension, tachycardia normalize within hours of last use |
| Psychosis | Clears 1–7 days for most; longer if severe or co-occurring psychotic dx |
| Sleep | REM rebound: intense, vivid dreams; fragmented architecture; total sleep time elevated |

**Clinical note:** Do NOT conduct MSLT here — catecholamine depletion renders results uninterpretable.

---

## Phase 2 — Early Recovery (Days 4–14)

- **Hypersomnia** continues (Mechanism 1 — catecholamine depletion); resolves over 1–3 weeks in most
- **Cognitive fog:** attention, working memory, processing speed all impaired (DA depletion in PFC/striatum)
- **Anhedonia:** blunted reward response; hedonic blunting often prominent — DA deficit state
- **Depression** peaks week 1–2; monitor for suicidality
- **Sleep:** total sleep time still elevated; MSLT validity window does not open until ≥2 weeks

---

## Phase 3 — Early Stabilization (Weeks 2–4)

- Hypersomnia typically resolving (Mechanism 1 clearing); persistent EDS after week 3 warrants reassessment
- Working memory and processing speed show first objective improvements
- Anhedonia and hedonic blunting persist — DAT recovery still early
- Cravings remain high; stress/cue reactivity peak window
- Neuroinflammation biomarkers (MCP-1, ICAM-1) beginning to normalize

**Decision gate — 2 weeks:** MSLT validity window opens. Test now only if EDS persists and acute withdrawal is clearly resolved.

---

## Phase 4 — Sub-Acute Recovery (1–3 Months)

- **DAT recovery:** +10–15% caudate/putamen density by 2–3 months (PET imaging data)
- **Cognition:** verbal fluency, attention, and processing speed accelerating; executive function lags
- **Sleep:** architecture increasingly normal; slow-wave and REM proportions stabilizing
- **Orexin (Mechanism 2):** orexin suppression largely reversing for most; if EDS persists past 6 weeks, track toward 6-month LP decision gate
- **Mood:** stabilizing; clinical MDD requiring pharmacotherapy identifiable now (≥4 weeks abstinent)
- **Cardiovascular:** hypertension resolving, LVEF and cardiac function improving

---

## Phase 5 — Consolidation (3–12 Months)

| Domain | Milestone |
|--------|-----------|
| DAT density | +19% caudate, +16% putamen at 12–17 months (Volkow et al., *J Neurosci* 2001) |
| Cognition | Most functions near-normal 6–12 months, light-moderate users; heavy/long-term users slower |
| Orexin/EDS | If EDS persists at 3 months → CSF orexin LP at 6-month mark (Mechanism 2 vs. 3 gate) |
| Psychosis | Functional recovery typically complete 3–6 months; structural (glia, axonal) recovery slower |
| Mood | Residual anhedonia possible even with partial DAT recovery; striatal metabolism lags DAT |
| Cardiovascular | Cardiac remodeling largely resolved; meth cardiomyopathy may persist in heavy users |
| Social | Recovery capital building: housing, employment, social support — critical relapse buffer |

**Decision gate — 6 months:** LP for CSF orexin if EDS is ongoing. Low orexin-A (<110 pg/mL) indicates Mechanism 2 (LH damage) vs. Mechanism 3 (primary narcolepsy unmasked).

---

## Phase 6 — Long-Term (12+ Months)

- **DAT:** near-normalization within normal range by 12–17 months; recovery rate inversely proportional to years of use
- **Cognition:** frontal executive function may not fully recover in long-term heavy users; other domains approach normal
- **EDS at 12 months:** if persistent EDS with low CSF orexin → likely permanent LH damage → lifelong narcolepsy treatment warranted
- **Relapse vulnerability:** remains elevated; recovery is fragile — stress reactivity, cue-induced craving persist neurobiologically
- **Neuropsychological note:** DAT recovery alone does not guarantee cognitive normalization — downstream metabolic and non-dopaminergic damage co-contributes

---

## Key Decision Gates Summary

| Timepoint | Gate | Action |
|-----------|------|--------|
| < 2 weeks | MSLT validity | Do NOT test — DA depletion confounds results |
| ≥ 2 weeks | MSLT window opens | Test if EDS persists and acute withdrawal resolved |
| 6 months | Orexin LP gate | CSF orexin-A if EDS ongoing; <110 pg/mL = Mechanism 2 LH damage |
| 12 months | Permanence determination | Low CSF orexin at 12 months → permanent LH damage → lifelong narcolepsy Tx |
