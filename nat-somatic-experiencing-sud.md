# Somatic Experiencing (SE) for Substance Use Disorders

**Developer:** Peter Levine, PhD. Core text: *Waking the Tiger: Healing Trauma* (1997); *In an Unspoken Voice* (2010). Training body: Somatic Experiencing International (SEI), SEP (Somatic Experiencing Practitioner) credential.

---

## Core Thesis: Trauma as Stuck Physiology

Levine's central claim: trauma is not the event itself but the **incomplete defensive response** stored in the nervous system. In wild animals, threat activates the full survival sequence — orienting, fight/flight, discharge — and completes. Humans typically abort that sequence (social constraints, overwhelm, perceived immobility) → defensive activation is truncated → the undischarged energy remains mobilized in the body → chronic autonomic nervous system (ANS) dysregulation results.

SE's task is not to revisit trauma cognitively but to **facilitate titrated physiological completion** of the aborted response — allowing the frozen survival energy to discharge through the body.

---

## SE Mechanism in SUD

Chronic stored trauma → persistent ANS dysregulation in one of two directions:

| State | Polyvagal level | Phenomenology | SUD pattern |
|-------|----------------|---------------|-------------|
| **Hyperarousal** | Sympathetic overdrive | Hypervigilance, startle, tension, anxiety, insomnia | Meth, stimulants, alcohol to dampen; perceived as normalizing to already-activated baseline |
| **Hypoarousal/freeze** | Dorsal vagal shutdown | Numbness, dissociation, anhedonia, collapse | Stimulants to escape freeze; opioids to blunt without collapsing |

**Meth-specific pathway:** Meth's stimulant and dopaminergic effects can *feel normalizing* to a hyperaroused trauma survivor — the drug temporarily resolves the ANS mismatch between internal state and external demand. In recovery, the meth is removed but the stored activation resurfaces without the pharmacological buffer → ANS dysregulation re-emerges intact → relapse driver. SE addresses this residual physiological burden directly, rather than relying on the cognitive engagement required by CBT or sustained narrative required by Prolonged Exposure.

## Core SE Techniques

**Interoception:** Patient learns to track internal body sensations (temperature, pressure, movement, tingling) as the primary data stream — shifting attention from cognition/narrative to somatic felt sense.

**Pendulation:** Deliberately alternating between activating sensations (areas of stored trauma tension) and settling sensations (areas of relative safety/groundedness in the body). Creates a rhythm of expansion and contraction; avoids the sustained overwhelm that derails talk-based trauma exposure.

**Titration:** Trauma activation introduced in very small, manageable increments — a "touch and return" approach — rather than full narrative immersion. Prevents flooding, which retraumatizes rather than resolves.

**Somatic resources:** Clinician identifies and anchors sensations of physical groundedness, ease, or safety the patient can already access (a stable foot contact, a relaxed shoulder, a breath with no holding). These become the "settled pole" in pendulation.

**Physiological discharge:** SE tracks and facilitates autonomic discharge — spontaneous tremoring, warmth spreading, deep involuntary breath, subtle postural unwinding, yawning. These are signs of incomplete defensive energy completing, not pathology.

SE does **not** require verbal narration of traumatic content. This is clinically significant for:
- Patients whose trauma is pre-verbal or somatically encoded
- Meth-affected patients with cognitive impairment (working memory, attention, verbal fluency) who cannot sustain narrative engagement
- Patients with severe shame or dissociation for whom narrative triggers avoidance

## Polyvagal Theory Connection

Porges' polyvagal theory provides the neurobiological scaffold for SE. The hierarchy — **social engagement (ventral vagal) → sympathetic mobilization → dorsal vagal freeze/shutdown** — maps directly onto SE's treatment target. SE facilitates movement *up* this hierarchy from freeze or hyperarousal states toward ventral vagal social engagement, where learning, connection, and recovery become accessible. Meth disrupts the social engagement system; SE aims to restore it. The felt-sense polyvagal model (Dana/Porges 2018; Fleury *Treating Trauma and Addiction with the Felt Sense Polyvagal Model*, 2021) explicitly integrates SE with SUD treatment, framing addictive behavior as a resourceful attempt to regulate a body dysregulated by trauma.

## Evidence Base

SE's evidence base is **emerging but limited**; it does not yet meet APA Division 12's "strongly supported" threshold for PTSD.

- **Kuhfuß et al. 2021 (Frontiers in Psychology):** Pilot RCT, SE for chronic low back pain with trauma comorbidity; significant PTSD symptom reduction vs. waitlist. Small N; not SUD-specific.
- **Payne, Levine & Crane-Godreau 2015 (Frontiers in Human Neuroscience):** Theoretical review; SE for complex trauma, ANS dysregulation mechanism.
- **Fleury 2021 (Somatic Psychotherapy Today):** Clinical framework for felt-sense polyvagal model applied to SUD; not an RCT; practitioner-facing.
- **SEI application pages:** SE International lists addiction as a primary application area; no SEI-sponsored RCT in SUD published as of 2026.
- **APA Division 12 status:** SE listed under "emerging evidence" for trauma; not yet "well-established."
- **Evidence gap:** No published RCT in methamphetamine-specific SUD+trauma. Case reports and clinical series (Dietz 2023) exist. Larger SUD trauma trials are underway.

## Comparison to Adjacent Trauma Approaches in SUD

| Approach | Narrative required | Cognitive load | Meth impairment tolerance | SUD evidence level |
|----------|--------------------|---------------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| SE | No | Low | High | Emerging |
| EMDR | Brief image focus | Moderate | Moderate | Growing (2+ meta-analyses) |
| IFS | Moderate (parts dialogue) | Moderate | Moderate | Pilot RCT |
| Prolonged Exposure | Sustained narration | High | Low | Strong for PTSD; SUD dropout high |
| CPT | Written accounts + cognitive worksheets | High | Low | Strong for PTSD; harder with impairment |

SE's primary advantage in meth SUD: **no narrative requirement + low cognitive load** — the two clinical barriers that most frequently undermine CPT and PE in meth-affected populations.

## Clinical Application Notes

- **When to start:** Minimum 2–4 weeks stable abstinence; contraindicated during acute meth withdrawal, active psychosis, or severe dissociation requiring stabilization first. Parkinson's-like meth motor effects (tremor, bradykinesia) may complicate somatic tracking — clinician awareness required; doesn't preclude SE but requires adaptation.
- **Format:** Individual therapy (50–90 min sessions); not designed for group delivery without significant modification. Group SE-informed skills (interoception introduction, grounding via body sensation) can be delivered by trained SUD counselors without full SE protocol.
- **Practitioner requirement:** Full SE protocol requires SEP credential (3-year training program). Brief somatic grounding skills are teachable at lower credential levels.
- **Integration:** SE pairs well with EMDR (SE stabilizes the nervous system; EMDR processes discrete trauma memories), IFS (SE provides the somatic/physiological layer that complements IFS's relational-parts layer), and pharmacotherapy (not contraindicated; can be concurrent with naltrexone, buprenorphine, or MAT).
