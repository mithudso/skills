# MSLT and Narcolepsy Diagnostic Workup

## Core Concepts
- **Multiple Sleep Latency Test (MSLT)**: daytime objective measure of sleep propensity — 4–5 nap opportunities 2 hours apart; mean sleep latency (MSL) calculated across all naps
- **SOREMP**: Sleep-Onset REM Period — REM sleep occurring within 15 minutes of sleep onset on a nap; cardinal marker of narcolepsy-related REM dysregulation
- **Diagnostic threshold**: MSL ≤8 min AND ≥2 SOREMPs = narcolepsy (types 1 and 2 share same MSLT cutoffs)
- **PSG prerequisite**: full-night polysomnography the night before MSLT is mandatory; PSG SOREMP (REM within 15 min of sleep onset) can substitute for one MSLT SOREMP
- **CSF hypocretin-1**: ≤110 pg/mL confirms NT1 independently of MSLT; alternative diagnostic path when MSLT is inconclusive

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Narcolepsy type 1 (NT1): autoimmune destruction of ~70,000 hypothalamic hypocretin/orexin neurons → state-boundary instability — REM intrudes into wakefulness and sleep-onset
- Loss of orexin-A destabilizes the flip-flop switch between wake and REM, producing SOREMPs, cataplexy, hypnagogic hallucinations, and sleep paralysis
- Narcolepsy type 2 (NT2): same MSLT criteria but CSF orexin-1 >110 pg/mL and no cataplexy; mechanism less defined, possibly partial orexin loss
- REM intrusion at sleep onset (SOREMPs) reflects the same brainstem disinhibition that produces cataplexy — active muscle atonia entering conscious states
- MSLT sensitivity for narcolepsy: 70–90%; false negatives occur with sleep deprivation, circadian disruption, or REM-suppressing medications (SSRIs, TCAs must be tapered before testing)

## Clinical Picture
- Average diagnostic delay: **7–10 years** from symptom onset to confirmed narcolepsy diagnosis
- ADHD misdiagnosis mechanism: excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) → inattention, poor executive function, impulsivity — clinically indistinguishable from ADHD without objective sleep testing; stimulants reduce EDS, masking the underlying disorder
- Prevalence: narcolepsy affects ~1 in 2,000 (0.05%) in the US; ADHD affects ~8–11% of children — makes ADHD the statistically favored label when workup is skipped
- NT1 prevalence ~25–50 per 100,000; NT2 similar prevalence but likely underdiagnosed due to variable MSLT reproducibility
- Comorbid ADHD diagnosis occurs in 15–30% of confirmed narcolepsy patients, further clouding the diagnostic picture

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Pre-MSLT requirements (AASM 2021 guidelines)**: ≥2 weeks actigraphy + sleep diary to confirm adequate sleep (≥7 h/night); drug screen to rule out REM-altering substances; PSG the prior night with ≥6 h total sleep — failure to meet any condition invalidates the MSLT
- **PSG + MSLT together** have ~85–90% sensitivity for NT1; standalone MSLT misses ~10–30% of cases
- First-line pharmacotherapy: modafinil/armodafinil (wake-promoting, non-amphetamine); sodium oxybate (gold standard for cataplexy + EDS — consolidates nocturnal sleep); pitolisant (H3 inverse agonist, no DEA scheduling)
- Amphetamine-class stimulants prescribed for presumed ADHD blunt EDS without addressing cataplexy or nocturnal fragmentation — partial response that delays correct diagnosis
- NT1 is irreversible; no neuroprotective therapy currently restores orexin neurons

## Key Facts
- SOREMP cutoff: REM within **15 minutes** of sleep onset (nap or PSG)
- Diagnostic MSL threshold: **≤8 minutes** (population mean ~12–15 min for healthy adults)
- CSF hypocretin-1 ≤**110 pg/mL** (or <1/3 of normal mean) = NT1 regardless of MSLT
- MSLT must be preceded by PSG showing **≥6 hours** TST; otherwise result is unreliable
- NT1 MSLT is highly reproducible (test-retest ~90%); NT2 MSLT reproducibility is poor (~50%) — a negative retest does not rule out NT2
