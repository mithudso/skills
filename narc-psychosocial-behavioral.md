## Core principle
Standard CBT-I sleep restriction is **contraindicated** in narcolepsy — consolidating sleep worsens EDS. Narcolepsy-specific CBT targets cognition and emotion around the disorder, not sleep architecture.

## CBT adaptations for narcolepsy
- **Cognitive restructuring**: challenge catastrophic beliefs about attacks in public, shame around symptoms, hypervigilance to sleepiness cues
- **Behavioral activation**: counteract fatigue-driven avoidance and social withdrawal; graded activity scheduling that respects EDS peaks
- **Sleep-related fears**: address fear of cataplexy triggers → emotional blunting → isolation cycle; expose gradually to positive emotional experiences
- **NOT sleep restriction** — scheduled naps are therapeutic and must be preserved
- Telehealth delivery is effective and reduces burden; guided relaxation/meditation adjuncts have RCT support

## Cataplexy trigger management
- **Identify individual triggers**: laughter most common (~90%); surprise, pride, anger, excitement vary by patient — keep a trigger log
- **Behavioral modulation**: change how humor is expressed (quiet laugh vs. hearty); preview comedic content privately before social exposure
- **Emotional regulation skills**: paced breathing, grounding when trigger emotion rises; purpose is to prevent peak intensity, not suppress emotion entirely
- **Social communication**: scripted disclosure ("I have a neurological condition that affects muscle control when I laugh"); prepare witnesses
- **Intimacy**: cataplexy triggered by sexual arousal/pleasure strains relationships — see Relationship section below

## Occupational accommodations (US)
- **ADA coverage**: narcolepsy qualifies as a disability under ADA Title I; employer must provide reasonable accommodation unless undue hardship
- **FMLA**: intermittent leave for attacks, medication titration, or appointments
- **Key accommodations**: scheduled nap breaks (10–20 min every 3–4 hr); flexible start/end times; remote work option; desk near natural light; standing permission during meetings; low-risk task reassignment during peak EDS windows (early afternoon)
- **Disclosure strategy**: disclose to HR/accommodations coordinator — not required to disclose diagnosis details, only functional limitations
- **Documentation**: neurologist letter specifying functional limitations and recommended accommodations

## Driving safety planning
- Narcolepsy carries elevated crash risk, particularly before adequate medication trial
- **Timing rules**: no driving within 30–60 min of wake-up; avoid post-lunch window (1–3 pm EDS peak); no driving when medication dose is subtherapeutic
- **Medication optimization**: schedule stimulant/wakefulness agent dosing to cover expected driving times
- **State disclosure requirements**: vary by state — some mandate physician reporting; patient should know their state's law
- **Alternatives**: map public transit, rideshare, and carpool options; build emergency pickup plan for unexpected attacks

## Relationship and social impact
- Cataplexy triggered by laughter, playfulness, and sexual pleasure directly impairs intimacy
- **Partner psychoeducation**: explain mechanism (not controllable, not dangerous); normalize that emotional suppression in the relationship is a coping pattern, not rejection
- **Couple-based interventions**: joint sessions with sleep-informed therapist; reframe affected activities; develop shared communication signals during attack
- **Stigma management**: prepare scripted responses for employers, friends, and casual observers; normalize selective disclosure
- **QoL impact**: address social withdrawal, missed events, professional underperformance — all contribute to depression risk

## Support groups and peer community
- **Narcolepsy Network** (narcolepsynetwork.org): peer support, advocacy, provider directory
- **Wake Up Narcolepsy** (wakeupnarcolepsy.org): education, community events, research updates
- **Project Sleep**: workplace accommodation resources, stigma reduction
- Peer support improves QoL outcomes and reduces isolation — recommend early, not only at crisis

## Trauma-informed care: post-stimulant secondary narcolepsy
- Meth-induced hypocretin neurotoxicity creates **dual stigma**: addiction history + sleep disorder diagnosis
- Patients may internalize narcolepsy as "self-inflicted" — address shame explicitly; narcolepsy is a neurological diagnosis regardless of cause
- SUD recovery context: some narcolepsy medications (modafinil, solriamfetol) have low abuse potential; oxybates and amphetamines require careful weighing with addiction history — coordinate with SUD treatment team
- Trauma-informed approach: build safety, transparency, and collaborative goal-setting before behavioral prescriptions; avoid authoritarian sleep schedules that feel punitive
- See `narc-secondary-stimulant` for pharmacological considerations specific to this population

## Screening and tracking tools
| Tool | Purpose | Frequency |
|------|---------|-----------|
| Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) | EDS severity (0–24; ≥10 abnormal) | Each visit |
| FOSQ-10 | Functional impact on daily life | Quarterly |
| PHQ-9 / GAD-7 | Comorbid depression/anxiety | Quarterly |
| Cataplexy trigger log | Patient-maintained; identifies patterns | Ongoing |
