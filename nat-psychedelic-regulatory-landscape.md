# Psychedelic Therapy Regulatory Landscape

## Core Concepts
- **FDA Breakthrough Therapy Designation (BTD)**: expedited development/review for serious conditions with preliminary clinical evidence of substantial improvement over available therapy; does NOT guarantee approval
- **Schedule I status** (CSA 1970): psilocybin, MDMA, LSD, DMT — no accepted medical use, high abuse potential; clinical use requires DEA Schedule I researcher registration (Form 225)
- **Complete Response Letter (CRL)**: FDA mechanism to decline approval and request additional data — does not prohibit resubmission; issued to Lykos August 9, 2024
- **State-level service center model**: parallel regulatory track operating under state law despite federal Schedule I status; Oregon and Colorado are the only states with operational frameworks as of 2025
- **Rescheduling vs. approval**: FDA drug approval does not automatically reschedule; HHS must recommend rescheduling to DEA, which then initiates rulemaking

## Mechanisms / Biology
- Psilocybin (5-HT₂A agonist) and MDMA (serotonin/dopamine/norepinephrine releaser) act via distinct receptor profiles; neither is an opioid or benzodiazepine
- **MDMA** approved for 3 BTDs: PTSD (2017), eating disorders; cardiotoxicity and potential for therapist sexual misconduct flagged in Lykos trials
- **Psilocybin** holds BTD for treatment-resistant depression (COMPASS Pathways, 2018) and MDD (Usona Institute, 2019); COMP360 Phase 3 data expected 2025
- Functional unblinding is a core trial design problem: participants reliably identify active drug, undermining placebo control — cited in both FDA Advisory Committee (June 2024) and CRL
- DEA annually sets **Aggregate Production Quotas (APQ)** for Schedule I substances; 2026 final rule increased legal production of psilocybin and DMT for research

## Clinical Picture
- PTSD treatment gap: ~30–40% of patients fail to respond to SSRIs/SNRIs or psychotherapy; MDMA Phase 3 trials (MAPP1/MAPP2) showed ~67–71% response vs. ~32–48% placebo at 18-week primary endpoint
- COMPASS Pathways COMP360 Phase 2b: 25 mg psilocybin produced MADRS score reduction of –6.6 vs. placebo at 3 weeks (p<0.001) in treatment-resistant depression; Phase 3 enrollment ongoing
- Oregon Measure 109 (passed Nov 2020, ORS 475A): first licenses issued May 2023; first client access June 2023; psilocybin only, no diagnosis required, facilitator model (not prescriber)
- Colorado Prop 122 (Natural Medicine Health Act, Nov 2022): psilocybin, psilocin, DMT, ibogaine, mescaline (non-peyote); healing centers operational 2024; personal cultivation/possession decriminalized
- At least 36 psychedelics-related bills introduced across 12+ states in 2025 legislative session; Minnesota and California among states advancing regulated access frameworks

## Evidence & Treatment
- **Lykos CRL (Aug 9, 2024)**: FDA demanded a new Phase 3 RCT for MDMA/PTSD; primary grounds — functional unblinding, incomplete safety data, 3 MDMA trial papers retracted from *Psychopharmacology* Aug 10, 2024, ethical violations in trial conduct
- **DEA Schedule I researcher registration** (Form 225): required for any clinical administration of Schedule I substances; sponsors (Lykos, COMPASS, Usona) hold institutional Schedule I licenses; individual therapist-administrators work under sponsor's Schedule I registration, not independent DEA licenses
- **Expanded Access/Compassionate Use**: possible pathway for psilocybin outside trials; FDA has approved individual patient INDs for psilocybin in terminal illness cases
- **Executive Order on psychedelics (April 2026)**: directed HHS/VA/DOD to prioritize psychedelic research; signaled federal openness to rescheduling for therapeutic use; does not alter Schedule I status
- **Ibogaine**: Colorado Prop 122 inclusion notable; VA-funded RCT in veterans showed dramatic PTSD/TBI results (2024 Nature Medicine); Schedule I federally, but legal in Mexico where U.S. clinics operate

## Key Facts
- **August 9, 2024**: FDA CRL issued to Lykos for midomafetamine (MDMA) capsules + therapy for PTSD — first psychedelic NDA to reach FDA decision stage
- **25 mg psilocybin** is COMPASS Phase 3 dose; 1 mg used as active control (sub-perceptual)
- Oregon facilitators require 160+ hours of state-approved training; no prescriber license needed; sessions average 4–8 hours
- Colorado healing centers: minimum 4-hour preparation session + supervised experience session + 1 integration session required under regulations
- DEA APQ for psilocybin research: increased from ~3,000g (2024) to higher levels in 2026 final rule, reflecting expanded trial pipeline
- Psilocybin rescheduling would require FDA approval OR independent HHS scientific/medical evaluation → DEA rulemaking (18–24 month typical timeline post-HHS recommendation)
