# HLA-Autoimmune Etiology of Narcolepsy

## Core Concepts

- **Narcolepsy type 1 (NT1)** results from selective destruction of ~70,000 orexin/hypocretin-producing neurons in the lateral hypothalamus — up to 95% loss confirmed on postmortem
- **HLA-DQB1*06:02** is present in >90% of NT1 patients vs ~25% of the general population; homozygosity confers further risk; HLA association is the strongest known for any neurological disorder
- **T-cell receptor alpha (TCRA) locus** is the second major genetic risk factor — polymorphisms in TRAJ24 and TRBV4-2 increase risk, implicating antigen-specific T-cell responses
- **Autoimmune trigger model**: genetic susceptibility (HLA + TCR) + environmental antigen exposure → autoreactive T-cell activation → targeted neuronal killing
- NT1 is classified as *probable autoimmune* (not confirmed) because a reproducible disease-specific autoantigen has not been consistently identified

## Mechanisms / Biology

- **HLA-DQB1*06:02** presents orexin-derived peptides to CD4+ T helper cells; HLA class I alleles (B*35:01, A*11:01) implicated in CD8+ cytotoxic T-cell killing of orexin neurons
- **Autoreactive CD4+ T cells** recognizing orexin prepropeptide fragments found at elevated frequency in NT1 patient blood vs healthy HLA-matched controls
- **CD8+ T cells** recognizing peptides uniquely expressed by orexin neurons identified; class I-restricted cytotoxicity is the proposed final effector mechanism
- **Molecular mimicry** between influenza hemagglutinin (H1N1 strain AS03-adjuvanted) epitopes and orexin neuron surface antigens is the leading mechanistic hypothesis for vaccine-triggered cases
- **Bystander activation and epitope spreading** are alternative/complementary immune trigger mechanisms; natural H1N1 infection (not just vaccine) also raised NT1 incidence ~3-fold in China 2009–2010
- Neuronal loss is largely complete before cataplexy onset; ~80% of orexin neurons already gone by symptom appearance, explaining why early immunotherapy window is narrow

## Clinical Picture

- **NT1 prevalence**: ~1 in 2,000 (0.02–0.05%) in Western populations; onset peaks in adolescence (age 15) with a secondary peak ~35 years
- **Pandemrix (AS03-adjuvanted H1N1 vaccine)** caused a 5–14× increase in NT1 incidence among children/adolescents in Finland, Sweden, Ireland, and France (2009–2010); all post-vaccine cases carried HLA-DQB1*06:02
- Diagnostic hallmarks: CSF orexin-A ≤110 pg/mL (sensitivity ~87%, specificity ~99% for NT1), MSLT mean sleep latency ≤8 min with ≥2 sleep-onset REM periods
- Latency from presumed immune trigger to symptom onset averages 4–6 months in post-Pandemrix cases, consistent with a progressive autoimmune process rather than acute neurotoxicity
- No consistent serum or CSF autoantibodies (anti-orexin, anti-HCRTR2) have been validated for clinical diagnostic use despite multiple candidate studies

## Evidence & Treatment

- **Immunotherapy case series** (IVIg, steroids, plasmapheresis): anecdotal reports of symptom stabilization when initiated within weeks of onset; no randomized controlled trials completed as of 2025
- **Orexin receptor agonists** now represent the most promising symptomatic approach: oveporexton (OX2R-selective) achieved near-normalization of Epworth scores in Phase 3 (NEJM 2024); TAK-861 Phase 3 (FirstLight Study) ongoing
- Standard symptomatic treatment (sodium oxybate, modafinil, pitolisant, solriamfetol) does not address underlying neurodegeneration — purely compensatory
- **Early immunotherapy window hypothesis**: if >20% orexin neurons survive at onset, immune suppression may preserve residual function; this is the rationale for ongoing pediatric immunotherapy protocols
- 251-fold increased NT1 risk for HLA-DQB1*06:02 carriers is among the highest HLA odds ratios in autoimmune disease, supporting a causative rather than merely associative role

## Key Facts

- >90% of NT1 patients carry HLA-DQB1*06:02; ~25% population carrier rate → allele necessary but far from sufficient
- CSF orexin ≤110 pg/mL diagnostic threshold: rules out NT2 (normal orexin), idiopathic hypersomnia, and most secondary causes
- Post-Pandemrix NT1 incidence increase: 5–14× in children; ~3× increase also seen after natural H1N1 infection in China — implicates the H1N1 virus itself, not solely vaccine adjuvant
- Orexin neuron loss at death in NT1: median ~94%; loss is permanent — no evidence of regeneration or remyelination
- GWAS identifies >100 HLA haplotypes linked to NT1 risk; DQA1*01:02 almost always co-inherited with DQB1*06:02 as a risk haplotype
