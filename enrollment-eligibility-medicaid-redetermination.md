# Medicaid Eligibility Redetermination and Renewal

## Overview

All Medicaid enrollees must have their eligibility redetermined periodically to confirm they still qualify. The redetermination process is distinct from the initial application — it verifies ongoing eligibility using existing data when possible.

## Standard Redetermination Frequency (2026)

- **Most enrollees**: Annual redetermination (once every 12 months)
- **Expansion adults** (ages 19–64, no disability, ACA Medicaid expansion): Semi-annual redeterminations beginning **December 31, 2026**, per the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R.1, signed July 4, 2025)
- **Children, pregnant women, seniors, people with disabilities**: Remain on annual cycles

## The Redetermination Process

**Step 1 — Ex Parte Review (Automatic)**
States must first attempt to verify eligibility automatically using available data sources:
- Social Security Administration wage and benefits data
- IRS income data (through the Federal Data Hub)
- State wage records
- Other government data matches

If data confirms continued eligibility, coverage is renewed **without requiring action from the enrollee**. This is the preferred pathway.

**Step 2 — Prepopulated Renewal Form (If Ex Parte Fails)**
If automatic verification is insufficient, the state sends a prepopulated renewal form with the enrollee's information filled in. The enrollee must:
- Verify the information is correct (or correct it)
- Sign and return by the stated deadline
- Provide documentation if income or household has changed

**Step 3 — Termination (If No Response)**
If the enrollee does not respond by the deadline, coverage terminates. The state must:
- Send timely notice before termination
- Allow at least 10 days (30 days in some states) to respond

## Post-COVID Unwinding (2023–2026)

During COVID-19 (2020–2023), states were prohibited from disenrolling Medicaid enrollees (continuous enrollment provision). When this ended March 31, 2023, states began "unwinding" — conducting all the paused redeterminations.

- States had until December 31, 2025 to complete unwinding
- Millions of enrollees lost coverage, many for **procedural reasons** (returned mail, changed address) rather than actual ineligibility
- As of 2026, states have returned to regular operations; CMS requires full compliance with renewal regulations by December 31, 2026

## What Happens If You Lose Medicaid

1. You receive a **notice of adverse action** before termination (usually 10+ days notice)
2. You have the right to **appeal** — coverage continues during a timely appeal
3. If coverage ends, you can **reapply at any time** — Medicaid has no annual enrollment period
4. If disenrolled for **procedural reasons** (never received notice, form lost): many states allow **90-day reinstatement** without reapplication if you respond within 90 days of termination
5. If you lose Medicaid and are not eligible for employer coverage: a **Marketplace SEP** is triggered; you can enroll in an ACA plan (with APTC if income-eligible)

## Semi-Annual Redetermination Starting 2027

Under H.R.1 (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, 2025):
- ACA Medicaid expansion adults (ages 19–64, not disabled) must be redetermined **every 6 months** starting December 31, 2026
- States must notify enrollees in advance and stand up new systems
- Expected to increase coverage churn and administrative burden
- Exemptions: pregnancy, seniors 65+, people with disabilities, children under 19

## Tips for Enrollees

- **Update your address** with your state Medicaid agency — a significant share of procedural terminations occur because renewal notices are mailed to an old address
- **Watch for renewal notices** in the mail or through your state's online portal; respond immediately
- **Allow ex parte to work** — if your income and household haven't changed, coverage may auto-renew
- **Know your right to appeal** — if coverage is terminated, you can request a fair hearing; coverage typically continues while a timely appeal is pending
- **Reapply promptly** — losing Medicaid is not permanent; reapply immediately if circumstances change

## NC-Specific Notes

North Carolina uses NC FAST (Families Accessing Services through Technology) for Medicaid redeterminations. NC Medicaid expansion launched October 2023; expansion adults in NC will be subject to semi-annual redeterminations starting December 31, 2026. NC residents can update their information and complete renewals online at epass.nc.gov.
