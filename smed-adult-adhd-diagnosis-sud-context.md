# Adult ADHD Diagnosis in Substance Use Disorder Context

## Core Concepts

- ADHD and SUD produce overlapping symptoms: inattention, impulsivity, poor working memory, and emotional dysregulation are present in both active intoxication and withdrawal states
- Stimulant intoxication mimics ADHD inattention reversal; stimulant withdrawal (especially meth) mimics inattentive ADHD with hypersomnia, cognitive slowing, and poor concentration
- DSM-5 requires symptom onset before age 12 and impairment in two or more settings — both criteria demand retrospective validation that is compromised by memory distortion and motivation to obtain stimulant prescriptions
- Clinician bias runs in both directions: over-suspicion of drug-seeking produces underdiagnosis in SUD populations; ADHD prevalence in incarcerated populations reaches 25–45%, far above the ~5% general adult rate
- A multi-method protocol (structured interview + informant report + rating scales + cognitive battery) improves diagnostic validity more than any single instrument

## Mechanisms / Neurobiology

- Dopaminergic deficits underlie both disorders: ADHD involves hypofunctional PFC dopamine tone; chronic stimulant SUD downregulates DAT and D2 receptors — the resulting state resembles ADHD phenotypically even post-detox
- Stimulant withdrawal produces a hypodopaminergic crash lasting days to weeks that directly mimics inattentive ADHD; adequate washout (typically ≥4 weeks for methamphetamine) is required before symptom attribution is valid
- Prefrontal executive dysfunction caused by chronic meth or alcohol use overlaps with the cognitive profile of ADHD (inhibitory control, working memory, processing speed) and can persist months into abstinence
- Childhood ADHD is a robust independent risk factor for SUD onset: meta-analyses show ~2–3x elevated SUD risk; this etiological link supports thorough retrospective evaluation rather than dismissal

## Clinical Picture

- ADHD prevalence in adults seeking SUD treatment: 20–35% across studies; prevalence in incarcerated populations: 25–45%
- Rating scales used in SUD context: **DIVA 2.0 / DIVA-5** (structured diagnostic interview, DSM-IV/DSM-5, requires informant co-report); **CAARS** (self-report + observer, sensitivity ~97%, specificity ~83% vs. clinical interview gold standard); **WURS** (Wender Utah Rating Scale, retrospective childhood symptom recall, sensitivity ~88%, specificity ~70%)
- Cognitive testing (CPT, neuropsychological battery) has reduced validity during early abstinence — substance-induced deficits can produce false-positive ADHD profiles; testing is most reliable after ≥4 weeks abstinence, ideally longer for meth or heavy alcohol
- Retrospective childhood symptom reporting is vulnerable to telescoping (recalling adult symptoms as childhood) and intentional distortion; collateral informants (parents, siblings, school records) substantially improve validity and are a required component of DIVA 2.0
- DSM-5 requires ≥5 inattentive or hyperactive-impulsive symptoms (adults), onset before 12, impairment in two settings; clinicians must establish that symptoms predate or are independent of substance use — the hardest determination in active SUD

## Evidence & Treatment

- A combined model using ASRS screening + cognitive battery + DIVA interview achieves sensitivity ~90% and specificity ~81% in outpatient ADHD evaluation settings
- European guidelines (EUNETHYDIS) and Canadian Network guidelines recommend waiting at least 4 weeks of abstinence before formal neuropsychological testing; structured interviews (DIVA) can begin earlier if supplemented with informant data
- Underdiagnosis in SUD settings has documented consequences: untreated ADHD predicts worse SUD treatment retention, higher relapse rates, and more treatment episodes
- The ASRS-v1.1 has a high false-negative rate in SUD populations — a negative screen does not rule out ADHD; the 6-item screener should not be used as the sole exclusion criterion
- Non-stimulant ADHD treatment (atomoxetine, bupropion, viloxazine) is appropriate during early recovery when stimulant prescription carries unacceptable diversion/misuse risk

## Key Facts

- ADHD prevalence in SUD treatment settings: ~20–35%; in criminal justice / incarcerated populations: 25–45%
- Recommended abstinence before formal cognitive testing: ≥4 weeks minimum; longer preferred for methamphetamine or heavy alcohol (given protracted neurotoxicity)
- CAARS sensitivity 97% / specificity 83%; WURS sensitivity 88% / specificity 70% (relative to clinical interview)
- DSM-5 adult ADHD threshold: ≥5 symptoms (vs. ≥6 for children), onset before age 12, two-setting impairment
- Collateral informant data (parent, sibling, school record) is a required component of valid retrospective childhood ADHD assessment — single-informant self-report is insufficient in SUD populations
