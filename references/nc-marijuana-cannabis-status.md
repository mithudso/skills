# NC Marijuana and Cannabis Legal Status (2026)

> **Educational only, NOT legal advice.** CURRENCY WARNING: This is an actively changing area of law. Verified-as-of: 2026-06-22. Always confirm with current NC General Assembly session status.

---

## Bottom Line (2026)

**Recreational marijuana is fully illegal in North Carolina.** There is no medical marijuana program. NC has no licensed cannabis dispensaries and no legal path to personal possession, cultivation, or purchase under state law. However, the political landscape is shifting rapidly.

---

## Schedule Classification (GS 90-94)

Marijuana is classified as a **Schedule VI controlled substance** under GS 90-94 — NC's unique sixth schedule with no federal equivalent. The federal Controlled Substances Act places marijuana in Schedule I. This Schedule VI classification reflects the legislature's view that marijuana has a "low public-health risk" compared to higher schedules, explaining the lower-tier penalties for simple possession.

**Schedule VI also includes:** THC (delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol, the psychoactive compound), hashish, and resin extracted from marijuana.

---

## Possession Penalties (GS 90-95 / GS 90-95(d)(4))

| Quantity | Classification | Punishment |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 0.5 oz marijuana | **Class 3 misdemeanor** | Fine only (max $200); any jail sentence must be suspended |
| > 0.5 oz, ≤ 1.5 oz marijuana | **Class 1 misdemeanor** | Up to 120 days jail + fines |
| > 1.5 oz marijuana | **Class I felony** | 3–12 months under Structured Sentencing |
| ≤ 1/20 oz hashish | Class 3 misdemeanor | Fine only |
| > 1/20 oz, ≤ 3/20 oz hashish | Class 1 misdemeanor | |
| > 3/20 oz hashish OR any synthetic THC | Class I felony | |

**Key points:**
- Even at the Class 3 level (≤ 0.5 oz), marijuana possession is still illegal and carries a criminal record
- "Decriminalization" in NC means fine-only, not legal — arrest and charges remain possible
- Simple possession under 1.5 oz may qualify for conditional discharge under GS 90-96

## Trafficking Threshold for Marijuana (GS 90-95(h)(1))

- > 10 lbs = trafficking (separate from simple possession — see [nc-drug-trafficking-thresholds](nc-drug-trafficking-thresholds.md))

## Hemp and CBD (GS Chapter 106 / Farm Bill Framework)

**Industrial hemp** (< 0.3% delta-9 THC by dry weight) is federally legal under the 2018 Farm Bill and legal in NC under GS Chapter 106. Hemp-derived CBD products with < 0.3% THC are legal to purchase and possess in NC.

### CRITICAL — November 12, 2026 Federal Hemp Changes

Federal legislation (P.L. 119-37, effective November 12, 2026) will redefine "hemp" using **total THC** (including THCa) with a **0.4 mg per container cap**. This will effectively ban:
- All THCa flower products
- Most hemp-derived Delta-8 THC products
- Delta-9 products above the new cap

After November 12, 2026, hemp products exceeding the new total-THC threshold will be federally classified as marijuana, making them illegal under federal law. NC state law enforcement and prosecution may follow.

### Delta-8 THC and THCa (Current Status — Subject to Change)

- Delta-8 THC derived from hemp: currently legal in NC if derived from compliant hemp (< 0.3% delta-9)
- THCa flower: legal in NC through November 11, 2026 under hemp exemption; **illegal under federal law after November 12, 2026**
- NC legislative session 2025-2026 attempted to create state hemp regulatory framework (HB 328) but it died April 21, 2026

---

## Medical Marijuana: NC Compassionate Care Act

NC has repeatedly considered but not passed a medical marijuana program. The NC Compassionate Care Act has been introduced in multiple legislative sessions (2021, 2023, 2025) but has not been enacted. As of June 2026, there is no legal medical marijuana program in NC.

---

## NC Advisory Council on Cannabis (2026 Development)

- **June 2025:** Governor Josh Stein created the NC Advisory Council on Cannabis to study cannabis programs from other states and propose a framework for adult-use legalization
- **April 2026:** Council released its **interim report recommending legalization** of THC products for adults
- **Current status:** No legislation has been passed; the General Assembly must act for any change in law

---

## Sale, Distribution, Trafficking

- PWIMSD marijuana (all quantities): Class I felony (if Sch. VI)
- Trafficking marijuana (> 10 lbs): mandatory minimums apply — see [nc-drug-trafficking-thresholds](nc-drug-trafficking-thresholds.md)
- Sale near schools/daycares: enhanced penalties

---

## Federal vs. NC Law Conflict

Marijuana remains Schedule I under federal law regardless of NC's Schedule VI classification. Federal prosecution is possible in NC for marijuana offenses (though rare for personal-use quantities). Federal employees, federal contractors, and residents of federally assisted housing face federal law consequences separate from NC criminal penalties.
