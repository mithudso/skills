# NC Post-Conviction Relief

> **Educational only — NOT legal advice.** For any specific post-conviction matter, consult a licensed North Carolina attorney.

Post-conviction relief in North Carolina encompasses several distinct pathways: direct appeals, the Motion for Appropriate Relief (MAR), the NC Innocence Inquiry Commission, wrongful conviction compensation, and federal habeas corpus. Each has its own timing rules, grounds, and forum. This reference covers all major pathways.

`verified-as-of: 2026-06-22`

---

## 1. The NC Direct Appeal System

### Right to Appeal Criminal Convictions

A defendant convicted in **Superior Court** has an appeal of right to the **NC Court of Appeals** (NCCOA). A defendant convicted in **District Court** after a bench trial appeals to Superior Court for a de novo trial. [^1]

### Notice of Appeal — Rule 4 (NC App. Rules, effective Apr. 1, 2025)

**Oral notice:** Given "at trial" (i.e., within the same session of Superior Court, typically within one week of sentencing). Most common method. [^2]

**Written notice:** Filed within **14 days** after entry of judgment; served on the State. Missing this deadline is jurisdictional and forfeits the right to direct appeal.

- Service must be on the district attorney.
- Written notice must identify the court appealed from, the judgment, and the court appealed to.

**Consequence of missing Rule 4 deadline:** Loss of appeal of right. Defendant must seek a **Writ of Certiorari** (Rule 21) from the COA as a discretionary remedy, showing merit and why the deadline was missed.

### NC Court of Appeals (NCCOA)

- Three-judge panels hear most criminal appeals.
- Issues raised must have been **preserved at trial** (objection, motion, etc.) with limited exceptions (plain error for unpreserved constitutional issues if fundamental rights affected).
- **Plain error standard** (unpreserved): Error so fundamental it denied defendant a fair trial and probably affected the verdict.
- Briefing deadlines set by COA schedule; record on appeal typically due 35 days after docketing.

### NC Supreme Court Review

- **Mandatory review:** Cases where NCCOA split (dissent on legal issue); death penalty cases.
- **Discretionary review (PDR — Petition for Discretionary Review):** Filed within **15 days** of COA mandate. Filed with NCSSC. Granted when case involves significant legal question.
- **Certiorari to NCSSC (Rule 21):** Available when no right of appeal exists to the Supreme Court. Petition must show exceptional importance.

### Issues Properly Raised on Direct Appeal

| Issue Type | Direct Appeal? | Better via MAR? |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|
| Trial court errors (jury instructions, evidentiary rulings) | Yes | No |
| Ineffective assistance of counsel (IAC) | Only if record adequate | Usually MAR |
| Brady material disclosed post-trial | No | MAR (newly discovered evidence) |
| Newly discovered evidence | No | MAR |
| Sentencing errors | Yes | Yes (for illegal sentences) |
| Constitutional violations (suppression, 4th/5th/6th) | Yes (if preserved) | MAR alternative |

## 2. Motion for Appropriate Relief (MAR) — GS 15A-1411 through 15A-1422

The MAR is North Carolina's primary **collateral attack** on a conviction. It is a motion in the **original criminal case** (not a new proceeding) and is heard by a Superior Court judge, either the original trial judge or another assigned judge. [^3]

### Pre-Judgment MAR (GS 15A-1414)

- Filed **within 10 days** after entry of judgment.
- Addresses errors occurring before or during trial.
- Allows the trial judge to correct errors without requiring an appeal.
- Common uses: newly discovered evidence found immediately after verdict, reconsideration of sentence before it becomes final.

### Post-Judgment MAR (GS 15A-1415)

Filed more than 10 days after judgment. The central post-conviction mechanism.

#### Time Limits

| Case Type | Deadline | Basis |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| **Capital cases** | 120 days from the latest triggering event (judgment entry, appellate mandate, USSC denial, appointment of post-conviction counsel) | GS 15A-1415(a) |
| **Noncapital cases** | **7 years** from the latest triggering event (effective for judgments on/after December 1, 2025) | GS 15A-1415(a1) [^4] |
| Extension | Up to 30 days presumptively; longer for extraordinary circumstances | Court discretion |
| Hearing | Must be held within **24 months** of filing; continuance beyond requires written extraordinary-circumstances finding | GS 15A-1420 |

#### Grounds for Post-Judgment MAR (GS 15A-1415(b))

1. The acts charged did not constitute a criminal violation at the time committed
2. Trial court lacked **personal jurisdiction** over the defendant or **subject matter jurisdiction**
3. Conviction violated the **U.S. Constitution** or **NC Constitution** (includes IAC, Brady, coerced confessions, suggestive lineups)
4. Statute of conviction is unconstitutional
5. Conduct for which defendant was convicted was **constitutionally protected**
6. Sentence was **unauthorized, improper, or illegal** as a matter of law
7. Conviction resulted from human trafficking victimization (nonviolent offenses)

#### Claims Raisable at ANY Time (No Deadline)

Under GS 15A-1415(c):
- **Newly discovered evidence** unknown or unavailable at trial that could not have been found with due diligence, including **recanted testimony**, with a direct and material bearing on guilt or innocence
- **Significant retroactive change in law** (substantive or procedural) with retroactive application
- **Fully served sentence** + fundamental miscarriage of justice
- With **district attorney's consent**: any ground at any time

#### Procedure (GS 15A-1420)

1. File written MAR in the original case, in the county of conviction.
2. Serve on the State (district attorney).
3. State responds; court may allow reply.
4. Court may grant **without hearing** if the motion, files, and record conclusively show defendant is not entitled to relief.
5. If a hearing is required: defendant may subpoena witnesses, present evidence; rules of evidence apply; defendant has burden of proof.
6. **Attorney-client privilege is automatically waived** for IAC claims — counsel may testify about strategy decisions.

#### Remedies Available (GS 15A-1417)

- Vacate the conviction (State may retry unless charges also dismissed)
- Vacate and dismiss charges
- Order a new sentencing hearing
- Correct an illegal sentence
- Order a new trial on specific issues

## 3. Ineffective Assistance of Counsel (IAC) Claims

IAC is the most common ground for post-conviction MAR in NC. The standard is federal (*Strickland v. Washington*, 466 U.S. 668 (1984)) and NC has expressly adopted it under both the U.S. Sixth Amendment and the NC Constitution. [^5]

### The Strickland Two-Prong Test

**Prong 1 — Deficient Performance:**
Counsel's performance fell below **an objective standard of reasonableness** under prevailing professional norms. NC courts apply a strong presumption that counsel's conduct was reasonable professional assistance.

Common deficient-performance findings:
- Failure to investigate alibi or exculpatory witnesses
- Failure to file a suppression motion when Fourth Amendment violation is clear
- Failure to object to inadmissible evidence that clearly prejudiced defendant
- Failure to advise defendant of immigration consequences of a plea (*Padilla v. Kentucky*)
- Failure to advise defendant of the right to appeal

**Prong 2 — Prejudice:**
"A reasonable probability that, but for counsel's errors, the outcome of the trial or proceeding would have been different." A "reasonable probability" is less than "more likely than not" but sufficient to undermine confidence in the outcome.

### When to Raise IAC

- **Direct appeal:** Only when the record on appeal is **sufficient** to evaluate the claim without additional facts. Rare in practice.
- **MAR:** The typical vehicle. Allows factual development through an evidentiary hearing, subpoenas, and counsel's own testimony (privilege waived).
- If raised on direct appeal and dismissed without prejudice, it may be re-raised by MAR.

### IAC in Plea Context

Under *Hill v. Lockhart* (applying Strickland):
- Deficient performance: Counsel gave wrong advice about immigration consequences, sentence exposure, or parole eligibility.
- Prejudice: Defendant would have rejected the plea and gone to trial but for the error.

## 4. DNA Testing for Post-Conviction Innocence (GS 15A-269)

NC allows a convicted person to petition the trial court for **post-conviction DNA testing** of biological evidence when:
- The evidence is material and related to the investigation/prosecution leading to the conviction.
- The identity of the perpetrator was at issue at trial OR the evidence would establish factual innocence.
- Technology was not reasonably available at trial OR the evidence was not previously tested.

If testing results are favorable, the court may grant a new MAR on newly discovered evidence grounds. The NC Innocence Inquiry Commission often uses DNA evidence in its review process.

## 5. NC Innocence Inquiry Commission (GS 15A-1460 through 15A-1475)

The NC Innocence Inquiry Commission (NCIIC) is the **only state-funded innocence commission in the United States**, established in 2006. It is an entirely separate pathway from the MAR — it investigates factual innocence, not legal error. [^6]

### Eligibility (GS 15A-1461)

- Conviction in a **NC state court** for a **felony**
- Applicant is **living** and claims **complete factual innocence** (no criminal responsibility whatsoever — not just reduced culpability)
- Credible and verifiable evidence of innocence exists that was **not heard at trial or in any prior post-conviction proceeding**
- Applicant signs required agreements

### Multi-Stage Review Process

| Stage | Who Decides | Standard |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| **Stage 1: Initial Staff Review** | Commission staff and director | Meets basic eligibility |
| **Stage 2: Eight-Member Commission Review** | Commission (prosecutors, defense attorneys, judges, victim advocate, sheriff, public member) | Majority vote to advance |
| **Stage 3: Three-Judge Superior Court Panel** | Three Superior Court judges, all three must agree | "Clear and convincing evidence" of innocence |

- **Inquisitorial process:** Commission has broad subpoena power; can compel witnesses and evidence.
- **Outcome if three-judge panel finds innocence:** Charges dismissed; defendant declared innocent; cannot be retried.
- As of April 2025: **16 exonerations** since the Commission's founding. [^7]
- **No time limit** and the Commission may investigate even cases where other post-conviction remedies have been exhausted.

### NCIIC vs. MAR — Key Distinction

| Feature | MAR | NCIIC |
|---------|-----|-------|
| Focus | Legal error in the process | Factual innocence |
| Forum | Superior Court (original county) | Independent commission → three-judge panel |
| Standard | Varies by ground | Clear and convincing evidence of innocence |
| Can retry after grant? | Yes (unless charges dismissed) | No — declared innocent |
| Time limit | 7 years (noncapital) | None |

### NC Center on Actual Innocence (NCCAI)

A nonprofit (not a state agency) that investigates, advocates for, and represents wrongfully convicted individuals. Works in partnership with the NCIIC process and provides direct legal services. <https://nccai.org/>

## 6. Wrongful Conviction Compensation (GS 148-84 through 148-84.3)

Persons exonerated through certain pathways may seek compensation from the NC General Assembly through the **Industrial Commission**. [^8]

### Eligibility

- Received a **pardon of innocence** from the Governor after a conviction, OR
- Exonerated by the three-judge panel via the Innocence Inquiry Commission process

### Compensation Formula (GS 148-84)

- **$50,000 for each year** of wrongful incarceration (prorated for partial years)
- **Maximum: $750,000** total
- Includes **job skills training** and **tuition waivers** at NC community colleges and universities (for wrongfully incarcerated person and their dependent children)
- Must file a claim with the NC Industrial Commission

### Limitations

- Compensation is not available for conviction reversal on legal error alone (must have pardon of innocence or Innocence Commission exoneration)
- Awards are subject to Industrial Commission review and legislative appropriation
- Compensation critics have noted the cap has not been updated for inflation since the statute was enacted

## 7. Federal Habeas Corpus (28 U.S.C. § 2254)

After exhausting NC state remedies, a defendant may petition a **federal district court** for a writ of habeas corpus based on federal constitutional violations.

### Key Requirements

- **Exhaustion:** Must present the federal claim to the NC courts first (typically via MAR and direct appeal)
- **One-year statute of limitations** from final state court judgment (AEDPA)
- **Deferential standard:** Federal court defers to state court findings unless they are "contrary to, or an unreasonable application of, clearly established Federal law" (28 U.S.C. § 2254(d))
- **Procedural default:** Claims not properly presented to state courts may be defaulted (barred in federal court) unless cause and prejudice or actual innocence is shown

## 8. Quick Reference — Choosing the Right Pathway

| Situation | Best Pathway |
|-----------|-------------|
| Trial court error (preserved) | Direct appeal — Rule 4, 14 days |
| Trial court error (unpreserved) | Plain error on direct appeal; or Rule 21 certiorari if appeal missed |
| IAC claim | MAR (GS 15A-1415) — allows factual development |
| Newly discovered evidence | MAR — no time limit for new evidence |
| Actual innocence — new evidence | MAR + NCIIC application |
| Brady violation discovered post-trial | MAR |
| Illegal or unauthorized sentence | MAR — can be raised any time |
| Exoneration/pardon needed for compensation | Innocence Inquiry Commission → pardon of innocence |
